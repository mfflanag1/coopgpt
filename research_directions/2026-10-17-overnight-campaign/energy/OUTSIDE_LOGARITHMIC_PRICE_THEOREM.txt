SHARP LOGARITHMIC AGGREGATE PRICES FOR COALITION WITHHOLDING AND OUTSIDE PRODUCTION
17 October 2026. Analytic theorem checked by root and energy lane; independent
Fable audit found no correctness defect. Minor wording clarifications applied.
Historical priority remains unconfirmed. The architecture lower below is NOT
an unrestricted mechanism lower bound.

1. FIXED-OWNER MODEL AND REQUIREMENT
Actual owner i has a fixed two-resource bundle x_i=(a_i,b_i)>=0. Aggregate
capacities are A=sum_i a_i and B=sum_i b_i, and production is F=min(A,B).
Owners may have mixed bundles. An actual coalition S may bindingly withhold
d_i<=x_i, earn its mechanism payments at the reduced reports, and use the
withheld resources separately to produce min(sum_S d_{iA},sum_S d_{iB}).
The full coalition outside-production condition is
 sum_{i in S} y_i(x) >= sum_{i in S} y_i(x-d_S)
                       +min(sum_S d_{iA},sum_S d_{iB}).       (OUT)
No resource is used twice. Reports, actual identities and opponents' bundles
otherwise remain fixed. No private-cost or overreport incentive is asserted.

2. CONSTANT AND RULE
Let c be the unique root in(1/2,1) of
          c[2-exp(-1/c)]=1,
and let t0=1-exp(-1/c). Thus c(1+t0)=1. Numerically,
 c=0.5430633898250876..., t0=0.8414056604369606....
Existence and uniqueness: f(c)=c[2-exp(-1/c)] is continuous, f(1/2)<1<f(1),
and f'(c)=2-exp(-1/c)(1+1/c)>1, since exp(x)>1+x for x>0.

For0<t<=1 define
 p(t)=ct,
 q(t)=1-c-c(1-t)log(1-t)/t,             0<t<=t0;
      c/t,                             t0<=t<=1.
Put q(0)=1 and p(0)=0 by continuity. At t=t0 the two q formulas and their
first derivatives agree. At t=1, p(1)=q(1)=c.

When A>=B>0 put t=B/A and pay
          y_i=p(t)a_i+q(t)b_i.
When B>=A>0 exchange the two resource labels. When min(A,B)=0 set y_i=0.
The rule is symmetric under exchange of the two resource types. It treats
actual identities only through their bundles; no strategic splitting or
merging is used in the proof.

THEOREM. At every nonnegative real profile, this rule has nonnegative payments,
covers every actual-owner coalition, satisfies(OUT), and has total funding
          sum_i y_i <= (1+c)F =1.5430633898250876... F.       (3)
Both owner counts and capacities are unrestricted. The displayed constant is
the exact supremum for this rule. Within the continuous symmetric homogeneous
aggregate-price class specified in Section6, no smaller constant is possible.

3. PRICE IDENTITIES, COVERAGE AND BUDGET
On the logarithmic branch,
 q'(t)=c[log(1-t)+t]/t^2 <0,
 q(t)+tq'(t)=1+c log(1-t)>=0,
 p(t)+q(t)+t(1-t)q'(t)=1.                              (4)
The first inequality is log(1-t)<-t; the second uses t<=t0.
The limiting derivative at zero is-c/2, for example by the power series for
log(1-t). At t0, q+tq'=0, and
 t0 q(t0)=1-c t0=c,
which verifies both value and derivative agreement with q=c/t.
On the second branch q'<0, q+tq'=0 and
 p+q+t(1-t)q'=c(1+t)>=c(1+t0)=1.                        (5)
Therefore q decreases continuously from1 to c, and all prices are positive.

Equations(4)-(5) and q'<=0 imply p+q>=1. For any coalition with aggregate
holdings(a,b), its payment ap+bq>=min(a,b)(p+q)>=min(a,b), proving coverage.
For A>=B, total funding is
 Ap(B/A)+Bq(B/A)=B[c+q(t)]<=(1+c)B.
Symmetry proves the other orientation. The ratio tends1+c as t decreases to0,
so its supremum is exactly the claimed constant. At zero production every
owner's payment tends0 by0<=y_i<=sum_j y_j<=(1+c)F; thus the boundary rule is
continuous and satisfies coverage.

4. FULL COALITION OUTSIDE-PRODUCTION INCENTIVES
Fix S and let a,b be its aggregate currently reported holdings. On A>B>0,
write alpha=a/A and beta=b/B, both in[0,1]. If an own coalition coordinate
increases, differentiate its TOTAL payment Y=ap(t)+bq(t):
 g_A=partial_A Y=p-alpha t p'-beta t^2 q',
 g_B=partial_B Y=q+alpha p'+beta t q'.                  (6)
Here an A increase raises both a and A; a B increase raises both b and B.
Since p'=c>0 and q'<=0, their worst corners give
 g_A>=p-tp'=0,
 g_B>=q+tq'>=0,
 g_A+g_B>=p+q+t(1-t)q'>=1,                            (7)
using(4)-(5). The same statements hold after swapping resource labels.

For arbitrary withheld aggregate amounts(Delta_A,Delta_B), grow the coalition's
reports on the straight segment from the underreported profile to the full
profile. On every differentiable piece, (7) implies
 dY/ds=g_A Delta_A+g_B Delta_B
       >=min(Delta_A,Delta_B)(g_A+g_B)
       >=min(Delta_A,Delta_B).
There are finitely many ratio and side-switch points on this affine segment;
the formulas are continuous there. If the whole segment lies on A=B, then
Delta_A=Delta_B and Y=c(a+b), whose derivative is2c Delta_A>=Delta_A.
Zero-production endpoints are handled by the continuity established above;
a segment with permanently zero production has zero outside-production worth.
Integration proves(OUT) for arbitrary coalitions and simultaneous ownerwise
underreports. Taking a one-sided deviation also gives the usual coalition
total-payment monotonicity. This is stronger than payment-only truthfulness.

5. COMPLETE-ACCESS PUBLIC SOURCE-PRICE EXTENSION
Energy capacities have fixed public cost tiers c_j and hardware capacities have
fixed public service-value tiers v_k. Every energy tier can serve every admitted
hardware tier. Actual owners may hold arbitrary fixed mixtures of both roles.
Net coalition production is the divisible maximum of sum(v_k-c_j)z_jk, subject
to the reported capacities; unprofitable service can be left unused.
The already established exact fixed-threshold identity is
 F(x)=integral min(E_t(x),H_t(x))dt,
with source tiers c_j<=t and service tiers v_k>t. Aggregate retained tiers
within each actual owner, apply the rule above, and integrate owner payments
using the fixed public interval lengths. Coverage, funding and nonnegativity
integrate. For a withheld coalition bundle d, the same identity gives its
outside worth F(d). Integrating the two-pool OUT inequalities therefore proves
the full weighted OUT inequality with the SAME factor1+c.

This extension assumes that the withheld coalition has the same complete-access
production opportunities and public net values specified by the model. It does
not claim a guarantee on arbitrary access graphs: their threshold values are
graph-production problems, not generally a single two-pool minimum. Likewise,
the theorem imposes no extra grand-marginal participation floors. Those floors
are a separate contractual requirement and are not implied by(OUT).

6. MATCHING LOWER WITHIN THE AGGREGATE-PRICE ARCHITECTURE
Consider rules of the form y_i=a_i p(t)+b_i q(t) for A>=B>0,t=B/A, swapped
for B>=A. Assume p,q are nonnegative and continuous on(0,1], locally absolutely
continuous on every compact subinterval of(0,1), with p(1)=q(1) so the symmetric
rule joins continuously. Suppose it has a finite universal funding factor kappa
and satisfies full coalition OUT on arbitrary fixed-owner profiles. These are
the architecture assumptions; no differentiability at0 is required.

At almost every t, local coalition deviations imply the derivative constraints
in(6). The relevant corners can be obtained as limits of interior coalition
shares, so zero holdings do not license infeasible infinitesimal withdrawals.
Specifically, one-sided monotonicity at(alpha,beta)=(1,0) gives
          p-tp'>=0,
and at(0,1) gives
          q+tq'>=0.
Balanced outside-production deviations at(0,1) give
          p+q+t(1-t)q'>=1.                              (8)
Only these necessary consequences are needed; no sign assumption on p' or q'
is made for the lower bound.

The first condition says k(t)=p(t)/t is nonincreasing. Nonnegative payments and
the funding bound k(t)+q(t)<=kappa imply a finite limit
          C=lim_{t down to0} k(t),
and p(t)<=Ct. Thus(8) implies
          q+t(1-t)q'>=1-Ct.
Use the exact differential identity
 [tq(t)/(1-t)]'=[q+t(1-t)q']/(1-t)^2 >=(1-Ct)/(1-t)^2.
Integrating from an epsilon to t and sending epsilon to0 gives
          q(t)>=Q_C(t):=1-C-C(1-t)log(1-t)/t.            (9)
Indeed q is bounded by kappa, so epsilon*q(epsilon)/(1-epsilon) tends0.
The same calculation when C=0 gives q>=1. In all cases liminf_{t down to0}
q(t)>=1, and hence
          kappa>=C+1.                                  (10)

The second local condition says z(t)=tq(t) is nondecreasing. Consequently
 q(1)>=sup_{0<t<1} tQ_C(t).
If C=0 this contradicts q(1)=p(1)<=C. For C>0, differentiating
 tQ_C(t)=(1-C)t-C(1-t)log(1-t)
shows its maximum is attained at t_C=1-exp(-1/C) and equals
          1-C+C exp(-1/C).
But the continuous symmetric tie and p(1)<=C imply q(1)<=C. Therefore
          C[2-exp(-1/C)]>=1,
so C>=c by the strict monotonicity proved in Section2. Combining with(10)
yields kappa>=1+c, exactly matching the constructed rule.

7. WHAT THIS DOES AND DOES NOT ESTABLISH
This improves the root's elementary full-coalition OUT factor2 to approximately
1.543, and proves optimality within the explicitly stated aggregate-price class.
The companion UNRESTRICTED_OUTSIDE_SHARP_CANDIDATE.txt now establishes the same
constant for unrestricted mechanisms, and SHARP_MULTIPOOL_OUTSIDE_CANDIDATE.txt
extends it sharply to arbitrary resource dimension. Those lower bounds have
been independently internally audited. The price-class lower proved directly
in this note is one ingredient of that stronger conclusion.
The separate individual-OUT rule has a smaller funding constant because its
deviation requirement is weaker. No statement here supersedes that distinction.

Public linear cost reimbursement can be accounted for separately as in
ECONOMIC_SETTLEMENT.txt, but this theorem explicitly adds the withheld coalition's
nonlinear outside-production worth. Historical absence-proofness literature and
the exact economic scope require original-source and specialist comparison.
The numerical checker is a diagnostic supplement; all-real correctness and
architecture optimality rest on the analytic arguments above.
