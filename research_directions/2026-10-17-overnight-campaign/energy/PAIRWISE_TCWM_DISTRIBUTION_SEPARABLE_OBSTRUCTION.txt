EVERY IDENTICAL STRICTLY CONVEX SEPARABLE COVER PROJECTION FAILS PAIR-TCWM
17 October 2026. Analytic architecture obstruction; independent review pending.

1. EXACT SCOPE
For the two-pool technology F(A,B)=min(A,B), net each actual owner's standalone
value and consider the pure residual game
 v(Q)=min(sum_{i in Q} z_i^+, sum_{i in Q} (-z_i)^+).
The candidate selects the unique minimizer x of sum_i phi(x_i), subject to
 x_i>=0 and sum_{i in Q}x_i>=v(Q) for EVERY coalition Q.
Here phi:[0,infinity)->R is C1 and strictly convex, phi'(0)=0, and the same
report-independent phi is used for all owners. This includes every power
objective x^p/p with 1<p<infinity. No twice differentiability is needed.

THEOREM. Every such candidate fails pairwise total-payment monotonicity on
six fixed pure owners. Hence adding standalone values and applying this
projection to residuals cannot give pair-TCWM on the unrestricted mixed-owner
domain. The witness changes only one owner's report and stays pure. It does
NOT refute individual own monotonicity, report-weighted projection objectives,
or any unrestricted mechanism theorem.

2. CHOOSE SCALE AND PROFILES
The continuous strictly increasing derivative phi' has range [0,L), or [0,L]
when L=infinity is interpreted naturally. Choose v>0 small enough that
 phi'(2v)+phi'(v)<sup_{x>=0} phi'(x).
This is possible because phi'(0)=0 and strict convexity gives positive
derivatives at positive arguments. There is a unique s>2 satisfying
 phi'(sv)=phi'(2v)+phi'(v).                                  (1)
Equivalently normalize v=1 and write psi(t)=phi(vt); equation (1) becomes
 psi'(s)=psi'(2)+psi'(1). We use this normalization below; multiply capacities
and payments by v to obtain the original rule.

At the low profile take six signed capacities
 z=(-2,2,-(2+s),-(1+s),-1,2+s),
and candidate payment
 x=(1,1,2,1,1,s).                                           (2)
Restore delta>0 units of the second resource to owner 0, changing z_0 to
 -(2+delta). All other reports and all owner identities remain fixed.

For sufficiently small delta>0 there is a unique h in (0,delta) solving
 psi'(s+h)=psi'(2-h)+psi'(1+delta-h).                         (3)
Indeed the difference left-minus-right is strictly increasing in h. At h=0
it is negative by (1), and at h=delta it is positive. Existence follows from
continuity. As delta tends to zero, 0<h<delta forces h to zero.
The high-profile payment will be
 x'=(1,1,2-h,1+delta-h,1-delta,s+h).                         (4)
Choose delta<=1/2 and also small enough that
 r:=psi'(1+delta-h)-psi'(1-delta)<psi'(1).
This is possible by continuity. We have r>0 because h<delta.

3. FEASIBILITY, EXACTLY OVER ALL COALITIONS
For every s>=2 and 0<=h<=delta<=1/2, vector (4) covers the high-profile game.
This finite symbolic claim has an exact rational checker supplied alongside
this note. Here is a complete and independently reproducible proof method.

For each of the 64 masks Q, form affine functions P_Q,N_Q,X_Q of
 (1,s,delta,h), giving its positive capacity, negative capacity and payment.
The difference P_Q-N_Q has one constant sign throughout the polyhedron
 D={s>=2, 0<=h<=delta<=1/2}.
This is certified by checking its values at the three vertices
 (s,delta,h)=(2,0,0),(2,1/2,0),(2,1/2,1/2)
and its coefficient in the sole recession direction +s. Select the lesser
of P_Q,N_Q using that sign. Then X_Q-min(P_Q,N_Q) is affine and is nonnegative
at the same three vertices and in the recession direction. These statements
hold for all 64 masks in the supplied exact checker; no numerical search or
symbolic assumption about s is used. The polyhedron is exactly the convex
hull of those vertices plus the nonnegative +s ray, so this verifies every
real parameter value in D. Setting delta=h=0 also proves feasibility of (2).

The identically tight masks for (4) are 0,3,36,39,43,56,59. In particular
all masks used in the next KKT certificates are tight. At the low profile
mask56 remains tight; the three required low masks are 3,36,56.

4. GLOBAL OPTIMALITY CERTIFICATES
Masks use bit i for owner i. Write g(t)=psi'(t).
At the low profile the positive multipliers are
 lambda_3=g(1), lambda_36=g(2), lambda_56=g(1).
They give grad(sum psi(x_i))=sum_Q lambda_Q 1_Q by (1).

At the high profile use
 lambda_3=g(1)-r, lambda_36=g(2-h),
 lambda_43=r, lambda_56=g(1-delta).
All are positive by the chosen delta. Coordinates 0,1 have gradient g(1);
coordinate 2 has g(2-h); coordinate 3 has r+g(1-delta)=g(1+delta-h);
coordinate 4 has g(1-delta); and coordinate 5 has
 g(2-h)+r+g(1-delta)=g(s+h) by (3).
Thus these multipliers give exact KKT stationarity at (4).

Nonnegative multipliers, feasibility and tightness prove global optimality:
for any feasible w, convexity bounds objective(w)-objective(x) below by
 grad objective(x) dot (w-x)=sum_Q lambda_Q(w(Q)-x(Q))>=0.
Strict convexity gives uniqueness. All coordinates are positive, so the
nonnegativity constraints require no additional multipliers.

5. INCENTIVE FAILURE AND CONSEQUENCE
The protected pair {0,2} has payment 3 at the low profile and 3-h at the
high profile. Owner 0 alone restores capacity, while owner 2 changes zero;
weak coalition underreport definitions permit this protected member. Since
h>0, pairwise TCWM fails. In original units the loss is v*h.
The changed owner 0 retains payment v, so this certificate concerns the
negative payment externality on another protected owner. It is not an
individual-IC counterexample.

The obstruction is structural across all the stated objectives, including
all finite p-norm projections, rather than a failure of a chosen exponent.
Any successful distribution-sensitive pair-TCWM construction must leave
this entire class, for example through genuinely report-dependent weights,
different nonseparable selections, or a non-projection mechanism. None of
those alternatives is proved feasible by this note.
