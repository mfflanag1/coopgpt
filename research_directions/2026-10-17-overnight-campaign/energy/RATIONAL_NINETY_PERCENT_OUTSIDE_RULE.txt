To see completeness of (A), for arbitrary t in [0,1],

The function g(t)=(1+t)/2+E[(1-U)1_(U>t)] increases linearly between atoms,
and jumps downward only at an atom. Its minimum therefore occurs just after

Thus g(t)>=beta16 for all t, with equality at u_16 and at the intermediate

WHY THIS IS COMPUTATIONALLY DIFFERENT FROM NUMERICAL QUADRATURE.
The rule itself is exactly finite and rational. It needs sixteen exact value
evaluations per active resource, plus aggregation and payment arithmetic.
For rational explicit production LPs and rational reports, all queried values
and payments can be computed exactly as rational numbers. The exact scalar
certificate ensures all-profile incentives for the defined rule; there is no
claim that arbitrary floating evaluation errors preserve those inequalities.

GENERAL FINITE APPROXIMATION PRINCIPLE.
Partition the continuous beta0 law, of mean1/2, into N intervals of equal
probability and replace each interval by its conditional mean. Jensen for
the convex function (t-u)_+ preserves the coverage inequality and the mean
stays1/2, so funding remains exactly(k+1)/2. At any threshold t, all bins
except the one containing t contribute identically to the weighted tail R(t)
before and after this replacement. The possible loss in that one bin is at
most1/N. Thus the finite law guarantees at least beta0-1/N, where
beta0=0.9190487202635107... . This existence statement alone does not make its
nodes rational.

A rational construction follows from a small linear feasibility problem.
Fix equal atom weights1/N, sort the unknown nodes, impose mean1/2, impose
prefix constraints (A) with16 replaced by N, and impose (B) for a fixed
rational target beta<beta0-1/N. All coefficients are rational, and the
conditional-mean construction proves feasibility. A nonempty rational

u_1>=1/(2N)>0 and u_N<=1-1/(2N)<1, avoiding atom-at-zero face issues.

This supplies arbitrarily near-beta0 protection at the exact optimal funding
