GAIN-ONLY INDIVIDUAL OUTSIDE PROTECTION: GENERAL OWNER BOUND AND DIMENSION LEMMA
17 October 2026. Co-derived with root; complete candidate proof, independent
audit pending. The mask/endpoint mechanism and its numerical bounds are prior
project ingredients. The modified cap and gain normalization are the proposed
adaptation. Historical novelty is not confirmed.

1. MODEL AND CLAIMS
F:R_+^d->R_+ is finite, monotone, concave and degree-one homogeneous, F(0)=0.
There are n fixed actual owners with fixed eligibility and binding nonnegative
reports b_i. Put C=sum_i b_i, T=F(C), f_i=F(b_i), S=sum_i f_i, G=T-S>=0.
Coverage means sum_{i in Q} y_i>=F(sum_{i in Q}b_i) for EVERY coalition Q.
Individual full outside-production protection means

  y_i(b)-y_i(b_i-d,b_-i)>=F(d) for every 0<=d<=b_i.      (OUT-1)

No joint-deviation protection for multiple owners is asserted.

THEOREM A. There is an all-profile deterministic rule with coverage and
(OUT-1), satisfying

  sum_i y_i <= S+B_n(a)G,     0<a<=1,
  B_n(a)=na/2+n[1-(1-a)^(n+1)]/[(n+1)a].                (1)

In particular choose a=sqrt(2/(n+1)) before seeing reports to get

  sum_i y_i <= S+n sqrt(2/(n+1)) G.                     (2)

THEOREM B. The standard grand-marginal rule m_i=T-F(C-b_i) satisfies

  sum_i m_i <= S+rank([b_1 ... b_n]) G <=S+min(n,d)G.   (3)

The rank is an ANALYSIS bound for this one fixed rule; no mechanism is chosen
after observing report rank. Choosing once between the two rules using public
n,d gives coefficient min{d,n sqrt(2/(n+1))} for arbitrary F.

THEOREM C. If F is the minimum of K fixed nonnegative linear forms, the SAME
modified-cap mechanism with the manuscript's public choice a=1/j has

  sum_i y_i <=S+C_j(K)G, j>=2,
  C_j(K)=j+[log K+2log j+1]/alpha_j,
  alpha_j=log[j^3/(j^2+j-1)]>0.                          (4a)

With the manuscript's choice of j this is (1+o(1))log K/log log K. At K=1,
F is additive over owners, G=0, and the rule simply pays each f_i. These are
the established numerical rates applied to gain-only funding and OUT-1.

THEOREM D. For F(c)=min_{1<=j<=r}c_j, a corresponding fixed mask rule also has

  sum_i y_i <=S+D_r(a)G,
  D_r(a)=[1+2r(-a-log(1-a))]/a,  0<a<1.                 (4)

Consequently the prior manuscript bounds transfer to gain-only funding:

  S+min{r,n sqrt(2/(n+1)),1+2 sqrt(r),inf_{j>=2}C_j(r)}G. (5)

In particular the finite-form term is asymptotically stronger than the
square-root dimension term for min-r. It must be retained in recommendations.

The public parameter/rule choice is fixed BEFORE reports. The current proof
does not establish (4) for arbitrary F. Exact finite mask averaging may require
exponentially many evaluations; no polynomial-time implementation is claimed.

2. FIXED SUPPORTS ON ACTIVE FACES
For x!=0, restrict F to its coordinate face. At x in that face's relative
interior choose a nonnegative supporting linear majorant ell_x with
ell_x(x)=F(x). Finite concavity, monotonicity and homogeneity give such a
support even if F has downward jumps at other faces. Select it once as a
function of x, for example by the manuscript's minimum-norm convention.
Extend ell_x(z)=+infinity outside that coordinate face. At x=0 use ell_0(0)=0
and ell_0(z)=+infinity for z!=0. Thus ell_x(z)>=F(z) whenever finite.
For finite nonnegative min-linear F one may instead select a global minimizing
row with fixed priority; this avoids infinities entirely.

3. A MODIFIED CAP FOR EACH FIXED OWNER MASK
Fix a report-independent subset B of actual owners and C_B=sum_{h in B}b_h.
Define a payment to each owner by

  i in B:     y_i^B=F(C_B)-F(C_B-b_i);
  i notin B:  y_i^B=min{ell_{C_B}(b_i), T-sum_{h!=i}f_h}.                 (6)

The second term is f_i+G, not the manuscript's former grand-value cap T.
It is nonnegative and at least f_i. Open marginal payments are also at least
f_i by superadditivity. Thus EVERY owner always receives at least f_i.

COVERAGE FOR EACH MASK. If some closed member i of coalition Q hits its cap,
then, using the standalone floors of its other members,

  y^B(Q)>=T-sum_{h!=i}f_h+sum_{h in Q\{i}}f_h
         =T-sum_{h notin Q}f_h >=F(b(Q)),

where the final inequality follows from superadditivity applied to b(Q) and
the outside owners separately. If no closed member hits the cap, every closed
payment equals its supporting price. Every open member h has
F(C_B)-F(C_B-b_h)>=ell_{C_B}(b_h), because the support touches at C_B and
majorizes C_B-b_h. All relevant prices are finite: an outside-face closed
member would have hit its finite cap. Summing the support prices gives
y^B(Q)>=ell_{C_B}(b(Q))>=F(b(Q)). Empty coalitions cause no difficulty.

INDIVIDUAL OUT FOR EACH MASK. For i in B, the off term F(C_B-b_i) is fixed
when i restores a withheld bundle d, so its increment is
F(C_B)-F(C_B-d)>=F(d).
For i notin B, its selected support excludes i and is fixed. The cap increment
is F(C)-F(C-d)>=F(d), since all other f_h are fixed. If both reports lie in
the support face, the raw-price increment ell_{C_B}(d)>=F(d); the minimum of
two quantities each increasing by at least F(d) also increases by at least
F(d). If the low report is outside the face, both reports are outside and
both payments use the cap. If only the high report is outside, high payment
is the high cap, at least low cap+F(d), and low payment is at most low cap.
These cases prove (OUT-1) at all boundaries without continuity assumptions.

4. OWNER BOUND VIA THE RESIDUAL COALITION GAME
At a FIXED profile define

  w(Q)=F(b(Q))-sum_{i in Q}f_i.

This is a nonnegative monotone game, w(empty)=0, w(N)=G. Monotonicity follows
because adding owner i increases F by at least f_i. It is not assumed to be
a globally report-independent physical production function.

The excess over f_i of an open payment is w(B)-w(B\{i}). A closed payment's
excess is min{ell_{C_B}(b_i)-f_i,G}, lying in [0,G]. The infinite-price case
has excess G. Retain each owner in B independently with probability1-q and
write g(q)=E w(B). Let C(q) be expected TOTAL closed excess. Then C(q)<=nqG,
and differentiation of the finite mask polynomial gives

  total expected excess =C(q)-(1-q)g'(q).

Average this rule over q uniformly in [0,a]. The weights of the finitely many
masks are ordinary fixed real numbers; all payments remain deterministic.
Integration by parts yields

  integral_0^a -(1-q)g'(q)dq
  =G-(1-a)g(a)-integral_0^a g(q)dq.

Since the all-open mask alone contributes (1-q)^nG and all other terms are
nonnegative, g(q)>=(1-q)^nG. Substitution proves (1). The simpler upper bound
B_n(a)<=na/2+n/[(n+1)a], with a=sqrt(2/(n+1)), gives (2). For n=1 use a=1;
the exact expression is1. Coverage and (OUT-1) survive the fixed averaging.
At G=0 every open excess and every closed excess is zero, so y_i=f_i exactly.

5. COMPLEMENT-DEFICIT LEMMA IN THE RESOURCE DIMENSION
LEMMA. Let nonzero vectors b_1,...,b_n in R_+^d sum to C, and let H be a
nonnegative convex degree-one homogeneous function on the coordinate face
of C, with H(C)=0. Then

  sum_i H(C-b_i) <=(rank([b_i])-1) sum_i H(b_i).         (7)

Proof. Set k=rank([b_i]) and consider
P={alpha>=0:sum_i alpha_i b_i=C}. This is a nonempty bounded polytope: each
nonzero column b_i has a positive coordinate bounding alpha_i from above.
Every extreme point has at most k positive coordinates, by the elementary
linear-dependence perturbation criterion. Each alpha_i b_i<=C coordinatewise.

Define Phi(alpha)=sum_i H(C-alpha_i b_i), convex on P, and
Psi(alpha)=sum_i alpha_i H(b_i), linear. At an extreme point with m<=k
positive coordinates, the terms for zero coordinates are H(C)=0. By
subadditivity and homogeneity, each supported term satisfies

  H(C-alpha_i b_i)
  =H(sum_{j!=i}alpha_j b_j)<=sum_{j!=i}alpha_j H(b_j).

Summing over the m supported indices gives Phi(alpha)<=(m-1)Psi(alpha)
<=(k-1)Psi(alpha). Express the feasible point (1,...,1) as a convex combination
of extreme points. Convexity of Phi and linearity of Psi preserve the bound,
which is (7). Null b_i may be discarded: both H(0) and H(C) are zero. QED.

To apply it, choose a nonnegative support p at C on its active face and let
H(x)=p dot x-F(x). This is nonnegative, convex, homogeneous and subadditive,
and H(C)=0. Direct identities give

  G=sum_i H(b_i),
  m_i-f_i=H(b_i)+H(C-b_i).

Combining these identities with (7) proves (3). If C=0 or G=0 the same result
holds directly. Grand marginals cover every coalition by dominating the
efficient support allocation at C, and satisfy (OUT-1) by superadditivity.
This establishes the dimension baseline without assuming a residual physical
technology or a coordinatewise monotone owner-netting map.

6. FINITE-FORM RATE TRANSFERS TO THE RESIDUAL GAME
Write F(x)=min_{w in V} w dot x, |V|=K, w>=0. At a fixed profile put

  a_i(w)=w dot b_i-f_i>=0.

Then w(Q)=min_{w in V}sum_{i in Q}a_i(w), with EXACTLY the same finite family
of K rows. Minimizing the original masked score and the residual masked score
chooses the same row: they differ by the row-independent sum_{i in B}f_i.
Closed excess is min(a_i(w),G), and open excess is the residual endpoint
marginal. Thus all fixed-profile budget steps of the manuscript's finite-form
proof apply verbatim with grand value G and coefficient array a_i(w).

For clarity, the capped normalized coefficients lie in [0,1], and the selected
row has open normalized score at most1. The same Chernoff tail over K rows
bounds expected selected capped total by
[log K+lambda+1]/[-log(q+(1-q)exp(-lambda))]. The endpoint integral contributes
at most G. Taking q<=1/j and lambda=2log j proves (4a). The coefficient array
depends on the profile ONLY for this budget analysis; incentives are already
proved directly by (6), and are not inferred from a profile-dependent change
of physical technology. This distinction prevents a false transfer argument.

7. min-r: THE FULL SQUARE-ROOT DIMENSION BOUND TRANSFERS
For F=min_r define u_i=b_i-f_i*1, with aggregate U. Then G=min_j U_j and
EVERY coalition has the exact decomposition

  F(b(Q))=sum_{i in Q} f_i+min_j sum_{i in Q}u_ij.

A fixed-priority minimizing coordinate j_B of sum_{i in B}b_i also minimizes
sum_{i in B}u_i, because their difference is the same scalar in every row.
Applying the original mask/endpoint construction to the residual bundles and
adding f_i gives EXACTLY (6): an open residual marginal plus f_i is the
original marginal; a closed residual payment plus f_i is

  f_i+min(u_i,j_B,G)=min(b_i,j_B,T-sum_{h!=i}f_h).

Therefore all of the manuscript's fixed-profile dimension estimates apply
to U with grand value G, giving (4). Its public choice a=1/(1+sqrt r) gives
coefficient1+2sqrt r; compare whole fixed rules with (2) and grand marginals
to obtain (5). The incentive proof is §3 in ORIGINAL resources, not any
claim that u_i depends monotonically on b_i. The separate exact three-pool
failure of the integral-price residual wrapper therefore causes no conflict.

8. INTERPRETATION AND OPEN GATE
This is a gain-only funding contract under unilateral withholding, including
actual standalone use of withheld resources. It remains efficient on every
zero-gain profile and requires all coalition coverage. The strong coalition
version cannot share this feature on fully eligible min-r domains, by the
constant-value completion obstruction. We do not claim collusion resistance.

The general dimension-GAIN bound beyond (3) remains open here: the residual
game w has n owner coordinates, so the manuscript's d-resource reciprocal
estimate cannot simply be reapplied to w. A universal S+O(sqrt d)G rule would
need a valid geometric bound for the modified closed excesses or a different
construction. No private costs, dispatch measurement, or real-cluster gains
are established by this theorem.
