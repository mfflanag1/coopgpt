PAIRWISE OUTSIDE PRODUCTION FORCES UNBOUNDED GAIN-ONLY FUNDING
17 October2026. Candidate theorem, frozen for independent audit.

THEOREM AND SCOPE
For F(A,B)=min(A,B), fixed actual owners eligible for both resource types,
all nonnegative real reports, nonnegative payments, ALL-coalition coverage,
and full outside-production protection for single owners and pairs, there is
NO gain-only coefficient K uniform over the number of owners:

  sum_i y_i <= S+K G,
  S=sum_i min(a_i,b_i), G=F(sum_i(a_i,b_i))-S.

Pairs may include an owner who withdraws zero. Reports bind available resources;
owners and their eligibility do not change in any deviation. The proof does
not assume anonymous, continuous, homogeneous or measurable initial rules.
It does NOT assert impossibility for one fixed finite number of owners.
The companion PAIRWISE_GAIN_LINEAR_OWNER_LOWER.txt makes the divergence
quantitative and proves sharp order Theta(n).

This separates pairwise full outside-production protection from the accepted
individual-only gain bounds, including a constant for two-resource production.
The Banzhaf merger identity and compact averaging are established ingredients.
Priority of this gain-only impossibility synthesis remains to be checked.

DEPENDENCIES
PAIRWISE_GAIN_TRANSLATION_REDUCTION.txt, independently reviewed: after a
same-budget transformation, y_i=b_i+h_i(z), z_i=a_i-b_i, and for changes only
inside a protected pair Q,

 min(Delta,0)<=H_Q(z')-H_Q(z)<=max(Delta,0),
 Delta=sum_Q(z_i'-z_i).

In particular pair H-sums are invariant under redistributing their own signed
imbalance with fixed sum. Every h_i is coordinatewise1-Lipschitz. At G=0,
h_i=min(z_i,0). Finite owner/resource averaging preserves these properties.

1. A PROJECTIVELY CONSISTENT NULL-ZERO LIMIT
Suppose a common finite K works for every finite population N. Choose one
translated, owner-anonymous, resource-symmetric rule for each N. Regard every
finite-support report profile on countably many labelled owners as a profile
for all sufficiently large N, padding by zero reports. At each fixed profile,
coverage and the gain budget put every payment in the compact interval
[f_i,f_i+K G]. Product compactness gives a subnet along N tending to infinity
on which all fixed finite-profile payment coordinates converge simultaneously.
One may extend undefined coordinates for small N arbitrarily within their
intervals; eventual constraints are all that matter.

At a profile with r active owners, all N-r null owners have the same payment,
and nonnegativity plus the excess budget gives

  0<=y_null<=K G/(N-r).

Consequently EVERY null owner has zero payment in the limit. The limit is
consistent under padding, because padded profiles were literally the same
finite-support profile before the limit. It retains all finite coverage,
pairwise outside-production, translation, anonymity and resource symmetry
constraints. Its active-owner total obeys the same gain budget by dropping
nonnegative null payments before passage to the limit.

For each finite signed vector z, use its pure residual reports. The resulting
h_i(z) is unaffected by adding null owners. Its null coordinates are zero.
All subsequent arguments concern these limit functions, not strategic merging
of actual owners in the original contract.

2. PAIR NEUTRALITY IMPLIES A MERGER IDENTITY
Fix i,j. Redistribute their signed imbalance to (z_i+z_j,0), leaving outsiders
unchanged. Pair neutrality and null-zero give

 h_i(z)+h_j(z) = h_merged(z_i+z_j; outsiders).                 (1)

This is a mathematical identity across reports of the SAME owner population.
Owner j remains present and reports zero; no identity change is permitted.
Anonymity and consistency let us describe the nonzero report as one merged
coordinate when evaluating the function.

3. THE TWO-OWNER POTENTIAL DETERMINES EVERY FINITE PROFILE
Fix total signed imbalance Z. Define

 q_Z(x)=h_1(x,Z-x)

on a two-active-coordinate profile. At a same-sign profile G=0. Pair neutrality
therefore gives the exact complement relation

 q_Z(x)+q_Z(Z-x)=min(Z,0), q_Z(0)=0.                         (2)

For Z>0, q_Z(x)=0 throughout0<=x<=Z. Each h-coordinate is1-Lipschitz in each
argument, so q_Z is2-Lipschitz. In particular all expectations and limits below
are ordinary finite or bounded continuous expressions; no original-rule
measurability assumption is imported.

For any finite vector z with total Z, define the Banzhaf potential marginal

 B_i^q(z)=2^{-(r-1)} sum_{R subset [r]\{i}}
             [q_Z(z(R)+z_i)-q_Z(z(R))].                    (3)

Claim h_i(z)=B_i^q(z). For r=1 it follows from(2); for r=2, complementarity in(2)
makes(3) equal q_Z(z_i). For larger r, conditioning on inclusion of the other
member of a pair gives the standard exact Banzhaf merger cancellation:

 B_i^q(z)+B_j^q(z)=B_merged^q(z_i+z_j; outsiders).

By induction the right side equals the merged h-payment. Equation(1) therefore
makes every pair sum of h agree with the corresponding pair sum of B^q. For
r>=3, all individual values are determined by pair sums, for example
2h_i=(h_i+h_j)+(h_i+h_k)-(h_j+h_k). This proves the claim. Zero coordinates can
be omitted because both h and B^q pay them zero.

This is NOT an assertion that the rule is the ordinary Banzhaf value. The
potential q_Z is arbitrary subject to the inherited constraints and can depend
on total imbalance Z. That distinction is essential to the unrestricted proof.

4. A POSITIVE TWO-OWNER PAYMENT IS UNAVOIDABLE
Let w>0. At the balanced two-owner pure profile (w,-w), owner/resource symmetry
and pair efficiency force each actual payment to be w/2. Restore only the
positive owner's resource from w to3w, keeping the negative owner at -w.
Individual outside-production protection includes ordinary own monotonicity,
so

  q_(2w)(3w)>=w/2.                                         (4)

Null owners in the limit receive zero; even before the limit the active-pair
sum is efficient by pair neutrality, so this step does not rely on grand
budget equality.

5. DIFFUSE OUTSIDERS FORCE A COALITION SHORTFALL
Take the three-owner coalition with signed reports

  (2w,-w,-w).

Its actual production is2w. Add m outside owners, each with positive signed
report2w/m. The total signed imbalance is Z=2w. In the Banzhaf representation,
the total outside report included in a random subset is

  W_m=(2w/m) Binomial(m,1/2),
  E W_m=w, Var W_m=w^2/m.

Conditional on W_m=t, sum the three Banzhaf h-payments using(3). Direct finite
cancellation gives

 L(t)=[-q(t+2w)+2q(t+w)-2q(t-w)+q(t-2w)]/4,                 (5)

where q=q_(2w). Since q vanishes on[0,2w] and q(-w)=-q(3w),

  L(w)=-q(3w)/2<=-w/4.                                    (6)

The2-Lipschitz property of q makes L3-Lipschitz. Thus

 E L(W_m)<=-w/4+3 E|W_m-w|<=-w/4+3w/sqrt(m).

For m>144 this is negative. The coalition's actual payment is its negative
resource baseline2w plus E L(W_m), strictly less than its worth2w. This violates
all-coalition coverage. The contradiction proves the theorem.

The concentration step can be fully finite and rational: m=100 already works
because E|W_m-w|/w=binom(100,50)/2^100<2/25, giving a shortfall greater than w/100.
That exact identity and inequality are replayed by the quantitative companion's
stdlib Fraction checker. No numerical optimization is part of this proof.
