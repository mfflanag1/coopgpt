HOW FUNDING MUST GROW WITH THE ALLOWED COALITION SIZE
17 October 2026. Corollary of the internally accepted finite common-cap lower
and the bounded-coalition binomial upper (the latter under independent audit).

Let K_r(s) be the worst necessary funding factor over finite sparse-production
models with activity arity at most r and arbitrarily many fixed actual owners,
requiring full coverage of ALL coalitions and payment-only TCWM for coalitions
of size at most s. Let K_r^OUT(s) impose full pooled-withdrawal outside-production
protection on those same size-at-most-s incentive coalitions instead.
Both quantities allow arbitrary rules on all real reports. They do not impose
an aggregate-price or semivalue architecture. Monotonicity gives

             K_r(s) <= K_r^OUT(s) <= r*s^(r-1).             (1)

The right inequality is BOUNDED_COALITION_SPARSE_PRODUCTION.txt. For fixed s
it is independent of the number of owners, resources and branches.

ALREADY IN ARITY THREE, NO UNIFORM CONSTANT WORKS FOR ALL s

In FINITE_OWNER_ARITY_THREE_OBSTRUCTION.txt, inspect each use of TCWM:

 * The m-th bootstrap restores the coalition of its 2m active branch owners.
 * The transition C_(m-1) to C_m uses all 2M branch owners except b_m,
   a coalition of size 2M-1.

All other uses are nonnegativity, budget, symmetry, or coalition COVERAGE.
In particular, coalitions containing k power owners in the bootstrap only
invoke coverage; they are not asked to change any report. Thus every lower
inequality remains valid if incentives are required only for coalitions of
size at most s=2M-1, because K<M implies 2m<=2K<=2M-2<s.

For integer B>=2 take

 K_B=ceil(exp(8B^3/(B-1)+1)), M_B=B*K_B,
 s_B=2M_B-1, n_B=4B*K_B=2(s_B+1).

The accepted finite theorem rules out factor B in this model. Hence

 K_3(s_B)>B,   K_3^OUT(s_B)>B.

Since log s_B=8B^2+O(B+log B), and K_3(s) is nondecreasing in s,

 liminf_{s->infinity} K_3(s)/sqrt(log s) >= 1/sqrt(8).      (2)

To justify all s rather than only a subsequence, observe that
log s_(B+1)/log s_B tends1. For s_B<=s<s_(B+1), the same model is eligible
for the stronger size-s contract, so K_3(s)>=K_3(s_B)>B. This proves(2).
The identical lower holds for K_3^OUT(s), because outside protection implies
TCWM. Only n_B=2(s_B+1) actual owners are needed in this asymptotic sequence.

Combining(1)--(2), the current general rank-three interval is

 (1/sqrt(8)+o(1))*sqrt(log s) <= K_3(s)
                              <= K_3^OUT(s) <= 3s^2.      (3)

This establishes a qualitative transition: fixing the maximum colluding
coalition size permits a uniform finite guarantee, but allowing that size to
grow necessarily increases worst-case funding. The wide quantitative gap in(3)
is open. Neither the lower nor the upper is claimed sharp in s.

BOUNDARY OF THIS COROLLARY

The original proof's strong requirement is not that coalitions may cover large
standalone production; coverage was deliberately retained for every size.
The accumulation arises from large coalitions LINKING REPORT CHANGES. Dropping
those incentives while retaining all coverage constraints is exactly what the
bounded-coalition rule exploits. No claim about bilateral private-price bargaining
or unverified provider independence is inferred from the mathematical size cap.
