1. EXACT MIN2 GAME-DUMMY CHARACTERIZATION

If an owner holds both resources positively, its empty-coalition marginal is
positive, so a nonzero dummy must be pure. For a pure-A owner of amount a>0,
any other owner j with b_j>a_j supplies singleton marginal
min(a,b_j-a_j)>0. Conversely, if every other owner has a_j>=b_j, every sum of
other owners has its A total at least its B total, and adding pure A changes
no coalition value. This proves necessity and sufficiency. The symmetric case
and the zero owner are immediate. A grand-coalition marginal of zero alone
would not suffice; both notes correctly use every predecessor coalition.

For a pure-A game dummy the netted opposite residual total V is zero. Scalar
nonnegative payments and funding against min(U,V)=0 then force every scalar
payment to zero. Its standalone term is zero as well. The balanced holdings
of other owners do not invalidate this argument; they remain in their own
standalone terms. Thus the new property holds for all fixed owner populations.

2. NETTING REQUIRES THE CORRECT COEFFICIENT

The composition note uses the linear map(t,x)=(a+b,a-b). Cone production is
2min(a,b), the cone standalone value is2f_i, and signed features are u_i,v_i.
The accepted formula is kappa(2f_i)+2pi_i; dividing by2 gives exactly
kappa f_i+pi_i. Both retained and restored nonnegative original bundles map
to feasible cone bundles, so all actual-owner incentive comparisons transfer.
The unrestricted note's half-sized map gives the same formula because the
scalar payment rule is degree-one homogeneous. No factor2 is missing.

The direct Huber verification also checks: on U>=V, W_u is between0 and1;
W_v=1+(u-v-V)/(2U) is between0 and3/2. Restoring an owner's coordinate either
increases the corresponding residual, or raises its f_i while decreasing the
opposite residual. Multiplication of f_i by3/2 covers the latter loss. Bounded
piecewise derivatives and continuous payments handle balance and zero seams.
Replacing kappa f_i by f_i is not licensed by this argument. The full-OUT claim
uses the accepted logarithmic signed-compensation theorem, not Huber TCWM.

Coalition coverage follows from the exact ownerwise netting identity, and
funding is at most kappa times TOTAL production. This is not an S+constant*G
gain-only guarantee: at a balanced zero-gain profile, kappa f_i exceeds f_i.
The existing unrestricted lower bounds remain lower bounds after adding the
dummy axiom, so the claimed sharpness is uniform over growing finite models,
not a claim of attainment for every fixed n or eligibility pattern.

3. POINTWISE ADAPTIVE POSITIVE MARGINAL REPRESENTATION

All whole-owner marginals are nonnegative by monotonicity. Their finite maximum
is positive exactly when the owner is not a game dummy. Assigning y_i divided
by that maximum to one maximizing predecessor therefore represents any finite
nonnegative dummy-zero payment. A first positive predecessor also works, as in
the other note. Conversely, any finite nonnegative weighted sum of these
marginals pays a game dummy zero. These are pointwise identities at every report.

The coefficients may depend on the full report, may be unbounded as marginals
approach zero, and are not normalized semivalue weights. Neither continuity nor
efficient evaluation follows. Those exclusions are explicit. The accepted
fixed-weight lower permits owner/population dependence but forbids report
dependence; therefore the min2 escape demonstrates a valid scope separation.
It does not invalidate that lower or solve the min3 problem.

4. TWO-OWNER AGGREGATE-PRICE OBSTRUCTION

At C^-=(1/2,1,...,1), owner2's bundle(0,1/2,...,1/2) has zero marginal both
alone and with owner1=(1/2,...,1/2). Dummy-zero implies the SUM of the long-row
prices is zero. Grand coverage then implies p1(C^-)>=1. It is not necessary
to infer that the individual long-row prices vanish or have either sign.

At C^+=(2,1,...,1), the decomposition into(1,0,...,0) and(1,...,1) makes the
first owner a dummy, forcing p1(C^+)=0. In a third pair of profiles, leave
owner2=(0,1,...,1) fixed and restore owner1 from(1/2,0,...,0) to(2,0,...,0).
The totals are precisely C^- and C^+, so its cash falls from at least1/2 to0.
This is an exact individual-monotonicity violation on one fixed, fully eligible
two-owner domain. No funding bound, differentiability or price sign assumption
was used. Using other ownership decompositions to constrain the same prices
is valid precisely because these prices depend only on the aggregate.

For owner-label-specific p_i(C), averaging the original rule with the relabeled
two-owner rule yields the common price(p1+p2)/2. The fully eligible domain and
all the listed axioms are invariant under this finite average. Hence that
extension is also excluded. Ownership-dependent nonlinear allocation remains
outside the obstruction. The aggregate-price existence reduction cannot be
augmented with dummy-zero, although its original theorem remains unaffected.

5. MIN3 STOP AND FINAL LIMITS

Both displayed min3 examples really have four zero whole-owner marginals for
their specified dummy, including the two-other-owner predecessor. Standalone
minima are all zero, so the proposed simple netting removes nothing. In the
unrestricted example, the stated integral is exactly1/15. This disproves that
specific route to dummy-zero, not the existence of other min3 mechanisms.

Fixed-n feasibility is consistent with the accepted p=1/n scaled binomial
rule: every dummy term is zero, and its finite funding factor grows with n.
Uniform-owner min3 feasibility remains unresolved. Neither note asserts a
novelty confirmation, new measured infrastructure consequence, strategic
