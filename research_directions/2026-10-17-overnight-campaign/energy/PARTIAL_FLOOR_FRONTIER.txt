1. RESULT
For any parameter a in [1/3,(1+theta)/4], define
 B(a)=1+a exp(1/a-3),
 D_theta(a)=theta+a+(1-theta)^2/(4a).
There is an explicit all-profile own-monotone coalition-covering rule with
funding factor K_theta(a)=max{B(a),D_theta(a)}. This holds for arbitrary
finite owner counts, multiple public tiers per actual same-side owner, and all
nonnegative real capacities. Public-price integration preserves the theorem.
B is strictly decreasing and D_theta strictly increasing on this interval.
The unique intersection gives the best displayed bound. At theta=1 it is
a=1/3 and K=4/3, the sharp endpoint theorem. For theta<1 the claimed result
is an improved UPPER, not an optimality theorem for unrestricted mechanisms.

Universal lower bounds retained are max{1+theta/4, 2(1+theta)/3, C_0}, where
C_0 is any independently established floor-free lower (currently at least9/8).
The first is static and the second links two reports. For theta>=4/5 the
two-report bound is the larger of these stated numerical bounds.

2. UNIT RULE
Let T=min(E,H), and pay zero if T=0. Put R=1/a-1, so 1<=R<=2.
Set
 r(rho)=a exp(1-rho),
 q(rho)=1-a rho for 1<=rho<=R;
 q(rho)=a exp(R-rho) for rho>=R.
If energy is short E<=H, set d_i=min(h_i,E), rho=sum_i d_i/E.
Pay hardware owner i r(rho)d_i, and energy owner j
 max{theta,1-r(rho)}e_j.
If hardware is short H<=E, set d_j=min(e_j,H), rho=sum_j d_j/H.
Pay hardware owner i (1-q(rho))h_i, and energy owner j
 max{q(rho)d_j,theta (H-E+e_j)_+}.
At E=H, the common energy coefficient is max{theta,1-a} and hardware
coefficient is a. The branches are continuous, as are cap and price switches.

3. INCENTIVES, COALITION COVERAGE AND FLOORS
The usual capped-side convex-combination argument proves coverage before
floor raises in both regimes; raises preserve it. Grand marginal floors are
immediate. Long-side unraised payment p(rho)d is own-monotone when p+p'>=0.
For r this is equality. For q it holds on the linear segment because
q-a>=0 until rho=R; on the exponential segment it is equality.
The own marginal T-min(H,E-e_j) is own-monotone, so the maximum of q d_j and theta M_j preserves energy incentives when
energy is long.
For a short-side actual owner x, the complement payment (1-p(rho))x has
nonnegative derivative whenever 1-p+rho p'>=0, by the same cap-cell proof
as in the sharp full-floor theorem. For p=r, this holds because
r(1+rho)<=2a<=1. For p=q, it equals0 on the linear piece and equals
1-q(1+rho)>=0 on the exponential piece: at R it is0 and then increases.
A short energy owner takes the maximum of this monotone complement payment
and theta e_j, also monotone. Fixed actual same-side bundles are aggregated
before the rule. Continuity completes the all-real, all-profile proof.

4. BUDGET
Energy short: total/T=max{theta,1-r}+rho r is at most
 max{theta+a,1+a/e}.
Hardware short before floor raises: total/T=1+q(rho)(rho-1).
On the linear segment its derivative is1+a-2a rho, which remains nonnegative
through R because3a-1>=0. On the exponential segment it rises until rho2
and falls afterward. Thus its maximum is B(a). Moreover B(a)>=1+a/e since
a<=1/2, and D_theta(a)>=theta+a.

It remains to bound active energy floors when hardware is short. Normalize H1.
If at least two energy quantities are >=1, all marginal floors arezero.
For exactly one large energy owner, put t=sum of all other energy quantities.
If t>=1 no floor is active. If t<1, rho=1+t and total is at most
 max{base total,1+(theta-q(1+t))(1-t)}.
If every energy quantity is <=1, put E=rho=1+delta. For delta>=1 all marginal
floors vanish. For delta in [0,1], let B be the set of raised owners, k its
size and e_B its total energy. Total is
 1+q delta+(theta-q)e_B-k theta delta.
For k0 this is the base total. If k1, e_B<=1 gives at most
 1+(theta-q)(1-delta).
If k>=2, e_B<=1+delta gives excess <=theta-q-(k-1)theta delta,
which is at most (theta-q)(1-delta), because q>=0. Thus it suffices to bound
 f(t)=(theta-q(1+t))(1-t), 0<=t<=1.
On its linear segment this is
 f(t)=theta-1+a+(1-theta)t-a t^2.
Its maximum occurs at t_*=(1-theta)/(2a). The stipulated condition
 a<=(1+theta)/4
is exactly t_*<=R-1. On the exponential segment,
 f'(t)=-theta+q(1+t)(2-t), f''(t)=-q(1+t)(3-t)<0.
The derivative is continuous at R-1, so no later point improves the linear
maximum. Therefore total <=1+max f = D_theta(a). This proves the bound.

5. PARAMETER CHOICE AND SIZE OF THE REMAINING GAP
B'(a)=exp(1/a-3)(1-1/a)<0. D_theta'(a)=1-(1-theta)^2/(4a^2)>0 on the stated
range. At a1/3, B>=D, with equality only theta1. At a_bar=(1+theta)/4,
a_bar>=9/20 and B(a_bar)<=B(9/20)<5/4, whereas D_theta(a_bar)>=5/4.
The strict estimate follows from exp(7/9)>9/5, for example by its first
three positive Taylor terms. Hence an intersection exists uniquely (endpoint
at theta1). Implicitly specifying it involves only a scalar public parameter.

An entirely explicit alternative is a=(2-theta)/3+(1-theta)^2/4, which lies
in the allowed interval for theta in [4/5,1]. Its upper differs from the
linear lower 2(1+theta)/3 by O((1-theta)^2) as theta approaches1. This is a
local asymptotic bound on the displayed construction, not a proof of the
unrestricted optimal partial-floor frontier.

6. EXACT OBSTRUCTION TO ATTAINING THE TWO-REPORT LOWER BY ONE CONCENTRATION PRICE
Consider the broader hardware-short architecture:
 hardware i gets (1-q(rho))h_i;
 energy j gets max{q(rho)min(e_j,H),theta M_j};
 rho=sum_j min(e_j,H)/H,
where q may be ANY function (no smoothness assumed). If theta in (1/2,1),
this architecture cannot be own-monotone with universal funding
 kappa=2(1+theta)/3. This is a mechanism-architecture obstruction, not an
unrestricted impossibility theorem.

Proof. Write a=1-q(1). At a balanced profile total is max{theta,1-a}+a>=theta+a, so a<=kappa-theta=(2-theta)/3=:a0. Fix energy quantities1,1 and
one hardware owner of report h in [1,2]. At h2, its payment is2a. At h1,
its payment is1-q(2), and the zero-floor total is1+q(2)<=kappa.
Own-hardware monotonicity implies1-q(2)<=2a, hence
 2-2a<=1+q(2)<=kappa=2-2a0.
Consequently a=a0 and both endpoint hardware payments equal2a0. Monotonicity
then forces hardware payment constant throughout h in [1,2], that is
 q(rho)=1-a0 rho for every rho in [1,2].

Now fix hardware quantity1, one energy quantity1, and vary the other actual
energy owner's report x in [0,1]. Its grand marginal is0 throughout and
rho=1+x, so its payment is x[1-a0(1+x)]. At
 x_*=(1-a0)/(2a0)<1
it receives (1-a0)^2/(4a0), whereas at its full report x1 it receives1-2a0.
The gain from underreporting to x_* is exactly
 (1-3a0)^2/(4a0)>0,
contradicting own-monotonicity. All owners and all other reports remain fixed.
This identifies the precise failure of simply tuning the full-floor
concentration-price proof to close the partial-floor gap.
