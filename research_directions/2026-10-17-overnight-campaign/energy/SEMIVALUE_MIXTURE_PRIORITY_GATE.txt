1. CASAJUS: PAIR NEUTRALITY IS ESTABLISHED, WITH A DIFFERENT CONTRACT

Andre Casajus, Collusion, symmetry, and the Banzhaf value, original author
manuscript dated August31,2011:
https://home.uni-leipzig.de/casajus/texts/colluba.pdf
Read Definitions(3)--(6), Section2 axioms, Theorems1,3,4 and Remark2.

The 2-efficiency axiom equates a merged player's payoff with the original
pair's sum. Proxy neutrality keeps the player set fixed, makes one partner
null, and preserves that sum. Association and distrust neutrality instead
modify what the two partners can produce separately. Theorem1 equates these
three neutrality axioms. Theorem3 characterizes Banzhaf by any one plus the
dummy axiom, for fixed populations other than size2. Theorem4 derives these
neutralities from 2-efficiency across populations. Remark2 credits the
pair-sum induction to Lehrer1988 and an earlier Casajus result.

Comparison: these are exact invariances under specified game transformations,
on all TU games. Our target is an inequality under binding partial resource
underreports, with technology and actual owners fixed, plus coverage and a
funding limit. Neither contract can be silently substituted for the other.
Banzhaf pair cancellation and its merger induction must remain attributed;
the theorem supplies no stated subsidy exponent for our resource contract.

2. GIMENEZ--LLONGUERAS--PUENTE: CLOSE PROBABILISTIC/PARTNERSHIP COMPARATOR

Jose Miguel Gimenez, Maria Dolors Llongueras and Maria Albina Puente,
Partnership formation and multinomial values, Discrete Applied Mathematics
170(2014),7--20. Free original publisher PDF in the authors' university archive:
https://upcommons.upc.edu/bitstreams/797c7964-14ff-4f3b-a267-dbd862144cb4/download
Read printed pp8--13 and AppendixA pp17--18 directly, including the formulas.

Section2.1 gives owner-specific nonnegative marginal weights summing to1,
crediting Weber; anonymity yields semivalues. Definition2.1 uses independent
owner-specific inclusion probabilities. Remark2.2(c) recovers binomial values.
Definitions2.3--2.4 replace v(T) by v(T\S) whenever T omits any partner: partial
partnerships cannot contribute. Proposition3.1 calculates payoff changes.
Corollary3.2 controls the pair-sum sign by p_i+p_j-1. Proposition3.6 compares
partnership to merged alliance through the factor
sum_i product_(j in S\i) p_j - 1, multiplying a nonnegative expected block
marginal on monotone games. For equal p this is s*p^(s-1)-1.

Comparison: neither transformation is our ordinary partial resource underreport.
The pair threshold is a close precedent, while the displayed general factor
differs from our threshold-star factor 1-s*p. No measure-support necessity or
coverage/funding theorem is stated there. Do not claim owner-specific marginal
weights, binomial formulas, or coalition-payoff comparisons as newly introduced.

RECOMMENDED CLAIM AND SCOPE CORRECTIONS

(a) A classical normalized semivalue preserves additive-game payoffs. Our rule
with measure mass M pays M times each additive contribution. Use 'scaled
positive mixtures of binomial semivalues' or 'positive marginal-contribution
rules'; when M is not1, the resulting rule is not itself a normalized semivalue.
If a general owner-specific row has mass M_i, its additive payoff is likewise
scaled by M_i. Subsidy freedom is central to the comparison.

(b) Keep all-coalition coverage distinct from incentives restricted to at most
s actual owners. Allowing an unchanged member in a protected coalition is
part of the resource contract, not an unspoken classical collusion convention.

(c) The current common-measure limit uses inequalities from arbitrarily many
finite fixed populations. It does not create strategic identities within any
report comparison. It also does not imply a theorem for population-dependent
laws without the parent's separate argument. Generic finite-n semivalue weights
must not be asserted to share one measure merely by using the word semivalue.

(d) Safe contribution wording, conditional on independent proof acceptance:
'For this resource-underreport contract we derive an architecture-specific
support restriction and funding lower bound for positive marginal rules.'
Do not call this unrestricted incentive impossibility, the first collusion
result for semivalues, or confirmed novelty. The unrestricted mechanism gap
remains a different research question.
