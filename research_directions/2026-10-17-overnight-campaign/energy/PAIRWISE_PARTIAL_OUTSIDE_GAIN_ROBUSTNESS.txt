DOMAIN AND CLAIM

all-coalition coverage, nonnegative payments, and gain funding Y<=S+K G.

This includes pairwise total-payment monotonicity. Let K_n(beta) be the optimal

 K_n(beta)>=(n-5) max{0, beta-341/342}.                    (2)

1. TRANSLATION REGULARIZATION STILL WORKS
The compact-convex owner-diagonal-translation argument in
PAIRWISE_GAIN_TRANSLATION_REDUCTION.txt uses only closed finite inequalities
and the invariant gain budget. The operators T_t y=y(b+t)-t preserve(1), since
both compared profiles have the same translation. Thus one may again assume

 y_i=b_i+h_i(z), z_i=a_i-b_i,

and owner/resource symmetry, without regularity assumptions.

For arbitrary changes of signed coordinates within Q, write
P=sum_Q(Delta z_i)_+, N=sum_Q(-Delta z_i)_+. Restoring the minimal nonnegative
bundles and reversing the operation gives the necessary finite inequalities

 beta min(P,N)-N <= Delta H_Q <= P-beta min(P,N).          (3)

They are also sufficient for the translated rule: extra balanced restoration
amount t contributes exactly t to payment and at most beta t to the required
outside protection. In particular a signed redistribution inside a pair with
fixed pair sum, moving an amount u from one coordinate to the other, obeys

 |Delta H_pair|<=(1-beta)|u|.                             (4)

Single-coordinate changes have P=0 or N=0, so singleton and pair totals remain
coordinatewise monotone with slopes at most1 in signed coordinates. The usual
subtraction argument still makes each h_i coordinatewise1-Lipschitz. The proof
below only needs(4) and ordinary own monotonicity.

2. TWO-ACTIVE-OWNER PAIRS ARE EXACTLY EFFICIENT EVEN AT beta=0
At a pure profile with two active owners (a,0),(0,b), suppose a>=b. Restore the
second owner's missing first resource to b. Production stays b while that
owner's standalone value becomes b and every other owner's standalone remains
zero. Thus G becomes zero. The funding cap and singleton coverage force the
restored pair total to be exactly b. Pairwise monotonicity makes the original
pair total at most b, and pair coverage makes it at least b. Hence equality.
For a<b exchange the roles; same-sign reports have production and payments zero.
This completion uses fixed owners and only binding-report restoration.

Consequently, on total signed imbalance2, define q(x)=h_1(x,2-x,0,...,0).
The efficiency just proved and owner symmetry give exactly

 q(x)+q(2-x)=0, q(x)=0 on[0,2].                           (5)

At the balanced two-owner profile(2,-2), resource/owner symmetry gives each
payment1. Ordinary own monotonicity upon restoring the first report to4 yields
q(4)>=1. These facts do not require beta=1 or exact pair neutrality.

3. APPROXIMATE MERGING AND THE SAME FIVE-OWNER WITNESS
Use the class of profiles with total signed imbalance2, negative mass at most2,
and at most M=5 active owners. For n>5 every null price is at most

 epsilon=2K/(n-5).

To merge two nonzero signed coordinates into their sum and zero, transfer the
coordinate with the smaller absolute value. Its magnitude is at most2: for
same-sign positives their combined mass is at most4; for two negatives their
combined mass is at most2; for opposite signs the negative magnitude is at
most2. Equation(4) therefore bounds the pair-neutrality defect by

 delta=2(1-beta).

Compare h with the potential Banzhaf marginal B^q as in the full-OUT proof.
The two-active-owner representation is exact by(5). At r>=3 the pair error is
at most E_(r-1)+epsilon+delta. A zero-sum merge needs the same explicit exception
as before: at r=3 it leaves G0, so its two null prices vanish; at r>=4 the two
null prices contribute at most2epsilon, which is at most E_(r-1)+epsilon because
E_3=(3/2)(epsilon+delta)>=epsilon. In either case the same recursion holds:

 E_2=0,
 E_r=(3/2)(E_(r-1)+epsilon+delta)
    =3(epsilon+delta)[(3/2)^(r-2)-1].                    (6)

At the five-owner signed profile(2,-1,-1,1,1), the first three owners form a
coalition of worth2. The exact potential calculation from the full-OUT proof
gives its h-total -q(4)/8<=-1/8. Its true h-total must be nonnegative by coverage.
Thus

 1/8 <=3E_5=(171/8)(epsilon+delta).

Substituting epsilon and delta gives

 1/171 <= 2K/(n-5)+2(1-beta),

which is(2). All ingredients are finite all-profile inequalities after compact
translation regularization. No numerical discovery or smoothness assumption is
used.

4. A SIMPLE FAILURE THAT DOES NOT SETTLE THE TCWM CASE
The accepted individual gain-only residual Huber rule fails even pairwise TCWM.
With only two nonzero owners (10,0),(0,1), its total is1.45. Restoring a small
first-resource amount t to the second owner leaves total production1, while
its total payment is

 t+(1-t)[3/2-(1-t)/20],

whose derivative at t=0 is-2/5. Thus the individual-only construction cannot
be imported as a positive answer to beta=0. This is one mechanism failure,
not a general lower for pairwise TCWM.
