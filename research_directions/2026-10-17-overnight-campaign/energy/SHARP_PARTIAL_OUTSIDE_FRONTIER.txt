SHARP UNIVERSAL TRADEOFF FOR PARTIAL OUTSIDE-PRODUCTION PROTECTION
17 October 2026. Internally audited: root and deep independent Fable review
021222 found no substantive errors. External specialist review remains open.
Joint derivation by the energy lane and root. Historical priority unconfirmed.

MODEL.
Let F:R_+^d->R_+ be finite, monotone, concave and degree-one homogeneous.
Actual owners and resource eligibility remain fixed; arbitrary mixed bundles
and binding coordinatewise underreports are allowed. Payments are nonnegative
and must cover the FULL standalone production of every coalition. For a
public beta in [0,1], impose the partial outside-production requirement

    Y_S(high)-Y_S(low)>=beta F(d_S),                    (P-OUT)

where d_S is the coalition's aggregate withheld bundle. Coverage remains
F(b_S), not beta F(b_S). beta=0 is TCWM; beta=1 is full OUT. Intermediate
beta represents an explicitly weaker economic protection contract, not a
claim that all profitable outside production is deterred.

THEOREM.
There is a scalar coefficient mu(beta) in [1/2,c], where
c=0.5430633898250876... solves c[2-exp(-1/c)]=1, such that the sharp universal
funding factor in resource dimension d is

                     K_d(beta)=1+(d-1)mu(beta).

The supremum is over all such technologies and all finite fixed owner models;
the upper is one all-profile rule and the matching lower already holds for
F=min of d resource pools. No regularity, symmetry, linearity or anonymity
restriction is imposed on mechanisms in that lower.

The coefficient stays exactly 1/2 for beta up to

  beta0=1-a0(1-a0)/2=0.9190487202635107...,
  a0 in (0,1/2),  -log(a0)+2a0=2.

Thus the sharp TCWM funding factor (d+1)/2 also protects beta0 of outside
production, at no additional worst-case funding. For beta>beta0 the factor
strictly increases according to the parametric construction below, reaching
the previously audited full-OUT factor at beta=1. This is a universal sharp
tradeoff; a particular technology or fixed owner count can require less.

1. THE PARAMETRIC LAW

For 0<=a<=a0, let b=b(a) be the unique root in (a,1) of

       log((1-a)/(1-b))=1+b-a-a^2.                       (1)

Define

       mu=1/(1+b+a-a^2),
       beta=mu(1+b)=1-mu a(1-a).                       (2)

Let U have density

       2mu             on (0,a),
       mu/(1-u)        on (a,b),
       0               elsewhere.

Its mass is 2mu a+mu log((1-a)/(1-b))=1 by (1)-(2). Its mean is

  mu a^2+mu[-(b-a)+log((1-a)/(1-b))]
   =1+mu(a^2-b-a)=mu.

At a=0, equation (1) gives -log(1-b)=1+b, so b=t0=1/c-1 and mu=c.
The law is exactly the already audited full-OUT logarithmic law.
At a=a0, b=1-a0+a0^2 solves (1), and mu=1/2. Its density is 1 up to a0,
then 1/[2(1-u)] up to b. This gives beta=beta0. For beta<=beta0 use this
same law; its stronger beta0 guarantee implies the requested weaker level.

The parameterization is one-to-one for beta in [beta0,1]. To see this,
the left side of (1) minus its right side has derivative b/(1-b)>0 in b,
is a^2-1<0 at b=a, and tends to infinity as b increases to1. Implicit
differentiation gives

  b'=-a(1-2a)(1-b)/[b(1-a)]<=0,
  (1+b+a-a^2)'=(1-2a)(b-a)/[b(1-a)]>0.

Consequently mu decreases strictly with a. For beta=(1+b)/(1+b+a-a^2),
its derivative numerator is
b' a(1-a)-(1+b)(1-2a)<0, so beta also decreases strictly. Endpoint identities
give the stated interval. The root a0 is unique because -log a+2a decreases
strictly on (0,1/2), diverges at zero, and is below2 at a=1/2.

2. TWO SCALAR CONDITIONS FOR THE LAW

For a probability law on (0,1), with mean mu, write

  q(t)=E min(1,U/t),
  R(t)=E[(1-U)1_(U>t)].

Our law satisfies, for every 0<t<=1,

        q(t)+mu t>=1,                                  (3)
        mu(1+t)+R(t)>=beta.                             (4)

For (4), define g(t)=mu(1+t)+R(t). On [0,a], integration of the initial
constant density gives g(t)=1-mu t(1-t), decreasing since a<1/2, and its
value at a is beta. On [a,b], g'=mu-(1-t)mu/(1-t)=0. On [b,1],
g(t)=mu(1+t), increasing from mu(1+b)=beta. Thus beta is the exact minimum.

For (3), use the stop-loss gap

           D(t)=mu t^2-E(t-U)_+.

Equation (3) is equivalent to D>=0. On [0,a], D=0. On [a,b],
D(a)=D'(a)=0 and D''=mu[2-1/(1-t)]. This derivative changes sign once,
from positive to negative, at1/2. Therefore D increases and then possibly
decreases; its minimum on [a,b] is at an endpoint. At b,

          D(b)=mu(1+b^2)-b >= (1-b)^2/2>=0,

because mu>=1/2. On [b,1], D(t)=mu(1+t^2)-t>=(1-t)^2/2>=0.
This proves full coverage even though the incentive contract protects only
the beta fraction of outside production.

3. A GENERAL COMMON-SCALAR TRANSFER LEMMA

Suppose a probability law U on (0,1) has mean mu and satisfies (3)-(4),
with 2mu>=beta and 0<=beta<=1. Define

  H_j(C)=E F(C_j e_j+U C_-j),
  p_j(C)=H_j(C)/C_j on active coordinates,
  y_i=b_i.p(C),

with absent contributions zero. Then these payments cover every coalition,
satisfy P-OUT, and use at most [1+mu(k-1)]F(C) at a k-active-coordinate
profile. The full-OUT theorem is the special case beta=1 and the logarithmic
law; the transfer is not restricted to that specific density.

Funding follows by Jensen exactly as before. Finite TCWM follows from the
nonnegative share-times-monotone-face expression. For coverage, dominate
each face value by F(d) times the minimum coordinate ratio C_j/d_j. A shortest
and second-shortest coordinate contribute at least q(t)+mu t, which is at
least1 by (3). Directions with zero coordinates follow by a positive
perturbation on the active face and monotonicity of F.

If the active face has one coordinate, homogeneity makes F linear on that
ray. The formula pays every owner exactly its resource amount times the
linear coefficient, giving efficient coverage and full OUT directly. The
zero face has zero production and payments. The remaining proof assumes
at least two active coordinates.

For P-OUT, first smooth F on its positive relative faces as in the audited
general theorem. At a coalition holding corner J, its derivative along d is

       sum_(j in J) B_j(d)+sum_(j notin J) A_j(d),

where A_j=H_j d_j/C_j>=0 and
B_j=E[U grad F(v_j).d+(1-U)partial_j F(v_j)d_j]>=mu F(d).
The empty corner is covered by (3), and corners with at least two coordinates
are covered by 2mu>=beta. If beta<=mu, every nonempty corner already works.
It remains to assume beta>mu and treat a singleton J={j}.

Use the audited normalization G(1)=1, min_(l!=j)x_l=1, t=x_j and V=G(x),
with F(d)>0 and d positive on the active face. The base derivative B_j
contributes mu, and the other-coordinate A terms contribute at least mu V.
If t>=1 then V>=1, and 2mu>=beta settles the case. For 0<t<1 define

         H(u)=G(t e_j+u x_-j),  H(t)>=t, H(1)=V.

The extra derivative is E[(1-U)h(U)], where
h(u)=[H(u)-uH'(u)]/t. We claim

  mu H(1)+E[(1-U)h(U)] >= (beta-mu)H(t)/t.             (5)

This is linear in the nonnegative monotone concave function H. Check its
constant, linear, and hinge generators. A constant A contributes to left
minus right the nonnegative A[mu+(1-beta)/t]. A linear term B u contributes
B(2mu-beta)>=0. For a hinge min(u,s), 0<s<=1, its three relevant values are

  H(t)/t=min(t,s)/t,  H(1)=s,
  E[(1-U)h(U)]=(s/t)R(s).

If s<=t, (5) follows from mu t+R(s)>=mu s+R(s)>=beta-mu by (4).
If s>=t, it follows from mu s+(s/t)R(s)>=mu s+R(s)>=beta-mu.
Thus (5) holds for finite positive hinge combinations. General H follows
by its positive concave hinge representation, or monotone approximation of
the associated Stieltjes measure; the constant and linear terms are retained.
There is no atom at U=0 in the laws used here, and their support ends at
b<1, so all derivative integrals are controlled by the same Euler bounds as
in the audited general theorem. Endpoint downward jumps are filled only for
this scalar integral, without changing the actual payment definition.

Since H(t)/t>=1 and beta>mu, (5) yields mu V+extra>=beta-mu. Adding the
base mu proves the singleton derivative bound beta. Therefore every holding
corner, and hence every actual coalition, has derivative at least beta F(d).

Integrate restorations on the fixed active face for positive path parameter.
At the initial face entry, finite TCWM ensures a nonnegative payment jump.
Diagonal log smoothing of F preserves all-face relative bounds and the
concavity/homogeneity class, so the finite inequalities pass to nonsmooth F.
This is the same endpoint argument already independently audited in the
full-OUT theorem; no new continuity assumption is imposed here.

4. THE PAIR CAP LOWER LEMMA

Let an aggregate-price rule for min(A,B) be homogeneous, symmetric, locally
Lipschitz on positive profiles, coalition covering and P-OUT. Suppose its
long-resource price p(t), with t=B/A<=1, obeys p(t)<=C t. Write q(t) for the
short-resource price and z(t)=t q(t). TCWM, coverage and symmetry imply

        z' >=0,   z(t)>=t-Ct^2,   z(1)=q(1)=p(1)<=C.    (6)

The first is the coalition derivative when all short resources are held by
the increasing coalition. The second follows from p+q>=1. In particular
C>=1/2 from the balanced profile. A coalition holding all short resources
and none of the long resources, which restores equal amounts of both, gives

        p+q+t(1-t)q'>=beta.

These holding-corner derivative inequalities are legitimate limits of finite
comparisons: first take strictly interior coalition fractions, for example
epsilon of the long resources and 1-epsilon of the short resources, apply
the all-profile P-OUT inequality to a sufficiently small positive restoration,
and differentiate at a price differentiability point. Then let epsilon go to
zero. The derivative expression is affine in the holding fractions and the
prices are locally Lipschitz, so the corner limit is valid almost everywhere.
No derivative at a zero actual-owner report is being silently assumed.

Using p<=Ct and rewriting in z yields the necessary differential inequality

        z+(1-t)z'>=beta-Ct.                             (7)

Fix C in [1/2,c], and choose the unique a,b in Section1 with mu(a)=C.
Let beta_C=C(1+b). The constructed law has
z_C(a)=a-Ca^2, z_C(b)=C, and equality in (7) on [a,b] with beta=beta_C.
Integrating (7) with factor (1-t)^-2 gives

  z(b)/(1-b)-z(a)/(1-a)
    >=integral_a^b (beta-Ct)/(1-t)^2 dt.

If beta>beta_C, use z(a)>=a-Ca^2=z_C(a) to obtain z(b)>z_C(b)=C, contrary
to (6). Thus beta<=beta_C. At a=0 one uses bounded q near zero and z(0)=0;
the already audited full-OUT endpoint lower proves the same conclusion.
For beta<=beta0 the necessary C>=1/2 suffices. The lemma makes no claim that
the original mechanism was an aggregate-price rule; that reduction is next.

5. UNRESTRICTED LOWER FOR min_r AND THE UNIVERSAL CLASS

The accepted COALITION_PRICE_REDUCTION_CANDIDATE.txt applies with the deviation
term D(d)=beta F(d). Its proof uses only the linear inequalities and degree-one
homogeneity of D, and P-OUT implies TCWM since beta>=0. Every compactness,
dilation, rational regularity and cross-face step is unchanged. Hence any
uniform funding bound over arbitrary fixed finite owner populations yields
an aggregate-price mechanism without loss, even if the original mechanisms
were owner-aware, discontinuous, nonlinear, or asymmetric.

For F=min_r, symmetrize resource labels. Suppose the uniform funding factor
is kappa and put C=(kappa-1)/(r-1). At capacities (A,M,...,M), let q be the
short price and b the common long price. Coverage and funding give

       q+(r-1)b>=1,
       A q+(r-1)M b<=kappa A,
       M b<=C A M/(M-A).

TCWM for a coalition holding all B units of one long resource, B<=M, gives
B p_long(A,B,M,...,M)<=M b. Send the other r-2 capacities to infinity and
take the rational diagonal limit, exactly as in the independently audited
SHARP_MULTIPOOL_OUTSIDE_CANDIDATE.txt. The finite relative-continuity bound
there ensures that replacing M by M-w changes finite-coordinate prices by
O(1/M). Give a deviating coalition w=min(two-coordinate withheld amounts)
of every extra resource and let it withhold those resources too. Its outside
term is now beta w, rather than w. The payments for those extra resources
vanish in the limit. Thus the induced pair rule has P-OUT at the SAME beta,
coverage, and long-price cap p(t)<=C t. A second dilation average makes this
limit homogeneous while preserving the cap and all beta-linear inequalities.

For r=2 the same long-price cap follows directly by TCWM and M->infinity;
no extra-coordinate limit is needed. The pair cap lemma gives C>=mu(beta).
Therefore kappa>=1+(r-1)mu(beta), matching the construction. For beta below
beta0 the already audited sharp TCWM lower also gives the same coefficient
1/2. The argument is a lower for arbitrary mechanisms, not merely this
face-average architecture. The universal class contains min_r, so this also
matches the universal upper. The one-dimensional case is efficient directly.

6. MARGINAL-FLOOR CONTRACT COROLLARY

If in addition each actual owner must receive theta times its full deletion
marginal M_i=F(C)-F(C-b_i), 0<=theta<=1, the sharp universal factor is

          K_d(beta,theta)=1+(d-1)max{mu(beta),theta}.

Every face vector is at least U C, so H_j>=mu F(C). The share argument in
GENERAL_OUTSIDE_FLOOR_FRONTIER.txt gives y_i>=mu M_i. If theta>mu, mix with
the proportional full-OUT/full-floor d-factor rule, weight
(theta-mu)/(1-mu). This preserves at least the requested beta protection,
because the proportional rule supplies protection1, and yields coefficient
theta. The min_r P-OUT lower and the independent floor lower combine to give
the matching maximum. This is a routine corollary once the frontier is proved.

7. STATUS AND INTERPRETATION

All claims above have analytic proofs and passed independent internal
adversarial review. Numerical constants and finite diagnostics are not the proof.
Historical priority and external significance remain unconfirmed. Partial
outside protection does not mean full truthfulness when a coalition can keep
the entire outside payoff: beta<1 is an explicitly weaker contract. It may
also represent a model with a public beta fraction of usable outside value,
but that economic interpretation must be established for the application.
Public linear opportunity costs, private costs, and physical infrastructure
performance should not be conflated with this mathematical parameter.
