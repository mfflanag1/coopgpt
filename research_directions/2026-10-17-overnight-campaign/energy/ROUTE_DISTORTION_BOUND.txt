PUBLIC ROUTE-DISTORTION BOUND FOR FIXED-OWNER COOPERATION
17 October 2026. Candidate corollary, independent audit outstanding.
The mathematical ingredients are classical difference constraints, public
production comparison, and this campaign's coarea/unit-mechanism transfer.
No historical novelty or participation-floor claim is made.

1. BIPARTITE ACCESS MODEL
Fix a finite bipartite resource graph G=(A union B,E). Each activity ab consumes
one unit of both endpoints and has public profit w_ab>0. Owners hold arbitrary
fixed mixed resource bundles, with nonnegative real capacities. Reports are
binding coordinatewise underreports; utility is mechanism payment only. The
number of owners, resources, access edges, and cycles is unrestricted.
Zero-profit optional activities can be discarded before constructing G;
negative-profit optional activities likewise never improve the production LP.

Define the PUBLIC additive distortion
 rho(G,w)=min rho subject to rho>=1 and potentials pi satisfying
        w_ab <= pi_b-pi_a <= rho w_ab for every ab in E.       (1)
The minimum exists and is finite: assigning all pi_a=0 and all pi_b=max_e w_e
gives a feasible rho=max w/min w. Isolated vertices impose no restriction.

THEOREM. There is a nonnegative, coalition-covering mechanism, truthful against
whole-bundle underreports by fixed actual owners, with total funding at most
        (3/2) rho(G,w) F_w.
It also inherits the unit mechanism's stronger coalition TOTAL-payment
underreport monotonicity. In particular rho<4/3 improves on funding factor2.

PROOF. Choose a public solution to(1), and put s_ab=pi_b-pi_a. Shift every
potential by the same constant so both lists are nonnegative. Then s is a
public source-price matrix, with source cost pi_a and service value pi_b,
on the original fixed access graph. ACCESS_PRICE_COAREA.txt and the internally
audited Huber unit-graph mechanism give a fixed-owner mechanism with payments
covering every coalition's F_s and total at most(3/2)F_s. For every feasible
allocation, w<=s<=rho w implies
        F_w(S)<=F_s(S)<=rho F_w(S)
at every coalition and report profile. Consequently its payments cover F_w
and its total is at most(3rho/2)F_w. The mechanism uses a fixed public technology
s throughout; it is never renormalized or refitted in response to reports.
Thus all incentive properties remain those of the s-mechanism. QED.

2. EXACT CYCLE FORMULA
For each simple undirected cycle C in this bipartite graph, let A(C),B(C)
be the sums of its edge profits in the two alternating parity classes. Then
        rho(G,w)=max{1, max_C A(C)/B(C), max_C B(C)/A(C)}.     (2)
An acyclic graph has rho=1. The maximum is over a finite set; all denominators
are positive because every retained edge has positive profit.

Proof. Conditions(1) are difference constraints: add arc a->b of length
rho w_ab and arc b->a of length -w_ab. They are feasible exactly when this
directed constraint graph has no negative directed cycle. The two-arc
backtrack on an edge has length(rho-1)w_ab>=0. Every longer simple directed
cycle alternates the positive upper-bound and negative lower-bound arcs and
corresponds to a simple even cycle of the original bipartite graph. Its two
orientations have lengths rho A-B and rho B-A. Thus(2) is necessary and
sufficient for every simple directed cycle to be nonnegative. Every closed
directed walk decomposes into simple directed cycles, so no other constraint
is missing. When no negative cycle exists, shortest-path potentials from an
added zero-length super-source satisfy all difference constraints. This also
proves attainment at(2). Rational public profits give rational rho and public
potentials. A linear program with one rho variable, one potential per vertex,
and two constraints per edge computes the same optimum; formula(2) does not
claim that explicitly listing all cycles is efficient. QED.

Example: on a four-cycle with successive profits(10,10,10,11), alternating
sums are20 and21, so rho=21/20 and funding at most63/40=1.575. This example
illustrates a general bound for arbitrary-size graphs, rather than serving as
its evidence. For additive profits every alternating sum agrees and rho=1.

3. UNDIRECTED RESOURCE-GRAPH COROLLARY
Let an arbitrary finite undirected simple graph have public positive edge
profits w_uv. Define the smallest vertex-additive majorant distortion
 rho_add=min{rho>=1: w_uv<=alpha_u+alpha_v<=rho w_uv for all uv}. (3)
Then the same funding bound(3rho_add/2)F_w holds for arbitrary fixed mixed
owners, including coalition-total underreport monotonicity.

This follows directly from the composition lane's mechanism for vertex-additive
weighted graphs and the same production sandwich. Its exact distortion can be
computed using(2) on the bipartite double cover: replace v by L_v,R_v, and uv
by L_u R_v and L_v R_u with the same public profit. A solution alpha to(3)
gives pi_{L_v}=-alpha_v and pi_{R_v}=alpha_v, hence a feasible double-cover
majorant with the same rho. Conversely any double-cover potentials produce
        alpha_v=(pi_{R_v}-pi_{L_v})/2.
For original edge uv, alpha_u+alpha_v is the average of the two oriented
majorants pi_{R_v}-pi_{L_u} and pi_{R_u}-pi_{L_v}, so it also lies between
w_uv and rho w_uv. Thus the optimum distortions agree exactly.

Equivalently rho_add is the largest ratio of the two alternating parity sums
over EVEN CLOSED WALKS in the original graph, together with1. Multiplicities
count. Every such walk lifts to a closed walk of the double cover; conversely
every double-cover cycle projects to an even closed walk. Simple cycles of the
finite double cover suffice for the maximum. Simple even cycles of the original
graph alone need NOT suffice: two odd cycles joined by a path generate an
additional even closed walk with repeated path edges. This is the quantitative
counterpart of the established node-induced-weight characterization discussed
in composition/WEIGHTED_ADDITIVE_GRAPH_THREE_HALVES.txt.

4. CONTRACT LIMITS AND ECONOMIC INTERPRETATION
The extension permits public route-specific transfer or conversion overheads
that leave the profit matrix close to a separable source/service matrix. The
distortion is a property of public technology, not observed owner reports or
an estimated strategic cost. No private-cost truthfulness, capacity-dependent
cost, nonlinear outside self-production, integer job, or latency claim follows.

Crucially, this theorem is FLOOR-FREE. A production majorant does not preserve
grand-marginal participation floors: with one hardware unit and two energy
units, profits w=(2,1) give first energy owner's marginal1, whereas majorant
s=(2,2) gives that marginal0. Therefore F_s>=F_w alone does not license
transferring a floor F_w-F_w(without owner) from the s-rule. An additional floor
argument would be required. The sharp4/3 complete-access floor theorem remains
a different result, and arbitrary-access full-floor funding remains open here.

The linear feasibility/cycle-ratio characterization and the sandwich argument
are routine mathematical ingredients. The useful proposed consequence is the
explicit scope and bound for the fixed-owner mechanism obtained from the
audited unit-price rule. Historical priority remains unconfirmed.
