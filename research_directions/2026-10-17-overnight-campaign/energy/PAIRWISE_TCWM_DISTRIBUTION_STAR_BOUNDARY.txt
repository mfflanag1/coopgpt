NONCONIC STAR-BOUNDARY DATA AND THE PRECISE EXTENSION OBSTRUCTION
17 October2026. Boundary-selection lemma and structural necessary condition.
Internally audited by root and independent Fable053200; no substantive error.

LIMITATION FIRST
The explicit formula below covers profiles with at most ONE owner of one
residual sign and arbitrarily many owners of the other sign. It verifies the
pair-Lipschitz conditions for every coordinate variation staying in that
chart. Creating a second owner of the initially unique sign leaves the chart.
Thus this is compatible boundary data for the unrestricted problem, not an
all-profile mixed-owner solution and not a new main-paper theorem. Its value
is a nonconic construction plus an exact architecture-free extension condition.

1. THE BOUNDARY FORMULA
Let one negative owner have capacity B>=0 and positive owners have capacities
x_i>=0. For B=0 all payments are zero. For B>0 put
 r_i=min(x_i/B,1), R=sum_i r_i, A=sum_i x_i.
If A<=B, pay u_i=x_i/2 to each positive owner and u_B=A/2.
If A>=B (hence R>=1), set
 p(R)=1/(2R) for1<=R<=2,
 p(R)=exp(-(R-2)/2)/4 for R>=2,
 u_i=B r_i p(R),  u_B=B(1-p(R)).                       (1)
The two formulas agree at A=B, and p is C1 at R=2. When R=1 but A>B, there
is one saturated positive owner and the capped formula applies; it pays each
active owner B/2, not A/2. Use the reflected rule
when the unique owner has the positive sign. On the overlap of the charts
(one owner per sign), both receive half the smaller capacity, so they agree.
Null residual owners always receive zero.

2. ALL-COALITION COVERAGE AND FUNDING
Coalitions without the unique negative owner have worth zero. For a coalition
with that owner, if its positive total X<=B then its capped total equals X;
if X>=B then its capped total is at least B. Thus for R>=1 its payment is
 p*sum_Q min(x_i,B)+B(1-p)>=min(X,B).
For A<=B its payment is(X+A)/2>=X, since X<=A<=B.

Funding is efficient when A<=B. When A>=B grand production is B and
 Y/B=1+(R-1)p(R).
On[1,2] the excess is(R-1)/(2R)<=1/4. On[2,infinity), elementary
differentiation gives its maximum at R=3, equal to1/(2 sqrt(e)). Hence
 Y<=[1+1/(2 sqrt(e))]F, approximately1.30326533 F.       (2)
Both owner count and capacities are unbounded in this boundary statement.

3. EVERY REQUIRED PAIR SLOPE ON THE CHART LIES IN[0,1]
Each protected pair contains the owner whose capacity is being changed; its
other member may leave its report unchanged. Singleton conditions are included.
Work away from cap ties and R=1,2; continuity then gives finite inequalities
across these finitely many breakpoints on each coordinate line. For R>1 write
 a=-p'/p=1/min(R,2), U=sum_{i:x_i<B} r_i.
We have 0<p<=1/(2R), 0<=U<=R, and r_i+r_k<=min(R,2).

When an unsaturated positive owner i increases x_i, the derivatives are
 D u_i=p(1-a r_i), D u_k=-p a r_k(k!=i), D u_B=p a.
Thus a positive-positive protected pair has derivative
 p[1-a(r_i+r_k)] in[0,1], and a pair containing B has derivative
 p[1+a(1-r_i)] in[0,1]. A saturated positive owner's increase changes nothing.
Singleton derivatives satisfy the same bounds.

When B increases, Dp=p a U/B. A saturated positive owner's derivative is
 p(1+aU); an unsaturated one's is p aU r_i; the negative owner's is
 1-p(1+aU). Their protected-pair sums are respectively1 and
 1-p[1+aU(1-r_i)], all in[0,1]. The bound p(1+aU)<=1 follows from
 p<=1/(2R): for1<R<=2, aU<=1; for R>=2, aU<=R/2.
When A<B, the rule is simply half of each positive capacity and half of A,
and the same inequalities are immediate. Payments vanish continuously as
B tends to zero. Cap crossings, A=B, and R=1,2 therefore create no jumps.

This verifies precisely the finite pair-Lipschitz inequalities on coordinate
segments lying in the star chart. It does not assert them for paths leaving
the chart and later returning.

4. ARCHITECTURE-FREE DOMINANT-OWNER DELETION INEQUALITY
Consider ANY nonnegative residual allocation satisfying all-coalition coverage
and the upper pair-Lipschitz condition, with no sampling or projection form.
Suppose owner i has positive capacity x_i at least B=sum_{j in J}b_j, where
J is the set of m>=2 negative owners. Other positive owners are arbitrary.
Let z^{-j} denote the profile with negative owner j set to zero. For each j,

 u_i(z)+u_j(z)<=u_i(z^{-j})+u_j(z^{-j})+b_j.

Sum these inequalities. Coverage of{i} union J gives
u_i(z)+sum_Ju_j(z)>=B. Subtraction yields the necessary condition

 (m-1)u_i(z)<=sum_J u_i(z^{-j})+sum_J u_j(z^{-j}).      (3)

Under a null-zero rule the last sum vanishes. In particular, for m=2 a
dominant owner's payment at a two-sided profile cannot exceed the sum of its
payments on the two star boundaries obtained by removing one negative owner.
This is a constraint on ANY compatible extension, not just formula(1).

Finite-null version: gain-K means total payments Y<=S+K G, with
S=sum_i min(a_i,b_i) and G=min(sum_i a_i,sum_i b_i)-S. For an anonymous
n-owner gain-K rule and a pure profile
with k active owners, each deleted profile has n-k+1 null owners. Its total
production is B-b_j because owner i remains dominant. Nonnegativity and the
gain budget bound each null payment by K(B-b_j)/(n-k+1). Thus(3) implies

 u_i(z)<=sum_Ju_i(z^{-j})/(m-1)+K B/(n-k+1).           (4)

This uses finite owner symmetrization, not strategic owner splitting. It also
explains why bounded gain budgets make the exact null-zero inequality relevant
to projective limits as the fixed owner population grows.
At these pure profiles S=0, so the total bound is K F, not (1+K)F. Subtracting
active coverage could sharpen the null allowance, but is unnecessary here.

5. THE NATURAL GLOBAL CAPPED-PRICE EXTENSION FAILS
One tempting extension uses(1) whenever total A>=total B, pays each positive
owner min(x_i,B)p(R), and splits B(1-p(R)) among negative owners in proportion
to their capacities. It covers all coalitions and preserves funding(2), but
fails pair-Lipschitz, so those two easy checks do NOT yield a mechanism.

Take signed profile z=(2,1/2,-1,-1/2). Total B=3/2, R=4/3 and p=3/8, so
the dominant positive owner is paid9/16. Removing the negative capacity1/2
gives its star payment1/3; removing the negative capacity1 gives1/8. Their
sum11/24 is smaller than9/16 by5/48. Null payments are zero. Condition(3)
therefore excludes this extension exactly.

Locally the same defect is transparent: if the changed negative owner has
share s_j=b_j/B<1 and a positive partner is saturated, their pair derivative
is1+(1-s_j)p aU>1 whenever another positive owner is unsaturated. The missing
correction must therefore depend on the DISTRIBUTION of opposite capacities
among actual owners, and must lower or reallocate the dominant owner's payment
relative to the aggregate-opposite-capacity formula. Common proportional
splitting cannot supply it.

STOPPING DECISION
The bounded conceptual gate produced compatible nonconic star data and the
unrestricted necessary condition(3), but no extension to all signed profiles.
The general uniform-K pair-TCWM gain-only question remains OPEN. A meaningful
successor must solve the coupled selection problem subject to(3) and the full
pair-Lipschitz inequalities, or derive an unbounded lower from such inequalities.
Another scalar sampling law, separable projection, or proportional splitting
does not reopen the routes already excluded in this campaign.
