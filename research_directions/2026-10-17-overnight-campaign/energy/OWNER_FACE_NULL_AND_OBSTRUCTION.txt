NULL-OWNER REFINEMENT AND EXACT OWNER-FACE ARCHITECTURE BOUNDARY
17 October 2026. Candidate companion; root already has the basic owner bound
in root/OWNER_FULL_OUT_SIMPLE_UPPER.txt. No novelty claim.

1. A ZERO-NULL VERSION OF THE (n+1)/2 FULL-OUT UPPER

For finite nonnegative monotone concave degree-one homogeneous F, fixed
actual owner bundles b_i, and C=sum_i b_i, define

  y_i=F((C+b_i)/2) if b_i is nonzero, and y_i=0 if b_i is zero.

If k owners are active, Jensen and homogeneity give

          sum_i y_i <= (k+1)F(C)/2.

This is an active-owner bound for ONE all-profile rule, not a choice among
different mechanisms based on whichever profile bound happens to be smaller.

Full OUT has a direct finite proof. Let a coalition withhold total d, and
let d_i be its member i's withheld bundle. For a member active both before
and after the report change, the high evaluation argument minus its low
argument is (d+d_i)/2. Concavity and homogeneity imply superadditivity,
F(x+z)>=F(x)+F(z), so that member's payment loss is at least F(d)/2.
For a member active only at the high report, its low payment is zero and
its high evaluation argument is at least d/2, hence its payment loss is
again at least F(d)/2. Owners inactive at the high report have no withheld
resources and zero payment at both profiles.

If the coalition has at least two high-report active members, the summed
loss is at least F(d). If it has exactly one, all its withheld resources
belong to that owner. If this owner remains active at the low report, the
argument difference is (d+d_i)/2=d and superadditivity gives loss F(d).
If it becomes inactive, then b_i=d and C_high>=d, so its high evaluation
argument is at least d and its payment is at least F(d). The empty case is
trivial. This proves full OUT, including all activity changes and zero faces.

Coverage follows by restoring an entire coalition from zero reports.
No derivative, boundary continuity, or resource eligibility change is used.
The factor is not asserted optimal among all n-owner mechanisms. For example,
with exactly two owners the efficient two-owner Shapley split is full OUT:
y_i=[F(b_i)+F(C)-F(b_other)]/2. Its singleton restoration gain is at least
F(d_i) by two superadditivity applications, and the grand coalition's payment
is F(C). The efficient formula is a separate all-profile rule for n=2.

2. NO FIXED POSITIVE OWNER-FACE BLEND CAN BEAT (n+1)/2

Consider the following broad fixed architecture, for a single fixed public
technology and n fixed fully eligible owners:

  y_i(b)=sum_l w_il F(sum_j a_ilj b_j),

where all w_il and a_ilj are nonnegative constants chosen in advance and
independent of the report profile. A finite sum suffices; a positive measure
version has the same proof if the coefficient integrals are finite. One may
optionally set y_i=0 whenever its own report is zero. The constants may depend
on n and the public technology. No resource-count bound is being claimed.

THEOREM. Already for the fixed two-resource technology F(x)=min(x_1,x_2),
full OUT forces the architecture's worst-case funding factor to be at least
(n+1)/2. Thus Section 1 is sharp within this architecture, even though a
different nonlinear aggregate-price mechanism achieves the fixed factor
1.543063... on this same two-resource technology for every n.

Proof. All comparisons below use the same fixed fully eligible owners and
the same F. At a singleton profile b_i=(1,1), every other report zero, full
OUT for owner i's complete withdrawal requires

                  sum_l w_il a_ili>=1.                 (A)

For each unordered pair {i,j}, use the profile b_i=(1,0), b_j=(0,1), all
other reports zero. The pair can produce one with its withheld bundle, so
full OUT for its complete joint withdrawal requires

  sum_l w_il min(a_ili,a_ilj)
   +sum_l w_jl min(a_jli,a_jlj)>=1.

Since each minimum is at most its cross-owner coefficient, this implies

       sum_l w_il a_ilj + sum_l w_jl a_jli>=1.          (B)

Now use ONE common final profile in which all n owners report (1,1). For
any fixed linear combination in the architecture,

                   F(sum_j a_ilj b_j)=sum_j a_ilj.

Total payment is therefore the sum of all weighted own and cross-owner
coefficients. Summing (A) over owners and (B) over unordered pairs gives
total payment at least n+n(n-1)/2=n(n+1)/2. Grand production is n, so the
funding factor is at least (n+1)/2. Optional null-owner gating changes none
of these necessary comparisons or the final all-active profile. QED.

This is a method obstruction, not an unrestricted owner-count lower. It
rules out improving the leading n/2 coefficient by changing the fixed
distribution of scalar owner-face weights, adding more fixed positive face
evaluations, or using a fixed nonuniform owner matrix. Profile-dependent
nonlinear rules and signed differences are outside the architecture. Any
future use of signed differences must separately establish coalition OUT;
individual underreport monotonicity alone is insufficient.

3. SHARP OBSTRUCTION FOR ALL GLOBALLY MONOTONE INDIVIDUAL PAYMENTS

There is a stronger architecture theorem, requiring no oracle-evaluation
representation. Say a rule has global individual-payment monotonicity if
every owner's payment is nondecreasing under every coordinatewise increase
of ANY owner's report. This is stronger than TCWM: under TCWM a bystander's
payment may fall, provided the increasing owner's gain compensates the
coalition's total loss.

THEOREM. For n>=2 fixed fully eligible owners and F=min(x_1,x_2), any
nonnegative coalition-covering globally monotone rule has funding factor at
least (n+1)/2. The null-zero rule in Section 1 attains this factor and full
OUT. Thus the bound is sharp within the entire globally monotone class.

Proof. Average a putative rule over all permutations of the fixed owner
labels and both resource labels. The domain, F, funding bound, nonnegativity,
coverage and global monotonicity are preserved. At a profile with only two
active owners, reporting (1,0) and (0,1), symmetry interchanging these owners
and the resource labels gives them equal payments. Their pair coalition
produces one, so each receives at least 1/2.

Now use the single final profile with n-1 owners reporting (1,0) and one
distinguished owner reporting (1,1). Grand production is one. For each pure
owner, its preceding two-owner complementary profile, with the distinguished
owner reporting (0,1), is coordinatewise below this final profile. Global
monotonicity therefore gives that pure owner at least 1/2 at the final profile.
The distinguished owner's singleton profile (1,1), with all other reports
zero, is also below the final profile. Singleton coverage gives it at least
one there and hence at least one at the final profile. Summing yields
(n-1)/2+1=(n+1)/2, as required. All comparisons keep owners fixed. QED.

In particular, nonnegative jointly concave homogeneous payment functions of
the entire report vector cannot improve this bound: each such payment is
superadditive and therefore globally nondecreasing. The theorem also covers
all fixed positive oracle blends in Section 2, with or without null gating.
It does not cover the accepted nonlinear aggregate-price mechanisms, whose
individual payments may decrease when other owners increase their reports.

Consequently negative payment externalities are necessary for any sublinear
owner-count funding theorem, even on the elementary two-resource minimum
technology. A dynamic envelope that makes every individual payment monotone
in every owner's report has already lost the needed possibility of such a
bound. An envelope must protect coalition TOTALS while permitting controlled
redistribution among coalition members.

4. CURRENT GENERAL GAP

For a fixed n and resource dimension d, one may choose in advance either the
universal dimension rule or the owner rule, yielding the upper

                 min{(n+1)/2, 1+c(d-1)}.

Choosing one rule in advance based on the fixed public n and d is valid.
Selecting the lower realized payment rule separately at each report profile
is not justified by these proofs. The accepted fixed-owner min_r lower
provides an order-sqrt(n) obstruction when d also grows; the owner upper
still grows linearly. This gap is a substantive next frontier. The closed
fixed-positive-face class cannot close it, so repeating its parameter search
would not meet the campaign's progress gate.
