SHARP RESOURCE-DIMENSION BOUND FOR FULL COALITION OUTSIDE PRODUCTION
17 October 2026. New theorem candidate; independent review pending.
Joint derivation by the energy lane and root. Historical priority unconfirmed.

THEOREM.
Let F:R_+^d -> R_+ be finite, coordinatewise nondecreasing, concave, and
positively homogeneous of degree one. There is one all-profile payment rule,
for arbitrary finitely many fixed actual owners of arbitrary mixed bundles,
with nonnegative payments, coalition coverage, and full coalition OUT:

  Y_S(b) >= Y_S(b-withheld_S) + F(sum_(i in S) withheld_i).

Every withheld coordinate is nonnegative and bounded by its actual owner's
reported resource. The coalition can produce with its aggregate withheld
bundle. No identity changes, overreports, private costs, or outside resources
are allowed. If k resource coordinates are active at the report profile, then

  sum_i y_i <= [1+c(k-1)] F(sum_i b_i),

where k=0 means zero payment and

  c[2-exp(-1/c)]=1,  c=0.5430633898250876...,
  t0=1-exp(-1/c)=1/c-1,  c(1+t0)=1.

The root is uniquely in (1/2,1): the left side is below 1 at c=1/2 and
above 1 at c=1, and its derivative is
2-exp(-1/c)(1+1/c)>1, using exp(x)>1+x. In particular 2c>1 and 0<t0<1.

For every d>=2, the factor 1+c(d-1) is optimal uniformly over technologies
and all finite fixed owner populations. The already independently audited
min_d unrestricted OUT lower in SHARP_MULTIPOOL_OUTSIDE_CANDIDATE.txt matches
this universal upper. The d=1 factor is exactly 1.

This is a stronger contract than payment-only TCWM, whose sharp universal
dimension factor is (d+1)/2. Neither result is a sharp bound for the original
individual-payment-only incentive problem. The theorem makes no claim of
efficient evaluation for an arbitrary oracle F, and no claim of novelty.

1. THE RULE

Draw ONE common scalar U, with density c/(1-u) on 0<u<t0. Its total mass is
-c log(1-t0)=1, and E U=c[-t0-log(1-t0)]=1-c t0=c.
For aggregate capacity C and each j with C_j>0 define

  H_j(C)=E F(C_j e_j + U sum_(l!=j) C_l e_l),
  p_j(C)=H_j(C)/C_j,
  y_i(b)=sum_(j:C_j>0) b_ij p_j(C).

Absent-coordinate contributions are zero. The same random scalar multiplies
ALL other coordinates within each face average. This is different from the
independent uniform averages in the TCWM theorem. The formula depends only
on the fixed reported technology and actual owner bundles; no strategic
splitting or merging of identities is involved.

2. FUNDING AND FINITE PAYMENT MONOTONICITY

For k positive coordinates, Jensen applied first to U and then across j gives

  sum_j H_j(C)
  <= sum_j F(C_j e_j + c sum_(l!=j) C_l e_l)
  <= k F(((1+c(k-1))/k) C)
   = [1+c(k-1)] F(C).

For a coalition with bundle a and outsiders o, its coordinate-j payment is

  [a_j/(a_j+o_j)] H_j(a+o).

Both nonnegative factors are coordinatewise nondecreasing in a. Each old
coordinate contribution therefore increases under an arbitrary restoration
of coalition resources. A newly active coordinate contributes nonnegatively;
the old integrands increase when that coordinate changes from zero to
positive. Thus finite TCWM holds on and across all faces, even when F has
downward jumps at the boundary of a face.

3. TWO ELEMENTARY LEMMAS

Lemma A (ray coverage). For any positive x in R_+^k, k>=2, write m=min_j x_j,
S=the second-smallest coordinate counting multiplicity, and t=m/S. Then

  sum_j E min(x_j,U min_(l!=j) x_l)/x_j >=1.

Proof. Choose one shortest coordinate. Its term is

  q(t)=E min(1,U/t)
      =1-c-c(1-t)log(1-t)/t,  0<t<=t0;
      =c/t,                    t0<=t<=1.

Every other term is c m/x_j; in particular a second-shortest coordinate
contributes ct. For t<=t0,

  q(t)+ct-1=c(1-t)[-1-log(1-t)/t]>=0,

using -log(1-t)>=t. For t>=t0, q(t)+ct=c(t+1/t)>=2c>1.
This proves the lemma, including shortest-coordinate ties.

Lemma B (concave scalar inequality). If H is nonnegative, nondecreasing and
concave on [0,1], and 0<=t<=t0<1, then

  I := integral_0^t0 [H(u)-u H'(u)]du
     =2 integral_0^t0 H(u)du-t0 H(t0)
     >=t0 H(t)-t H(1).

The derivative is needed only almost everywhere. A possible downward jump
at zero can be filled with its right limit, without changing any integral.
For a continuous piecewise-linear concave nondecreasing H, decompose it as
a nonnegative constant, a nonnegative multiple of u, and a positive sum of
hinges min(u,s), 0<s<=1. Constants and linear functions satisfy the assertion.
For a hinge, the residual I+t H(1)-t0 H(t) is

  s(t-s)                    if 0<s<=t;
  (s-t)(t0-s)               if t<s<t0;
  t(s-t0)                  if t0<=s<=1.

All are nonnegative. General H follows by uniform piecewise-linear concave
approximation after filling the endpoint. The integral identity makes this
limit legitimate without convergence of derivatives.

4. THE FULL OUT DIFFERENTIAL INEQUALITY ON AN ACTIVE FACE

First suppose F is smooth on the positive relative interior of the current
active face. Let C>0 on that face, let a be an arbitrary coalition bundle
with 0<=a<=C, and let d>=0 be a restoration direction. Put alpha_j=a_j/C_j.
Differentiation of the coalition payment along simultaneous restoration gives

  D Y_S[d] = sum_j { alpha_j B_j(d)+(1-alpha_j) A_j(d) },

  A_j(d)=H_j(C)d_j/C_j,
  B_j(d)=E[U grad F(v_j(U)).d
                 +(1-U) partial_j F(v_j(U)) d_j],
  v_j(u)=C_j e_j+u sum_(l!=j) C_l e_l.

Gradients are nonnegative. By concavity and homogeneity, every such gradient
is a supporting price: grad F(v).d>=F(d) for nonnegative d on this face.
Hence B_j(d)>=c F(d), and A_j(d)>=0. The expression is affine in each alpha,
so it suffices to consider corners alpha_j in {0,1}. Let J be the set of
coordinates with alpha_j=1.

If |J|>=2, the corner expression is at least 2c F(d)>=F(d).
If F(d)=0, nonnegativity already proves the desired inequality.
Assume now F(d)>0 and initially d>0 on the active face.

If J is empty, put x_j=C_j/d_j. Monotonicity and homogeneity imply

  F(v_j(u))/F(d) >= min(x_j,u min_(l!=j) x_l).

Therefore sum_j A_j(d)>=F(d) by Lemma A. For a one-coordinate active face,
the assertion is directly equality by homogeneity.

It remains to treat a singleton J={j}. Normalize by F(d), and define
G(z)=F(d*z)/F(d), with coordinatewise product, so G(1)=1. By common scaling
of all x_j=C_j/d_j, which leaves these directional expressions unchanged,
normalize min_(l!=j)x_l=1. Write t=x_j and V=G(x). Monotonicity gives

  sum_(l!=j) A_l(d)/F(d)
    >= c V sum_(l!=j) 1/x_l >= c V.

Indeed, every face vector is at least u x, and one outside coordinate has
x_l=1. Also B_j(d)/F(d)>=c. If V>=t0, these estimates already give at least
c(1+t0)=1. If t>=1 then V>=G(1)=1, so that case is included.

The only remaining case is 0<t<=V<t0. Define

  H(u)=G(t e_j+u sum_(l!=j)x_l e_l),  0<=u<=1.

It is nonnegative, nondecreasing, and concave; H(t)>=t and H(1)=V.
Euler's identity gives, for u>0,

  partial_j G(t,u x_-j)=[H(u)-u H'(u)]/t.

Because (1-u) times the density of U is the constant c, the second,
nonnegative term in B_j supplies an additional

  c integral_0^t0 partial_j G(t,u x_-j)du
  = (c/t) I >= c(t0-V),

where Lemma B was used. Thus the singleton corner is at least

  c+c(t0-V)+cV=c(1+t0)=1.

Every corner, hence every coalition holding fraction alpha, satisfies

                         D Y_S[d]>=F(d).

Directions with some zero coordinates follow by applying the result to
d+delta*1 and letting delta decrease to zero. The derivative expression is
linear in the direction, and F(d+delta*1)>=F(d); no boundary continuity of F
is required for this passage.

Differentiating the face averages is legitimate on each compact positive
capacity set. Euler's identity and nonnegative gradients give
C_j partial_j F(v_j(u))<=F(C) and u C_l partial_l F(v_j(u))<=F(C).
These bounds dominate every differentiated integrand uniformly down to u=0.

5. FINITE DEVIATIONS, FACE CHANGES, AND NONSMOOTH F

For a finite coalition restoration d, write a(s)=a(0)+s d and C(s)=o+a(s),
0<=s<=1. For every s>0 the support of C(s) is the fixed support of C(1).
On that face, integrate the proved derivative inequality from epsilon to1:

  Y_S(1)-Y_S(epsilon)>=(1-epsilon)F(d).

Finite TCWM from Section 2 gives Y_S(epsilon)>=Y_S(0), even if crossing into
this face creates upward payment jumps. Consequently
Y_S(1)-Y_S(0)>=(1-epsilon)F(d), and epsilon down to zero proves full OUT.
If the active face has one coordinate, the same conclusion follows directly
from linearity on that ray; the zero face is trivial.

To remove smoothness, choose a smooth probability density supported in
[-1,1]^d and set

  F_epsilon(x)=E_z F(diag(exp(epsilon z_j)) x).

This averages fixed diagonal linear transformations, so it preserves
concavity, monotonicity, nonnegativity and degree-one homogeneity. Logarithmic
convolution makes F_epsilon smooth on every positive relative face. Further,

  exp(-epsilon) F(x)<=F_epsilon(x)<=exp(epsilon) F(x)

at EVERY x, including faces with possible downward jumps. The finite OUT
inequality therefore holds for F_epsilon and its face-average payments.
The relative bounds imply pointwise convergence of F_epsilon and all its
face-average payments to their F counterparts at every report and withheld
bundle. Passing to the limit proves full OUT for the stated entire class.

Coalition coverage also follows directly from full OUT by restoring the
coalition's entire bundle from its zero report. Its low-profile payment is
zero, so its high-profile payment is at least F(its aggregate bundle).

6. SHARPNESS, SPECIALIZATION, AND STATUS

For F(C)=min_j C_j, every non-shortest face average is c min C, while the
shortest face average gives q(T/S). Thus this rule specializes exactly to
MULTIPOOL_OUTSIDE_LOGARITHMIC.txt. Its independently audited unrestricted
lower proves the universal factor sharp for every dimension d. Lower-bound
mechanisms need not be anonymous, continuous, homogeneous, or aggregate-price
rules; the separate reduction theorem establishes the necessity.

The construction works for arbitrary fixed mixed owners and arbitrary finite
dimension. It includes public energy and hardware production LPs, resource
networks, weighted activities and nonlinear concave technologies. A graph's
special factor-two OUT guarantee can be better than this dimension bound;
this theorem is a sharp universal result, not a replacement for all structural
graph bounds. Public linear opportunity-cost settlement can be added using
the previously defined reimbursement contract. Private costs and physical
cluster efficiency remain separate economic and empirical questions.

This analytic proof is the evidence. Numerical checks can challenge its
algebra but do not establish the transcendental constant or universal claim.
Independent review and original-source priority comparison remain required.
