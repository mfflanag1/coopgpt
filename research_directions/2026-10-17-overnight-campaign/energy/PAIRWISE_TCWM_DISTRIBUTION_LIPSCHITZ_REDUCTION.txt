PAIRWISE TCWM AS A PURE-RESIDUAL LIPSCHITZ COVER-SELECTION PROBLEM
17 October 2026. Exact structural reduction; independent review pending.

DOMAIN. n fixed actual owners each may hold both nonnegative resource types,
with full real unbounded report orthant. F(A,B)=min(A,B). Payments cover every
coalition; incentive protection concerns singleton/pair binding underreports,
and permits a protected member changing zero. Gain funding is Y<=S+K G,
S=sum_i min(a_i,b_i), G=F(total)-S. No outside-production protection is imposed.

THEOREM. Existence of such a pair-TCWM mechanism with a given K is equivalent
to existence of a map u:R^n->R_+^n satisfying (i)--(iii):

(i) For every signed profile z and coalition Q,
 u(Q;z)>=min(sum_Q z_i^+,sum_Q (-z_i)^+).
(ii) sum_i u_i(z)<=K min(sum_i z_i^+,sum_i (-z_i)^+).
(iii) For every singleton or pair Q, every j in Q, every fixed z_-j, every
 sign sigma in {-1,+1}, and 0<=x<=x',

 0 <= u(Q;z_-j,sigma*x')-u(Q;z_-j,sigma*x) <= x'-x.       (L)

The corresponding original payment is
 y_i(a,b)=min(a_i,b_i)+u_i(a-b).                          (P)

This is a finite-difference characterization on all real profiles. It does
not assume differentiability, measurability, anonymity, owner-merger neutrality,
or a vanishing payment for a zero residual owner. In particular, no derivative
test is silently promoted to an all-profile incentive proof.

1. WHY TRANSLATION EQUIVARIANCE IS WITHOUT LOSS
Use the compact finite-translation averaging proof in
PAIRWISE_GAIN_TRANSLATION_REDUCTION.txt, sections1--3. Those sections preserve
pair-TCWM just as they preserve full OUT: translating both compared profiles
and subtracting the same owner's constant leaves every incentive difference
unchanged. They use only singleton coverage and finite gain funding to bound
the excess coordinate in [0,K G]. The resulting rule is equivariant under
adding t_i to both reports of actual owner i and adding t_i to its payment.
Consequently it has form (P), where u is its payment at the canonical pure
residual profile ((z_i)^+,(-z_i)^+). This uses the full orthant; it does not
justify the reduction on a bounded report cube or pure-only eligibility.

2. COVERAGE AND BUDGET BECOME EXACTLY (i)--(ii)
For any coalition Q and f_i=min(a_i,b_i),
 F(sum_Q(a_i,b_i))=sum_Q f_i+min(sum_Q z_i^+,sum_Q(-z_i)^+).
Applying this also to the grand coalition yields (i) and (ii) directly.
Singleton coverage implies u_i>=0. Thus there is no lost economic constraint.

3. NECESSITY OF THE TWO SIDES OF (L)
Start at canonical pure residual profiles, with all outsiders fixed.
Increasing owner j's pure capacity from x to x' is an ordinary restoration of
one resource and leaves its standalone value zero. Pair-TCWM gives the lower
inequality in (L), including when Q contains an unchanged protected member.

For the upper inequality, start owner j instead at pure capacity x'. Restore
x'-x of the OTHER resource to this same actual owner. Its standalone value
increases by x'-x, and its signed residual magnitude decreases to x. All other
standalones and imbalances remain fixed. Pair-TCWM therefore says
 (x'-x)+u(Q;z_-j,sigma*x)>=u(Q;z_-j,sigma*x'),
which is the required upper inequality. This argument works for either sign
and includes x=0 exactly.

4. SUFFICIENCY FOR ALL REAL JOINT RESTORATIONS
Consider a restoration by one member j of a protected singleton or pair Q.
Restore its resource coordinates sequentially. When adding the resource of
its current residual sign, f_j stays fixed and the residual magnitude grows;
the lower half of (L) gives a nonnegative payment increment. When adding the
opposite resource before the sign crossing, f_j rises by delta and the residual
magnitude shrinks by delta; the upper half of (L) bounds the residual payment
loss by delta. After reaching zero, split the restoration at that point and
apply the first case. The same finite splitting works for either resource.
Then perform the other member's restoration, if any. At every intermediate
profile only the same protected members act. Summing the nonnegative finite
increments proves pair-TCWM for the original whole-bundle restoration.
No assumption on outsider payment monotonicity is used. Sign crossings are
handled by the common value at zero in (L), not by an unproved limiting step.

5. CONSEQUENCES FOR A CONSTRUCTIVE ATTACK
Any differentiable candidate must have every protected pair total slope in
[0,1] with respect to either member's pure capacity. A pure-profile TCWM rule
only guarantees the lower bound; ignoring the upper bound explains the failed
residual aggregate-price and waterfilling wrappers. The upper bound enforces
incentives when a mixed owner restores its opposite resource.

At every two-active-owner profile the active pair's total is exactly F.
For opposite pure capacities a>=b, restore a first-resource amount b to the
second owner. Grand production stays b and the resulting G is zero. The gain
budget then forces its standalone payment b and zero for all others. Pair
TCWM makes the initial active pair total at most b, while coverage makes it
at least b. Same-sign profiles have F=0. This argument also allows arbitrarily
many unchanged null owners and uses no assumed null-zero axiom.

This is a consequential search reduction, not a construction or lower bound.
The unrestricted uniform-K pair-TCWM gain-only question remains open. The
accepted full pair-OUT linear owner-count obstruction does not settle it.
Historical novelty of the reduction remains unconfirmed.
