EXACT BOUNDARY OF POSITIVE TWO-INPUT DECOMPOSITION FOR UNIT RESOURCE GRAPHS
2026-10-17. Candidate structural theorem; independent review and priority pending.

Let G be a finite simple unit-profit resource graph, with fractional matching
production F_G(c)=max sum_e x_e subject to sum_(e incident v)x_e<=c_v, x_e>=0.
Capacities are arbitrary nonnegative reals. A positive binary-minimum
decomposition means a finite exact identity, valid for EVERY capacity vector,

             F_G(c)=sum_l w_l min(a_l.c,b_l.c),           (1)

where w_l>0 and a_l,b_l are fixed public vectors with nonnegative coordinates.
An optional fixed linear term does not affect the obstruction below. This class
includes arbitrary same-owner nonnegative linear resource copies followed by
independent two-input minimum technologies, not just original graph edges.

THEOREM. Such a decomposition exists if and only if every connected component
of G is complete multipartite (isolated vertices are allowed).

This class therefore admits the sharp two-input full-OUT funding factor1+c,
uniformly over graph and owner counts, via exact positive transfer. The result
does not preclude other mechanisms with the same factor on graphs outside the
class. It excludes this exact decomposition approach on those graphs.

1. SUFFICIENCY: AN EXPLICIT DECOMPOSITION

For a complete multipartite component, write C_j for the total capacity in part
j and C=sum_j C_j. Fractional matching on this graph has value

          F=min(C/2,C-max_j C_j)
           =(1/2)sum_j min(C_j,C-C_j).                   (2)

For the first formula, no matching can use over half the total capacity or more
than the capacity outside any one part. If one part exceeds C/2, match all other
capacity into that part. Otherwise pair capacities from different parts to
exhaust all capacity fractionally; equivalently, the complete graph on part
totals has a feasible fractional matching saturating all vertices whenever
max_j C_j<=C/2. One elementary construction places disjoint intervals of lengths
C_j around a circle of circumference C and matches antipodal points: no part
overlaps its own antipodal translate in positive length when its length<=C/2.
The induced undirected pairing uses each resource once and has value C/2.

For the second formula, if all C_j<=C/2, the summands total C. Otherwise exactly
one part is larger; its term is C-C_j, and all other terms sum to the same amount.
Within a part, vertex capacities can be divided to realize the part-level flows
because every cross-part edge is present. Disjoint components add their values.

Apply the two-input logarithmic full-OUT mechanism to each term of(2), multiply
payments by1/2, and sum. Each actual owner retains all copies of its resources.
Coalition values, withheld-bundle values, and total budgets add exactly. Every
nonempty graph in the class contains an edge; setting other capacities to zero
recovers the sharp two-input lower, with unbounded owner counts. Hence its
universal optimal factor is1+c. Edgeless graphs have identically zero value.

2. A POSITIVE-HINGE LEMMA

Suppose a concave piecewise-linear function F has an open interior patch of a
hyperplane H on which it equals min(L1,L2), with L1-L2 a nonzero multiple of
H's defining linear form. Suppose another positive interior point of H has a
neighborhood on which F is linear. Then F cannot have a finite representation
as a positive sum of minima of two linear forms, plus a linear term.

Proof. Every nonlinear binary summand has a full kink hyperplane H_l, where its
two forms agree. If none of these hyperplanes equals H, choose a point in the
open kink patch avoiding the finitely many intersections H_l intersect H. All
summands are differentiable there, contradicting F's kink. Thus H_l=H for at
least one nontrivial summand. At the point where F is locally linear, choose a
short line segment crossing H. This summand has a strictly positive midpoint
concavity gap. Every other concave summand has nonnegative midpoint gap, and a
linear term has zero gap, so their positive sum cannot be linear there. QED.

The positivity restriction is essential: signed hinge combinations can cancel.
The theorem concerns finite positive identities. No unrestricted integral-
representation or approximation assertion is needed.

3. THE TWO MINIMAL INDUCED OBSTRUCTIONS

P4: Label the path 0--1--2--3 and capacities x0,x1,x2,x3. Bipartite matching
duality, or direct elementary optimization, gives

 F_P4=min(x0+x2, x1+x3, x1+x2).

On H={x0=x1}, near (1,1,1,2), the first and third forms are the two active
forms and the second is strictly larger. Near (1,1,2,1), the second form is
strictly smaller, so F is linear even though the point still lies on H. The
positive-hinge lemma excludes(1).

Paw: Use a triangle with capacities A,B,C and attach a pendant D only to A.
Its production function is

 F_paw=min(A+B,A+C,B+C+D,(A+B+C+D)/2).                  (3)

One verification: send t along the pendant edge. The remaining triangle has
value min((A-t+B+C)/2,A-t+B,A-t+C,B+C), so total value is
min((A+B+C+t)/2,A+B,A+C,B+C+t), nondecreasing in t. Set t=min(A,D), simplify,
and obtain(3). The triangle formula follows by matching a dominant vertex to
the others, or by the standard three half-sum edge flows when balanced.

On H={A+C=B+D}, at (A,B,C,D)=(2,2,1,1), exactly A+C and half the total are
minimal. At (1,1,3,3) on the same H, A+B is strictly minimal. Again the positive-
hinge lemma excludes(1).

4. HEREDITY AND THE GRAPH CLASSIFICATION

The decomposition property is hereditary under induced subgraphs: set all
capacities outside a vertex subset to zero. All incident matching flows vanish,
and restricting the nonnegative linear forms preserves the proposed identity.
Thus a graph admitting(1) can contain neither an induced P4 nor an induced paw.

For completeness, a connected graph that is not complete multipartite contains
one of these two obstructions. The relation 'equal or nonadjacent' is an
equivalence relation exactly when the graph is complete multipartite. If this
fails, there are an edge uv and a vertex z nonadjacent to both u and v. Take a
shortest path from z to {u,v}. Its length is at least two. If the length is at
least three, its first four vertices induce P4. If the length is two, write it
z--w--u. If w is not adjacent to v, z,w,u,v induce P4. If w is adjacent to v,
u,v,w form a triangle and z is pendant, giving an induced paw.

Consequently every connected graph with neither obstruction is complete
multipartite. Combined with heredity and Section1, this proves the theorem.

when unrestricted nonnegative linear resource copies are allowed. Thus the
next general-graph full-OUT improvement cannot be obtained by simply searching
for a better exact positive decomposition into two-input minima. This says
nothing against coupled aggregate-price mechanisms, approximate decompositions,
or different primitives. The known unrestricted graph interval remains
[1.5430633898...,2].
