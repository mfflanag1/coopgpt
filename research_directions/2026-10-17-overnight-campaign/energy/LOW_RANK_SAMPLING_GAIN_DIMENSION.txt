1. A FINITE SAMPLING LEMMA FOR ARBITRARILY SELECTED NONNEGATIVE VECTORS
Fix n, G>0, and a linear subspace L of R^n of dimension D. For EVERY subset
B of [n], choose an arbitrary vector a(B) in L intersect R_+^n satisfying

  sum_{i in B}a_i(B)<=G.                                (1)

The choice may depend arbitrarily on B; it need not optimize a common problem.
Let each owner be retained in B independently with probability p=1-q>0.
Then

  E sum_{i notin B}min(a_i(B),G) <=[2Dq/(1-q)]G.          (2)

Proof. For a fixed retained set B, and each i in B, restrict the vector
a(B\{i}) to the coordinates in B and normalize by its coordinate sum. This
gives the i-th column v^i of a square |B|-by-|B| matrix V. If that sum is zero,
use the zero column. Every column is nonnegative, with sum either one or zero.
All columns lie in the coordinate projection of L, so rank(V)<=D.

The induced l1 matrix norm is at most one, hence every complex eigenvalue
has modulus at most one. The number of nonzero eigenvalues, counted with
algebraic multiplicity, is at most rank(V). Therefore

  0<=trace(V)<=sum |eigenvalues(V)|<=rank(V)<=D.           (3)

This argument does not require V to be symmetric or diagonalizable. The
trace is a nonnegative real number because the diagonal entries are nonnegative.

For each i in B, write z=a_i(B\{i}). The training bound (1) gives
sum_{j in B}a_j(B\{i})<=G+z. Consequently

  min(z/G,1)<=2z/(G+z)<=2v_i^i.                         (4)

If the column sum vanishes then z=0 and all terms are zero. Summing (4) and
using (3) bounds the leave-one-out capped total for THIS B by2D. Finally,
independent Bernoulli conditioning on the own bit gives the exact identity

 E sum_{i notin B}min(a_i(B)/G,1)
   =[q/(1-q)] E sum_{i in B}min(a_i(B\{i})/G,1).

This proves (2). Empty B contributes zero on the leave-one-out side; q=0
gives a zero left side. No asymptotic or probabilistic approximation is used.

2. APPLICATION TO THE MODIFIED-CAP MECHANISM
Use the contract and fixed mask mechanism from
GAIN_ONLY_MASK_AND_DIMENSION_THEOREM.txt. At one fixed profile, let
f_i=F(b_i), S=sum f_i, T=F(C), G=T-S>0. For finite global supports put

  a_i(B)=ell_{C_B}(b_i)-f_i.

These residual coefficients are nonnegative, and
sum_{i in B}a_i(B)=F(C_B)-sum_{i in B}f_i<=G.
For every mask they lie in the SAME linear subspace spanned by the d owner
coordinate columns (b_1j,...,b_nj) and the standalone column (f_1,...,f_n).
Its dimension D is at most d+1. The modified closed excess is min(a_i(B),G),
so (2) bounds its expected total C(q) by2(d+1)qG/(1-q).

3. BOUNDARY FACES: FINITE SURROGATES ONLY FOR THE ANALYSIS
The actual mechanism uses the manuscript's extended support ell_{C_B}, which
may be infinite off the coordinate face of C_B. It remains own-excluding;
we do NOT replace that mechanism by a support selected using other reports.

At a fixed profile there are finitely many masks and owners. For every mask,
retain its finite nonnegative support coefficients on active coordinates,
and replace every off-face coefficient by one common sufficiently large
finite number M. Choose M large enough that, for EVERY owner outside that
mask's face, the finite residual price is at least G:

  p_B(M) dot b_i-f_i >=G.

Such a finite M exists because each relevant owner has a strictly positive
sum of off-face coordinates and only finitely many inequalities occur. On-face
owners already have nonnegative residual prices, and their prices are unchanged.
For B=empty, use zero active coefficients and the same large off-face M; a
zero bundle remains priced at zero. Thus every surrogate vector is nonnegative,
and its capped coordinates agree EXACTLY with the original infinite-price
closed excesses. Its training sum is unchanged, since every owner in B has
zero capacity outside the face of C_B.

All surrogate vectors still lie in the span of the d coordinate columns
and f. The sampling lemma needs only that common span, nonnegativity, and
the training bound; it does not require common global majorants. Hence the
same C(q) bound is valid for arbitrary finite concave homogeneous F, including
downward discontinuities at coordinate faces. M is a proof device at a fixed
profile, not a mechanism parameter or an incentive-sensitive report change.

4. UNIVERSAL GAIN-ONLY RESOURCE THEOREM
The endpoint identity from the companion theorem says that the average
excess over standalone payments for q uniform on[0,a], 0<a<1, is at most

  [G+integral_0^a C(q)dq]/a
  <=[1+2(d+1)(-a-log(1-a))]G/a.                         (5)

Choose the PUBLIC parameter a=1/(1+sqrt(d+1)). The elementary inequality
-a-log(1-a)<=a^2/[2(1-a)] gives

  sum_i y_i <= S+[1+2sqrt(d+1)]G.                       (6)

The same fixed mechanism covers ALL coalitions and satisfies individual full
outside-production protection at EVERY profile, as proved for each mask in
the companion theorem. If G=0, every excess is exactly zero and y_i=f_i;
the normalized sampling lemma is unnecessary. Thus no positive-gain or
interior-report restriction is hidden in (6).

Choose whole rules once from public n,d and (when available) K. Combining
the companion owner, grand-marginal, and finite-form bounds yields

 sum_i y_i <=S+min{d,n sqrt(2/(n+1)),1+2sqrt(d+1),inf_j C_j(K)}G.          (7)

Omit the finite-form term when no finite K representation is supplied.
For a single min-r technology the exact residual physical representation
allows the older1+2sqrt(r) term, and K=r also gives the stronger asymptotic
log r/log log r term. These numerical ingredients are established; the
gain-only individual-OUT contract and the trace sampling transfer are the
