SHARED PUBLIC SOURCE PRICES WITH ALL ENERGY-OWNER MARGINAL FLOORS
17 October 2026. Candidate conventional theorem; independent audit required.
A constructive subdomain of SHARED_LOCAL_CONTRACT.txt. It permits unbounded
energy-owner and hardware-owner counts. This is NOT the arbitrary route-matrix
case, not a sharp optimality claim and not historical-novelty confirmation.

THEOREM
Fix disjoint finite sets of actual energy owners and hardware owners. Owner j's
energy quantity e_j>=0 has a PUBLIC constant opportunity/operating cost c_j>=0
already settled by the operator. Hardware owner i's quantity h_i>=0 supports
PUBLIC service value v_i>=0. One service unit consumes one energy and one
hardware unit; every energy source can serve every admitted hardware unit.
Net coalition production is the continuous transportation LP
 F(S)=max Σ_ij (v_i-c_j) z_ij,
 Σ_i z_ij<=e_j(S), Σ_j z_ij<=h_i(S), z>=0.
Negative-surplus matches may remain unused. Reports are nonnegative real
capacities with binding own-coordinate underreports, fixed identities and
payment-only utility after public costs are settled. No private costs are added.

There is a nonnegative all-profile own-bundle-monotone coalition-covering rule
paying EVERY energy owner j at least its grand marginal
 M_j=F(N)-F(N\{j}),
with total at most 3F(N)/2. Thus subsidy above net production is at most F/2.
The result is uniform in both owner counts and all public costs/values.
For public theta∈[0,1], a fixed mixture pays each energy owner at least theta M_j
with total at most [1+(1-theta)/(2e)+theta/2]F. The 3/2 factor is an upper bound,
not claimed optimal. Multiple fixed cost tiers or service-value tiers per actual
owner are permitted PROVIDED owners remain on only one side; see section5.

1. UNIT-INPUT PRIMITIVE (ESTABLISHED INPUT, REPROVED HERE)
For energy quantities e_j and hardware quantities h_i put E=Σe_j,H=Σh_i,
T=min(E,H). At T=0 pay everyone0. Otherwise call a side of total T the short
side (either choice at equality). For each long-side owner r set d_r=min(x_r,T),
ρ=Σ_r d_r/T>=1 and q=(1/2)exp(1-ρ). Pay each short-side owner (1-q)x_r and each
long-side owner q d_r. At equality ρ=1,q=1/2 and every owner gets x_r/2.
Total is T[1+q(ρ-1)] <= [1+1/(2e)]T.

Coverage: for any coalition, let s be its short quantity and l its long quantity.
Its worth is min(s,l); s<=T and its capped-long total is >=min(l,T), so both
s and that capped total are >=min(s,l). The payment is their convex combination.

Own monotonicity: while an owner remains long, T is fixed and its own capped
quantity d has payment d exp(1-(d+D)/T)/2, whose derivative is q(1-d/T)>=0.
While an owner remains short, its report x increases T at unit rate, and
ρ(T)=Σmin(l_r,T)/T. Away from finitely many cap thresholds,
 q'(T)=q Σ_{l_r<T}l_r/T² <=qρ/T.
Hence d[(1-q(T))x]/dx >=1-q-qρ >=0, because x<=T and
(1+ρ)exp(1-ρ)/2<=1 for ρ>=1. At cap thresholds the rule is continuous; when the
owner crosses the total-side equality, both branches give x/2. Thus the payment
is nondecreasing over the entire real report domain. Zero boundaries also agree.
This proves the needed unit primitive rather than relying on numeric checks.

2. RAISE EACH ENERGY PAYMENT TO ITS GRAND MARGINAL
For the unit game define m_j=T-min(H,E-e_j), an own-e_j-monotone nonnegative
quantity since the subtracted term is independent of e_j. Replace every energy
payment by max{its primitive payment,m_j}; keep hardware payments unchanged.
Taking the maximum of two own-monotone functions preserves incentives. Raising
payments preserves coalition coverage. We prove total<=3T/2.

Case E<=H. Here T=E and m_j=e_j for every energy owner. The patched energy
payments sum exactly T, and hardware payments sum qρT. Since
ρ exp(1-ρ)<=1 for ρ>=1, the total is at most T+T/2.

Case H<=E. Normalize H=T=1. Let d_j=min(e_j,1), ρ=Σd_j>=1,
q=(1/2)exp(1-ρ). Hardware payments total1-q, while m_j=(1-E+e_j)_+.
If all m_j<=q d_j, no patch is active and total<=1+1/(2e)<3/2.

If some energy owner b has e_b>=1, all other owners' marginals are zero. If
there are two such owners every marginal is zero. Otherwise put t=Σ_{j≠b}e_j.
Only t<1 needs consideration, and then ρ=1+t, q=(1/2)exp(-t), m_b=1-t.
Total is max{1+qt, 1+(1-t)(1-q)}. The first is <=1+1/(2e). For the second,
f(t)=(1-t)(1-(1/2)exp(-t)) has derivative
 -1+(1/2)exp(-t)(2-t)<=0 on[0,1], so f(t)<=f(0)=1/2.

It remains that all e_j<=1. Write E=1+δ, δ>=0; then ρ=1+δ,q=(1/2)exp(-δ).
Let B={j:(1-q)e_j>δ}, k=|B| and e_B=Σ_B e_j. These are exactly the owners
whose floor exceeds their primitive payment. Total is
 1+qδ+(1-q)e_B-kδ.                                            (2)
If k=0 the ordinary primitive bound applies. If k=1, e_B<=1, so the excess
above1 is at most (1-q)(1-δ). An active owner implies δ<1; the same f inequality
bounds this by1/2. If k>=2, e_B<=1+δ, so excess is at most
 1-q-(k-1)δ <=1/2,
because this expression equals1/2 at δ=0 and has derivative
q-(k-1)<=1/2-1<0. This proves the bound in every case, including equality.

3. EXACT SOURCE-PRICE LAYER DECOMPOSITION
For every coalition S and every real t>=0, retain energy rows whose public
cost c_j<=t and hardware rows whose public service value v_i>=t. Set
 E_t(S)=Σ_{c_j<=t}e_j(S), H_t(S)=Σ_{v_i>=t}h_i(S).
Then
 F(S)=∫_0^∞ min(E_t(S),H_t(S))dt.                             (3)
This is a classical one-dimensional supply/demand surplus identity. For a direct
proof, order energy units by increasing cost c(x) and hardware units by decreasing
value v(x), using fractional quantiles. For a fixed total quantity x, minimum
cost is obtained from the first x energy units and maximum service value from
the first x hardware units. The optimum selects precisely the initial range
where v(x)>=c(x), up to irrelevant zero-surplus units. Its value is
∫(v(x)-c(x))_+ dx. By integrating the indicator of c(x)<=t<=v(x), the inner
x-length at price t is exactly min(E_t,H_t); Fubini's theorem for a nonnegative
bounded step function gives(3). This is one jointly feasible allocation, not a
sum of physically inconsistent threshold optima. The argument applies separately
to every coalition and to all nonnegative real capacities.

4. COMPONENTWISE PAYMENTS, FLOORS AND BUDGET
Run section2's patched unit rule at every threshold, on the SAME actual owner
set. Integrate payments over t. All retained sets depend only on public c,v;
components have nonnegative fixed interval weights. Equation(3) gives actual
coalition coverage and total<=3F/2. Each owner's report maps to an allowed own
report in every retained component, so monotonicity survives integration. For
energy owner j, integrate its component floor:
∫[T_t(N)-T_t(N\{j})]dt=F(N)-F(N\{j})=M_j.
Thus every actual energy owner's required floor is respected. All payments vanish
at F=0, because all nonnegative component production values then vanish. An owner
with zero own quantities gets zero. Fixed convex mixing with the unpatched
primitive yields the stated theta tradeoff. No mixture coefficients depend on
reports. There are at most |c|+|v| distinct breakpoints in the simple model.

5. ACTUAL OWNER BUNDLES AND ECONOMIC INTERPRETATION
An energy owner may hold multiple public cost tiers e_jr,c_jr, and a hardware
owner multiple service tiers h_is,v_is. At each threshold sum retained quantities
WITHIN each actual owner before applying the primitive. Its own whole-bundle
underreport decreases that one pooled component contribution. Removing an owner
removes all its retained tiers, so the floor identity remains exact. This extends
to arbitrary fixed same-side bundles and overlapping cost tiers, with both owner
and resource counts unbounded. It does not allow one actual owner to hold both
energy and hardware roles: that needs a new incentive proof.

Prices c are verified public net operating/opportunity costs settled once by the
operator. A paid marginal floor is a negotiated share of incremental surplus,
not payment for hidden opportunity cost or post-contract resale. Nonlinear
public supply costs can be represented by publicly fixed marginal-cost tiers
when the contract makes their cumulative capacities enforceable; no private
cost elicitation or general battery intertemporal dispatch is implied. Every
energy source must actually be deliverable to every admitted hardware tier.
Route-specific transmission losses, pod-dependent tariffs and source-specific
hardware productivity can destroy the separable v_i-c_j structure; the separate
unit-graph obstruction shows that an arbitrary route matrix cannot simply be
reduced by fixed positive subgraph weights.
