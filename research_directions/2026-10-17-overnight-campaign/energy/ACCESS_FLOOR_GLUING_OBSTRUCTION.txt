EXACT OBSTRUCTION TO BOTTLENECK-COMPONENT GLUING OF THE 4/3 FLOOR RULE
17 October 2026. Method obstruction, not a lower bound against all mechanisms.
The arbitrary-access all-energy-floor problem remains open in this campaign.

TARGET AND CANDIDATE
We seek the sharp4/3 complete-access energy-floor guarantee on an arbitrary
fixed bipartite access graph, with disjoint fixed pure-role owners, unit net
activity profits, and binding own-capacity underreports. Public source-price
weights would then follow from ACCESS_PRICE_COAREA.txt. Finite all-profile LPs
on tested small graphs have optimum4/3, but do not prove the general theorem.

A natural candidate takes the unit-Huber optimal vertex prices, partitions the
graph into their tight-edge components, and applies the complete-pool4/3 rule
inside each component, grouping the component's energy and hardware holdings.
That candidate fails own-report monotonicity, even on a path and even when every
owner has just one resource. The failure is away from a tie in the positive
edge-multiplier decomposition; choosing a different convention at exact ties
does not repair it.

FIXED FOUR-OWNER EXAMPLE
Energy owners A,B have one unit each. Hardware owners X,Y have capacities
x and3. Access edges are A--X, B--X, B--Y, all of unit profit. Compare the
same owner's reports x=4 and x=2. Every profile has production2; both energy
owners have grand marginal1. The technology and ownership never change.

At x=2, the unique Huber optimal prices are
 p_A=3/4, p_X=1/4, p_B=5/6, p_Y=1/6.
Edges A--X and B--Y are tight, whereas p_B+p_X=13/12>1.
KKT edge multipliers on(A--X,B--X,B--Y) are(1,0,1).
Indeed each energy stationarity equation is capacity*1=1; hardware X has
capacity2 times derivative2p_X=1/2, and hardware Y has capacity3 times
derivative2p_Y=1/3. Hence the two tight components are single edges.
The complete-pool floor rule pays each energy owner1 and hardware owner X
one third of its component's short-side quantity1, namely
        y_X(x=2)=1/3.

At x=4, the unique Huber optimal prices are
 p_A=p_B=6/7, p_X=p_Y=1/7.
All three edges are tight. KKT multipliers are(1,1/7,6/7), strictly positive
on every edge. Hardware stationarity is8/7 and6/7, exactly capacity times
2/7; both energy stationarity equations are1. Thus the graph is one tight
component, regardless of whether zero multipliers are excluded from components.
Its energy total is2. Both hardware capacities exceed2, so the capped hardware
quantities are2 and2 and the cap ratio is rho=2. The sharp floor primitive pays
        y_X(x=4)=(exp(1-2)/3)*2=2/(3e).
Since e>2, this is strictly less than1/3. A hardware owner with actual capacity4
therefore gains by bindingly underreporting capacity2. No limiting sequence,
rounding issue, fictitious owner, or changing eligibility is involved.

Coverage and the energy floors are not the problem at these two profiles:
energy owners receive their full unit quantities, which already cover the
energy use of every coalition. Both displayed profiles also respect the4/3
funding limit. It is the missing cross-profile incentive control that fails.

STRUCTURAL LESSON AND STOPPING DECISION
The Huber prices themselves are continuous and have the proved own-report
monotonicity property. Replacing their payments by a nonlinear owner-cap rule
inside tight components discards that invariant. Components can merge while
the capped-owner normalization changes abruptly, so the original derivative
proof does not transfer. Report-dependent bottleneck decomposition is not an
automatic bridge from complete-access floors to arbitrary-access floors.

This exact obstruction closes this particular gluing approach. It does not
show that arbitrary-access4/3 is impossible, that another canonical allocation
cannot work, or that a larger universal constant is necessary. A useful next
attack needs a global invariant controlling both floor raises and own-report
effects when access bottlenecks move; another grid of selected profiles would
not supply it. The floor-free source-price access theorem and the public
route-distortion corollary do not rely on this failed construction.
