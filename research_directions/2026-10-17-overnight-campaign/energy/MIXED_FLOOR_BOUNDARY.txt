MIXED OWNERSHIP CLOSES AT FACTOR TWO UNDER FULL OWNER MARGINAL FLOORS
17 October 2026. Elementary model-boundary result, not a claim of historical
novelty. Candidate proof; independent audit outstanding.

Keep source-price production and binding capacity reports, but permit an actual
owner to hold both energy and hardware. If the contract promises its FULL OWNER
grand marginal to every owner holding any energy, the sharp uniform funding
factor is2. The upper below even pays the full marginal to EVERY actual owner.
The definition must be fixed by the contract, not changed after each report.

UNIT UPPER. Let E=sum e_i,H=sum h_i,T=min(E,H), with actual owners fixed.
If E<=H, set y_i=e_i+(E-H+h_i)_+.
If H<=E, set y_i=h_i+(H-E+e_i)_+.
At equality the branches agree y_i=e_i+h_i. At T0 all payments vanish.

Coverage: in the first regime a coalition receives at least its energy total;
in the second it receives at least its hardware total. This covers min(E_S,H_S).
Grand marginal M_i=T-min(E-e_i,H-h_i) is, in the first regime,
max{e_i,E-H+h_i}; the sum defining y_i dominates it. The second regime is
symmetric. Thus this rule pays all actual owner grand marginals.

Own-bundle incentives: in E<=H, e_i is increasing in own energy, and
(E-H+h_i)_+=(E-H_without_i)_+ is increasing in own energy and independent of
own hardware. In H<=E the same argument swaps roles. Increasing one own
coordinate is monotone within each regime and the formulas join continuously
when E=H. Any simultaneous own-bundle increase is a sequence of such coordinate
increases. Fixed actual identity is preserved; no virtual splitting is used.

Budget: if E<=H, put D=H-E>=0. The extra sum is sum_i(h_i-D)_+<=E:
if its positive set has k>=1, the sum is at most H-kD<=H-D=E; if k0 it is0.
Thus sum y_i<=2E=2T. The symmetric regime gives2H. Public source-price
integration applies without the disjoint-owner assumption: at every threshold
aggregate both sides within each actual owner and use this unit rule. Removing
an owner removes both resources and integrated marginals telescope exactly.
Consequently the same2F upper holds for arbitrary fixed multi-tier mixed owners.

LOWER AND SCOPE. Take public energy cost0 and hardware value1. Actual owner A
holds energy1 and no hardware; actual owner B holds energy epsilon and hardware1,
where0<epsilon<1. Both are energy owners, hence both qualify for their FULL OWNER
marginal floor. Grand F1, F_without_A=epsilon, F_without_B=0, so required
payments total at least(1-epsilon)+1=2-epsilon. Letting epsilon tend0 gives
uniform factor at least2. This is already a static floor obstruction: no
incentive argument is needed. It does not invalidate the disjoint-role4/3 result.

A family with all owners owning positive quantities and both resource/owner
counts diverging can be obtained by additional tiny active pure energy/hardware
owners, retaining the limiting2 lower by continuity of the finitely described
profile values. That embellishment is unnecessary to identify this general
class boundary; the full theorem upper already allows arbitrary owner counts.

INTERPRETATION. A seemingly minor vertical integration change (a hardware owner
also owns onsite energy) can make an owner-level floor protect its entire
hardware complement as well. Thus calling the floor 'energy participation'
would be misleading under mixed ownership. A genuinely new mixed-ownership
research direction would need a RESOURCE-SPECIFIC marginal floor, subtracting
only the owner's energy while retaining its hardware, or another contract; it
must be defined before attempting a better bound. That is a different theorem.
