WHAT JUSTIFIES A PARTICIPATION FLOOR IN AN AI/POWER COOPERATION CONTRACT?
17 October 2026. Economic/model-boundary gate. Exact structural arguments;
free original-source comparison. No empirical validation or novelty claim.

CONCLUSION
Standard participation does NOT imply a positive fraction of full marginal
contribution in the current model. The hard marginal-floor requirement is an
additional bargaining entitlement. Concrete power reservations and public
outside-use opportunities can support nonzero ordinary participation values,
but those must enter ownership or the baseline welfare ledger. They should
not be appended as unfunded surplus floors while keeping the old denominator.

1. THREE DISTINCT PAYOFF BENCHMARKS
For fixed actual owner i at profile b, write

  d_i=F(b_i),                    stand-alone production,
  M_i=F(C)-F(C-b_i),             full-owner marginal contribution,
  r_i,                          separately contracted outside value.

Under nonnegative superadditive production, M_i>=d_i. Coalition coverage
already implies y_i>=d_i. Full OUT is stronger: y_i(b)-y_i(b_i-d,b_-i)
must cover production from each withdrawn bundle d, and analogously for
coalitions. Neither implication requires y_i>=theta M_i for any fixed
theta>0 uniformly over technologies, profiles and ownership institutions.

Exact witness. In the two-pool technology min(P,H), owner i has (epsilon,1)
and another owner has (1,0), where0<epsilon<1. Then F(C)=1, d_i=epsilon,
and M_i=1. The efficient allocation (epsilon,1-epsilon) covers every coalition
at THIS profile and satisfies ordinary participation. For any fixed theta>0,
take epsilon<theta: that allocation violates y_i>=theta M_i. This is a static
logical counterexample to deriving the floor from participation/coverage;
it is not a claim that this efficient allocation extends to an all-profile
full-OUT mechanism.

A stronger ALL-PROFILE witness also permits positive independent hardware
earnings. Give three pure fixed owners the resources P,a,b, and set

  W(P,a,b)=epsilon(a+b)+min(P,a,b), epsilon>0.

The epsilon terms are public outside rents on unused private inputs. Pay
z_P=min(P,a,b), z_A=epsilon a, and z_B=epsilon b. These payments are efficient,
cover every coalition, and have full OUT: the min component is the efficient
power-veto rule, and the linear component transfers exactly on withdrawal.
At (P,a,b)=(1,1,1), hardware owner A has positive stand-alone value epsilon,
receives epsilon, and has marginal contribution1+epsilon. Its payout/marginal
ratio epsilon/(1+epsilon) tends to zero. Thus even genuine positive ordinary
IR, exact efficiency, and full OUT together imply no uniform positive
grand-marginal fraction. The checker verifies finite inequalities for this
formula; the sum-of-linear-plus-superadditive-veto proof covers all real reports.

In the common-power hard family, a pure hardware owner cannot produce without
the other complement and power, so d_i=0, while at C_1 its M_i=1. Promising
theta M_i therefore creates positive rents even though the model's literal
stand-alone alternative is zero. This is precisely what seeds the growing
ladder in CONCENTRATED_POWER_HARDWARE_FLOOR_LOWER.txt.

2. A CREDIBLE NONZERO PARTICIPATION MODEL: OWNED SERVICE AND POWER RIGHTS
Suppose a provider controls a bundled qualified hardware envelope h_i AND an
enforceable deliverable power entitlement e_i for the same short window.
Its no-pooling service output is min(e_i,h_i). A coalition can use only rights
its members own and are contractually permitted to combine. Thus the baseline
is an actual resource outside option, not a marginal-value promise.

Under complete access, public equal net service value and divisible envelopes,
the production is min(sum e_i,sum h_i). The accepted two-pool full-OUT rule
covers all standalone min(e_i,h_i), all coalitions, and all partial withholding
with factor1+c independent of the number of providers. These ordinary positive
participation values do not create the split-complement rank-three lower.
Bundled local complements can first be compressed by AI_OWNERSHIP_BOUNDARY.

This is credible as a MODEL TO VALIDATE, not an assertion that existing tenant
contracts permit resale. Equinix's official Power documentation describes a
deployment-level contractual Draw Cap; its colocation description separates
customer-owned IT equipment from facility infrastructure. The actual contract
must say whether a tenant holds a usable entitlement, whether it can share it,
and what happens to it if the tenant declines the pooling arrangement.
https://docs.equinix.com/colocation/about/colo-power/
https://www.equinix.com/what-is-colocation

Counting the facility operator as the only power owner while also treating
tenants as entitled to independent service from its power double counts control.
If tenants have enforceable reservation rights, represent those rights; if they
do not, their power-dependent outside production is not freely available.

3. PUBLIC OUTSIDE RENT BELONGS IN A BASELINE/OPPORTUNITY-COST LEDGER
Let resource capacity c earn a fixed public linear outside rent ell*c when
unused, ell>=0. A feasible internal activity plan x uses Ax<=c and earns
public gross revenue pi*x. The total attainable value is exactly

  W(c)=max_(x>=0,Ax<=c) [pi*x + ell*(c-Ax)]
      =ell*c + F_net(c),
  F_net(c)=max_(x>=0,Ax<=c) (pi-ell*A)*x.                  (2)

Discard negative-net-profit activities, which cannot be optimal. This is a
standard opportunity-cost identity, not a new economic principle. Suppose y
is a kappa-funded full-OUT mechanism for F_net. Give owner i total utility

  z_i=ell*b_i+y_i.                                      (3)

Then z covers every coalition for W and has full OUT for W, because its payoff
loss on withholding d is ell*d plus the full-OUT loss for F_net. Its funding is

  sum z <=ell*C+kappa F_net(C)<=kappa W(C),
  sum z-W(C)<= (kappa-1)F_net(C).                         (4)

One implementation lets unused resources keep their outside rent, reimburses
used resources for the same public opportunity cost, and pays y as surplus.
The owner's fixed true-endowment baseline cancels from report incentives, as
in ECONOMIC_SETTLEMENT.txt. Do not count retained rent, reimbursement, and a
second floor for the same cost as three separate benefits. Physical operating
cost reimbursement is an alternative analogous ledger, not an extra baseline.
Private prices, nonlinear resale demand, and state-dependent costs need a
different model; (2) assumes fixed public linear values.

Why the denominator matters. If a hardware-only owner has a positive outside
rent but every complement reports zero, the OLD production F is zero. Requiring
a positive gross payment while insisting sum payments<=kappa F is impossible
for every finite kappa. This elementary zero-production obstruction is removed
only by accounting for outside value or fixed retainers separately, or by
restricting the feasible participation domain. It is not evidence of a deep
cooperation impossibility. In the active-branch ladder, positive rent on the
many inactive b_j inputs would also increase W(C_m); one may not keep the old
normalization F(C_m)=1 while charging those rents as additional subsidy.

An elementary fallback example is a bundled hardware owner who can always
buy outside electricity at a fixed public cost lambda in[0,1] per service
unit, with gross service revenue1. Cheap committed pool power has zero cost.
With aggregate hardware H and cheap power P,

  W(P,H)=(1-lambda)H+lambda min(P,H).

Pay z_i=(1-lambda)h_i+lambda y_i^pair. The two-pool full-OUT rule gives exact
ordinary outside protection and total at most
(1-lambda)H+(1+c)lambda min(P,H). The added stabilization funding is bounded
by c times the INCREMENTAL savings lambda min(P,H), not by a second charge
for the fallback electricity. This example assumes the fallback is genuinely
available and publicly priced; no current tariff is claimed to satisfy it.

4. WHAT CAN JUSTIFY theta M_i: AN EXPLICIT BARGAINING CLAUSE
Consider a separate two-party negotiation between i and the aggregate of all
other owners. Their disagreement values are d_i=F(b_i) and d_-i=F(C-b_i).
Their additional cooperation surplus is

  Delta=F(C)-d_i-d_-i=M_i-d_i>=0.

The weighted Nash-product maximization of
(u_i-d_i)^theta (u_-i-d_-i)^(1-theta), with total utility F(C), gives

  u_i=d_i+theta Delta=(1-theta)d_i+theta M_i.              (5)

For0<theta<1 and Delta>0, maximize theta log x+(1-theta)log(Delta-x);
the derivative vanishes exactly at x=theta Delta and the objective is strictly
concave. Endpoints and zero surplus follow by limits. When d_i=0, (5) equals
theta M_i. Thus the floor has an intelligible bargaining interpretation if
the parties explicitly negotiate this benchmark.

But conducting that counterfactual negotiation separately for EVERY owner
does not produce one feasible multilateral agreement. The other-side coalitions
overlap. Even a single three-input serial activity with three pure owners has
M_i=1 and d_i=0 for all three. Applying the symmetric bilateral benchmark
theta=1/2 to each would demand total1.5 from total value1. This is an additional
fairness/negotiating-rights policy, not ordinary individual rationality, and
its simultaneous cost is exactly the subject of the floor theorems.

Nash's original 1950 article supplies the classical disagreement-normalized
bargaining-product ingredient; (5) is the displayed elementary weighted
specialization, not a novelty claim about bargaining theory:
https://jmvidal.cse.sc.edu/library/nash50a.pdf
The original's no-cooperation normalization and bargaining construction were
checked in the freely indexed original text; browser PDF image extraction did
not expose a viewable page in this run. No verbatim passage is reproduced.

5. CLOSE PRIMARY COMPARATOR: NETWORK RESERVE-EXCHANGE MECHANISMS
Karaca, Delikaraoglou and Kamgarpour, Operations Research Letters49(2021),
501-506, A market-based approach for enabling inter-area reserve exchange:
https://www.research-collection.ethz.ch/server/api/core/bitstreams/17b8f02d-9785-455d-9d50-70453af55d71/content
Original inspected: Section2.1, Definitions1-5, Proposition1, Lemma1,
and Appendix A Proposition2. IR is nonnegative gain relative to a default
network allocation. Clarke-pivot utility is V(B)-V(B_-a); efficient core utility
is bounded ABOVE by that marginal, with an epsilon relaxation for the least
core. A star's center can receive the whole value. Private valuation-function
bids and approximate incentive/core guarantees differ from our binding capacity
underreports and full OUT. This is direct prior art for the economic distinction
and static veto allocation, not proof that our cross-profile constants are new.

6. DEMAND RESPONSE IS A SEPARATE OUTSIDE CONTRACT, NOT A FREE MARGINAL FLOOR
PJM's official June2026 fact sheet describes qualifying customer payments for
load reduction, commitments and possible nonperformance penalties; allocation
between an aggregator and customer is privately agreed. Ordinary operating
reductions are not automatically eligible. Hence a real curtailment payment
may be an opportunity cost, but only after checking the actual enrollment,
baseline, dispatch obligation and settlement. It is not a generic rent on each
unused kW and does not imply theta times an AI pool's grand marginal.
https://www.pjm.com/-/media/DotCom/about-pjm/newsroom/fact-sheets/demand-response-fact-sheet.pdf

RECOMMENDATION / GATE OUTCOME
Use an auditable owned-entitlement or public fallback benchmark for the first
AI/power application. Preserve ordinary IR through coalition coverage and full
OUT, and report incremental surplus separately from baseline compensation.
If stakeholders want a share of incremental bargaining value, label the
theta-marginal requirement as a negotiated fairness clause and show its cost.
There is no verified reason to present that clause as required by standard
participation. Do not add private costs or real-cluster claims to rescue it.
