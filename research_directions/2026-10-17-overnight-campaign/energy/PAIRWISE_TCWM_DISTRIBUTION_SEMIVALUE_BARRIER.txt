POSITIVE MIXTURES OF BERNOULLI SEMIVALUES CANNOT REPAIR PAIR-TCWM COVERAGE
17 October 2026. Structural stopping lemma; independent review pending.

SCOPE. At a pure residual min2 profile z, let B_i^p(z) be the expected marginal
 v(R union {i})-v(R), where each other actual owner belongs to R independently
with probability p. This is the established probabilistic Banzhaf/binomial
semivalue ingredient. Consider u_i(z)=integral B_i^p(z) dmu(p), where mu is a
fixed probability measure on [0,1], independent of reports. Then use the
standalone-plus-residual wrapper y_i=f_i+u_i(a-b). Owners remain fixed.

THEOREM. For any fixed n>=4, no such mechanism has all-coalition coverage,
pairwise TCWM and any finite all-profile gain coefficient. The probability
measure may depend on the public fixed n. The obstruction is structural:
two-active efficiency and a single three-active report comparison force mu
to be the point mass at p=1/2. The resulting raw Banzhaf rule fails coverage.

1. TWO ACTIVE OWNERS PIN THE FIRST MOMENT
Use one unit positive owner and one unit negative owner, with other owners
reporting zero. Each B_i^p=p for an active owner; inactive owners get zero.
The exact two-active efficiency lemma in
PAIRWISE_TCWM_DISTRIBUTION_LIPSCHITZ_REDUCTION.txt gives total payment1.
Consequently integral p dmu(p)=1/2.                         (1)
The efficiency lemma needs only coverage, pair-TCWM, a finite gain budget and
the full mixed report domain, even though this displayed profile is pure.

2. THREE ACTIVE OWNERS PIN THE SECOND MOMENT
Use positive owners A1=1 and A2=epsilon, and negative owner B=1, with
0<=epsilon<=1. The protected pair is {A1,A2}; only A2 changes its report.
Directly conditioning on inclusion of the two other active owners gives
 B_A1^p=p-p^2 epsilon,
 B_A2^p=p(1-p)epsilon.
Hence their sum is p+epsilon(p-2p^2). Pairwise TCWM from epsilon0 to any
positive epsilon forces integral(p-2p^2)dmu>=0. By (1),
 integral p^2 dmu<=1/4.
Jensen/Cauchy gives the reverse inequality. Equality therefore holds and
Var_mu(p)=0. Thus mu is exactly the point mass at1/2.
No differentiability assumption on the mechanism is used: the displayed
comparison is affine in epsilon and is an exact finite report comparison.

3. THE FORCED RULE DOES NOT COVER FOUR-OWNER COALITIONS
At signed profile z=(2,-1,-1,1), raw Banzhaf pays owners0,1,2 a total15/8.
They can produce2, so coverage fails by1/8. The exact eight-subset marginal
enumeration per owner is reproduced by the supplied checker. Adding inactive
owners does not change a Bernoulli expected marginal, so this proves the
claim for every fixed n>=4.

4. OPTIONAL UNNORMALIZED UNBOUNDED-OWNER EXTENSION
Suppose mu is any finite nonnegative measure, fixed across arbitrarily many
owner counts. Its atom at p0 is irrelevant on pure min2 games and can be
discarded. The singleton upper-Lipschitz condition from the residual reduction
forces mu((0,1])<=1: use one positive owner of capacity x in[0,1] and m negative
owners of unit capacity. Its payment is
 x integral[1-(1-p)^m]dmu(p),
so the bracket integral is at most1 for every m. Monotone convergence gives
the asserted mass bound M<=1. The same two/three-owner arguments give
 integral p=1/2, integral p^2<=1/4.
Cauchy then yields1/4<=(integral p^2)M<=M/4<=1/4. All equalities force M=1
and mu=delta_(1/2), up to the irrelevant atom at zero. Coverage again fails.
This extension does NOT exclude owner-count-dependent unnormalized measures.

CONSEQUENCE AND PRIORITY
Positive averaging of sampling probabilities cannot repair Banzhaf's
coalition deficit while preserving the requested pairwise incentives. This
includes the usual uniform-p averaging associated with the Shapley value,
which already violates the three-owner pair comparison above.
The semivalue construction, the Banzhaf special case, and its familiar
pair-merger cancellation are established ingredients. No historical novelty
claim is made for those ingredients or for this short moment argument pending
source comparison. A successful construction must use report dependence,
signed combinations with separate verification, or a genuinely different
nonlinear rule. This is not an unrestricted impossibility theorem.
