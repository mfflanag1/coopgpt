GAIN-ONLY PAIRWISE OUT: A SIGNED-IMBALANCE REDUCTION WITHOUT REGULARITY ASSUMPTIONS
17 October 2026. Proposed structural theorem. Root checked the reduction idea;
complete finite-averaging proof supplied here for independent audit. Standard
compact-convex averaging is an established ingredient, not a novelty claim.

DOMAIN
There are n fixed actual owners, each eligible for BOTH resource types, with
the full nonnegative unbounded report domain. Production is F(A,B)=min(A,B).
Payments are nonnegative, cover ALL coalitions, and protect full outside
production for coalitions of size at most s, where2<=s<=n. Suppose a finite
K satisfies at all profiles

  Y<=S+K G, S=sum_i min(a_i,b_i), G=min(sum a_i,sum b_i)-S.

The proof uses arbitrarily large diagonal translations of reports. It does
not apply automatically to a fixed bounded cube or a restricted eligibility
institution. Every translated comparison keeps the SAME actual owners.

THEOREM
If any mechanism satisfies this contract, then one does so with the same K
and the additional property

  y_i((a_j+t_j,b_j+t_j)_j)=y_i((a_j,b_j)_j)+t_i          (1)

for every vector t>=0. No measurability, continuity, differentiability,
anonymity, or homogeneity of the starting mechanism is assumed.

Such a mechanism is equivalently described by functions h_i:R^n->R of the
signed imbalances z_i=a_i-b_i, with

  y_i=b_i+h_i(z),                                       (2)

satisfying all of the following FINITE inequalities:

  H_Q(z):=sum_{i in Q}h_i(z) >= min(sum_{i in Q}z_i,0)    for every Q;       (3)

  sum_i h_i(z) <= sum_i min(z_i,0)+K G(z),               (4)
  G(z)=min(sum_i z_i,0)-sum_i min(z_i,0);

and whenever z,z' differ only inside Q with |Q|<=s,

 min(Delta,0)<=H_Q(z')-H_Q(z)<=max(Delta,0),
 Delta=sum_{i in Q}(z_i'-z_i).                          (5)

In particular, for each pair{i,j}, h_i+h_j depends on its own two coordinates
ONLY through z_i+z_j, with all outsiders held fixed. As a function of that
pair sum it is nondecreasing and1-Lipschitz. Each h_i itself is nondecreasing
and1-Lipschitz in z_i, and is1-Lipschitz in EVERY other coordinate as well.

1. COMPACTNESS OF THE FEASIBLE MECHANISM SET
For a fixed profile b, singleton coverage gives y_i(b)>=f_i(b)=min(a_i,b_i).
The gain budget and other singleton floors then imply

  0<=y_i(b)-f_i(b)<=K G(b).

Thus all feasible mechanisms lie in a product of compact real intervals,
indexed by every real report profile and every owner. The coverage, incentive,
and budget requirements are closed inequalities involving finitely many
coordinates of that product. Their feasible set X is therefore compact and
convex, and is nonempty by hypothesis. This use of product compactness is
standard; it does not assume a measurable payment function.

For t>=0 define the affine operator

  (T_t y)_i(b)=y_i(b+t)-t_i,

where b+t adds t_j to BOTH resource reports of owner j. Grand production and
standalone sum both increase by sum_j t_j, so G is unchanged. Every coalition's
production increases by the sum of its members' t_j. It follows directly that
T_t maps X into X: coverage, the gain budget, and every outside-production
inequality survive, because each compared profile is translated identically.
The operators commute and are continuous for the product topology.

2. FINITE AVERAGING GIVES A COMMON FIXED POINT
Take any finite collection t^1,...,t^m and any y in X. For an integer L>=1,
average the L^m mechanisms

  T_(k_1 t^1+...+k_m t^m)y,   0<=k_j<L.

Call the average y^L. It belongs to X by convexity. Applying one T_t^j shifts
one summation index, so all interior terms cancel in the difference. At a
fixed profile and owner, every transformed excess above f_i lies in the SAME
interval[0,K G(b)]. Hence

  |(T_t^j y^L)_i(b)-y_i^L(b)|<=K G(b)/L.

A cluster point in compact X is fixed by all m translations. Consequently the
closed sets Fix(T_t) have the finite-intersection property; compactness gives
a point fixed by EVERY t>=0 simultaneously. This proves(1). Only finite sums
and compactness were used, so nonmeasurable starting rules cause no gap.

3. REDUCTION TO SIGNED IMBALANCES
For z in R^n use the canonical pure residual profile
(a_i,b_i)=((z_i)_+,(-z_i)_+), and write g_i(z) for its payment. Any original
owner bundle equals this residual plus f_i(1,1), so(1) implies

  y_i=f_i+g_i(z)=b_i+h_i(z),
  h_i(z)=min(z_i,0)+g_i(z).

For every coalition Q,
F(b(Q))=sum_{i in Q} b_i+min(sum_{i in Q}z_i,0).
Thus coverage is exactly(3), and the gain budget is exactly(4). Singleton
coverage ensures y_i>=f_i>=0, even though h_i may be negative.

4. OUTSIDE PRODUCTION IS EXACTLY THE FINITE SUM-SLOPE CONDITION
Fix arbitrary z,z' differing only in Q, and write Delta_i=z_i'-z_i. Starting
from canonical reports at z, restore to owner i the nonnegative bundle
(Delta_i^+,(-Delta_i)^+). The new signed imbalance is z_i'. Translation
invariance lets its payment be read using the SAME h_i(z') even if these
high reports are not canonical. Set P=sum_Q Delta_i^+ and N=sum_Q(-Delta_i)^+.
The outside-production guarantee says

  N+H_Q(z')-H_Q(z)>=min(P,N),

or H_Q(z')-H_Q(z)>=min(P-N,0)=min(Delta,0).
Repeat the argument from z' to z using the opposite nonnegative restoration.
This gives the upper inequality in(5).

Conversely, for any actual joint restoration d_i=(d_ai,d_bi), signed changes
are Delta_i=d_ai-d_bi. Formula(5) implies that the payment increment is at least

  sum_Q d_bi+min(sum_Q(d_ai-d_bi),0)
  =min(sum_Q d_ai,sum_Q d_bi)=F(sum_Q d_i).

Therefore(3)--(5) define a genuine all-profile mechanism satisfying the
original contract, not just necessary differential conditions.

5. CONSEQUENCES THAT ADVANCE THE PAIRWISE PROBLEM
For a pair, changes with Delta=0 leave its total h unchanged. This is exact
pairwise transfer neutrality in SIGNED IMBALANCES, derived without treating
owners as strategically mergeable identities. A single-owner change gives
0<=Delta h_i<=Delta z_i when Delta z_i>=0. For a different owner j, pairwise
condition(5) gives0<=Delta(h_i+h_j)<=Delta z_j, while the singleton condition
gives0<=Delta h_j<=Delta z_j. Subtracting yields
|Delta h_i|<=|Delta z_j|. Thus every h_i is globally Lipschitz in every
coordinate, even though the starting payment mechanism could be arbitrary.

At G=0, all nonzero imbalances have the same sign and the budget plus singleton
coverage forces h_i=min(z_i,0). If s=n, condition(5) makes sum_i h_i depend only
on total imbalance. Transfer that total to a same-sign vector with G=0 to get
sum_i h_i=min(sum_i z_i,0) everywhere. The resulting rule is globally efficient.
The existing three-owner min2 TCWM lower rules this out when n>=3. This recovers
the no-finite-gain-coefficient obstruction for full coalition protection.

For s=2 and n>=3, only PAIR sums are constrained this way. There can be
nontrivial correction functions; the reduction does not prove uniqueness
of Banzhaf. For example partition-dependent equal-and-opposite contributions
can preserve pair-sum neutrality, although coverage and the slope bounds may
still rule them out. The next mathematical task is to construct h satisfying
(3)--(5) with uniform K, or derive an owner-growing contradiction from these
finite constraints. This is a substantially smaller structural problem than
searching arbitrary mechanisms on the original two-resource report domain.

ROUTINE OPTIONAL HOMOGENEITY/SYMMETRY COROLLARY
Inside the closed compact convex set of translation-equivariant feasible rules,
S_lambda y(b)=y(lambda b)/lambda, lambda>0, is a commuting family of continuous
affine self-maps. Translation equivariance is preserved because
S_lambda y(b+t)=S_lambda y(b)+t. The same finite rectangular averaging and
compactness argument gives a common fixed point, hence a degree-one homogeneous
rule. Finite averaging over owner permutations and exchange of the two resource
types then preserves feasibility, homogeneity and translation equivariance.
None of the subsequent lower proofs requires homogeneity; anonymous owner and
resource symmetry suffice. This is a routine reduction, not a new lower bound.
