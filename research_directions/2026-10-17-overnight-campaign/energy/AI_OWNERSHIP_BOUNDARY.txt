OWNERSHIP, NOT MACHINE COUNT, DETERMINES THE COMMON-POWER OBSTRUCTION
17 October 2026. Conventional proof and institutional scope note. Root and
Fable030237 found no mathematical defect; explicit all-coalition and floor-free
scope clarifications applied. These are structural corollaries of accepted mechanisms, not
claims of historical novelty or measured data-center pooling gains.

1. MODEL AND WHAT THE NEW LOWER BOUND DOES REQUIRE

The divisible public-value technology is

  F(P,a,b)=min(P, sum_(j=1)^M min(a_j,b_j)).

One service unit at branch j uses one unit of each of P,a_j,b_j. Input units
have been normalized for a fixed service class and fixed short window. All
resource quantities are additive across fixed actual owners. Reports are
binding coordinatewise underreports of those owners' eligible resources.
No owner identities are split, merged, or changed strategically. TCWM means
that no coalition can increase its total mechanism cash by such underreports.
Full OUT additionally requires its cash loss to cover F(d), where d is the
aggregate withheld resource bundle available for the coalition's own production.
Coverage is required for ALL coalitions at EVERY profile.

The companion FINITE_ARITY_THREE_LADDER_CERTIFICATE.txt gives a growing lower
bound using many independently controlled common-power shares and separate
owners for each branch's complementary a_j and b_j inputs. It does not imply
that a real campus with many GPUs has that ownership institution. The following
results prove that several natural ownership restrictions remove its growing
funding requirement. A count of electrical generators is not a count of owners
with independent binding withholding rights over the modeled common allowance.

2. A FIXED OWNER COVER GIVES A CONSTANT FULL-OUT RULE

General lemma. Let F be finite, nonnegative, monotone, superadditive, with
F(0)=0. Fix resource eligibility, with ALL coalitions admitted, and an owner
set H such that every coalition disjoint from H has zero production at every
eligible report profile. In particular subsets and the support of a withdrawn
bundle are eligible coalitions. Define

  y_i(b)=F(C) if i is in H and b_i is nonzero; otherwise y_i(b)=0,
  C=sum_i b_i.

This rule has nonnegative payments, coalition coverage, full OUT, and total
funding at most |H| F(C). More precisely it pays k_H(b) F(C), where k_H counts
the currently nonzero-report owners in the FIXED set H. A null owner gets zero.

Proof of coverage. If S produces, deleting its zero-report members preserves
its value, so at least one nonzero member of S lies in H. That member receives
F(C)>=F(b_S). If S does not produce, nonnegativity suffices.

Proof of OUT. Let only S reduce reports, with aggregate loss d and C'=C-d.
If F(d)=0, F and the nonzero-report indicator are monotone on restoration, so
S's cash loss is nonnegative. If F(d)>0, the owners with nonzero withheld
bundles form a productive coalition and include some i in H. This i is active
at the high profile. If also active at the low profile, its cash loss is
F(C)-F(C')>=F(d) by superadditivity; if newly active on restoration, its loss
is F(C)>=F(d). Every other member's cash loss is nonnegative. This proves the
finite inequality, including zero-production and activation boundaries.

Common-power corollary. If only R fixed actual owners are eligible to hold P,
take them as H. One may gate their payments by P_i>0 instead of b_i!=0:
the same proof works because productive withdrawn resources need a positive
P contribution. Thus funding is at most R F, independently of M and all other
owners. For R=1 the rule is EFFICIENT: total exactly F whenever F>0. A sole
owner of all necessary bandwidth entitlements can similarly be a veto owner.
This is not a theorem that every fixed R institution has optimal factor R.

Optional fractional version. Fix nonnegative weights w_i such that every
admissible productive coalition S satisfies sum_(i in S) w_i>=1. Payments
w_i F(C) for nonzero-report owners, zero for null reports, have full OUT,
coverage and factor sum_i w_i. For OUT, each high-active member of S loses
at least w_i F(d); the support of d is productive when F(d)>0 and its weight
is at least one. The ordinary owner-cover rule is the 0/1 special case.
This is an elementary fixed-weight covering construction, not a new payment
principle or an optimality statement about arbitrary mechanisms.

Prior-art clarification. Static allocation of the whole value to a veto owner
is classical. Karaca, Delikaraoglou and Kamgarpour (2021), Appendix A,
Proposition 2, explicitly use this allocation for a star's central area:
https://www.research-collection.ethz.ch/server/api/core/bitstreams/17b8f02d-9785-455d-9d50-70453af55d71/content
The finite report-restoration argument above adds full OUT under superadditive
resource production; the static coverage argument itself is not a novelty claim.

Economic limitation. The efficient veto-owner rule pays all incremental
surplus to that owner. Hardware suppliers may have zero surplus even when
their full-owner marginal contribution is positive. Hence no hardware fairness
floor, private operating-cost reimbursement, or ex-ante willingness to join
follows. Such constraints must be separately contracted. Fixed availability
retainers and public costs remain separate ledgers, as in ECONOMIC_SETTLEMENT.
Moreover, a hardware provider able to buy unmodeled outside power and serve
customers independently is NOT a zero-worth coalition in this closed model.
The theorem requires that the specified outside technology be the relevant one.

3. SUPERADDITIVE OWNER COMPRESSION PRESERVES FULL OUT

Transfer lemma. Each fixed actual owner i has a nonnegative original bundle x_i
in a downward-closed domain and a fixed map T_i into nonnegative virtual-resource
coordinates. Suppose T_i(0)=0, T_i is coordinatewise monotone, and

  T_i(x_i)-T_i(x_i-d_i) >= T_i(d_i)                 coordinatewise          (1)

for all allowed 0<=d_i<=x_i. Assume the original coalition production is
exactly G(sum_(i in S) T_i(x_i)) for every S and every report profile, for a
monotone transformed technology G. Run any coalition-covering transformed
mechanism on the SAME actual owners and bundles T_i(x_i). Its funding ratio,
individual underreport monotonicity, TCWM, and beta-OUT guarantee transfer.

Proof. Coalition values and grand value agree exactly, proving coverage and
funding. A binding underreport maps to a coordinatewise binding underreport by
monotonicity, with the SAME coalition of actual owners. Write Delta_i for the
left side of (1). Transformed beta-OUT guarantees cash loss at least
beta G(sum Delta_i), at least beta G(sum T_i(d_i)), which is the original
withheld coalition's worth. All comparisons are finite, so no differentiability,
continuity at zero, invertibility, or strategic use of virtual identities is
assumed. Full owner-deletion marginals agree too, so any such payment floor
also transfers. A superadditive T_i satisfies (1); homogeneity is not needed
for this lemma, though the concrete maps below are homogeneous and concave.

Why merely monotone maps are insufficient for OUT. T(x)=sqrt(x) is monotone
but T(4)-T(3)<T(1). Monotonicity alone transfers payment-only incentives; it
does not prove that the transformed capacity loss covers actual withheld
production. Condition (1) is the load-bearing extra hypothesis.

4. BUNDLED GPU/MEMORY RIGHTS COLLAPSE TO TWO POOLS

Assume that, for each branch j, ALL eligible a_j and b_j capacity is controlled
by one fixed actual provider owner o(j). The same provider may own many branches
and may hold part of P. Distinct providers may exist in arbitrary numbers.
Every original owner remains in the transformed model, including mixed owners.
Define

  e_i=P_i,  h_i=sum_(j:o(j)=i) min(a_j,b_j),  T_i=(e_i,h_i).

For every coalition S the exact original value is min(e(S),h(S)): a branch's
entire reported pair belongs to its provider and is absent when that owner is
absent. The key inequality is

  min(a,b)-min(a-da,b-db) >= min(da,db),

because min is superadditive. Summing this inequality proves (1), including
mixed ownership, arbitrary unequal reductions, ties, and zero reports.

Therefore accepted TWO-POOL mechanisms give, uniformly over M and owner count:

* TCWM and coalition coverage with funding at most 3/2;
* full OUT and coverage with funding at most 1+c, c=.5430633898250876...;
* full OUT plus every full-owner marginal floor theta M_i with factor
  1+max(c,theta), 0<=theta<=1;
* more generally beta-OUT with factor 1+mu(beta), or beta-OUT plus the floor
  with factor 1+max(mu(beta),theta).

Here c solves c(2-exp(-1/c))=1 and mu is the accepted sharp partial-OUT
frontier. For beta up to about .9190487203, mu(beta)=1/2. The simple rational
16-node rule gives over .9088 protection at exactly 3/2 funding. Full OUT can
be implemented with finitely many exact rational evaluations and arbitrarily
small funding overhead, as in FINITE_LOGARITHMIC_FACE_IMPLEMENTATION.txt.

These constants are sharp for the UNIFORM bundled-ownership class because
that class contains every two-pool ownership profile: create one branch per
hardware-holding owner and set a_j=b_j equal to its hardware capacity. Mapping
a two-pool underreport to equal reductions of its pair preserves production,
actual owner identity, and withheld outside worth. This is not sharpness for
each fixed ownership institution; a veto owner can reduce the optimum to one.

5. ONLY t SPLIT BRANCHES: AN INTERMEDIATE STRUCTURAL PARAMETER

Suppose all but t branches satisfy the bundled condition in Section 4. Keep
each of the t exceptional a_j,b_j coordinates separate with its actual mixed
ownership. Compress every bundled branch into its provider's h_i as above.
The transformed technology, in at most d=2t+2 resources, is

  G(P,h,a_E,b_E)=min(P, h+sum_(j in E) min(a_j,b_j)).

This is monotone concave homogeneous, and the same owner-compression proof
applies. Accepted universal dimension theorems therefore give

  TCWM factor <=(d+1)/2 <=t+3/2,
  full-OUT factor <=1+c(d-1) <=1+c(2t+1),

independent of the number of bundled branches and providers. Inactive omitted
coordinates can only improve these upper bounds. If an owner-cover H is also
known in advance, select whichever proved rule has the better GLOBAL bound
from fixed eligibility information. Do not select arbitrary payment rules
profile by profile: that would need a new incentive proof.

Thus a diverging FLOOR-FREE obstruction in this family requires BOTH an unbounded number
of independent common-power owners and an unbounded number of split branches.
Those are necessary structural conditions for unbounded worst-case funding,
not sufficient conditions for difficulty or a claim about typical campuses.

6. WHAT OFFICIAL SOURCES SUPPORT, AND WHAT THEY DO NOT

All sources below are freely available primary documentation, inspected
17 October 2026. Their role is institutional grounding, not theorem priority.

NVIDIA MIG User Guide, Concepts (updated 13 October 2026):
https://docs.nvidia.com/datacenter/tesla/mig-user-guide/concepts.html
The guide defines GPU slices as combining memory and compute slices, and GPU
instances as bundles with dedicated memory resources and memory QoS. Compute
instances can share their parent GPU instance's memory. This supports treating
a reserved GPU instance as a bundled service entitlement; it does NOT establish
separate legal ownership of compute, HBM, and memory bandwidth. Nor does it
justify our continuous linear throughput approximation or a latency guarantee.

AWS EC2, Share Capacity Blocks:
https://docs.aws.amazon.com/AWSEC2/latest/UserGuide/capacity-blocks-share.html
Sharing occurs within an AWS Organization. The owner manages the reservation,
pays its upfront reservation charge, and consumers can launch instances in the
shared capacity. This supports a committed-window entitlement abstraction,
with distinct billing/control roles. It does not establish independent-firm
resale rights, legal ownership of hardware components, or a customer-held
transferable share of the data center's electricity allowance.

Equinix official product documentation, Power:
https://docs.equinix.com/colocation/about/colo-power/
The documented Draw Cap is a contractual limit on a deployment's total load
across its circuits, measured in kVA. This is evidence for enforceable power
constraints. A deployment-level draw cap is NOT automatically a fungible
site-wide trading right, and kVA must not be silently equated to GPU throughput
or kWh. Circuit limits and location must remain in the feasibility model.

Equinix official colocation description:
https://www.equinix.com/what-is-colocation
Colocation places an organization's own servers and computing hardware in
space owned or operated by a third party. This supports distinguishing a
hardware provider from a facility provider. It does not determine which party
may resell, reassign, or withhold already contracted power capacity.

DOE Better Buildings, Colocation Data Centers:
https://betterbuildingssolutioncenter.energy.gov/data-center-toolkit/colocation
The official indexed page describes the facility owner leasing space, power,
and cooling to tenants and identifies split incentives around efficiency.
Direct page fetch returned HTTP502 in this audit; the indexed text supports
only that narrow institutional summary. No quantitative claim relies on it.

7. RECOMMENDED APPLICATION AND FALSIFICATION GATE

Begin with one campus and one precommitted service window. Each tenant/provider
contributes an already qualified bundled service envelope; a fixed agreement
states who controls each deliverable power entitlement and which entitlements
can be reassigned within the campus. Use public net service values and settle
verifiable public costs once. Inventory actual withholding rights, not labels
such as GPU, memory, network, power, or the number of technical meters.

If all hardware complements are jointly controlled, the correct first theorem
is the two-pool compression. If one indispensable allowance holder controls
the entire common row, the unconstrained surplus problem is efficient; the
interesting next question is an explicit participation/fairness or outside-use
contract, not the rank-three impossibility. If rights are genuinely fragmented,
record how many split branches and independent allowance holders exist before
applying the lower bound. The exact lower bound is a worst-case theorem, not
a prediction that every fragmented contract needs large subsidy.

Reject this mapping if separate network or memory rights change feasibility
by coalition in a way not encoded in owned resources, if workloads cannot be
represented by the fixed divisible service envelope, or if outside production
uses resources not included in the contracted technology. A real provider's
private opportunity costs, nontransferable SLAs, queueing/latency, failures,
battery state of charge, and time-coupled power costs are not supplied by these
theorems. The Mac experiments do not measure this institutional model.
