THEOREM CLAIM
For F(A,B)=min(A,B), nonnegative coalition-covering mechanisms satisfying full
coalition outside-production protection(OUT), the optimal universal funding
factor over finite fixed actual-owner models is
             K*=1+c=1.5430633898250876...,
where c is the positive root c[2-exp(-1/c)]=1.
The upper allows arbitrary fixed mixed owner bundles and all real capacities.
The proposed LOWER imposes no anonymity, continuity, homogeneity, price,
separability, or split/merge condition on the original mechanisms. It is already
forced by fixed PURE-role owners and their binary0/1 capacity reports, as owner
counts grow. The constant is a universal supremum, not a claim of equality for
each finite owner count. The proof is compactness-based and gives no effective
finite owner count needed to approach the sharp constant.

The complete-access public source-price extension has the same sharp universal
constant: the prior fixed-threshold construction gives the upper, and the unit
two-resource model is a subclass. No additional participation floor is imposed.

1. START FROM ARBITRARY FINITE-OWNER RULES
Fix kappa<K*. Suppose, for contradiction, that every finite owner model admits
a rule with the stated properties and funding at most kappa F. For each positive
integer N, use2N fixed actual owners: N can report only resource A, and N only
resource B. Each owner's capacity domain includes[0,1]. Average its rule over
permutations within each role and over simultaneous exchange of the two role
groups and the two resource labels. This finite averaging preserves every
coverage, nonnegativity, budget, and OUT inequality because all are linear in
payments and the domain/production are invariant under these relabelings.
No actual identity changes in any deviation comparison.

At a binary profile with m active A owners and n active B owners, m,n<=N,
let x^(N)_{mn} be the common payment of an active A owner and y^(N)_{mn} the
common payment of an active B owner. Inactive owner payments may be positive;
we do NOT assume them away. Nonnegativity and the budget imply
             m x^(N)_{mn}+n y^(N)_{mn}<=kappa min(m,n).   (1)
Thus each active payment lies in[0,kappa]. At zero production all payments
are zero. Accordingly ALL coordinates x_{m0},y_{m0},x_{0n},y_{0n} are zero,
including coordinates for an absent owner type, assigned zero by convention.
Symmetry gives x^(N)_{mn}=y^(N)_{nm}.

Take any fixed coalition consisting of r active A owners and s active B owners,
with0<=r<=m and0<=s<=n. Coalition coverage says
             r x^(N)_{mn}+s y^(N)_{mn}>=min(r,s).         (2)
Let u<=r of its A owners and v<=s of its B owners withhold their complete unit
reports. The remaining profile has m-u active A owners and n-v active B owners.
Their withheld resources produce min(u,v). The coalition also receives any
payments of its newly inactive owners at that reduced profile; those payments
are nonnegative. Dropping them from the right side of OUT gives the NECESSARY
inequality
 r x^(N)_{mn}+s y^(N)_{mn}
 >=(r-u)x^(N)_{m-u,n-v}+(s-v)y^(N)_{m-u,n-v}+min(u,v).    (3)
All labels and roles remain fixed. Both the high and reduced profiles are
admissible binary reports of the SAME2N owners.

2. AN INFINITE COUNT ARRAY, WITHOUT A POPULATION-INVARIANCE ASSUMPTION
Use a diagonal subsequence as N tends to infinity to make every fixed finite
array coordinate x^(N)_{mn},y^(N)_{mn} converge. This is possible because there
are countably many coordinates, each in the compact interval[0,kappa]. Every
fixed inequality(1)-(3) involves finitely many coordinates and is available
for all sufficiently large N. Its limit therefore holds.

Let C be the set of all such ABSTRACT nonnegative arrays satisfying(1)-(3),
the symmetry x_{mn}=y_{nm}, and the zero-production conventions for ALL finite
nonnegative integer counts. C is nonempty by the preceding limit, convex, and
closed in the countable product of[0,kappa]. Coordinatewise subsequences are
compact by the same diagonal argument. We use only these necessary constraints;
we do not claim that every member of C comes from an actual mechanism.

For each positive integer k define the count-dilation operator
       (T_k x)_{mn}=x_{km,kn},   (T_k y)_{mn}=y_{km,kn}.
It maps C into C. For(1), apply the original constraint at(km,kn) and divide
by k. For(2)-(3), use coalition counts(kr,ks), withholding(ku,kv), and divide
by k, using min(ku,kv)=k min(u,v). Symmetry and nonnegativity are preserved.
These linear operators commute: T_k T_l=T_{kl}. This operation acts on the
abstract array; it is not a strategic replacement of actual owners by clones.

3. ELEMENTARY AVERAGING CREATES SCALE INVARIANCE
Start with any z=(x,y) in C. Let p_1,p_2,... enumerate the primes. For L>=1 form
the finite average
 z_L=(1/L^L) sum_{0<=e_1,...,e_L<L} T_{prod_{j=1}^L p_j^{e_j}} z.
Each z_L is in C. For a fixed prime p_j and L>=j, subtract z_L from T_{p_j}z_L.
The sum telescopes in exponent e_j. At each array coordinate the absolute
difference is at most kappa/L: only its upper and lower exponent boundary
terms remain, and each coordinate lies in[0,kappa].

Take a coordinatewise convergent subsequence z_L->z_* in C. For every fixed
p_j, coordinatewise continuity of T_{p_j} and the bound kappa/L imply
T_{p_j}z_*=z_*. Prime factorization gives T_k z_*=z_* for EVERY positive integer k.
Hence x^*_{km,kn}=x^*_{mn} and y^*_{km,kn}=y^*_{mn}. This is a derived property
of a feasible limiting array, not an assumption about the original rules.

4. RATIONAL AGGREGATE PRICES AND THEIR EXACT FINITE INEQUALITIES
For rational0<t=n/m<=1 define p(t)=x^*_{mn}, q(t)=y^*_{mn}. This does not depend
on the representation: if n/m=n'/m', scale the two count pairs by m' and m,
respectively, and use the count-dilation invariance. The values for reversed
aggregate sizes are obtained by resource symmetry. In particular p(1)=q(1).

For ANY nonnegative rational aggregate capacities A,B, coalition holdings
a<=A,b<=B, and withheld amounts d<=a,e<=b, multiply all six rationals by a
common integer denominator. Use the corresponding integer counts in(1)-(3)
and divide by that denominator. Thus the artificial payment formula
       Y_S=a p(B/A)+b q(B/A), when A>=B>0,
obeys coverage, the factor-kappa budget, and full OUT on EVERY such rational
comparison. Resource reversal handles A<B. In particular
       p(t)/t+q(t)<=kappa,  p(t),q(t)>=0.                (4)
This is the desired price rule on a rational domain, obtained from necessary
inequalities of entirely unrestricted original mechanisms.

5. FINITE-DEVIATION INEQUALITIES FORCE LIPSCHITZ REGULARITY
The following arguments use only rational comparisons already licensed by(3).
No derivatives or continuity have yet been assumed.

Let0<t<u<=1 be rational. The coalition of all A owners can increase its total
A report with B held fixed. Its payment monotonicity implies
             k(t):=p(t)/t is nonincreasing in t.          (5)
Likewise increasing all B reports at fixed A implies
             z(t):=t q(t) is nondecreasing in t.         (6)
For example, for(5) hold B=1 and compare A=1/u with A=1/t. For(6) hold A=1
and compare B=t with B=u. These are direct instances of the rational inequality
derived from array(3). No assumption on the original rule's payments to inactive
owners is made or needed.

Next take a coalition holding ALL A=1 and an additional B amount u-t. The
outside owners hold B=t. Compare the coalition's report of its B amount with
zero. Payment monotonicity gives
             p(u)+(u-t)q(u)>=p(t).                     (7)
By(4), q(u)<=kappa. Thus p(t)-p(u)<=kappa(u-t). Conversely(5) implies
p(u)<=u p(t)/t, so p(u)-p(t)<=(u-t)p(t)/t<=kappa(u-t).
Therefore p is kappa-Lipschitz on the rational interval(0,1].

To control q, let a coalition hold ALL B=1 and A amount1/t-1/u, with outside
owners holding A=1/u. Its increase from zero A yields
             q(t)+(1/t-1/u)p(t)>=q(u).                  (8)
Hence q(u)-q(t)<=kappa(u-t)/u, using p(t)<=kappa t.
Meanwhile(6) gives q(t)<=u q(u)/t, and so
q(t)-q(u)<=kappa(u-t)/t. Thus q is kappa/epsilon-Lipschitz on every rational
interval[epsilon,1]. The endpoints at1 are included in these inequalities.

Consequently p and q have unique continuous extensions to(0,1], locally
Lipschitz and therefore locally absolutely continuous. Their symmetric tie
p(1)=q(1) remains valid. Every rational coverage, budget and OUT inequality
passes to the real domain by continuity; alternatively the following lower
needs only their local derivative consequences. Zero aggregate boundaries are
irrelevant to this limiting-price lower and can be set to zero payments using
the preserved finite budget.

6. APPLY THE ANALYTIC PRICE LOWER
The preceding construction has DERIVED all hypotheses needed by Section6 of
OUTSIDE_LOGARITHMIC_PRICE_THEOREM.txt. For self-containment, its essential
argument is repeated here. At almost every t, necessary local OUT conditions
for coalition shares approaching the relevant corners give
       p-tp'>=0,   q+tq'>=0,
       p+q+t(1-t)q'>=1.                               (9)
The last inequality corresponds to a coalition holding almost all short-side
resources and an arbitrarily small positive amount of the long side, withdrawing
equal small amounts of both. Corner limits follow from linearity in coalition
shares and local Lipschitz prices, and use feasible positive withdrawals before
taking the share limit.

Set C0=lim_{t down to0}p(t)/t. It exists by(5), is finite by(4), and p(t)<=C0 t.
Use the identity [tq(t)/(1-t)]'=[q+t(1-t)q']/(1-t)^2 and integrate the last
inequality in(9), after dividing by(1-t)^2, to obtain
 q(t)>=1-C0-C0(1-t)log(1-t)/t,                          (10)
because q is bounded by kappa and the lower-end integration term vanishes.
Thus liminf q(t)>=1, so the funding bound yields kappa>=1+C0.
The second inequality in(9) makes tq(t) nondecreasing; the tie gives
q(1)=p(1)<=C0. But the maximum of t times the right side of(10) is
1-C0+C0 exp(-1/C0) for C0>0. C0=0 is immediately impossible. Therefore
       C0[2-exp(-1/C0)]>=1,
which forces C0>=c and kappa>=1+c=K*, a contradiction.

This proves the proposed unrestricted universal lower. The logarithmic rule
already supplies the matching upper on every real profile and fixed mixed
owner model. No regularity of the original arbitrary mechanisms entered.

7. GROWING RESOURCE COUNTS AND WHAT COMPACTNESS DOES NOT GIVE
The same lower can be forced while resource counts also grow. For any K use K
disjoint unit activities, each with its own two resource rows and N fixed pure
owners of each role. A candidate mechanism may couple payments across blocks.
Symmetrize over block permutations, within-role owner permutations, and common
resource reversal. Evaluate diagonal profiles with the same active counts(m,n)
in every block. At these profiles all active owners of a given role receive the
same payment. Coverage of the coalition using r A and s B owners in every block
has worth K min(r,s). A simultaneous withdrawal of u A and v B owners in each
block has outside value K min(u,v). Divide all payment inequalities and the
budget by K to recover(1)-(3). Thus the same compactness contradiction applies
for arbitrary K, including unbounded K and N. Each considered block has positive
production when m,n>0; extra resource rows are not null padding.

The proof establishes that every factor below K* fails on some finite fixed-
owner binary domain. It does NOT provide an explicit near-sharp finite profile
list or an effective owner-count rate. Finite LPs returning only the previous
3/2-1/(2m) bound at small m are compatible with this asymptotic argument; they
neither prove nor refute it. An explicit finite certificate is a worthwhile
follow-up, not part of the present proof claim.
