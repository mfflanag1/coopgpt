GENERAL CONSTANT-VALUE COMPLETION LEMMA
Let a fixed-owner report domain be closed under the particular completions
used below. Write F(b)=F(sum_i b_i), S(b)=sum_i F(b_i), G(b)=F(b)-S(b)>=0.
Suppose EVERY profile b has a feasible coordinatewise larger profile h with
the SAME actual owners, F(h)=F(b), and G(h)=0. Then grand-coalition total
payment monotonicity alone implies

  [Y(h)<=rho F(h) for every G(h)=0] => [Y(b)<=rho F(b) for every b].

Indeed Y(b)<=Y(h)<=rho F(h)=rho F(b). No mechanism homogeneity, aggregate
prices, anonymity, differentiability, or null-owner convention is assumed.
This is a general completion criterion, not a claim that every production
function or every fixed eligibility domain admits such a completion.

MINIMUM OF r POOLS: COMPLETION FOR EVERY r
For F(c)=min_{1<=j<=r}c_j, choose a global bottleneck j*, so C_j*=F(C).
Leave every owner's j* coordinate unchanged and set

  h_ij=max(b_ij,b_i,j*) for j!=j*.

All coordinates increase. The aggregate j* coordinate is unchanged, every
other aggregate coordinate is at least C_j*, and every owner's minimum is
exactly b_i,j*. Thus F(h)=F(b)=sum_i F(h_i), as required. This completion is
feasible on the nonnegative orthant and on an identical-coordinate cube
[0,B_i]^r for each owner (the B_i may differ between owners). It need not be
feasible under resource-specific eligibility or unequal coordinate caps.
Zero production and bottleneck ties are included. For r=1, G is identically
zero and the sharp reserve is zero.

TWO-POOL FINITE CERTIFICATE MODEL
There are n fixed actual owners, each eligible for both resource types, with
reports b_i=(a_i,b_i) in [0,1]^2 (or all nonnegative pairs). Production is
F(A,B)=min(A,B), A=sum a_i,B=sum b_i. Let

  S(b)=sum_i min(a_i,b_i),       total standalone production,
  G(b)=F(A,B)-S(b)>=0,          actual gains from pooling.

Payments are nonnegative, cover every coalition, and have TCWM for ALL
coalitions. Full OUT is stronger and will be considered as a corollary.
The fixed domain is important: an owner may restore a deficient coordinate
up to its other coordinate. This holds on [0,1]^2 for every owner; it need
not hold for every restricted eligibility or unequal-cap institution.

THE RESTORATION LEMMA
For ANY such TCWM mechanism, if

  Y(b)<=rho F(b) whenever G(b)=0,                          (1)

then in fact Y(b)<=rho F(b) at EVERY profile. Here Y is total payment.

Proof. Suppose A>=B. Restore each a_i to a_i'=max(a_i,b_i), leaving b_i
unchanged. This is a feasible joint coordinatewise increase by the SAME
fixed actual owners. At the high profile every owner has a_i'>=b_i, so its
standalone production is b_i and total standalone production is B. Grand
production also remains B. Thus G(high)=0 and F(high)=F(low)=B. TCWM for
the grand coalition gives Y(low)<=Y(high)<=rho B. The case B>=A follows
by restoring each b_i to max(a_i,b_i). Ties, zero coordinates, and F=0 are
included. Only grand-coalition monotonicity is needed in this lemma. QED.

This is a finite owner-preserving comparison; it does not assume aggregate
prices, anonymity, homogeneity of the mechanism, differentiability or compactness.

CONSEQUENCE: NO FINITE SUBSIDY MULTIPLE OF POOLING GAINS
No all-profile bound of the form

  Y(b)<=S(b)+K G(b), any finite K,                         (2)

can coexist with coverage and full TCWM uniformly, already for n=3 fully
eligible owners. At G=0, (2) would force Y<=F, so the restoration lemma would
force efficiency at EVERY profile. The exact finite certificate below shows
this impossible. Full OUT cannot rescue (2), since it implies TCWM.

More generally, consider a stabilization reserve plus gain-dependent budget

  Y(b)<=(1+delta)F(b)+K G(b), K>=0.                       (3)

Its restriction to G=0 forces the GLOBAL factor1+delta regardless of how
large K is. Therefore a reserve proportional to present total production is
unavoidable in this strong incentive contract; gain-dependent allowance alone
cannot substitute for it.

EXACT n-OWNER RESERVE CERTIFICATE
For n=s+1>=3, any rho in (1) must satisfy

  rho>=3/2-1/(2s), or delta>=(s-1)/(2s).                 (4)

Symmetrize over the finite group of owner permutations and exchange of the
two resource labels. The fully eligible domain, G=0 condition, all coalition
coverage, and TCWM are invariant, so averaging preserves every requirement.
This is an analysis of fixed actual owners, not strategic identity changes.

At P0, one owner reports(1,0), another(0,1), and s-1 owners report(0,0).
The two active owners receive the same b; each null owner receives w>=0.
Coverage of the active pair gives 2b>=1, hence b>=1/2.

Restore each of the s-1 null owners to(1,0), obtaining P1. Let x be the common
payment of its s pure-A owners and q that of the pure-B owner. TCWM for the
coalition consisting of the original pure-A owner and the restored owners
implies sx>=b+(s-1)w>=1/2. Coverage of the pure-B owner plus any one pure-A
owner gives q+x>=1. Hence

  Y(P1)=sx+q>=1+(s-1)x>=1+(s-1)/(2s).                    (5)

Finally restore the pure-B owner's A coordinate from0 to1. The resulting
anchor profile H1 has s pure-A owners and one(1,1) owner. Its grand production
and its total standalone production both equal1, so G(H1)=0. Grand-coalition
TCWM gives Y(H1)>=Y(P1). Thus (1) implies (4).

This proof uses only binary reports. For n=3, at least one zero-gain profile
must have funding at least5/4. There is no need to assume null-owner payments
vanish: they only strengthen the comparison. Formula (4) approaches3/2 as
the actual fixed owner count grows. This is a lower certificate, not a claim
of exact optimality for each finite n.

SHARP UNIFORM RESERVE COEFFICIENTS
On the fully eligible NONNEGATIVE ORTHANT, for every r the accepted sharp
min-r TCWM factor is (r+1)/2, and the accepted
sharp full-OUT factor is 1+c(r-1), with c defined below. The completion lemma
therefore proves that the SHARP uniform reserve coefficients, even when the
funding objective is measured only on the G=0 slice, are

  delta_TCWM(r)=(r-1)/2,       delta_OUT(r)=c(r-1).

The TCWM upper is root/GENERAL_FACE_AVERAGE_TCWM.txt and the full-OUT upper is
energy/MULTIPOOL_OUTSIDE_LOGARITHMIC.txt, both for arbitrary mixed ownership;
their sharpness follows from their separately audited min-r lower dependencies.
The accepted lower domains embed into the fully eligible orthant by setting
unused coordinates to zero. The lower bounds range over finite owner sets;
they do not assume an actually infinite population. The all-profile upper
mechanisms achieve the same reserve with K=0. This is a transfer of the
accepted general-r constants, not a new proof of those constants. Completion
also holds on per-owner cubes, but the full arbitrary-r sharpness statement
here is for the orthant; no unproved cube lower is silently imported.

In particular, across all finite fully eligible owner sets, the accepted two-pool
TCWM factor is3/2. Its upper applies to every profile, while (4) supplies the
matching lower even when the objective measures only profiles with G=0.
Thus the sharp uniform TCWM reserve coefficient in (3) is delta=1/2.

For full OUT, the independently audited unrestricted two-pool theorem gives
the sharp global factor1+c, c=.5430633898250876..., where
c(2-exp(-1/c))=1. The restoration lemma converts ANY bound1+delta on the
zero-gain slice into that same bound globally. The full-OUT lower's pure-role
binary domains embed in the fully eligible domain by restricting unused
coordinates to zero, with actual identities unchanged. Hence the sharp uniform
zero-gain reserve coefficient is delta=c. Conversely the global1+c rule
achieves that coefficient with K=0. No new derivation of the logarithmic
constant is asserted here; this is a structural transfer of the accepted result.

ECONOMIC INTERPRETATION AND LIMITS
The denominator distinction is substantive. S is what owners can actually
produce independently with their OWN two-resource bundles, not a fictitious
marginal-value fairness floor. G is genuine additional production from pooling.
The theorem shows that preserving coverage and strong all-profile coalition
incentives can force extra transfers at a profile where G=0. An owner can hide
part of an abundant complement to alter the apparent scarcity; consistency
with payments at the resulting cooperative profile creates the reserve need.

This is NOT a claim that independent production physically requires subsidy.
Giving each owner its own standalone output is efficient on G=0 profiles,
but that allocation cannot be extended to the stated all-profile incentive
and coverage contract with a gain-only funding promise. One may instead
change participation/report domains, weaken coalition incentives, subsidize
from an ex-ante reserve, or adopt a different mechanism objective. These are
contract changes, and their welfare consequences need separate analysis.
The theorem also does not apply to every ownership pattern: pure fixed
eligibility need not permit the restoration, and a single indispensable
power owner remains an efficient floor-free case as previously proved.
