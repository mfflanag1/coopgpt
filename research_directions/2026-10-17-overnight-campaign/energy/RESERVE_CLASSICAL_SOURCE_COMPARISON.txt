1. Result and evidentiary status

Denault's original is accessible and its relevant definitions/theorems have
been read. Fuzzy-core/Euler allocation does NOT imply our fixed-owner payment
monotonicity, even for proportional changes in one existing portfolio. An
exact example below proves this, including zero outside production. Moulin's
publisher abstract and original working-paper record were verified; his full
1999 article/1995 preprint was not obtained. That comparison remains open at
the theorem-hypothesis level. No purchase, access-control bypass or outreach.

2. Denault: what is established

Michel Denault, Coherent allocation of risk capital, Journal of Risk 4(1),
2001, DOI 10.21314/JOR.2001.053. Original author-upload and course-hosted
preprint agree on the relevant statements. Read preprint Definition 10;
Definitions 12--13, equations (3),(7); equations (9)--(11); Theorems 6--7 and
Corollary 1 (printed pages 14--20; PDF pages 14--20).

Fractional involvement is lambda_i/Lambda_i of an unchanged portfolio.
Full allocation is Lambda.k=r(Lambda). Fuzzy-core feasibility requires
lambda.k<=r(lambda) for every 0<=lambda<=Lambda. The coherent-value axioms
include aggregation invariance, continuity, a sign condition when r is
nondecreasing, and dummy allocation. Theorems 6--7, credited there to Aubin,
identify the homogeneous fuzzy core with the subdifferential; convexity gives
existence and differentiability gives the unique gradient. Hence the
Aumann--Shapley/Euler allocation is classical. The sign condition is not a
comparison of two profiles' payments.

3. Exact separation, within proportional fractional participation

Our analysis, not a claim made by Denault: take bounded Z uniformly on the
unit circle, portfolio payoff W_i=t_i-x_i.Z, and coherent worst-case risk
rho(W)=ess sup(-W). Then the risk cost is
r(lambda)=||sum lambda_i x_i||_2-sum lambda_i t_i. Adjoin an unchanged riskless
reference portfolio b_3=(1,0), payoff W_3=1, matching Denault's designated
cash instrument. It changes neither the aggregate exposure nor owner 1's
Euler net credit. This embeds the example in his L-infinity domain with the
cash-translation axiom, without treating Gaussian value-at-risk as globally
coherent.

For feasible reserve bundles b_i=(t_i,x_i), t_i>=||x_i||, minus the Euler
risk allocation is the net credit y_i=-k_i=t_i-p.x_i,
p=(sum x_i)/||sum x_i||. It exactly funds
F(sum b_i)=sum t_i-||sum x_i|| and covers every coalition; subtracting the
linear t-credit from the norm support inequality proves this directly.

Set b_1=(1,(0,1)), b_2=(2,(1/2,-1)). Initially p=(1,0), y_1=1.
Restore D=(1/10,(0,1/10)) to the SAME owner 1, i.e. scale its whole
portfolio and credit by 11/10. This is a cone-feasible proportional change,
F(D)=0. The new aggregate vector is (1/2,1/10), so

  y_1(new)=11/10 - 11/(10 sqrt(26)) < 1 = y_1(old).

The strict inequality is equivalent to sqrt(26)<11. Both aggregates are
nonzero, so each Euler/fuzzy-core choice is unique. Withholding D therefore
raises cash despite its zero outside value. Static fractional-core stability
does not imply even individual payment monotonicity, hence cannot imply the
stronger pooled-outside-production contract. Precisely, full OUT requires
sum_{i in A}[y_i(b+D)-y_i(b)]>=F(sum_{i in A}D_i) for every fixed
coalition A and feasible restorations D_i, with outsiders unchanged. A singleton
with F(D)=0 gives the payment monotonicity violated above. Static exact funding supplies no
subsidy guarantee for that stronger contract. The proposed contribution must
be the incentive-compatible funding theorem, not Euler/core allocation.

4. Moulin: verified scope, unresolved exact comparison

Herve Moulin, Incremental cost sharing: Characterization by coalition
strategy-proofness, Social Choice and Welfare 16(2), 279--320 (1999),
DOI 10.1007/s003550050145. The publisher's abstract describes indivisible
user demands, joint production costs, convex preferences and strong-equilibrium
selection. Cost curvature restrictions govern sequential/incremental methods.
This is a strategic demand-and-cost-sharing environment; the verified abstract
does not specify our resource-withholding payment inequality, a retained-bundle
outside-production term, or a bound on external funding for coalition covers.
