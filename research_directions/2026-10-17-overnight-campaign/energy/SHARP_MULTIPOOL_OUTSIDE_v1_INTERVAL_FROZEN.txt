SHARP FULL-OUT FUNDING FOR ARBITRARILY MANY COMPLEMENTARY RESOURCE TYPES
2026-10-17. Candidate theorem. Parent/independent review pending.

THEOREM. For r>=2, the optimal funding factor, uniformly over finite fixed
owner populations and arbitrary nonnegative reports, for F(C)=min_(1<=j<=r)C_j
under nonnegative payments, coalition coverage, and full coalition OUT is

               K_r = 1+c(r-1),
               c[2-exp(-1/c)]=1,
               c=0.5430633898250876... .

Full coalition OUT includes the coalition's production from its own withheld
bundle. It is stronger than payment-only TCWM and individual truthfulness.
The upper permits arbitrary mixed-resource owners. For the lower, unbounded
finite populations of pure-resource owners already suffice. This is a worst-
case universal factor, not a lower bound for each particular owner population.

UPPER. MULTIPOOL_OUTSIDE_LOGARITHMIC.txt proves the all-profile upper with
explicit continuous positive-profile prices, including ties and zero faces.
This note proves the matching lower, using two previously proved lemmas:

 (A) The aggregate-price existence reduction, proved in
     COALITION_PRICE_REDUCTION_CANDIDATE.txt. A uniformly k-funded all-owner
     family of full-OUT rules yields a single homogeneous aggregate-price rule
     with the same properties. It suffices that the original family exists on
     pure-owner models. The resulting prices are continuous in each positive
     support face, including the full positive orthant.

 (B) The two-input price lemma in OUTSIDE_LOGARITHMIC_PRICE_THEOREM.txt:
     for any symmetric, homogeneous, bounded-funded full-OUT price rule for
     min(A,B), if C0=lim_(t->0) p_long(1/t,1)/t, then C0>=c. The limit exists
     by TCWM. In particular, a uniform long-resource expenditure bound
     B p_B(A,B)<=D A whenever B>=A implies D>=c.

For clarity, the final analytic step establishing (B) is repeated in Section5.
No regularity or symmetry is assumed of the original mechanisms.

1. RESOURCE SYMMETRIZATION AND A BASELINE EXPENDITURE BOUND

Suppose funding k is feasible uniformly over finite owner counts. Apply (A)
and average its price mechanism over all r! resource permutations. All required
inequalities are linear, so this preserves them. Write p_j(C) for the resulting
resource-symmetric prices. Homogeneity and positive-profile continuity remain.

At the profile (A,M,...,M), with 0<A<M, let q be the first resource's price and
b the common price of every other resource. Coalition coverage for a coalition
holding A units of every resource gives q+(r-1)b>=1. Budget gives

                  A q+(r-1)M b <= k A.

Subtracting A times the first inequality gives

       M b <= D A M/(M-A),      D=(k-1)/(r-1).             (1)

Now use a coalition owning the ENTIRE amount B of the second resource and no
other resource, where A<=B<=M, at profile (A,B,M,...,M). Increase that coalition's
second resource from B to M, keeping all outsiders fixed. OUT implies TCWM
because the withheld one-coordinate bundle has zero outside value. Therefore

 B p_2(A,B,M,...,M) <= M p_2(A,M,M,...,M)
                  <= D A M/(M-A).                        (2)

The analogous bound holds for resource1 if B<=A. This is the key monotonicity
step: one cannot give a moderate long input more total money than it would
receive after becoming as abundant as all the other long inputs.

2. REMOVE THE OTHER r-2 BOTTLENECKS WITHOUT REMOVING THEIR OWNERS

Assume r>=3. For R>max(A,B) define averaged pair prices

 P_1^R(A,B)=(1/R) integral_R^(2R) p_1(A,B,M,...,M) dM,
 P_2^R(A,B)=(1/R) integral_R^(2R) p_2(A,B,M,...,M) dM.

The integrands are continuous and bounded on this interval. Budget gives
0<=p_j<=k min(A,B)/C_j; in particular the first two prices are bounded by k.
Use a diagonal subsequence R->infinity over all nonnegative rational pairs to
obtain limits P_1,P_2. At a pair with zero production set both prices to zero;
all payments are then zero by the original budget and nonnegativity as well.

The limit is symmetric in the two resources. It has pair budget at most
k min(A,B), and (2) gives the sharper cap

       B P_2(A,B)<=D A whenever B>=A,                    (3)

with its symmetric counterpart.

To obtain coalition coverage for pair holdings (a,b)<= (A,B), give the same
coalition an additional min(a,b) units of each of the other resources. These
are ordinary fixed-owner resource holdings in the aggregate-price mechanism.
Their total price contribution is at most

    min(a,b) sum_(j>=3)p_j <= min(a,b)*k min(A,B)/M,

because all extra resource totals are M. It vanishes uniformly for M>=R.
The r-resource coalition worth is exactly min(a,b). Taking averages and limits
therefore proves aP_1+bP_2>=min(a,b).

3. FULL OUT SURVIVES THE LIMIT; WHY THE INTERVAL AVERAGE IS NEEDED

Fix rational pair aggregates (A,B), coalition holdings (a,b), and withheld
amounts (u,v), with 0<=u<=a<=A and 0<=v<=b<=B. Put w=min(u,v).
Give the coalition w units of EVERY extra resource and let it withhold all of
them. The high r-profile has extra totals M, the low one extra totals M-w,
and the coalition's outside production is exactly w.

The high extra-resource payment is O(1/R), by the estimate in Section2. The
low coalition has no retained extra resources. Its first two prices are

        p_j(A-u,B-v,M-w,...,M-w).

For a bounded function f of M with |f|<=k, the means over [R,2R] and the shifted
interval [R-w,2R-w] differ by at most 2kw/R. Thus the average low pair prices
have the same subsequential limit as P_j(A-u,B-v). This argument requires no
unproved pointwise convergence as M tends to infinity. If the low pair has
zero production, its payment terms are zero directly.

The averaged r-resource OUT inequality therefore tends to

 aP_1(A,B)+bP_2(A,B)
 >=(a-u)P_1(A-u,B-v)+(b-v)P_2(A-u,B-v)+min(u,v).       (4)

Thus a rational two-input aggregate-price rule satisfies all coalition OUT
inequalities, budget, coverage, and the long-expenditure cap (3).

4. HOMOGENIZE THE LIMIT, RETAINING THE EXPENDITURE CAP

The interval limit need not be homogeneous. This cannot be silently assumed.
Consider the nonempty compact convex set of all rational pair-price arrays
satisfying the inequalities just obtained, resource symmetry, and (3). Coordinate
bounds 0<=P_j<=k min(A,B)/C_j make the countable product compact. Zero coordinates
are assigned zero and their products are immaterial. Every constraint involves
finitely many coordinates and is closed.

For every positive integer h the operation

                  (T_h P)(A,B)=P(hA,hB)

preserves all these constraints: scale aggregates, holdings, and withdrawals
by h and divide each inequality by h. The T_h commute. Average over products
of the first L primes, with exponents 0,...,L-1, and take a diagonal subsequential
limit as L grows. For each fixed prime, shifting its exponent leaves a boundary
error at most k/L in each coordinate. The limiting array is invariant under
every integer dilation, hence under every positive rational dilation.

This gives symmetric homogeneous rational pair prices obeying (3). The finite
TCWM inequalities imply local Lipschitz continuity on positive rational pairs,
exactly as proved in the earlier two-input and general reduction notes; extend
continuously to real positive pairs. The one-dimensional ratio prices are
therefore locally absolutely continuous, including at the balanced endpoint.
The full OUT inequalities pass to real profiles by rational approximation.

This second averaging is mathematically separate from the interval average:
the first removes finite capacity shifts, the second removes scale dependence.
Both preserve the long-expenditure bound and neither alters actual owners in
an original strategic comparison.

For r=2 Sections2--4 are unnecessary: the price rule from (A) is already a
homogeneous symmetric pair rule, and (1) plus TCWM gives the same cap (3).

5. THE TWO-INPUT ANALYTIC OBSTRUCTION

At aggregate (A,B) with A>=B, write t=B/A, long price p(t), and short price q(t).
The cap gives p(t)/t<=D. TCWM gives p-tp'>=0 and q+tq'>=0 almost everywhere, so
C0=lim_(t->0)p(t)/t exists, C0<=D, and p(t)<=C0 t. Bounded budget ensures q is
bounded. Full OUT for the extremal coalition holding the short input yields

             p+q+t(1-t)q' >=1.

Hence

       [tq/(1-t)]' >= (1-C0 t)/(1-t)^2.

Integrating from zero, using bounded q, gives

 q(t)>=1-C0-C0(1-t)log(1-t)/t.

The function tq is nondecreasing, and resource symmetry plus continuity gives
q(1)=p(1)<=C0. Consequently

 C0 >= max_(0<t<1) [(1-C0)t-C0(1-t)log(1-t)]
    = 1-C0+C0 exp(-1/C0).

The case C0=0 is impossible directly from the same inequality or its limit.
Thus C0[2-exp(-1/C0)]>=1. The left side is strictly increasing for C0>0,
so C0>=c. Since D>=C0, (1) proves

                        k>=1+c(r-1).

Together with the explicit upper this establishes the claimed universal sharp
constant for every finite r. A purely finite-owner lower follows by the
contrapositive of the compactness reduction; this proof does not provide a
useful owner-count threshold for r>=3. The explicit enormous two-input finite
threshold is recorded separately in FINITE_OUTSIDE_OBSTRUCTION.txt.

SCOPE AND REVIEW. This candidate theorem settles this complementary-input
full-OUT funding problem, not arbitrary production networks, private costs,
or the original individual-only incentive problem. It has not received an
external specialist review, and historical priority remains unconfirmed.
