CONTINUOUS NORMALIZED GAIN WEIGHTS FORCE A LINEAR OWNER COEFFICIENT
17 October 2026. Elementary scoped obstruction; not a payment impossibility.

Let m>=2,n>=2 and suppose a_i(X_1,...,X_n) are nonnegative coefficients,
jointly continuous at the all-zero profile, and satisfy the residual coverage
condition
 sum_(i in Q) a_i(X) >= F(sum_Q U_i)/G                         (1)
whenever G>0, where F=lambda_min, f_i=F(X_i), U_i=X_i-f_i I,
and G=F(sum_N U_i). The stronger productive-coalition condition
sum_Q a_i>=1 whenever F(sum_Q U_i)>0 also implies (1).
Then
                           sum_i a_i(0)>=n/2.                (2)
Moreover the supremum of sum_i a_i over productive profiles in EVERY
neighborhood of zero is at least n/2.

Proof. Fix distinct owners i,j. For epsilon>0 report
 X_i=epsilon*diag(1,0,...,0),
 X_j=epsilon*diag(0,1,...,1), all other reports zero.
Both active reports are singular, all standalone values vanish, and their
sum is epsilon I. Thus G=epsilon and coalition {i,j} has residual value G.
Equation(1) implies a_i(X^epsilon)+a_j(X^epsilon)>=1. Let epsilon decrease
to zero and use continuity. The result a_i(0)+a_j(0)>=1 holds for every pair.
Summing all n(n-1)/2 pairs gives (n-1)sum_i a_i(0)>=n(n-1)/2, proving(2).
Continuity of the total coefficient and the same productive sequence give
the neighborhood claim. No own monotonicity, anonymity, or boundedness of
individual coefficients was needed.

The same argument works around any pure standalone-reserve profile
X_i=s_i I, provided normalized weights are continuous there: add epsilon
complementary singular matrices only to i,j. The residuals have the same
pair certificate and G=epsilon, while original owners and their reserves
remain fixed. Thus the issue is continuity of normalized weights through a
zero-gain profile, not specifically a zero-total economy.

CONSEQUENCE AND LIMIT
A rule y_i=f_i+a_i G has exact total S+(sum_i a_i)G. If its normalized
coefficients extend continuously through zero gain as above, it cannot have
a uniform gain factor below n/2. This already applies to the weaker
value-sensitive condition(1); binary productive-coalition weights are not
the source of this particular obstruction.

This DOES NOT prohibit continuous payments. Since G tends to zero, the
products a_i G can be continuous even when bounded normalized weights jump
or depend on the approach direction. Ordinary homogeneous mechanisms often
have exactly that feature. The theorem also does not prohibit profilewise
small coefficients away from zero gain. It is a stopping condition for a
specific smoothing requirement, not an unrestricted funding lower bound.

COMPARISON WITH THE EARLIER CORE-MAJORANT BARRIER
APPROXIMATE_CORE_MAJORANT_BELOW_TWO.txt proves a separate, stronger type of
architecture obstruction: if an own-monotone payment box always contains a
cover funded by beta*T for one fixed beta<2, its funding can be Omega(n).
That obstruction has no continuity requirement. The min-k technology there
embeds EXACTLY into PSD_k as diagonal matrices, so it also obstructs such
majorant architectures in the PSD domain, with no exponential pencil-size
loss. It does not apply merely because y_i=f_i+a_i G is a gain cover: one
must actually establish the existence of the beta-funded subcover in the
payment box. No such assumption is silently imposed here.

Hence a useful gain-sensitive successor must allow direction-dependent
normalized weights at zero gain and, for sublinear owner funding, sometimes
have no beta<2 cover inside its payment box. The existing original-rank rule
already allows discontinuous weights; its payment discontinuities elsewhere
are a separate issue. Neither observation resolves the general PSD gap.
