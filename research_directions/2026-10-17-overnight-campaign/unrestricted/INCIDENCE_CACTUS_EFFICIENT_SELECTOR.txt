EFFICIENT OWN-MONOTONE SELECTION FOR COALITION INCIDENCE CACTI
2026-10-17. Proposed extension of the forest theorem; independent audit pending.

SETUP AND STATEMENT.
Fix a finite 0/1 generator matrix A with nonempty rows. Its bipartite incidence
graph has generator-row vertices, owner vertices, and edges at its 1 entries.
A cactus means every graph edge lies in at most one simple cycle; distinct
cycles may meet at a vertex. Disconnected cacti are allowed. Row sizes, owner
frequencies, numbers of cycles, and owner counts are all unrestricted.

For K(b)={x>=0:Ax>=b}, b>=0, and T(b)=min_K sum x, let e(b) be the unique
minimum-Euclidean-norm point of the efficient face sum x=T(b).

THEOREM. If the incidence graph of A is a cactus, e_i cannot decrease when
an arbitrary subset of incident RHS demands increases. The same own-row
monotonicity holds for projection of ANY fixed target onto K(b), and hence
for every regularized selector min_K [sum x+(epsilon/2)||x||^2].
Thus every fixed adequate cactus generator system admits an efficient,
individually own-row monotone allocation for all nonnegative demands.

CENTRAL DERIVATIVE LEMMA.
Let B be full row rank and have cactus incidence graph. For any source row r,
the minimum-norm solution to Bd=e_r has d_i>=0 for every owner i in r.

PROOF: REDUCTION AT THE SOURCE ROW.
Delete row vertex r from its connected incidence component. Each remaining
component meets the neighbors of r in either one or two owner vertices.
It cannot meet three: its minimal connecting tree would make some edge lie
in two cycles after r is restored. A component with two interface owners u,v
has a unique simple path between them corresponding to its cycle through r;
every component off that path attaches to it at just one vertex. Otherwise
there would again be overlapping cycles. Other cycles are permitted inside
these one-articulation attachments.

All remaining row equations have zero RHS. The component energy is its sum
of squared owner coordinates. It is a positive definite quadratic objective
on a homogeneous linear feasible subspace.

A component with ONE interface value t either forces t=0 or has conditional
minimum energy alpha*t^2, alpha>=1. This is the homogeneous image argument
in INCIDENCE_FOREST_EFFICIENT_SELECTOR.txt: a linear image in R is {0} or R,
and scaling the minimum-norm solution at t=1 gives every t.

We show that a component with TWO interfaces has a similar positive quadratic
cost in their TOTAL h=d_u+d_v, or forces h=0; whenever h>=0 is feasible its
minimum-energy endpoint split has d_u,d_v>=0.

TWO-INTERFACE PATH REDUCTION, INCLUDING ALL PINS.
Write the alternating interface path as owners 0,...,k and intervening rows
1,...,k. At a path owner j, all attached homogeneous modules either force
x_j=0 or add a nonnegative multiple of x_j^2 to the energy. Including the
owner's own square therefore gives alpha_j*x_j^2 with alpha_j>=1, or pins
x_j=0. This uses only a one-dimensional linear-image argument, so the
attachments themselves may contain arbitrarily many cycles.

At path row j, combine all off-path modules into their total contribution s
to that row. Their homogeneous feasible subspace either forces s=0 or has
minimum energy beta_j*s^2 with beta_j>0. A nonzero prescribed sum of finitely
many coordinates has strictly positive minimum squared norm. The row equation
is x_(j-1)+x_j+s=0. Hence this attachment contributes
             beta_j*(x_(j-1)+x_j)^2,
or enforces the hard equality x_(j-1)+x_j=0 if no nonzero s is feasible.
All attachments are disjoint except for their stated articulation vertex,
so these eliminations add their exact conditional energies without cross terms.

Set z_j=(-1)^j*x_j. The reduced energy becomes
     sum_j alpha_j*z_j^2 + sum_(soft edges j) beta_j*(z_(j-1)-z_j)^2,  (1)
with pinned z_j=0 and hard equalities z_(j-1)=z_j. Contract hard equalities.
Any contracted node containing a pin is zero; remove it, adding energies of
its incident soft edges as grounding terms at their other ends. An interior
pin may disconnect the path; the same argument then gives zero off-diagonal
coupling between the endpoint components. Every remaining
node has strictly positive grounding because its alpha coefficients sum to
a positive number. Thus (1) is a strictly grounded weighted path quadratic.

If both endpoints survive as distinct nodes, eliminate all interior nodes.
Its two-variable Schur complement H is positive definite with
                  H_01<=0,  H_00>=-H_01, H_11>=-H_01.       (2)
For completeness, eliminating a grounded node with ground a>=0 and edge
conductances c_j>=0 produces between its neighbors conductances c_j*c_l/D
and adds ground c_j*a/D, where D=a+sum c_j>0. Thus nonnegative grounds and
conductances are preserved. This proves (2), without assuming the interior
weights are equal. Positive definiteness follows from the positive original
grounds and the Schur complement identity.

Returning to original endpoint signs multiplies H by a diagonal sign matrix.
The resulting positive definite 2x2 matrix Q satisfies
                  Q_00>=|Q_01|, Q_11>=|Q_01|.
Consequently
           Q^(-1)*1 = (Q_11-Q_01,Q_00-Q_01)^T/det(Q) >=0.
Under a fixed total h, the unique minimizing split is
        (d_u,d_v)^T = h*Q^(-1)1/(1^T Q^(-1)1),               (3)
which is componentwise nonnegative for h>=0. Its energy is a strictly positive
constant times h^2.

If one endpoint is pinned, the other equals h and the same conclusion holds.
If both are pinned, only h=0 is possible. If both endpoints contracted to the
same unpinned node, their original signs are either equal or opposite. Equal
signs give d_u=d_v=h/2. Opposite signs force h=0, whose minimum-energy solution
is zero throughout that module. These cases also cover hard constraints at
arbitrary positions, including contractions that meet a pin. Thus the asserted
two-interface property holds in every case.

FINISHING THE DERIVATIVE LEMMA.
For every component after deleting r, write h_l for the sum of its one or two
interface values. Each component either forces h_l=0 or has minimum energy
gamma_l*h_l^2, gamma_l>0, with nonnegative interface values when h_l>=0.
The source equation is exactly sum_l h_l=1. Full row rank makes Bd=e_r
feasible, so at least one component has free total. The minimum-energy totals
are h_l=(1/gamma_l)/sum_(free t)(1/gamma_t)>0 on free components, and zero on
pinned-total components. Every neighbor of r therefore has nonnegative
coordinate. This proves the derivative lemma.

ALL-PROFILE AND EFFICIENCY CONSEQUENCE.
The active-face proof from INCIDENCE_FOREST_EFFICIENT_SELECTOR.txt now applies
verbatim: fixed-target projection is continuous and finite piecewise affine
under one-row RHS variation. KKT places target minus optimizer in the active
normal span, so on each face the optimizer is its affine-face projection. The
finitely many faces and their linear feasibility/normal conditions yield finite
affine critical intervals. Restricting to positive owner coordinates and
independent active rows preserves the cactus property. Its derivative is the
minimum-norm solution of Bd=e_r, or zero for an inactive changed row. Hence
every own-coordinate derivative is nonnegative. Continuity joins the pieces,
and sequential changes handle any subset of incident rows.

The targets -(1/epsilon)*1 give the regularized efficient selectors. Comparing
their objectives with e bounds their norms by ||e|| and forces every limit
as epsilon->0 to be efficient with norm at most ||e||. Uniqueness gives their
pointwise convergence to e, preserving each own-monotonicity inequality.

EXACT OBSTRUCTION ON TWO OVERLAPPING CYCLES.
The cactus hypothesis cannot simply be replaced by allowing overlapping
cycles. Take three rows {1,2}, {1,3}, {1,2,3}, whose incidence cycles share
an edge. Demands (1,1,1) have T=1 and unique efficient cover (1,0,0).
Increasing only the third demand to3/2 gives T=3/2 and minimum-norm efficient
cover (1/2,1/2,1/2). This is an own-row increase for owner1, but its payment
falls from1 to1/2. In fact along grand demand t in(1,3/2), the efficient
selector is (2-t,t-1,t-1), with derivative(-1,1,1).

This incidence graph has cycle rank2. It refutes the selector conclusion for
a minimal overlapping-cycle pattern; it does not prove that every noncactus
matrix fails or that any funding subsidy is necessary on this particular
veto instance. MINIMAL_PROJECTION_OWN_MONOTONICITY_FAILURE.txt supplies a
fixed-owner monotone concave homogeneous production realization of these two
profiles. The separate five-cycle-triple12/11 argument in
EFFICIENT_CORE_SELECTOR_INCIDENCE_TWO.txt is a mechanism-independent subsidy
obstruction, with a larger and denser incidence pattern.

SCOPE AND ESTABLISHED INGREDIENTS.
These are COALITION-GENERATOR incidence cacti, not resource-technology cacti.
All-profile adequacy of the fixed generator representation is essential.
The incentive conclusion is individual payment monotonicity, not coalition
total monotonicity or outside-production protection.

ABV2016 Section5.2.2 already studies minimum-norm efficient-core selection.
Grounded quadratic-network elimination, shifted projection sensitivity, and
quadratic regularization are established methods. The proposed contribution
is the cactus incidence condition ensuring own-row monotonicity and efficiency.
Historical priority for that conclusion remains unconfirmed.
