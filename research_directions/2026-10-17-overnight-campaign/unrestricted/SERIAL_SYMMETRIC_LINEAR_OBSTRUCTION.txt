SERIAL PAYMENTS CAN REQUIRE A LINEAR COVERAGE MULTIPLIER
17 October 2026. Exact analytic architecture obstruction; not an unrestricted
mechanism lower bound. Candidate independently derived in lane C.

This disposes of a tempting extension outside the fixed-mask architecture:
clipping every resource coordinate to a shared increasing water level and
sharing each production increment equally among coordinates still increasing.
For symmetric production, the rule is efficient and own-monotone. It need not
cover coalitions, even approximately by a universal constant.

DOMAIN AND RULE
There are n actual fixed owners, owner i reporting one coordinate x_i>=0.
F is symmetric, finite, nonnegative, monotone, concave and degree-one homogeneous.
For distinct positive report levels0=t_0<t_1<...<t_q, put
  c^j_i=min(x_i,t_j),  A_j={i:x_i>=t_j},
  y_i(x)=sum_{j:i in A_j} [F(c^j)-F(c^{j-1})]/|A_j|.
This is the usual increasing serial path formula, specialized to symmetric
multivariate production; historical source attribution remains to be finalized.

CONSTRUCTION
Take integers m>=2,k>=m. Let n=k+m, alpha=1/k^2, and M=k^3.
For z>=0 define
  F(z)=min{ alpha sum_i z_i, sum of the k smallest coordinates of z }.
The second expression is min_{|J|=k}sum_{j in J}z_j, so F is a finite minimum
of fixed nonnegative linear forms. It meets every stated technology hypothesis.

At the profile x=(1 repeated k times, M repeated m times), grand production
is T=k. At the first common level1, F(1,...,1)=alpha n: alpha n<=k because
k>=m>=2. Every owner receives alpha from this initial increment.
The only remaining increment, from common level1 toM on the m large owners,
equals k-alpha n and is shared equally. Hence
  y_small=alpha,
  y_large=alpha+(k-alpha n)/m = k(1-alpha)/m.

Let S contain ALL k small owners and exactly ONE large owner. At its
coalition profile there are m-1 zero coordinates, k ones, and one M.
The sum of its k smallest coordinates is k-m+1, while
alpha(k+M)=k+1/k>=k-m+1. Therefore
  v(S)=k-m+1,
  y(S)=k alpha + k(1-alpha)/m.
Any scalar multiplier making these serial payments cover this coalition
must be at least
  R(k,m)=(k-m+1)/(k alpha+k(1-alpha)/m).

In particular take k=2m, so n=3m. Then
  R(2m,m)=(m+1)/(2+(1-1/m)/(2m))
          =(1/6+o(1))n.
Thus this efficient serial mechanism needs a LINEAR-in-owner-count coverage
multiplier on a fully symmetric finite min-linear class with axis-supported
ownership. Increasing the number of symmetric forms or refining the numerical
serial integration cannot repair the architecture.

SMALL EXACT INSTANCE
m=2,k=4,n=6,alpha=1/16,M=64. Full value4.
Small-owner payment1/16; each large owner payment15/8.
Coalition of four small owners and one large owner has worth3 but receives
4/16+15/8=17/8. Required multiplier>=24/17.

WHY THIS IS NOT AN UNRESTRICTED LOWER BOUND
Other payment rules may depend differently on individual reports. The argument
only evaluates one fixed serial formula. It does not show that fully symmetric
production lacks an efficient truthful covering rule. The preceding conjecture
that serial itself supplies such a rule is withdrawn.

DISCOVERY PROVENANCE
10,000 random exact finite tests found no counterexample; an adversarial LP
over symmetric supporting forms produced one at n=9. Its structure simplified
to the above two-family technology, yielding a diverging analytic obstruction.
This is why random checks were never treated as a proof.
