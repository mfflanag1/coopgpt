RESULT AND THE GATE IT CLOSES
For every1<=p<=infinity, the reserve technology F(t,x)=t-||x||_p with
exposure x in R^2 has sharp uniform-owner TCWM factor3/2 and sharp full
coalition outside-production factor1+c, c[2-exp(-1/c)]=1. This follows from
an explicit fixed linear L1 isometry below and the accepted signed transfer
in ../composition/CONE_L1_EMBEDDABLE_SHARP_THEOREM.txt. Sharpness is inherited

Consequently no unrestricted lower growing with p can be established using
only exposure dimension2. The lower1+(p+1)/8 in
../composition/P_NORM_GROWTH_RADIAL_LINEAR_BARRIER.txt remains valid for its

that growing-dimensional lower or of the original individual-IC theorem.

1. SELF-CONTAINED PLANAR ell_p L1 REPRESENTATION
For1<p<infinity, define
 f(s)=(1+s^p)^(1/p),
 w_p(s)=f''(s)=(p-1)s^(p-2)(1+s^p)^(1/p-2), s>0.
The density is nonnegative. Since f'(0+)=0 and f'(infinity)=1,
                         integral_0^infinity w_p(s) ds=1.
Integration by parts gives
 integral_0^R s w_p(s)ds=R f'(R)-f(R)+1.
Here f(R)-R f'(R)=(1+R^p)^(1/p-1) tends to0, so
                         integral_0^infinity s w_p(s) ds=1.

For a>=0, split at a and use those two antiderivatives:
 integral_0^infinity max(a,s)w_p(s)ds
  =a f'(a)+[f(a)-a f'(a)]=f(a).
By homogeneity, for every real x,y, including y=0,
 ||(x,y)||_p=integral_0^infinity max(|x|,s|y|)w_p(s)ds
  =(1/2)integral_0^infinity [|x+s y|+|x-s y|]w_p(s)ds.       (1)
The last equality is the elementary identity
max(|a|,|b|)=(|a+b|+|a-b|)/2.

Take Omega=(0,infinity)x{+1,-1}, measure dmu=w_p(s)ds/2 on each sign,
and L(x,y)(s,sign)=x+sign*s*y. Equation(1) is precisely a fixed linear
isometry into L1. The finite zeroth and first moments also show each basis
image belongs to L1; therefore the representative and integrability
conditions used in the signed-transfer theorem hold explicitly.

At p=1 use L(x,y)=(x,y) with counting measure. At p=infinity use the two
linear forms x+y and x-y, each with weight1/2. These give the endpoint
identities without taking a limit of mechanisms or report populations.

2. ECONOMIC CONSEQUENCE AND PRECISE INCENTIVES
Actual owner i reports (t_i,x_i) with t_i>=||x_i||_p. Both its retained and
withdrawn bundles must remain in this same cone. Use the fixed L above,
u_i=Lx_i, a_i=u_i^+, b_i=u_i^-, f_i=t_i-||x_i||_p. The already audited rule
is y_i=kappa*f_i+2 integral pi_i(a,b)dmu, using the sharp two-input rule
with kappa=3/2 for TCWM or kappa=1+c for full OUT. The signed compensation
lemma, not monotonicity of the nonlinear positive-negative map, proves
the incentive statement. All owners remain actual fixed owners.
Coalition production has the exact identity
 F(sum_S b_i)=sum_S f_i+2 integral min(sum_S a_i,sum_S b_i)dmu.
Thus its coverage and funding proofs apply without approximation.
The constants are uniform in p and owner count for this fixed exposure
dimension. This does not imply any similar upper for ell_p in all dimensions.

3. THE COMMON-PRICE REDUCTION CANNOT BE SILENTLY EXTENDED TO THIS CONE
The L1 rule above is owner-aware. Its scalar aggregates
A(omega)=sum_i u_i^+, B(omega)=sum_i u_i^-
depend on the ownership decomposition, not merely on sum_i x_i. Its
standalone reserve terms also depend on individual owner bundles. It
therefore need not be expressible as a common linear price depending only
on the aggregate (T,X).

The frozen necessary price reduction in
../energy/COALITION_PRICE_REDUCTION_v1_INTERIOR_FROZEN.txt is for a FINITE
NONNEGATIVE ORTHANT of resource coordinates. If one chooses finitely many
cone generators and applies that theorem to their nonnegative counts, the
resulting prices may depend on the generator-count representation. Distinct
count vectors can have the same aggregate cone bundle. Descending those
prices through the linear relations to a common cone price would require
a new representation-invariance theorem. None is supplied by the count
dilation proof. The same issue arises on taking a dense set of generators.

Thus the planar constant upper rules out an unrestricted growing-p lower,
but does NOT alone refute a lower bound for every aggregate-only common
cone-price field. Nor does it establish that a constant common cone-price
field exists. These are distinct questions. The support-radial lower also
does not cover arbitrary reserve-coefficient or nonradial common fields.

4. WHY SIGNED/PERMUTATION SYMMETRY DOES NOT FIX ANGULAR DERIVATIVES
Even at exposure dimension2, equivariance only fixes a field's value at
equal-coordinate rays, not its transverse derivative. For example the
polynomial perturbation
 H(x1,x2)=(x1(x1^2-x2^2), x2(x2^2-x1^2))
is equivariant under coordinate permutations and independent sign changes,
and vanishes whenever |x1|=|x2|. At (a,a), however,
                   DH(a,a)(1,-1)=4a^2(1,-1).
Multiplying it by any symmetric smooth radial cutoff preserves equivariance
and changes the transverse derivative while leaving the diagonal values
unchanged. This algebraic observation is NOT a claim that the perturbation
preserves TCWM. It pinpoints the missing implication in an attempted
symmetry-only extension of the radial theorem; incentive constraints would
have to control these derivatives separately.
