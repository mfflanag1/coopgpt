1. FINITE TCWM LOWER: SYMMETRY IS DERIVED, NOT ASSUMED
For fixed r>=2,s>=2 choose n=(r-1)s+1 fully eligible owners, each with the
same public unit upper bound in every resource. This domain is invariant under
owner and resource permutations. Finite averaging preserves every covering,
funding and TCWM inequality, so proving impossibility for the averaged rule
does not assume anonymity of the original rule. No report comparison splits,
merges, adds or deletes an actual owner.

At P0, the abundant groups contain s owners each, the last two resource groups
one each, and s-1 owners report zero. Write their payments a,b,w respectively,
with w>=0. Coverage and budget imply
 (r-2)a+2b>=1,
 (r-2)s a+2b+(s-1)w<=kappa,
 hence 2b>=1-(kappa-1)/(s-1).
Activating the zero owners in one scarce group gives P1. Protect the coalition
containing the old owner AND those initially zero owners. Its payment cannot
fall, and symmetry among the now equally sized first r-1 groups gives total
payment to their union C at least(r-1)b. At P2 every C owner restores all of
the first r-1 resources. C is unchanged as a set of actual owners.
With m=(r-1)s equal C owners, coverage of one such owner plus the unchanged
last-resource owner yields p+q>=1, while mp+q<=kappa. Thus
 y_C(P2)<=m(kappa-1)/(m-1).
Combining gives precisely
 kappa>=1+(s-1)((r-1)s-1)/(2s^2+(r-3)s-1).
All denominators are positive, and for each fixed r the limit is(r+1)/2.
The r=2 case works with the absent abundant-group terms set to zero.
Crucially, zero reporters were NOT assumed to receive zero at positive output.
This direct finite proof does not depend on the price-reduction theorem.

2. FROM ARBITRARY RULES TO COMPATIBLE COUNT ARRAYS
For each N, use N pure owners per resource, each with fixed unit capacity.
Average only within identically eligible roles. At a binary profile m, all
active owners of type j then receive pi_j^(N)(m). For0<=u<=r<=m,
 m.pi(m)<=kappa F(m), r.pi(m)>=F(r),
 r.pi(m)>=(r-u).pi(m-u)+F(u)                           [OUT]
are necessary. The last coalition contains r owners of the respective types;
u of them completely withhold and the remaining members stay unchanged.
Newly inactive payments occur on the right and are nonnegative, so dropping
them weakens the constraint in the correct direction. The budget similarly
drops nonnegative inactive payments. No null-payment assumption is introduced.

For m_j>0, 0<=pi_j^(N)(m)<=kappa F(m)/m_j independently of N. Each finite
collection of count constraints is available once N exceeds its largest count.
A countable diagonal subsequence therefore gives one compatible global array.
The product is compact with coordinate-dependent bounds; a single uniform
bound over all m is unnecessary. No regularity of any original rule is used.

3. DILATION AVERAGING AND THE ROLE OF GROWING OWNER COUNTS
T_k pi(m)=pi(km) preserves each constraint by multiplying all integer counts
by k and dividing by k, using F(ku)=kF(u). The T_k commute. Averaging over
products of the first L primes with exponents0,...,L-1 leaves, for any fixed
prime, a boundary error at most the coordinate bound divided by L. A common
subsequence is therefore invariant under every integer dilation.
Rational prices p(c)=pi(Lc) are independent of the common denominator L,
and rational aggregate/holding/withholding inequalities follow exactly.

This step uses arbitrarily large EXOGENOUS populations across different finite
models. It does not authorize strategic cloning within any model. It is valid
for the uniform-owner theorem because all scaled coalitions are themselves
covered by its hypotheses. Individual IC alone would not preserve these
constraints under dilation. Finite sums and countable limits, not integration
of the original mechanism, suffice; no original measurability is hidden.

4. REGULARITY AND ZERO FACES
For rational c,c'>0, let lambda=min_j c_j/c'_j and d=c-lambda c'. A coalition
owning all of coordinate v at low aggregate lambda c', then restoring d, gives
 p_v(c')-p_v(c)<=d.p(c)/(lambda c'_v).
The reversed comparison gives the other sign. On[a,b]^d, bounded funding and
nonnegative prices bound the numerator by a constant times||c-c'||_infinity,
while lambda c'_v>=a^2/b. This proves local Lipschitz regularity; it is a
CONCLUSION of the finite inequalities, not an imposed mechanism assumption.

The same ONE count array already contains every zero pattern. Repeat this
argument on each support face. For a real comparison write the outside,
retained and withheld vectors o,r,d separately and approximate each by rational
vectors with exactly its zero pattern. Both aggregate support faces then stay
fixed. Relative-face continuity passes
 (r+d).p(o+r+d)>=r.p(o+r)+F(d)
to the limit. At a zero aggregate all components vanish; on a zero-value face
nonnegativity and budget force active prices to vanish. No continuity ACROSS
different faces and no separate incompatible face selections are assumed.
The reduction assumes continuous F, which the min_d lower witness satisfies.
The universal upper's broader boundary-discontinuous class therefore causes
no mismatch in the sharpness argument.

5. REMOVING ABUNDANT BOTTLENECKS: THE SHIFTED LIMIT IS JUSTIFIED
For symmetric prices at(A,M,...,M), write q for the scarce price and b for
each long price. Coverage gives q+(r-1)b>=1. Subtracting its A multiple from
the budget gives Mb<=D A M/(M-A), D=(kappa-1)/(r-1).
Restoring the entire second-resource holding from B to M transports this to
 B p2(A,B,M,...)<=D A M/(M-A), A<=B<=M.
Permutation averaging of the constructed price field preserves the complete
contract; it is not a symmetry axiom for the original mechanism.

A diagonal subsequence over rational(A,B) yields pair prices. Extra holdings
of min(a,b) per discarded resource contribute at most min(a,b)kappa min(A,B)/M,
which tends to zero, so pair coverage survives. For outside production give
the protected coalition w=min(u,v) in every extra resource and withdraw all
of it. The high and low extra totals are M and M-w. Pointwise convergence
alone would NOT justify this step. The proved relative comparison instead
bounds each relevant price difference by constants times w/M and w/(M-w).
Hence the shifted low-profile limit equals the previously extracted pair limit.
Zero-valued low pairs contribute zero directly. The limit retains full OUT
and long expenditure B P2(A,B)<=D A. A second integer-dilation average retains
this cap and supplies homogeneous pair prices; homogeneity is not incorrectly
inferred from the first subsequence alone.

6. THE SCALAR OBSTRUCTION, INCLUDING ENDPOINTS
At A>=B>0 put t=B/A, with long/short prices p(t),q(t). Local Lipschitz
regularity permits almost-everywhere differentiation and integration on compact
subintervals. Finite TCWM gives p-tp'>=0 and q+tq'>=0. Thus p(t)/t decreases
with t and has finite limit C0<=D as t decreases to zero. Budget bounds q.
The full OUT comparison that restores(delta,delta) to a coalition whose low
holding is(0,B) is feasible: its high holding is(delta,B+delta), with the
same outsiders. It gives p+q+t(1-t)q'>=1. A zero initial long holding is not
an infeasible withdrawal; the comparison is oriented from low to high.

Using p<=C0 t and integrating yields
 q(t)>=1-C0-C0(1-t)log(1-t)/t.
The lower endpoint term vanishes because q is bounded. Also tq(t) is
nondecreasing and symmetry plus continuity at the positive balanced profile
gives q(1)=p(1)<=C0. Therefore
 C0>=max_{0<t<1}[(1-C0)t-C0(1-t)log(1-t)]
    =1-C0+C0 exp(-1/C0).
If C0=0 the preceding inequalities already contradict q(1)=0; otherwise
C0(2-exp(-1/C0))>=1, whose left side is strictly increasing. Hence C0>=c,
D>=c and kappa>=1+c(r-1). No floating root/log comparison is used.
For r=2, extending the long coordinate to M and letting M grow gives the cap
directly; no abundant-resource subsequence is needed.

7. FINITE-OWNER AND INFIMUM QUANTIFIERS
For any kappa below the claimed constant, assuming a kappa-funded rule for
every finite pure-owner model produces the impossible price field above.
Therefore at least one FINITE model has no such rule. This is the compactness
contrapositive, not an infinite-owner counterexample. If the supremum over
finite models of their infimal feasible factors were smaller than the claimed
constant, choose kappa strictly between them; every model would admit such a
rule, yielding the same contradiction. Attainment of the individual infima
is unnecessary. The proof supplies no useful finite-owner threshold for r>=3.

8. HEADLINE UPPER/LOWER MATCH AND CLAIM LIMITS
The actual TCWM upper proof uses nonnegative face integrals: each coalition
coordinate payment is an increasing share times an increasing face value.
Coverage follows by averaging homogeneous supporting gradients, with a
nonnegative endpoint term dropped; Jensen gives(d+1)/2. The full-OUT upper's
holding-corner argument, scalar hinge inequality, diagonal smoothing and
finite monotonicity across newly activated faces were checked against the
stated model. The minimum technology is a valid continuous member of the
universal class, so its lower matches each upper. For d=1, coverage gives
factor>=1 and linear payments give equality.

Load-bearing assumptions already present in the statements are nonnegative
payments, protection of coalitions that may include unchanged members, all
feasible report comparisons within each fixed model, and a factor uniform over
finite owner populations. Neither proof should be relabeled individual IC,
fixed-n sharpness, arbitrary-cone price reduction, historical novelty or formal
proof-assistant verification. No additional assumptions or proof repairs are
needed for the stated orthant headline.
