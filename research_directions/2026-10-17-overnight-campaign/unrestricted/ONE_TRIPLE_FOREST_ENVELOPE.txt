ONE TRIPLE ROW ADDED TO A FOREST: EXACT NEGATIVE-SENSITIVITY LOCALITY
2026-10-17. Structural result for a fixed generating system.
This is NOT an improved general funding theorem: grand marginals already
fund fixed rank-three generating systems by3. The consequence below bounds
this particular minimum-norm envelope by4, while locating every possible
own-monotonicity failure in arbitrarily large systems.

SETUP.
Let G be any forest on n coordinates. Allowed generator rows are its edges,
any singleton rows, and ONE specified triple H={h1,h2,h3}. Let q(b) be the
minimum Euclidean-norm nonnegative cover of the nonnegative demands b.
Let T=min sum x over this full cover polyhedron. Define M_i(b) as the
supremum of q_i over all reductions of generator demands on rows containing
i, with every other generator demand unchanged.

THEOREM.
(A) If the three vertices of H are not all in one component of G, or they
have the same bipartition parity there, then q is own-monotone and M=q.
(B) Otherwise there is a unique minority-parity vertex h in H. Let v be the
median of the three H vertices (the common intersection of their three
pairwise paths). Write the path from v to h as v0=v,v1,...,vL=h.
Every owner outside this path is own-monotone. On the path, the ONLY own row
whose increase can decrease q_vi is
 R_vi={vi,v_(i+1)} for i<L, and R_h=H.
Every negative derivative is exactly -1. Consequently
 M_i <= max{q_i(b), b_(R_i)} on the path, and M_i=q_i(b) off it.
(C) Uniformly in n and in the length of the exceptional path,
 sum_i M_i <=4T.
Singleton rows never cause negative own sensitivity. All conclusions include
zero capacities, degeneracy and active-face seams.

ACTIVE-FACE DERIVATIVES.
Quadratic projection is continuous and finite piecewise affine in one RHS
coordinate. On each open piece choose independent active equalities Bx=c;
its derivative is the minimum-norm solution of the differentiated equalities.
If H is absent from B, the rank-two lemma gives nonnegative own derivatives.
If H is present, the remaining basis rows form forest components with at
most one singleton anchor each. Extra active nonnegativity rows are anchors.

First change an edge ij. An anchored component has its response fixed by
its tree equations and anchor: the two changed-edge endpoint responses are
0 and1. Suppose instead its component is unanchored, and remove ij into
parts A and B. Orient alternating signs from i in A and from j in B.
Write endpoint responses alpha,beta, so alpha+beta=1. Let a,b be the signed
numbers of H vertices in these two parts. Other unanchored components have
responses gamma_l, sizes n_l, and signed H counts h_l; anchored components
have zero response. The H equation is
 a*alpha+b*beta+sum_l h_l*gamma_l=0.
The response minimizes
 |A|alpha^2+|B|beta^2+sum_l n_l*gamma_l^2.
Put C=sum_l h_l^2/n_l. When C>0, elimination gives
 alpha=[|B|-b(a-b)/C]/[|A|+|B|+(a-b)^2/C].
For alpha<0 one needs a,b of the same sign with |a|>|b|>=1. This would
already require at least three H vertices in A union B, forcing C=0,
a contradiction. The same argument gives beta>=0.
When C=0, independence forces a!=b and
 alpha=-b/(a-b), beta=a/(a-b).
A negative alpha requires (a,b)=(2,1) or(-2,-1), and then alpha=-1,beta=2.
Thus all three H vertices lie in this active tree, two same-parity vertices
on i's side and the minority on j's side. In the underlying fixed forest,
these are exactly the edges of the path from v to h, and i is the endpoint
closer to v. No other edge can have a negative own derivative.

Now change H itself. In an unanchored component C choose signs s_j in{+1,-1}
and put h_C=sum_(j in H intersect C)s_j. Its response at coordinate i is
 s_i*(h_C/|C|) / sum_D(h_D^2/|D|).
For i in H this is negative only if the same component contains the other
two H vertices with parity opposite i. All three vertices then lie in this
component, h_C=-s_i, and the derivative is exactly-1. Anchored coordinates
have derivative0. A changed own singleton has derivative1 when active and0
when inactive. This proves the asserted exhaustive negative-derivative list.
Continuity across finite seams extends it to arbitrary demand changes.

THE COORDINATE CAP.
For a path owner i, every own-demand change except R_i is nondecreasing in
the increasing direction. Its decrease therefore cannot raise q_i. During
a decrease of R_i, any interval on which q_i rises must have that row active,
so q_i<=q(R_i)=current b_(R_i)<=full b_(R_i). Hence no sequence of own-row
decreases can raise q_i above max{q_i(full),full b_(R_i)}. Equivalently one
may first lower all other own rows, then R_i. This proves the cap in(B).

FUNDING CONSEQUENCE.
If(A) applies, M=q and the routine maximum-row-cardinality bound gives3T.
In case(B), let K contain the path vertices i for which q_i<=b_(R_i).
We pay at most b_(R_i) on K and q_i on its complement. If a is any full
efficient nonnegative L1 cover, then
 sum_(i in K)b_(R_i)
 <=sum_(e on the v-to-h path)b_e+b_H
 <=sum_(e on that path)a(e)+a(H) <=2 sum_j a_j=2T.
The last incidence count is crucial: interior path vertices occur twice;
the minority endpoint occurs once in the path and once in H; the median
occurs once in the path and possibly once in H; other H vertices occur once.
For a path of length0, the sum is just b_H<=T.

For the remaining raw q coordinates, KKT gives q=A^T lambda, lambda>=0,
with positive lambda only on tight rows. Define omega_S=lambda_S/q(S) for
positive multipliers, zero otherwise. Its vertex degrees are at most1,
so L1-cover duality gives sum_S lambda_S<=T. (A positive multiplier has
q(S)>=|S|lambda_S>0.) Every pair or singleton row has at most two vertices
outside K. If h belongs to K, the triple also has at most two outside K.
If h does not belong to K, q_h>b_H, so H is strictly slack and lambda_H=0.
In either case,
 sum_(i notin K) q_i=sum_S |S outside K|lambda_S<=2T.
Adding the two estimates proves4T. This constant is not claimed sharp.

ARBITRARILY MANY FAILING OWNERS, DESPITE ONE TRIPLE.
Take a hub v, attach two paths of length2 ending at the two majority H
vertices, and one path of any odd length L ending at the minority H vertex.
The edge rows plus H form an invertible n by n matrix B (the triple's
alternating signed sum is1). Set all multipliers to1, q=B^T 1, b=Bq.
All primal coordinates and dual multipliers are strictly positive. For each
of the L edges on the minority arm, its inner endpoint has derivative-1.
A sufficiently small decrease of that owner's incident edge therefore
strictly raises its projection payment. This gives L distinct failing
owners as L grows, not merely a bounded counterexample. It rules out the
idea that only the three owners IN the exceptional row need envelope repair.

SCOPE.
The generating system must be fixed throughout the local demand array.
Previously redundant coalition rows cannot be discarded if the intended
array keeps them independently fixed. Balanced closure can realize selected
fixed-generator snapshots by one production function, but this by itself
does not prove that EVERY other report of that production function remains
in the same generating class. No unrestricted all-profile bound follows.
