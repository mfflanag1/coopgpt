AN EFFICIENT INDIVIDUALLY MONOTONE CORE SELECTOR UNDER EITHER INCIDENCE-TWO CONDITION
2026-10-17. Proposed structural theorem; independent audit and priority check pending.

THEOREM.
Fix a finite 0/1 matrix A with nonempty rows. For every b>=0 write
 K(b)={x>=0:Ax>=b},  T(b)=min_(x in K(b))sum_i x_i.
Let e(b) be the UNIQUE minimum-Euclidean-norm point of the efficient face
 {x in K(b):sum_i x_i=T(b)}.
Suppose either:
 (R) every ROW of A has at most two1s; or
 (C) every COLUMN of A has at most two1s.
Then e_i(b) is nondecreasing whenever only RHS entries of rows containing
owner i increase. The result holds for arbitrary nonnegative real demands,
zero demands, degeneracies, and simultaneous changes to several incident rows.
Thus e is an efficient coalition-covering, INDIVIDUALLY own-row monotone
allocation on every local monotone report array retaining this fixed generator
matrix. Its funding factor is exactly1. Counts of owners and rows are unbounded.
There is NO claim here of coalition-total monotonicity or outside-production IC.

REGULARIZED SELECTORS.
For epsilon>0 define x^epsilon(b) as the unique minimizer on K(b) of
             f_epsilon(x)=sum_i x_i+(epsilon/2)||x||^2.
Equivalently it is the Euclidean projection of the fixed vector
             c_epsilon=-(1/epsilon)*1
onto K(b). We first prove that each x^epsilon is individually own-row monotone.
The projection target changes with epsilon; it does not depend on b.

CONTINUITY AND FINITE AFFINE PIECES.
Fix epsilon and change one RHS row along a bounded interval. Feasibility is
never an issue, since a sufficiently large nonnegative constant vector is
feasible. If the demands change by at most delta, translating any feasible
point by delta*1 restores feasibility, because rows are nonempty. The coercive
strictly convex objective then bounds minimizers on compact parameter intervals.
Feasible recovery points and compactness show that every subsequential limit
is the unique minimizer at the limit demand. Hence x^epsilon is continuous.

There are finitely many active faces of this fixed polyhedron family. On an
open piece with fixed zero-coordinate set and active affine face, restrict to
the positive coordinate set J. Choose independent active cover rows B on J
spanning the remaining affine equalities. Then
 x_J=c_J+B^T(BB^T)^(-1)(b_B-Bc_J), and x_(outside J)=0.       (1)
This follows from perpendicularity of x_J-c_J to the face's tangent space.
The finitely many candidate faces have polyhedral parameter-feasibility and
normal-cone conditions, so they yield a finite piecewise-affine subdivision.
Zero multipliers and dependent active constraints are covered by selecting
an independent active basis on each open piece.

An inactive changed row has derivative0 on that piece. If the changed row
is active, it can be chosen in the independent basis. A dependent changed
equality cannot stay active on an open interval while every other RHS remains
fixed. A coordinate i outside J has derivative0. Thus it remains to check the
response for a positive owner i in an active changed row r:
                   d x_J/d b_r=B^T(BB^T)^(-1)e_r.           (2)
The fixed shift c_J has disappeared. Restricting columns to J preserves both
hypotheses (R) and(C), including the conversion of some pair rows to singleton
rows. Empty restricted rows cannot be members of an independent basis.

PROOF UNDER(R).
The active-basis derivative proof in RANK_TWO_PROJECTION_MONOTONICITY.txt
applies directly to(2). For clarity, pair rows form a graph and singleton
rows are anchors. An independent connected component is an unanchored tree,
a tree with one anchor, or an odd unicyclic graph with no anchor. An own
singleton derivative is1. For a changed pair ij, deleting its edge gives
endpoint response b/(a+b)>0 in an unanchored tree with split sizes a,b;
responses0 or1 in an anchored tree or a branch off an odd cycle; and1/2
at both endpoints of an odd cycle edge. Other components contribute0.
All own derivatives are nonnegative. This calculation concerns the derivative
of the fixed-target projection and does not require target0.

PROOF UNDER(C).
Put G=BB^T. It is symmetric positive definite, nonnegative entrywise, and
G_rr>=sum_(s!=r)G_rs, because a column contributes to at most one off-diagonal
intersection for every row containing it. For w=G^(-1)e_r the maximum principle
proved in COLUMN_TWO_PROJECTION_MONOTONICITY.txt gives |w_s|<=w_r and w_r>0.
In brief, a maximum absolute entry at a nonsource forces equality in diagonal
dominance and opposite signs of equal size at all neighbors. Propagation either
reaches the positive source, or forms a closed alternating null component,
contradicting positive definiteness. A positive own coordinate has either
one basis incidence r, with derivative w_r, or two basis incidences r,s, with derivative
w_r+w_s>=0. Hence(2) has nonnegative own response under(C) also.

Combining the nonnegative derivatives with continuity proves one-row own
monotonicity of x^epsilon. Increase several incident demands sequentially to
obtain the full own-row statement. This does not assert monotonicity of sums
of arbitrary owners' payments.

PASSAGE TO AN EXACTLY EFFICIENT SELECTOR.
The efficient face is nonempty and compact: x>=0 and sum x=T(b). Its strictly
convex quadratic minimizer e(b) therefore exists and is unique. Optimality of
x^epsilon against e gives
 sum_i x_i^epsilon+(epsilon/2)||x^epsilon||^2
       <=T(b)+(epsilon/2)||e(b)||^2.
Since sum_i x_i^epsilon>=T(b), we get ||x^epsilon||<=||e(b)||. The family is
bounded. Any accumulation point as epsilon tends to0 belongs to K(b) and has
sum at most T(b), hence lies on the efficient face; it also has norm at most
||e(b)|| and so equals e(b) by uniqueness. Thus the ENTIRE family converges
pointwise to e(b). For any fixed pair of own-comparable demand profiles, the
inequality x_i^epsilon(b_low)<=x_i^epsilon(b_high) holds for every epsilon>0.
Taking limits proves the claimed exact efficient own monotonicity of e.

WHAT THIS DOES AND DOES NOT SAY ABOUT PRODUCTION.
The generator matrix must remain sufficient at EVERY report in the claimed
domain, and an owner may change only its incident generator demands, in the
monotone direction. A rank-two or frequency-two representation solely at a
full profile does not justify dropping larger coalition inequalities that can
become binding later. This is a theorem about fixed local cover systems;
production instantiations require verifying that hypothesis separately.

There is no contradiction with the sharp3/2 funding result for weighted/unit
resource GRAPH technologies with actual mixed owners: a graph of productive
resources is not the same object as a fixed graph of two-owner COALITION
constraints, and mixed ownership need not preserve either incidence condition.
Moreover the3/2 theorem is formulated with the stronger coalition-total
incentive requirement, whereas the theorem here asserts individual monotonicity.
There is also no contradiction with the general efficient-core-envelope linear
obstruction, whose growing coalition generators lie outside these restrictions.

ESTABLISHED INGREDIENTS AND PRIORITY.
Strictly convex tie-breaking of the efficient core, active-face sensitivity,
and the regularization limit are established methods. The proposed contribution
is the incidence-two monotonicity conclusion and its efficient fixed-generator
mechanism consequence. No historical novelty confirmation is claimed. Existing
minimum-norm efficient-core literature, including ABV2016, must be compared
before presenting the selector itself as new.

FAILURE BEYOND INCIDENCE TWO: FREQUENCY THREE AND ARITY THREE CAN REQUIRE SUBSIDY.
Five owners and five cyclic triple rows S_r={r,r+1,r+2} (indices modulo5)
already rule out an efficient individually monotone mechanism on a finite
local report star. At the high profile every row demand is3; its minimum
L1 value is5, attained by the all-ones vector. For low profile i reduce only
row(i-1), which contains owner i, to3/2. Its minimum L1 value is9/2, attained
by the vector that has3/2 on owners i,i+2,i+3 and0 on i-1,i+1.
Summing all five row inequalities and dividing by3 proves both optimum values.

Suppose a coalition-covering, own-monotone rule funds every displayed profile
within kappa times its minimum L1 value. At low profile i, the two unchanged
triples S_i and S_(i+3) intersect only at i and together include every owner.
Their inequalities imply
           y_i(low_i)+sum_j y_j(low_i)>=6.
Hence y_i(low_i)>=6-(9/2)kappa. Own monotonicity implies the same lower bound
for y_i(high). Summing over all five owners and using the high-profile budget
5kappa gives
              5kappa>=5[6-(9/2)kappa], so kappa>=12/11.
The argument uses no symmetry assumption on the mechanism. Both row arity
and column frequency are exactly3. It shows that neither incidence-two
hypothesis can simply be raised to3 while retaining an efficiency theorem.
The12/11 value is an exact rational lower certificate, not a claim here of
the optimal funding factor for this finite family. It is not an improvement
of the general unrestricted subsidy lower bound.

At kappa=1 one can also see the failure directly from unique efficient covers:
all rows must be tight because the uniform positive row dual1/3 is optimal.
The cyclic triple matrix is invertible (subtract consecutive homogeneous
row equations to obtain x_r=x_(r+3); coprimality of3 and5 then forces all
coordinates equal and hence0). Thus full owner i gets1 while low owner i
gets3/2. This is a selector-independent efficiency obstruction. The accepted
balanced closure/Boolean realization lemmas embed the finite report star
into one fixed-owner monotone concave homogeneous production model.
