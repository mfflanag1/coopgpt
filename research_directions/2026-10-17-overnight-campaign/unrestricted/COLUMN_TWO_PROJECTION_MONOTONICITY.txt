AT MOST TWO GENERATOR INCIDENCES PER OWNER: RAW PROJECTION IS OWN-MONOTONE
2026-10-17. A geometric companion to the row-arity-two theorem.

Let A be any finite 0/1 matrix with nonempty rows, and assume each COLUMN
has at most two1s. Row cardinalities, owner count and row count may grow.
For b>=0 let q(b) be the minimum-Euclidean-norm cover of Ax>=b, x>=0.
For every owner i, increasing any collection of RHS entries on rows containing
i cannot decrease q_i. Equivalently, its own-row downward envelope equals q_i.
Thus the uniform-base amplification target has E=||q||^2=rho on this class;
it cannot violate E<=2rho. In fact the original diagonal bound holds directly,
since the minimum-norm cover has norm at most that of any efficient L1 cover.
All assertions allow arbitrary nonnegative real RHS values and arbitrary
continuous simultaneous changes in the incident demands.

NONNEGATIVITY AND ACTIVE BASES.
First project onto Ax>=b without explicit coordinate bounds. KKT stationarity
is q=A^T lambda for lambda>=0, so q>=0 automatically; the two projections
coincide. Thus no signed nonnegativity anchor rows need to be inserted.
Along a one-row RHS change, the projection is continuous and finite piecewise
affine. On each open affine piece, choose independent active rows B spanning
its active face. The minimum-norm formula is q=B^T(BB^T)^(-1)b_B. If a changed
row is inactive throughout that piece, its derivative is0; if active, it can
be included in the independent basis. A dependent changed equality cannot
remain active over an open interval with every other RHS fixed. Degenerate
positive/zero multipliers do not affect the finite active-face argument.
The remaining argument is purely a matrix calculation for any such B.

GRAM MAXIMUM PRINCIPLE.
G=BB^T is positive definite, with nonnegative entries. Since every column
of B has at most two1s,
 G_rr >= sum_(s!=r) G_rs.
Indeed G_rr counts columns incident to r; each such column contributes to
at most one off-diagonal intersection. Fix a source row r and put
 w=G^(-1)e_r. Then
                        |w_s|<=w_r for every s.             (1)
Here w_r=e_r^T G^(-1)e_r>0. For completeness, at any nonsource s,
 G_ss*w_s=-sum_(t!=s)G_st*w_t.
If |w_s| attains a positive global maximum, diagonal dominance forces either
a contradiction or equality in every triangle inequality: the row must be
weakly, not strictly, diagonally dominant, and every adjacent t has equal
absolute value and sign opposite to w_s. Propagate across positive off-diagonal
entries. If the source is reached, its absolute value is the maximum, proving(1).
If it is not reached, the propagation gives a closed component with no strict
diagonal dominance and alternating signs. The vector with those signs on the
component and0 elsewhere is a nonzero kernel vector of G, contradicting positive
definiteness. Components not containing the source have w=0 directly. This
proves(1) in every case.

OWN DERIVATIVE.
Suppose row r contains owner i and changes at unit speed. If it is active,
let w above be its multiplier response. Column i of B has either one active
incidence r or two incidences r,s. The coordinate response is respectively
 w_r>0, or w_r+w_s>=0 by(1).
If r is inactive the response is0. Hence every affine segment has nonnegative
own derivative. Continuity across the finite seams proves monotonicity for
one row; changing the finitely many incident demands sequentially proves the
stated simultaneous result.

FUNDING CONSEQUENCE, WITH THE ROUTINE BASELINE MADE EXPLICIT.
This is NOT a new universal funding bound. At any profile, each positive q_i
has a positive incident KKT multiplier, hence touches a tight row. Therefore
 sum_i q_i <=sum_(tight rows r)q(S_r)=sum_(tight rows r)b_r
             <=sum_r b_r<=2T,
where T is the minimum L1 cover value and the last inequality follows by
summing all row constraints at an efficient cover: each owner occurs at most
twice. The raw rule is truthful on every local monotone report array retaining
this fixed generating matrix. The2T factor is asymptotically sharp for the raw
rule: two unit-demand rows share one owner and each has k private owners.
The full projection pays2/(k+2) to the shared owner and1/(k+2) to each private
owner, giving total2(k+1)/(k+2) against T=1.

A simpler per-row equal-share rule also proves the routine factor2: pay each
owner the sum over its incident rows of b_r/|S_r|. Every row is covered by its
own equal-share component, incentives are immediate, and total funding is
sum_r b_r<=2T. The specific contribution here is monotonicity of the RAW norm
projection, and its resulting exact diagonal identity E=rho. No historical
novelty claim is made pending original-source comparison.

RELATION TO PREVIOUS STRUCTURE AND PRODUCTION SCOPE.
RANK_TWO_PROJECTION_MONOTONICITY.txt assumes at most two OWNERS per row.
This result instead assumes at most two ROWS per owner. Neither assumption
implies the other; both give unbounded classes with exact raw monotonicity.
For production models, the same fixed generators must remain sufficient at
every permitted report. A single full-profile representation with column
frequency two is insufficient if previously redundant coalition rows can
become binding after a report change. The general all-profile envelope
conjecture remains open.
