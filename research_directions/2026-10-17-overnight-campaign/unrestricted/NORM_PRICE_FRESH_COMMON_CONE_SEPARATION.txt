A CONCRETE FAILURE OF SAME-FACTOR COMMON-PRICE REDUCTION ON CONES
17 October2026. Exact architectural lower; no historical novelty assertion.

THEOREM
Let K={(t,x,y):t>=max(|x|,|y|)} and F(t,x,y)=t-max(|x|,|y|).
An aggregate-linear rule has payments
                        y_i(b)=p(C) dot b_i, C=sum_i b_i.
The price field may be arbitrary: no regularity, homogeneity, differentiability,
anonymity of a more general mechanism, or reserve-coordinate normalization is
assumed. If this rule covers every coalition at every profile and is individually
monotone for every cone-feasible own restoration, its uniform funding factor is
at least2. It suffices to require these properties for TWO fixed actual owners,
both allowed reports0<=_K b_i<=_K(4,0,0). No owners are created or merged.

In contrast, in the SAME two-owner model an elementary owner-aware rule is
exactly efficient and satisfies full coalition outside-production incentives:
        y_1=[F(b_1+b_2)+F(b_1)-F(b_2)]/2,
        y_2=[F(b_1+b_2)+F(b_2)-F(b_1)]/2.
For every nonnegative superadditive F, its total is F(b_1+b_2), and
superadditivity implies y_i>=F(b_i)>=0, so every coalition is covered.
Restoring owner i by D_i increases both F(b_1+b_2) and F(b_i) by at least
F(D_i), giving Delta y_i>=F(D_i). Restoring both owners increases the total
by at least F(D_1+D_2). These are all nonempty coalitions for two owners.
This is the elementary two-player Shapley/core allocation, not a novelty claim.

Separately, the accepted L1 signed-transfer theorem provides an owner-aware
TCWM rule with total<=(3/2)F on every profile (funding factor3/2), and a full-OUT
rule with factor1+c<2, uniformly over arbitrary actual owner counts. Here
                        ||(x,y)||_infinity=(|x+y|+|x-y|)/2.
Thus a same-factor reduction from unrestricted cone mechanisms to common
aggregate cone prices is FALSE, even for two owners and a fixed planar
polyhedral norm cone: the respective factors are1 and at least2.
This does not contradict the valid finite-orthant price reduction.

The price lower uses only singleton monotonicity, so also applies to TCWM
and full coalition OUT. Both owner-aware comparisons satisfy stronger
incentive contracts. The two-owner factor1 and uniform-owner factors are
separate statements.
No matching common-price upper2 is asserted in this note.

1. FINITE SYMMETRY AVERAGING AND A NECESSARY RESERVE PRICE
Average a purported price field over the eight signed coordinate permutations
of(x,y), keeping t fixed. For each orthogonal symmetry g the transformed field
is p_g(C)=g^T p(gC), and p_g(C) dot b_i=p(gC) dot(g b_i).
These symmetries preserve K,F, the two owners' public bounds, every feasible
restoration and every coalition. Averaging preserves coverage, nonnegativity,
individual monotonicity and the same funding factor. Thus we may suppose p is
equivariant under these symmetries, without any measurability assumption.
At central, equal-coordinate and axis aggregates its forms are consequently
 (a_0,0,0), (a_d,b_d,b_d), (a,b,0), respectively.
Reflection of the second exposure gives(a_d,b_d,-b_d).

At every interior aggregate C, its reserve price a(C) is at least1. To see this,
let one owner hold eta(1,0,0), with0<eta<=F(C), and let the other hold the
remainder. Both reports are in K and below the stated public bound at all
aggregates used below. Singleton coverage gives eta*a(C)>=eta. This step
uses aggregate-linearity: changing the ownership decomposition at fixed C
must not change its common price.

2. FIRST RESTORATION: FORCE THE DIAGONAL RAY PRICE
Fix0<s<1 and write h=(1-s)/4. Consider TWO owners with low reports
                   U=(h,h,h), O=(h,-h,-h).
The low aggregate is C_0=(2h,0,0). Restore only owner1 by
                   D_1=(s/2,s/2,s/2) in K.
Its high report is U+D_1=((1+s)/4,(1+s)/4,(1+s)/4), and the high aggregate is
                   C_d=(1/2,s/2,s/2).
Write p(C_0)=(a_0,0,0) and p(C_d)=(a_d,b_d,b_d). Own monotonicity gives
                   ((1+s)/4)(a_d+2b_d)>=h*a_0.
Since a_0>=1,
                   c_d:=a_d+2b_d>=(1-s)/(1+s).           (1)
Also a_d>=1 by the separate singleton-coverage argument above.

3. SECOND RESTORATION: FORCE THE AXIS FUNDING RATIO
Use the reflected diagonal aggregate C'_d=(1/2,s/2,-s/2), whose price is
(a_d,b_d,-b_d), and the following reports for the SAME two actual owners:
 U'=((1+s)/4,(1+s)/4,-(3s-1)/4),
 O =(h,-h,-h).
Their sum is C'_d. Both belong to K for every0<s<1: the only nontrivial
inequality is |3s-1|<=1+s. Restore owner1 by
                         D_2=(1/2,1/2,s/2) in K.
The high report is
                  B=((3+s)/4,(3+s)/4,(1-s)/4),
and the high aggregate is
                  C_a=(1,(1+s)/2,0).
All displayed reports have reserve coordinate at most1 and hence lie below
(4,0,0) in K. No relation between owner reports in different comparisons is
needed; both comparisons are among allowed profiles of this fixed model.

Owner1's low payment is
 ((1+s)/4)a_d+(s)b_d
   =((1-s)/4)a_d+(s/2)c_d
   >=(1-s)(1+3s)/[4(1+s)],                            (2)
where(1) and a_d>=1 were used. At C_a write p=(a,b,0). Its high payment is
((3+s)/4)(a+b). Own monotonicity and(2) imply
                  a+b>=(1-s)(1+3s)/[(1+s)(3+s)].       (3)
Its reserve price a is again at least1. Writing r=(1+s)/2, total funding is
 a+r b=(1-r)a+r(a+b)
   >=(1-s)/2 +(1-s)(1+3s)/[2(3+s)].
Since F(C_a)=1-r=(1-s)/2, the funding factor must satisfy
                  kappa>=1+(1+3s)/(3+s)
                       =4(1+s)/(3+s).                 (4)
Letting s tend to1 proves kappa>=2. This limit is over perfectly valid finite
profiles in the same two-owner bounded model. No limit of prices is taken.
For example s=1/2 gives the explicit rational lower12/7>3/2.

4. WHY THE FINITE-ORTHANT REDUCTION DOES NOT DESCEND
Let the four square rays be
 r_0=(1,1,1), r_1=(1,1,-1), r_2=(1,-1,1), r_3=(1,-1,-1).
For nonnegative counts z=(z_0,z_1,z_2,z_3), direct algebra gives
 F(sum_j z_j r_j)=2[min(z_0,z_3)+min(z_1,z_2)].         (5)
Indeed (x+y)/2=z_0-z_3, (x-y)/2=z_1-z_2 and t=sum z_j.
Thus the generator-coordinate technology is just two disjoint min2 games,
and has an orthant common-price TCWM rule of factor3/2 by summing the two
sharp min2 rules. This is entirely consistent with the accepted orthant
price-reduction theorem.

The rays satisfy r_0+r_3=r_1+r_2. For prices q_j to arise from ONE cone
functional p at an aggregate, they must satisfy
                         q_0+q_3=q_1+q_2.             (6)
But pointwise(6) alone is insufficient: if different z,z' have the same
aggregate sum z_j r_j, the descended cone functional must also be the SAME.
Orthant aggregate fields may vary along the kernel direction(1,-1,-1,1).
The count-dilation proof does not average over these representation changes.

There is an explicit example using the Huber prices from(5). For a pair
with A>=B and A>0, prices for production2min(A,B) are q_A=B/A and
q_B=2-B/A; use the symmetric formula for B>=A. This is twice the min2
rule defined and proved in Section3 of
../composition/CONE_L1_EMBEDDABLE_SHARP_THEOREM.txt.
At
 z=(1,1,1,0), pair(0,3) prices are(0,2) and pair(1,2) prices(1,1), so
 q=(0,1,1,2), which descends pointwise to p=(1,-1/2,-1/2).
At z'=(3/2,1/2,1/2,1/2), the SAME cone aggregate is(3,1,1), but pair(0,3)
prices are(1/3,5/3) and pair(1,2) prices(1,1). Hence
 q'=(1/3,1,1,5/3), descending pointwise to p'=(1,-1/3,-1/3).
Both satisfy(6); the two cone functionals differ. The theorem above proves
this is not merely a defect of that chosen orthant formula: no repair that
produces same-aggregate cone prices can retain factor below2 and own IC.

APPENDIX: EXACT FINITE-GENERATOR DESCENT CRITERION
For clarity, the missing property can be stated as a precise equivalence.
Let K=A R_+^m be a pointed finitely generated cone spanning its ambient space,
and let G(z)=F(Az). An orthant common-price field q(z) descends to a cone
common-price field P(C) if and only if BOTH conditions hold:
 (i) q(z) annihilates ker A, equivalently q(z) is in range(A^T);
 (ii) q(z)=q(z') whenever Az=Az'.
Since A has full row rank, (i) gives a unique P_z with A^T P_z=q(z); condition
(ii) makes P_z depend only on C=Az. Conversely a pulled-back field
q(z)=A^T P(Az) plainly has both properties.

If an all-profile orthant field has these properties, its coverage, funding,
TCWM, and full-OUT guarantees transfer without loss to the cone field. For
each actual owner report B_i in K choose any nonnegative representation z_i.
For a cone restoration D_i in K choose a nonnegative representation d_i and
use z_i+d_i at the high profile. These are the SAME actual owners and a
coordinatewise orthant restoration. Prices and payments descend independently
of representation; G(sum d_i)=F(sum D_i) transfers the pooled outside option.
Conversely pulling back a cone field gives the same orthant guarantees. With
public cone upper bounds use their full preimages as the allowed count domain;
all chosen high and low representations remain feasible by construction.

Thus the failure identified above is exactly the absence of conditions(i)-(ii)
in the general orthant reduction. The concrete Huber example satisfies(i)
pointwise but violates(ii), and the sharp lower2 proves that no same-factor
repair can impose both conditions while retaining the stated incentives.
This appendix is elementary linear algebra and report lifting, not a claim
of a new abstract quotient theorem.
