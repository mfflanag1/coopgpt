SHIFTED HARMONIC PRICES CANNOT IMPROVE THE LEADING MATRIX-ORDER CONSTANT
17 October 2026. Exact architecture obstruction; not an all-mechanism bound.

For 0<=theta<1, consider the normalized common price
 P_theta(C)=(C-theta*T I)^(-1)/Tr[(C-theta*T I)^(-1)],
 where T=lambda_min(C)>0, and payments y_i=Tr(P_theta(C)X_i).
Put c=1-theta. The price is PSD of trace1, so coalition coverage is immediate.
The total payment is theta*T+m/D, where D=Tr[(C-theta*T I)^(-1)]. Since
D>=1/(cT), its worst-profile funding factor is at most mc+theta, and that
factor is approached by C=diag(T,L,...,L) as L tends to infinity.

Any such rule satisfying even INDIVIDUAL own monotonicity must have
                              theta<=1/m.                     (1)
Take two fixed owners X=E_11 and Y=I-E_11, with C=I. Restore epsilon E_11
to owner1, epsilon>0. Both reports and the restoration are PSD. Grand value
stays1 for m>=2. Initially the focal payment is1/m. Afterwards it is
                         c(1+epsilon)/(mc+(m-1)epsilon).
Its change is exactly
             epsilon(1-m theta)/[m(mc+(m-1)epsilon)].           (2)
This is negative whenever theta>1/m. The entire example is diagonal: no
matrix rotation or noncommuting reports are involved.

Consequently this family can reduce its worst-profile factor at most to
                          m-1+1/m,
and cannot approach the scalar TCWM lower's leading coefficient1/2. This
necessary condition is not claimed sufficient; in dimension2 the separate
noncommuting obstruction imposes a stronger limitation. Further parameter
tuning in this family is closed as a route to a leading-order improvement.
