FIXED POSITIVE BERNOULLI MIXTURES: EXACT SUPPORT AND QUADRATIC SIZE BARRIER
17 October 2026. Accepted core support lemma; general fixed-weight extension is a separate note.

CONTRACT
Fix an integer s>=2 and a finite nonnegative Borel measure mu on [0,1]. For
each finite actual-owner population define
 m_i(p;b)=E_R[F(b_i+b_R)-F(b_R)],
where the other actual owners independently enter R with probability p, and
 y_i(b)=integral_[0,1] m_i(p;b) dmu(p).
The SAME mu is used at every report profile and every owner count. Production
and eligible ownership are public and fixed within every report comparison;
reports are binding nonnegative divisible holdings. An unchanged member is
allowed in an incentive coalition. The target contract is TCWM for all actual
coalitions of at most s owners, with coverage still required for all coalitions.

THEOREM
(A) If this mixture satisfies the stated TCWM condition simultaneously on
every finite owner population with the SAME measure mu for the two-input minimum F(P,L)=min(P,L), then mu is supported on
[0,1/s]. The result already follows from the explicit rational profiles below.
It also holds for the SINGLE fixed three-input technology min(x1,x2,x3), with
all owners eligible for all three resources: represent a center bundle x by
(x,0,x) and a leaf bundle l by (0,l,0), giving exactly the same two-input game.

(B) For the three-input minimum, any such mixture that covers every coalition
and has all-profile total funding factor kappa satisfies
                         kappa >= s^2/3.
Therefore fixed positive mixtures cannot improve the quadratic s-exponent of
the accepted 3s^2 upper for arity-at-most-three production. This is a mechanism-
architecture lower, NOT an unrestricted-mechanism lower. It does not close the
unrestricted interval Omega(s^(1/4)) to O(s^2).

The measure may be chosen for this fixed technology and for s. Necessity still
uses the same measure across owner counts. The separate
SEMIVALUE_MIXTURE_GENERAL_FIXED_MARGINALS.txt extends the uniform funding lower
to owner-count-dependent fixed mixtures and all scaled positive fixed
whole-owner marginal-contribution rules. It does not impose support on their
finite-n measures. Report-dependent/signed weights and arbitrary mechanisms
remain outside scope. Classical normalized semivalues are the ingredients;
arbitrary scaled mixtures need not satisfy their unit-mass normalization.

1. EXACT THRESHOLD-STAR TRANSITION
Choose integers k>=1 and 0<=t<=k-1. There are k+s fixed actual owners:
 k outside center owners, each holding P=1/k and no leaf resource;
 s protected leaf owners, with no center resource.
The focal protected owner starts with leaf t/k and restores epsilon=1/k.
Each of the other s-1 protected owners holds leaf1 and changes nothing.
The same coalition of all s leaf owners is protected across the transition.
All reports are rational; all owner identities and eligibility sets are fixed.

Let
 T_(k,t)(p)=Pr[Bin(k,p)>=t+1].
Total sampled center capacity is at most1. If any nonfocal protected leaf is
present, output is already saturated and focal restoration changes no value.
If none is present, restoration changes value by exactly epsilon when at
least t+1 centers are sampled, and by zero otherwise. Thus the change in the
focal owner's Bernoulli marginal is
              epsilon(1-p)^(s-1) T_(k,t)(p).
For each unchanged protected leaf j, the change in its marginal is negative
only when the focal owner is sampled and every other protected leaf is absent.
Adding j then saturates output before and after, while the term without j gains
epsilon. Its exact change is
             -epsilon p(1-p)^(s-2) T_(k,t)(p).
Summing the same s owners gives the polynomial identity
 Delta sum_(i in S) m_i(p)
   = epsilon (1-p)^(s-2)(1-sp) T_(k,t)(p).                 (1)
It includes p=0 and p=1 by direct evaluation; for s=2 the exponent-zero factor
is1 everywhere. Integration is legitimate because finite-population semivalues
are bounded polynomials in p and mu is finite. TCWM implies
 integral (1-p)^(s-2)(1-sp) T_(k,t)(p) dmu(p) >= 0.        (2)
This is obtained from actual report transitions, not coefficientwise positivity.

2. THE ENDPOINT p=1 CANNOT HIDE
The hypothesis also protects coalitions of size2. Use the same construction
with two protected leaves and t=k-1. Then T_(k,k-1)(p)=p^k, so
                    integral (1-2p)p^k dmu(p) >= 0.       (3)
As k tends to infinity, the bounded integrand tends pointwise to0 on [0,1)
and to-1 at1. Dominated convergence yields -mu({1})>=0, hence mu({1})=0.
There is no assertion that an infinite population is strategic: each inequality
is valid in one finite population, and the common measure permits taking the
limit of these necessary scalar inequalities.

3. EXCLUDING ALL MASS ABOVE 1/s
Suppose mu((1/s,1))>0. Choose a in (1/s,1), not an atom of mu, such that
mu((a,1))>0. Such an a exists: finite measures have at most countably many
atoms, and (1/s,1) is the increasing union of intervals bounded away from1/s.
For each k put t_k=floor(ka), always an integer in {0,...,k-1}. All profiles
remain rational even if the chosen threshold a is irrational.

For p<a, T_(k,t_k)(p) tends to0; for p>a it tends to1. For completeness,
Bin(k,p)/k has mean p and variance p(1-p)/k. Chebyshev bounds give these limits
once its distance to a exceeds the rounding error1/k. No statement at p=a is
needed because mu({a})=0. Let h_s(p)=(1-p)^(s-2)(1-sp). Dominated convergence
in(2), using |h_s T|<=s-1 and finite mu, gives
                       integral_(a,1) h_s(p) dmu(p) >=0.
The integrand is strictly negative at every p in (a,1), and that interval has
positive measure. The integral is therefore strictly negative, a contradiction.
Together with Section2 this proves support(mu) subset [0,1/s]. Atoms at0 and
at1/s are not excluded, and are consistent with the known valid architecture.

4. FUNDING CONSEQUENCE ON ONE FIXED THREE-OWNER MODEL
Use F=min(x1,x2,x3), with three fixed actual owners each eligible for all three
resources. At the pure profile b1=(1,0,0), b2=(0,1,0), b3=(0,0,1), each owner's
Bernoulli marginal is p^2. Grand-coalition coverage therefore requires
                          3 integral p^2 dmu >=1.
Section3 implies integral p^2 dmu <= mu([0,1])/s^2, so mu([0,1])>=s^2/3.
At a balanced profile b1=b2=b3=(1,1,1) in the SAME eligible owner model,
every coalition's production equals its size. Each m_i(p)=1 for every p,
so total payment is3mu([0,1]) and total production is3. Hence kappa>=mu([0,1]),
proving(B). Zero-report owners could pad either profile without changing the
calculation, but padding or strategic cloning is not needed for this step.

5. CONSEQUENCE AND STOPPING DECISION
The fixed positive-mixture loophole in the old single-p stopping argument is
closed: averaging unsafe p-values cannot repair incentives uniformly over finite
owner counts. The required test functions come from thresholded marginal gains
inside a two-resource technology, despite its low activity arity. Necessity of
support was not inferred merely from signs of formal semivalue coefficients.

This result supports the ranking's requirement for a genuinely different
payment structure before trying to improve the general s-exponent. The core
proof does not restrict finite-n mixture support; the separate extension
closes uniform-budget n-dependent fixed positive marginal-weight rules.
Report-dependent rules remain open. Neither result proves the unrestricted
3s^2 upper sharp: min3 itself has the accepted general TCWM factor2 independent
of s. The exact leading constant inside the fixed-mixture architecture is not
investigated. No new target is launched here.

conventional analytic proof, not a claim of exhaustive computational coverage.
