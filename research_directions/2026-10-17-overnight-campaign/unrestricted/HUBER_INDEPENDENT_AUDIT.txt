1. Positive-capacity optimization
The global zero/one perturbation in Lemma1 is valid: there are finitely many
one/interior edges with positive slack, and decreasing every price1 while
increasing every price0 preserves zero/one equalities and one/one feasibility
for small epsilon. At least one positive-cost one-vertex exists whenever a
nonisolated zero exists. The first-order decrease defeats the quadratic cost
of all raised zeros. No hidden active-edge exception remains.

The uniqueness argument correctly uses strict convexity only below1/2.
If two minimizers differ at a coordinate, both endpoint values must be>=1/2.
Any coordinate strictly above1/2 has a tight low neighbor (otherwise decrease
that coordinate). The low neighbor is the same in both optima, ruling out a
decrease of the high coordinate. Exchanging the optima completes uniqueness.
Isolated actual graph vertices have unique price0 at positive cost.

2. KKT and funding
Slater holds, e.g. all prices3/4; nonisolated prices are interior. Multipliers
therefore satisfy degree(lambda)=c h'(p). Since h'<=1 they are feasible
fractional activity flows. The rounded vector r in{0,1/2,1} is dual feasible:
every low neighbor is high; half/half sums1; high/half and high/high are safe.
Every positive-multiplier edge has rounded sum1, and every r-positive vertex
is saturated. Thus lambda and r certify the ORIGINAL maximum production,
not merely a regularized surrogate. This also handles inactive edges.
Strict components yield t=B/(2A) and funding(3/2-t)B. Half-price components
are efficiently funded. The bound therefore adds across tight components
without multiplying by depth.

3. Mixed-owner own monotonicity
For one actual owner, the relevant sums a,b are her capacities over BOTH
sides of the entire changing tight component. Bounds a<=A,b<=B hold even
when she owns both sides and several vertices; they are exactly what is
needed by the displayed derivatives. Other tight components' rational
formulas are constant during a fixed-pattern interval. The high-side lower
bound is1-2t>=0; the low-side lower bound is t(1-a/A+b/A)>=0.

The seam argument is adequate: there is a finite collection of rational
candidate optimizer vectors along a one-coordinate segment, with positive
denominators. Distinct formulas intersect only finitely many times unless
identical. A continuous selection cannot move between unequal formulas
without an intersection; identical formulas have identical tight edges.
Hence finite-piece integration of the nonnegative derivatives is justified.

4. Boundary
At zero aggregate coordinates, owner capacities there all vanish. The
limiting objective reduces to the induced positive graph because zero
vertices can be assigned price1 free of charge. Positive-coordinate prices
therefore have a unique limit; arbitrary limiting zero-coordinate prices
do not affect payments. For the regularized objective c+epsilon1, retaining
actual report weights is legitimate: the same inequalities a<=A,b<=B hold,
and changing an actual coordinate changes its regularized aggregate by1.
The pointwise limit of own-monotone regularized payments proves the boundary
claim without introducing public resource capacity into the actual game.
