EXACT CROSS-OWNER NORMAL COUPLING FOR THE OPEN DIAGONAL PROBLEM
2026-10-17. Necessary witness identity; no aggregate theorem claimed.

Let Ax>=b be a fixed nonnegative0/1 cover system. For each owner i let q^i
be the norm projection after lowering only rows containing i. Select any
nonnegative active KKT multipliers lambda^i, so q^i=A^T lambda^i. For i!=j put
 w^{i,j}=sum_(S containing j)lambda^i_S*1_S.
Then0<=w^{i,j}<=q^i coordinatewise and its j-coordinate is q^i_j.

Every positive-multiplier row of q^i that excludes j is unchanged at witness
j, where its demand is the full b_S. At witness i it is active with demand
at most b_S. Therefore
 (q^i-w^{i,j}) dot (q^i-q^j)<=0.
Exchanging i,j and subtracting gives the exact cross-owner inequality
 ||q^i-q^j||^2 <=(w^{i,j}-w^{j,i}) dot (q^i-q^j).            (C)
This uses unchanged off-owner demands, unlike the separate ball bounds.
It holds with nonuniform demands, dependent rows and nonunique multipliers.

ONE PRECISE AGGREGATE SUFFICIENT CONDITION.
Write rho=||q(full)||^2 and qbar=(1/n)sum_i q^i. Every q^i has norm squared
at most rho. The variance identity and(C) give
 V=sum_i||q^i-qbar||^2
   <=(1/n)sum_(i<j)(w^{i,j}-w^{j,i}) dot (q^i-q^j) =: B.
The diagonal vector m_i=q^i_i consequently satisfies
 ||m|| <=||qbar||+sqrt(V)<=sqrt(rho)+sqrt(B).
Thus B<=(sqrt2-1)^2*rho would imply sum_i(q^i_i)^2<=2rho. This is only a
sufficient quantitative condition. No universal bound on B is established;
large non-focal witness variance may make it too strong. In particular the
pairwise inequality by itself is NOT a proof of the diagonal conjecture.

CURRENT BOTTLENECK.
The scalar ball bounds use only one full feasible cover and ignore locality.
(C) retains locality through the active-row overlap vectors. The unresolved
step is to control their aggregate effect without replacing each w by its
coordinatewise upper bound q, which loses precisely the coupling. The veto
extension lemma and column-frequency-two lemma are two classes where that
coupling has been controlled; the general case remains open.
