INDEPENDENT AUDIT: EXACT POLYMATROID PRICE EVALUATION
17 October 2026. Read-only audit of other lanes; no new mechanism claim.

VERDICT
No material mathematical or implementation defect found in
composition/POLYMATROID_ORACLE_COROLLARY.txt and
composition/POLYMATROID_ORACLE_PRICES.py. The lower-envelope recursion computes
the accepted two-block prices exactly, conditional on the stated exact maximal
SFM callback and normalized, monotone, nonnegative submodular rational rank.
Consequently it inherits the accepted TCWM/coverage/3/2 theorem; this audit does
not add full outside-production incentives or certify historical novelty.

ENDPOINTS AND FACES
Deleting zero-capacity private coordinates preserves the minimum: intersecting
any set with the active set J weakly decreases rank and leaves its capacity
intercept unchanged. This is restriction of the public rank, not contraction.
The allocation's absent-coordinate price is zero; actual owners remain fixed.
P=0, no active private coordinates, and r(J)=0 are legitimate zero-value faces.
For positive active capacities, J is the unique minimizer at parameter zero.
Submodularity implies the union L of zero-rank singleton loops has rank zero;
monotonicity implies every zero-rank set lies in L. Thus L is the unique
eventual minimizer. There is no need to compute a sufficiently large endpoint.

RECURSION AND TIES
For f_z(S)=zP r(S)-c(S), maximal minimizers nest downward in z. One direct
uncrossing proof compares A at alpha and B at beta>alpha with A union B and
A intersection B. Submodularity and monotonicity give the required weighted
rank inequality; optimality makes A union B a minimizer at alpha, so maximality
forces B subset A. The endpoints used by the recursion are maximal at their
anchors. Their line intersection is between their optimality anchors.

A strict improvement M at the intersection belongs strictly between B and A.
Equal ranks for distinct nested optimal-anchor sets are impossible: the larger
set has strictly smaller intercept because all active capacities are positive.
Therefore both recursive denominators are positive. With no strict improvement,
each competing affine line is above A at the left anchor and intersection and
above B at the intersection and right anchor. It cannot hide a positive-length
envelope segment. At the infinite right anchor, comparison is with the
zero-slope loop line, and all competing slopes are nonnegative.

The positive-interval assertion is also valid. At a positive parameter z0, the
maximal minimizer M contains every other tied minimizer S. If S is proper then
P(r(M)-r(S))=c(M\S)/z0>0. Thus M has strictly greater slope than every proper
tied minimizer and is uniquely optimal immediately to the left of z0. The
finitely many nontied lines remain separated on a sufficiently short interval.
This excludes intermediate sets that exist only at an isolated tie. Isolated
ties legitimately have zero mass under the continuous averaging law.

EXACT ORACLE COUNT
A simple induction avoids any ambiguity about retained segments. A recursive
call on A strictly containing B needs at most 2|A\B|-1 queries. A terminal call
uses one. A strict M splits A\B into nonempty disjoint A\M and M\B, so
  1+[2|A\M|-1]+[2|M\B|-1]=2|A\B|-1.
Initially this is 2|J\L|-1 <= 2|J|-1. Zero-rank faces need no maximal query.
The ordinary-to-maximal wrapper is correct: membership in the union of all
minimizers is equivalent to equality of unconstrained and forced-membership
minimum values. The minimizer lattice makes that union a minimizer. It uses
at most |J|+1 ordinary exact SFM calls per maximal query. The restricted forced
objective is submodular; a backend requiring normalization can subtract its
constant empty-set value without changing minimizers.

RANK ACCOUNTING CLARIFICATION APPLIED
The current corrected note states '|J|+2 rank calls to check normalization,
find loops and the initial rank' and explicitly separates additional chain/
oracle evaluations. This resolves the minor presentation concern in my first
audit. Outside rank queries made internally by SFM, the code evaluates
at most |J| singleton ranks, r(J), r(empty), and one new rank for each strict
query; terminal queries return the cached maximal endpoint. Thus <=2|J|+1
such external rank evaluations is a safe simple bound. SFM's internal rank
queries must also be counted if reporting full value-oracle cost, as the
corrected note now makes clear. The stated maximal-SFM bound is correct.

RATIONAL ARITHMETIC
Every breakpoint is a ratio of an original capacity sum and a difference of
two original rank values, multiplied by P. It is not obtained by repeatedly
substituting earlier breakpoints. Polynomially bounded rational input/rank
encoding therefore gives polynomially bounded breakpoint and oracle-objective
encoding. G is piecewise rational; at most |J|+1 rational weighted gradients
are summed, so exact prices also have polynomial encoding and arithmetic work.
This is conditional polynomial evaluation with an exact polynomial SFM backend;
it does not assert strong polynomial bit complexity, efficient arbitrary rank
representations, or correctness of approximate floating-point tie handling.

IMPLEMENTATION AND TARGETED REPLAY
The supplied code faithfully implements this recursion, original-to-active
index mapping, rational prices, and ordinary-to-maximal oracle conversion.
Its docstring correctly leaves rank submodularity and callback correctness as
assumptions; runtime comparisons alone do not certify an arbitrary callback.
The exhaustive backend is explicitly only a finite test oracle.

I inspected the author's independent full-subset reference and recorded output
(195 profiles, 290 restoration holding boxes, 32 ordinary-oracle comparisons,
16 all-pair reference cross-checks); I did not rerun that unchanged suite.
Instead I copied the evaluated algorithm into this lane and ran six independent
exact modular tests using the direct additive formula, with a non-enumerative
modular oracle. A forty-row distinct-breakpoint case attains exactly 79=2m-1
queries and 41 chain sets; a forty-row all-tied case uses one query; loops,
inactive rows, rank-zero, P-zero and private-zero faces all pass.
