THREE-HALVES IS OPTIMAL FOR AGGREGATE-ONLY LINEAR RESOURCE PRICES
17 October 2026. Conventional short proof, pending independent review.
This is an ARCHITECTURE lower bound, not an unrestricted mechanism lower.

Fix ONE edge with endpoint resources A and B, production F(A,B)=min(A,B),
and exactly THREE actual owners.
Owner1 can report only A, owner2 only B, and owner3 can report both. These
eligibilities and identities stay fixed at every profile. A rule in this
architecture chooses two real prices P(A,B),Q(A,B) depending only on aggregate
endpoint capacities (and the fixed public model), and pays owner i
  y_i = a_i P(A,B) + b_i Q(A,B).
Prices may be arbitrary, discontinuous, asymmetric, nonhomogeneous functions.
Suppose this rule is nonnegative, coalition-covering, and own-report monotone
on all nonnegative real profiles of these same three actual owners.

Then its worst positive-value total-payment factor is at least3/2.

PROOF
At aggregate(1,1), write p=P(1,1),q=Q(1,1). The profile
 ((1,0),(0,1),(0,0)) has grand worth1, hence p+q>=1. Nonnegative owner
payments there give p,q>=0.

For any M>=1, increase only owner1's report in this profile from1 toM.
Own monotonicity gives M P(M,1)>=p.
At the SAME aggregate(M,1), use the alternative allowed report profile
 ((M-1,0),(0,0),(1,1)). Owner3 alone produces1, so its coverage gives
 P(M,1)+Q(M,1)>=1. (Prices agree across these two profiles precisely because
this is an aggregate-only architecture.) Therefore at aggregate(M,1),
whose grand production is1, total payment obeys
 M P(M,1)+Q(M,1)
 >=1+(M-1)P(M,1)
 >=1+(1-1/M)p.
The mirrored increase of owner2 and mixed-owner profile similarly imply
factor at aggregate(1,M) >=1+(1-1/M)q.
Thus the worst factor is at least
  1+(1-1/M)max(p,q) >=3/2-1/(2M)
for EVERY M>=1. Taking the supremum over M proves>=3/2.

The Huber-price rule, internally accepted in root/ROOT_ADJUDICATION_01_HUBER.txt,
attains the matching general3/2 factor on arbitrary resource graphs. Consequently3/2 is
the exact worst-case optimum of this architecture over graph models, already
forced by a single edge with three fixed actual owners. No regularity or
symmetry assumption was used in the lower bound.

WHY THIS DOES NOT LOWER-BOUND ALL MECHANISMS
The comparison between two ownership decompositions at the same aggregate
uses the architecture assumption. An eligible rule may depend on individual
capacities, cap individual payments, or pay nonlinear bundle shares. Those
mechanisms need not reuse P,Q across the two profiles; the transfer fails.
