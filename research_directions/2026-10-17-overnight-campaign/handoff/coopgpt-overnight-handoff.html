What changed

We now have matching upper and lower bounds for broad coalition incentive contracts, rather than only particular ownership examples. Three contracts, different questions

Individual protection

One actual owner restores resources. Its own payment must not fall. With outside production, the increase must also cover what those resources can produce separately.

Coalition monotonicity

Members may coordinate their reports and transfer money among themselves. Their total payment must not fall when their resources are restored. We abbreviate this as TCWM.

Coalition outside production

The coalition can also pool the withheld resources and produce elsewhere. Its payment increase must cover that joint outside value. This is the strongest incentive contract displayed; additional bargaining floors are separate constraints.

The scalar-demand counterpart of TCWM is Moulin–Sprumont’s established Strong Group Monotonicity. The proposed contributions here concern quantitative funding and resource-report domains. A protected coalition member may restore zero resources while another member changes its report. Owner identities and eligibility remain fixed across reports. All-profile proofs cover real feasible withdrawals; finite computations are evidence or discovery tools, not substitutes for those proofs.

Results worth carrying forward

Universal concave production

coalition

Sharp total-output factors

For d nonnegative resources, every finite, nonnegative, monotone, concave, degree-one technology admits coalition payment monotonicity at (d+1)/2 and full coalition outside protection at 1+c(d−1). The lower examples match uniformly over growing fixed owner populations.

Scope and verification

Sharpness is over the class, not a claim that every technology needs the worst factor. This does not solve the original individual-incentive gap.

For 0≤β,θ≤1, K_d(β)=1+(d−1)μ(β), with μ=1/2 through β≈0.9190487203 and μ(1)=c≈0.5430633898. The additional floor yᵢ≥θ[F(B)−F(B−bᵢ)] replaces μ by max{μ,θ}.

Partial β<1 protection is weaker than protecting all reusable outside production. Finite rational implementation is available with stated approximation overhead.

Norm reserve pooling

coalition

Sharp factors independent of exposure dimension

For F(t,x)=t−‖x‖ on the reserve cone, an exact linear L1 embedding yields sharp 3/2 coalition monotonicity and 1+c full outside protection, uniformly over finite owner populations. This covers Euclidean norms, all finite-dimensional ℓp for 1≤p≤2, and every planar ℓp including p=∞.

Scope and verification

The signed compensation proof is essential. Auxiliary positive/negative features are not new strategic owners. Direct finite feature substitution can lose coverage.

One shared resource, many private resources

coalition

Sharp 3/2 beyond a fixed resource count

For continuous monotone concave homogeneous production F(P,c), zero on either block axis and with diminishing returns throughout the private block, two-block gradient averaging gives coalition coverage and TCWM for arbitrary fixed mixed owners at total funding at most 3F/2. Arbitrary public polymatroids give an explicit unbounded subclass.

Scope and verification

Sharpness is a supremum over growing finite owner counts. The general nonsmooth construction is existential. The polymatroid formula has an exact evaluation corollary using at most 2m−1 maximal submodular minimizations; the principal-partition algorithm is established. A practical general SFM backend is not supplied. For explicit weighted-coverage ranks, an exact rational max-flow backend is implemented and independently checked. Full outside protection is not claimed.

When ownership-aware payments beat common prices

boundary

An exact factor-2 versus factor-1 separation

On F(t,x,y)=t−max(|x|,|y|), common prices depending only on aggregate resources have sharp funding factor 2. On the same fixed two-owner model, an elementary ownership-aware rule is efficient and protects full outside production.

Scope and verification

The common-price lower assumes only individual monotonicity and no regularity of prices; the upper proves coalition monotonicity. This closes a price-reduction shortcut on cones, not the unrestricted problem. The efficient two-owner rule is classical.

Norms beyond p=2

boundary

Growing cost and a remaining gap

For ℓp reserve norms with p≥2, the general upper is p for coalition monotonicity and p/a_p for full outside protection, where a_p=1−½(1−1/p)^p. An all-mechanism TCWM lower grows as Ω(√(p/log p)).

Scope and verification

Owner count and dimension grow with p in the lower. It does not rule out a constant for any fixed p. Regular support-radial and one-norm compensation rules need Ω(p).

Individual incentives on gains

individual

Sublinear owner and dimension coefficients

Writing F(B)=S+G, closed pointed cone mechanisms for finite nonnegative monotone concave homogeneous production achieve S+min{d,n√(2/(n+1)),K_(d+1)}G. For PSD minimum eigenvalue, matrix order m replaces the cone bound: S+min{m,n√(2/(n+1)),K_m}G. Here K_D∼√(8D/π).

Scope and verification

Here d is ambient resource dimension and m is matrix order; the choice among rules uses fixed public information. Individual outside protection only. This is a proved improved sampling coefficient, not an optimal general constant. The true minimum-norm envelope inequality remains open.

Pairwise collusion on gains

coalition

A linear owner-count separation

For n≥5 owners each eligible for both inputs of min production on the full nonnegative report domain, full outside protection for singletons and pairs requires (n−4)/22≤K_n≤n/2 in the gain budget S+K_nG. Thus the order is Θ(n).

Scope and verification

The leading constant is open. Without outside production (payment-only pair TCWM), an owner-independent K is still unresolved.

Two-input resource graphs

coalition

Sharp 3/2, with a full-outside gap

Weighted graph production has sharp coalition-monotone factor 3/2. Unit-profit graphs whose components are complete multipartite reach full outside factor 1+c; arbitrary weighted graphs have upper 2. Full deletion-marginal floors make 2 sharp.

Scope and verification

The floor-free arbitrary-graph interval [1+c,2] is open. The four-resource path is the first obstruction to a positive binary-min decomposition.

Common capacity and ownership

coalition

An unbounded structural family with matching rates

A common capacity with M rank-three branches has sharp order Θ(M^(1/4)) in the uniform worst case over finite ownership models. Fixed k private inputs give Θ_k(M^((k−1)/(2k))). Ownership and marginal-floor parameters yield further matching frontiers.

Scope and verification

One owner of all indispensable common capacity is efficient without added floors. These are worst-case structural rates, not a claim about every ownership institution. Leading constants outside the stated price class remain open.

Matrix mechanism boundaries

Limits of fixed marginal-payment rules

boundary

A broad sharp-order obstruction with a finite certificate

Every eligible nonnegative, report-independent weighting of whole-owner marginal contributions requires Ω_r(s^(r−1)) funding. A matching-order construction makes the optimal architecture factor Θ_r(s^(r−1)) when activities use at most r resources and coalitions up to size s receive incentive protection. This includes asymmetric and population-dependent weights. An exact 16-owner min3 certificate requires factor 5 for groups up to four.

Scope and verification

These are architecture bounds. General mechanisms on that same min3 technology have factor 2. The unrestricted arity-three interval remains Ω(s^(1/4)) to O(s²). The limiting-measure proof does not impose a finite-population support restriction. The final root66 corollary shows that report-dependent positive weights escape the barrier on min2; the owner-uniform min3 dummy-zero question remains open.

Total output is not incremental gain

F(B) = S + G

S is the sum of what owners can already produce separately. G is the additional value created by cooperation.

A total-output bound is Σyᵢ ≤ κ(S+G). A gain-only bound is Σyᵢ ≤ S+KG. A constant κ does not imply a constant K.

A credible AI and energy bridge

The clean candidate is several independently owned load portfolios sharing one electrical delivery point and a common reliability standard. With public Gaussian factors Lᵢ=mᵢ+⟨xᵢ,Z⟩, Z∼N(0,I), a common positive reliability quantile zα, and firm capacity Cᵢ, define tᵢ=(Cᵢ−mᵢ)/zα. Admit owner portfolios only when tᵢ≥‖xᵢ‖₂, and require both retained and removed bundles to remain in that reserve cone. Total usable pooled headroom is zα[Σtᵢ−‖Σxᵢ‖₂]. The additional headroom created by pooling is zαG=zα[Σ‖xᵢ‖₂−‖Σxᵢ‖₂].

This gives an exact conditional mapping to the norm theorem. It does not establish that AI loads fit the model, that all relevant withdrawals are cone-feasible, or that a sponsor should pay the chosen mechanism’s subsidy.

Before an empirical claim: fix actual ownership units, capacity entitlements, feasible withdrawals, curtailment allocation, reliability targets and sponsor value. Then test measured simultaneous load traces on a chronological holdout with dependence-aware uncertainty. Existing Mac measurements and request traces do not validate this model.

An efficient Euler allocation can fail withholding incentives, yet two owners have an efficient full-outside alternative. A vulnerable baseline alone does not establish that subsidy is necessary. Sale proceeds valued at the same public price cannot finance payments above that value: the sponsor needs a separately identified benefit or budget.

Observed data gate: the public M100 archive now supplies verified same-time PSU input readings across 980 physical nodes. The April files cover only parts of four days, with a 51-hour gap in released timestamps. The separate total_power sensor differs materially from the PSU sum. This validates a data-intake path; it does not establish ownership, reliability or pooling gains.

  3. Resolve the graph outside-production gap. For arbitrary weighted resource graphs, improve the floor-free upper 2 toward 1+c, or prove a stronger lower. The four-resource path is a concrete first obstacle. Another positive binary-min decomposition is insufficient.
  4. Construct a pairwise gain-only mechanism. Payment-only pair TCWM remains open. The exact residual Lipschitz characterization supplies a testable target; full outside protection already has a linear owner lower bound.
  5. Return to the original individual gap. The true minimum-norm own-report envelope still lacks the desired diagonal bound. The closed deletion and endpoint shortcuts cannot be reused as proofs.
  6. Build beyond the shared/private theorem. The one-common-resource result now handles an unbounded private block. With two heterogeneous common resources, even aligned profits do not make uniform three-block averaging truthful. A new compensation argument is required.

What belongs to prior work

Minimum-norm allocation, core existence by convex duality, L1 norm embeddings, risk diversification and many sampling ingredients are established. Static fractional-core and Euler allocation are classical, as verified in Denault’s original. Young’s 1985 original supplies the exact five-player core-selection conflict. Csóka–Pintér’s later risk-allocation work gives its explicit fixed-owner report realization. We matched all coalition values and reproduced the decisive inequalities exactly.

The proposed contributions are the quantitative contracts, constructions and scoped obstructions proved here. Their historical novelty is still being checked. Different terminology alone does not establish a new result.

Key freely accessible originals: Moulin–Sprumont on demand responsiveness; Denault on coherent allocation; Csóka–Pintér on risk-allocation impossibility; Bretagnolle–Dacunha-Castelle–Krivine on norm embeddings. The linked comparisons identify exactly which definitions and theorems were checked.
