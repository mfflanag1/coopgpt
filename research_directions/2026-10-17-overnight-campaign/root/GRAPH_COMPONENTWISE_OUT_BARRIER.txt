INDEPENDENT TIGHT-COMPONENT PRICES CANNOT BEAT FACTOR TWO FOR FULL OUT
17 October 2026. Candidate structural obstruction; root derivation, review pending.

MODEL / ARCHITECTURE.
Use arbitrary unit-profit fractional resource graphs and fixed actual owners with
mixed bundles. Compute the established Huber tight components. In a strict
bipartite component let A>B be the aggregate capacities on its low/high sides,
t=B/A. The proposed payment architecture assigns price ell(t) to every low
resource and s(t) to every high resource, using the SAME two nonnegative
functions on every component and graph. Payments are actual bundles dot prices.
Assume ell,s locally absolutely continuous on(0,1), with limiting compatibility
ell(1)=s(1) when a balanced component is approached, and a uniform finite
funding factor kappa. No interaction between distinct tight components is permitted in this class.

THEOREM.
If this architecture covers coalitions and satisfies full coalition outside-
production protection on every profile, then kappa>=2. The utilization rule
ell(t)=t,s(t)=1 attains2, so the architecture has sharp factor2.
This does NOT prove2 is necessary for arbitrary graph mechanisms; that gap is
still[1+c,2]. It explains why applying the sharp two-pool logarithmic prices
independently to Huber components cannot improve the general graph upper.

PROOF.
Put L(t)=ell(t)/t. A single edge gives production B and payment B[L(t)+s(t)].
Hence
 L(t)+s(t)<=kappa,                    (1)
 tL(t)+s(t)>=1.                      (2)
The latter follows either from dual coverage under arbitrary split owners,
or directly from a coalition holding the same small positive quantity of both
resources. All prices are nonnegative. In particular L,s are bounded by kappa.
Holding B fixed and increasing the long resource A, the long owner's payment
is B L(B/A). Since full OUT implies ordinary own monotonicity, L is
nonincreasing as a function of t. Let L0=lim_(t downarrow0)L(t). Equations(1),(2)
give L0<=kappa-1.                       (3)

Now use the four-vertex path with edges (a,b),(a,d),(c,d). Capacities are
 a=A1,b=B1=t1 A1,c=A2,d=B2=t2 A2, with0<t2<t1<1.
Its Huber optimizer has two strict tight components (a,b) and(c,d): the cross
edge a--d has price sum t1/2+1-t2/2>1. Each isolated edge KKT flow is B_j,
so this is indeed the unique Huber optimizer of the whole path.
Explicitly the objective is sum_v c_v h(p_v), where h(p)=p^2 for p<=1/2
and h(p)=p-1/4 for p>=1/2. At p=(t1/2,1-t1/2,t2/2,1-t2/2), the edge
multipliers are lambda_ab=B1, lambda_cd=B2, lambda_ad=0. Stationarity is
c_v h'(p_v)=sum_incident lambda, giving(B1,B1,B2,B2) on both sides.
Each separate positive-capacity edge has a unique optimizer: its strictly
convex low-price part determines p_low=B/(2A), and its active cover equality
determines p_high. The sum of the two separate minimum objectives is a lower
bound on the path objective and this feasible point attains it, proving the
claimed uniqueness without assuming uniqueness of the ordinary LP dual.
Let one fixed coalition hold all of a and d and none of b,c. It can withhold
epsilon of BOTH a and d, producing epsilon outside via the cross edge a--d.
At a profile where ell,s are differentiable, the derivative of its payment
when these two resources are restored at unit rates is
 [ell(t1)-t1 ell'(t1)] + [s(t2)+t2 s'(t2)]
 = -t1^2 L'(t1) + (t2 s(t2))'.
The finite full-OUT condition therefore requires
 -t1^2 L'(t1)+(t2 s(t2))'>=1           (4)
for almost every pair0<t2<t1<1. All owners and their eligibility stay fixed;
two owners suffice for this test, one holding a,d and the other b,c.

For almost every t, both derivative functions have a Lebesgue point. Average
(4) over t1 in(t,t+epsilon) and t2 in(t-epsilon,t); then let epsilon decrease
to zero. Local absolute continuity makes the derivative functions locally
integrable. This yields the diagonal inequality
 (t s(t))'-t^2 L'(t)>=1 a.e.          (5)
Integrate from epsilon to1-delta and let epsilon,delta decrease to0. Boundedness
of L,s kills the zero endpoint terms; monotonicity of L gives its finite
limit L1 at1, and (5) plus boundedness gives the required terminal limit of
s (alternatively integrate to a sequence of Lebesgue endpoints). Integration
by parts gives
 s1-L1+2 integral_0^1 t L(t)dt >=1.   (6)
Balanced compatibility s1=L1 gives2 integral tL>=1,
so L0>=1 and kappa>=2 by(3).

ATTAINMENT DEPENDENCY.
The arbitrary-graph full-OUT property for ell=t,s=1 is proved independently
in root/GRAPH_OUTSIDE_TWO_CANDIDATE.txt, Sections1--3, accepted in root
adjudication08 after Fable011749 and root exact checks. Its key component
derivative is t(1-a/A)l+(1+a/A)h>=h. The rounded graph cover with values
0 on strict-low,1 on strict-high and1/2 on half-price vertices covers even
cross-component edges. Thus summing derivatives dominates outside production;
regularization handles zero capacities. The lower proof above does not rely
on this attainment theorem, only the assertion of sharpness does.

COMPATIBILITY IS NATURAL AND FOLLOWS FROM ALL-MIXED-OWNER TCWM.
For clarity it is an explicit hypothesis above. It can also be deduced from
all-profile TCWM with two fully eligible owners and homogeneous prices. For
an increase of coordinate j by delta, assign an arbitrarily small amount of
that coordinate plus all of any other coordinate k to the changing owner.
Monotonicity then bounds any decrease of price k by O(delta), because prices
are locally bounded by funding. The changing coordinate's own price is also
bounded below by the same-owner pure holding comparison. Applying such
coordinate increases to pass from either capacity vector to their coordinatewise
maximum gives one-sided price control; homogeneity allows a scalar dilation
of the smaller vector to dominate the larger one, giving the reverse control.
This is the established relative-continuity argument in the aggregate-price
reduction. Thus universal TCWM price fields are continuous on positive faces,
forcing the two single-edge role limits at balance to agree. The lower proof
uses only the explicitly stated compatibility, so it does not depend on
reproving that general representation lemma here.

EXPLICIT FAILURE OF THE BINARY LOGARITHMIC TRANSFORM.
For the sharp two-pool full-OUT law, ell(t)=c t and
 s(t)=E min(1,U/t), where U has density c/(1-u) on(0,t0),
 c=1/(1+t0),t0=0.84140566... . For t>t0, s(t)=c/t.
Thus both terms in(4) vanish whenever t1>t2>t0. A coalition holding a,d
can restore a small equal amount of both, obtaining positive outside value,
while its transformed payment is locally constant. This gives an exact
symbolic counterexample throughout an open region; no numerical equality is
needed. For rational t1=19/20,t2=9/10 and sufficiently small restoration,
the payment is c(B1+A2), independent of a,d. The general sharp two-pool law
therefore fails full OUT even on the four-vertex path under this componentwise
extension. It remains TCWM as expected, so this is specifically an outside-
production obstruction.

A concrete finite deviation uses full capacities(a,b,c,d)=(20,19,10,9),
coalition ownerI holding a,d and ownerII holding b,c. OwnerI withholds1/10
of both resources, giving low capacities(199/10,19,10,89/10). At both
profiles the component ratios lie above t0 and satisfy t1>t2. The owner's
payment is29c at BOTH profiles, but its withheld bundle has production1/10.
All ownership and public production data are unchanged across the deviation.
