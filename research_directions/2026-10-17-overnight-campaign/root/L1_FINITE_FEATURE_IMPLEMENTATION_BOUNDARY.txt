WHY FIXED FINITE PROJECTIONS DO NOT IMPLEMENT THE SHARP SMOOTH-NORM RULE
17 October2026. Elementary implementation boundary, novelty not asserted.

Take a strictly convex norm on a real space of dimension at least2. Suppose
one replaces the exact L1 integral in the compensated rule by any fixed
finite list of linear scalar features ell_1,...,ell_M, with arbitrary
nonnegative weights. Keep the actual standalone reserve term
 kappa(t_i-||x_i||), and at each feature use a nonnegative two-input
mechanism whose total is zero when either aggregate input is zero.

Then this finite-feature architecture fails coalition coverage already with
two fixed owners. Ignore identically zero features. The complement of the
finitely many feature hyperplanes contains an open cone. Choose distinct,
noncollinear unit vectors u,v in that cone. Every feature has the same
strict sign on u and v, so every auxiliary two-input game has one empty
side and all its payments vanish. Give the owners bundles(1,u),(1,v).
Their standalone reserve terms also vanish. Total payment is zero, while
strict convexity gives F(2,u+v)=2-||u+v||>0. Coverage fails.

This is a failure of that specific direct quadrature substitution. It is
not a lower bound for arbitrary finite algorithms or other mechanisms.
For two owners, for example, the already-known efficient rule fi+G/2
works and does not need this representation. Nor does the obstruction
invalidate the exact integrated mechanism.

For the Euclidean Gaussian representation, two unit vectors with angle
theta in(0,pi) have oppositely signed Gaussian projections with probability
theta/pi. With M independent sampled directions, the probability that no
sample sees both signs is(1-theta/pi)^M. On this event the same naive
sampled mechanism pays zero despite positive grand production. As theta
decreases, no fixed M gives a uniform ex-post coverage guarantee. For the
Gaussian probability representation, a normalized average of fresh iid,
report-independent directions is unbiased for each owner's integrable
auxiliary payment. Its expected payment is therefore exactly the ideal
integral plus the unchanged deterministic standalone term. Linearity of
expectation preserves every fixed-profile incentive and coverage inequality
IN EXPECTATION. This is a different contract from deterministic/ex-post
coverage; it does not justify any guarantee conditional on the sampled seed.

The appropriate computational next task is certified evaluation of the
ideal integral, or a separately proved finite mechanism with explicit
approximate-incentive/coverage guarantees. Merely reporting Monte Carlo
funding ratios cannot establish the original all-profile theorem.
