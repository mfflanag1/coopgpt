THE CAPPED-UTILIZATION CLASS HAS ASYMPTOTICALLY SHARP CUBE-ROOT FUNDING
17 October2026. Root derivation; independent audit pending.

MODEL AND CLASS.
Fix M>=2 and F(P,a,b)=min(P,sum_j min(a_j,b_j)). Put
g_j=min(P,a_j,b_j), G=sum_j g_j, r=P/G.
Consider aggregate resource-price rules
 p_P=h(r), p_aj=f(r)g_j/a_j, p_bj=f(r)g_j/b_j,
where h,f>=0 are fixed functions, common to all profiles and actual owners.
Owner payments are their reported bundles dotted with these prices. Assume f
is locally absolutely continuous on(1/(M-1),infinity) and right-continuous
at1/(M-1). Assume all-profile coalition coverage, TCWM for arbitrary fixed
mixed ownership, and uniform total funding at most kappa*F. Owner/resource
identities remain fixed during every incentive comparison. The same public
functions must work across the ownership assignments used below; equivalently
take two fully eligible actual owners and vary their reports. One owner is
the test coalition in each construction and the other holds the remainder;
both remain eligible for every resource throughout. Reports of a fixed owner
can contain different resource mixtures at different profiles. No identity or
eligibility changes are needed, and no bound on latent truthful stocks is used.

THEOREM.
 kappa^3 >= 16(M-1)/27.                                      (1)
Together with composition/COMMON_CAP_CUBE_ROOT_UPPER.txt, this matches both
the exponent1/3 and leading constant(16/27)^(1/3) within this stated class.
It is NOT a lower bound for all mechanisms on this production family.

1. NECESSARY ELASTICITY FROM A MIXED COALITION.
Let r>r0=1/(M-1), normalize P=1, and choose any small epsilon>0 with
epsilon<1/r and (1/r-epsilon)/(M-1)<1. Set branch1 to a1=epsilon,b1=2.
Set every other branch's two stocks to u=(1/r-epsilon)/(M-1), so each g=u.
Let coalition S hold all stocks on branches2..M and all of a1, but none of
P or b1. Its payment is f(r)Z with Z=2G-epsilon. Restore a1 infinitesimally,
holding outsiders and other reports fixed. Then G'=1, Z'=1, r'=-r/G.
At every r where f is differentiable, TCWM requires
 f(r)-r f'(r)(2-epsilon/G)>=0.
The argument holds for arbitrarily small epsilon at this SAME r, hence
 f(r)-2r f'(r)>=0 almost everywhere on(r0,infinity).          (2)
There is no term involving h: S owns no power. Integrating the absolutely
continuous derivative of f(r)/sqrt(r) gives
 f(r0)>=f(R)*sqrt(r0/R) for every R>r0.                       (3)
Right-continuity at r0 justifies taking the endpoint limit. This is a
necessary condition for the stated class, not merely a sufficient derivative
test imposed on its construction.

2. COVERAGE AT AN IMBALANCED ONE-BRANCH PROFILE.
For any R>1 take P=1,a1=1/R,b1=L>1, all other branches absent. Then G=1/R,
r=R and total funding is R h(R)+2f(R) times F. Nonnegativity therefore gives
h(R)<=kappa/R. A coalition can hold a small equal quantity delta>0 of P,a1,b1;
its worth is delta and its price payment is delta[h(R)+f(R)+f(R)/(R L)].
Full coalition coverage for arbitrarily large finite L implies
 h(R)+f(R)>=1, hence f(R)>=1-kappa/R.                        (4)
This compares distinct admissible profiles to constrain the same h,f; it
does not change owners or technology inside a strategic deviation. Fixed
finite report-bound versions follow by choosing a sufficiently large bound
for each finite witness; the theorem concerns a uniform architecture over
all report profiles.

3. FUNDING AT THE CROWDED PROFILE.
Take P=1, M-1 branches with both stocks1, and one absent branch. Then
G=M-1, r=r0, F=1. Total funding is h(r0)+2(M-1)f(r0), so
kappa>=2(M-1)f(r0).                                         (5)
Coverage implies kappa>=1. Set R=3kappa>r0 in(3)--(4); then f(R)>=2/3.
Combining with(5) gives
 kappa>=4sqrt(M-1)/(3sqrt(3kappa)),
which is exactly(1).

4. MATCHING ARCHITECTURE AND CONSEQUENCE.
The separately audited candidate construction chooses
h=min(1,C/r), f=min(1,2sqrt(r)/(3sqrt(3C))),
C=max(1,(16M/27)^(1/3)), and has factor<=C+2. Thus dividing lower and upper
by M^(1/3) yields the same limiting constant. Improving the general-family
rate below cube root requires leaving this shared scalar capped-utilization
class, or dropping one of the stated incentives/coverage requirements.
Possible alternatives include reserve payments to unproductive branches and
prices depending on more than the scalar P/G. No such improvement is claimed.

SCOPE.
This class assumes nonnegative aggregate prices and regularity of f. It does
not cover arbitrary owner-dependent payments or irregular rules. No historical
novelty, full outside-production protection, sharp additive constant, or
field-level breakthrough is inferred. The general TCWM lower on the same
family remains only square-root logarithmic; this theorem cannot replace it.
