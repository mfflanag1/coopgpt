PRIMARY-SOURCE COMPARISON: CROSS-MONOTONIC PROFIT SHARING
17 October2026. Free original inspected through author-hosted PDF.

Nicole Immorlica, Mohammad Mahdian, Vahab S. Mirrokni,
Limitations of cross-monotonic cost-sharing schemes.
Author original: https://immorlica.com/pubs/costshare.pdf
Author bibliography confirms ACM Transactions on Algorithms2008 and SODA2005:
https://immorlica.com/research.htm
Relevant original: Section3.5, Definition3.3, Theorems3.6 and3.7, printedpp11–12;
Section4.1 and footnote7, printedp13. Also thesis Chapter4, pp116–118:
https://immorlica.com/pubs/PhDthesis.pdf

The original defines profit-sharing cross-monotonicity as each existing agent's
payment increasing when the participating set expands. It proves linear funding
lower bounds for maximum-flow and matching profit-sharing schemes. The flow
example already uses two parallel-edge pools in series, and the matching example
uses complete bipartite graphs. Its mechanism-design section studies reported
service values and explicitly excludes side payments within deviating coalitions.

PROJECT COMPARISON / INFERENCE.
This is direct prior art for the project's globally coordinate-monotone-payment
barrier. Do NOT present linear funding costs of that stronger monotonicity
requirement as a new general phenomenon. Our sharp(n+1)/2 all-real mixed-owner
statement may refine its domain/constant, but novelty is unconfirmed.
The new rank-three theorem uses weaker aggregate coalition-payment monotonicity
under binding capacity reports. It permits losses to individual outsiders when
someone contributes more; this is why rank-two constant funding is compatible
with their graph lower bounds. Their inspected theorems do not themselves prove
our rank-three separation. This is a model comparison, not a comprehensive
priority clearance. The same distinction applies to generic group-strategyproof
cost-sharing results: their private service-value reports and no-side-payment
notion must not be silently identified with our capacity reports and transferable
coalition payments.
