Hochbaum--Megiddo--Naor--Tamir (1993), Tight Bounds and 2-Approximation
Algorithms for Integer Programs with Two Variables per Inequality.
Original: https://theory.stanford.edu/~megiddo/pdf/2satful.pdf
Root read sections2--3. The paper doubles variables to obtain monotone
inequalities, then uses integer-level copies and threshold arcs in a closure/
minimum-cut formulation. These are established predecessors of threshold-copy
constructions. The text read concerns integer/half-integral systems, rather
than our finite continuous all-capacity identity with same-owner linked copies.
That distinction is a comparison, not proof of novelty for the new reduction.

Moulin--Sethuraman (2013), The Bipartite Rationing Problem.
Author original: https://www.columbia.edu/~js1353/pubs/consistentflowA6.pdf
Root read model/introduction and theorem overview. Its proportional extension
uses residual claims and a consistency requirement. Its Figure2 allocation
is not the normalized-utilization allocation here: equal source capacities
2,2,2 and sink capacities1,2 permit our equal source usages1,1,1, whereas the
paper reports unequal shares. This rules out identification with that specific
proportional rule, not with all established weighted lexicographic flow rules.
Those require a separate original-source comparison.

Debreu--Scarf (1963), A Limit Theorem on the Core of an Economy.
Free original scan: https://ucema.edu.ar/~fr05/curso_corto/Debreu-Scarf.pdf
Root read assumptions, Theorems1--3 and the productive extension in section4.
The paper derives competitive prices for allocations surviving every replica
level and includes a convex-cone production extension. It is incorrect to
describe it as pure exchange only. Our proposed theorem concerns existence of
one subsidized incentive-compatible payment field across reports, using
finite-owner arrays and dilation averaging; the replica-core result does not
state that theorem. Replication leading to prices is established background.
