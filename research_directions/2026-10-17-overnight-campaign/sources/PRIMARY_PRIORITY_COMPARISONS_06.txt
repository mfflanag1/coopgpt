ADDITIONAL ORIGINAL-SOURCE BOUNDARIES
17 October2026. Freely available material only; novelty remains unconfirmed.

OVERLAPPING COALITIONS.
Zick, Markakis and Elkind, Arbitration and Stability in Cooperative Games
with Overlapping Coalitions (2014), original author PDF:
https://people.cs.umass.edu/~yzick/papers/ocf_theoretical.pdf
Root read introduction, Definition3.1, Theorem4.1 and Section6. Partial resource
withdrawal and continued participation in other coalitions are established
subjects. Their arbitration functions depend on the original outcome, deviators
and deviation, and impose accountability conditions on retained payments.
Our recomputed all-report payment rule with external funding is a different
object. An exact equivalence has not been proved. Their question about generous
stable arbitration is not declared solved by our full-OUT theorem.

PROFIT SHARING.
Justin Leroux, Profit sharing in unique Nash equilibrium: characterization
in the two-agent case (2006 working paper), original:
https://www.hec.ca/iea/cahiers/2006/iea0611_jleroux.pdf
Root checked the two-agent model and equilibrium requirement. It uses a scalar
strictly concave production function, labor/output preferences and efficient
sharing, with a unique-equilibrium criterion. Those are different primitives
from fixed capacity owners, complementarity, binding underreports and subsidy.
This comparison does not certify historical novelty of our mechanism.

CORE MONOTONICITY.
van Zon, Spliet and van den Heuvel, The Effect of Algorithmic Capabilities on
Cooperative Games, EI2021-02, original:
https://repub.eur.nl/pub/135596/EI2021-02.pdf
Root checked Section5.2 and Theorem5.2 after independent delegate reading.
The paper studies one player's algorithm improvement in cost games, and
extreme allocations within efficient cores. Its stated monotonicity condition
protects that player when coalition costs containing it decrease. The checked
theorem supplies a bound on changes of a worst core allocation under an explicit
minimum-improvement assumption. It does not directly yield our quantitative
cover-majorant obstruction or our hub-dependent local upper bound.

Young (1985), Monotonic solutions of cooperative games, DOI10.1007/BF01769885,
and Housman--Clark (1998), Core and monotonic allocation methods: bodies were
NOT obtained. Abstracts and later restatements are secondary evidence for the
precise core-impossibility statements. Do not state that their full papers
lack subsidy results. The threshold in van Zon appears inconsistent with later
restatements; that is not a root-verified defect in Young's original theorem.

Izquierdo--Rafels (2002), Coalitionally Monotonic Set-solutions for Cooperative
TU Games, free original working paper:
https://hdl.handle.net/2445/11760
The delegate and opposite-model QA checked the four-player set-valued
impossibility (definitions and Theorem1). Root's direct URL fetch failed; this
original inspection is independently reported, not falsely labeled root-read.
Its full-TU-game domain, efficient core selection and coalition-worth changes
differ from our structured resource-report domain and funding relaxation.
Equivalence of one-coalition and one-player monotonicity notions by chaining
requires the solution to exist at every intermediate game. It must not be
silently applied to a domain containing only nonempty-core games.

VERIFIED CONCLUSION AND OPEN COMPARISONS.
None of the checked theorem statements directly subsumes the two supplied
quantitative claims. Young and Housman--Clark originals, Arin's later monotone
core work, and Producer incentives in cost allocation remain priority gaps.
The global beta<2 cover-majorant obstruction and the local hub-specific star
mechanism must be kept separate. Fable's literature draft overclaimed what
unread originals did not contain; Codex QA identified this and root adopted
the qualifications above. Failure to find prior work is not evidence of novelty.
