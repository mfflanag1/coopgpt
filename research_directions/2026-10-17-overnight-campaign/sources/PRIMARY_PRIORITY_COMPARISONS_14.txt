MOULIN–SPRUMONT, ON DEMAND RESPONSIVENESS IN ADDITIVE COST SHARING
Original manuscript24 May2004, Section2 printedp6; Proposition2 printedp12;
Proposition5 printedp18 and its appendix proof.
https://www.ruf.rice.edu/~econ/papers/2004papers/dmdrespr41.pdf
Their Strong Group Monotonicity requires a fixed coalition's aggregate cost
share not to decrease when one member increases its scalar demand; other
members may remain unchanged. This is the scalar-demand counterpart of our
TCWM inequality. Their economic direction is demand inflation under costs;
ours is resource withholding under receipts. The mathematical condition must
be credited, not presented as a new axiom.
Proposition2 excludes additive, separable, exactly balanced methods on their
full monotone integer-cost domain when n>=3. It does not establish our sharp
subsidy bounds on homogeneous concave production with coalition coverage.
Proposition5 concerns distinct-user externalities of its discrete Aumann–Shapley
method. It alone does not prove continuous whole-vector DR antitonicity; that
ingredient is explicit in the separate original below. The proposed new work
is the common/private restoration construction and quantitative bound.
This does not resolve the separate inaccessible Moulin1999 original gate.

OLIVEIRA–SINGH–SINGLA, PHILOSOPHER AND PROPHET INEQUALITIES FOR DIVISIBLE ITEMS
Original preprint2026, arXiv2607.11742v1, Section4.1 Lemma4.2 and proof.
https://arxiv.org/html/2607.11742v1
The paper explicitly uses q(x)=integral_0^1 grad v(tx)dt for monotone DR
valuations normalized by v(0)=0, proves x.q(x)=v(x), and obtains q(x)<=q(y) when y<=x.
Its dominated-bundle support is y.q(x)<=v(y), the opposite inequality to a
coalition payment cover. It studies online divisible-item welfare/prices,
not our all-profile production withholding contract. No direct implication
of the two-block3/2 mechanism was found in the inspected lemma. This is a
specific ingredient comparison, not an exhaustive paper-level priority search.
