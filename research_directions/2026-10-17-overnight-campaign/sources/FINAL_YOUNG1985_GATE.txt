YOUNG: EXACT FACTS INSPECTED
Page 66 defines allocation procedures to satisfy sum_i phi_i(v)=v(N).
Page 68, equation (5), requires phi_i(v)>=phi_i(w) when coalition values
containing i increase and all values of coalitions excluding i remain fixed.
Equation (4) changes one coalition value and protects each of its members.
Page 69, Theorem 1, proves that no core selector satisfies this coalitional
monotonicity on games with at least five players. Its five-player proof uses
the following nonzero generating coalition values:
  S1={3,5}:3; S2={1,2,3}:3; S3={1,3,4}:9;
  S4={2,4,5}:9; S5={1,2,4,5}:9; N:11.
Other coalitions take the maximum value of their contained generating sets,
or zero. The second game changes only S5 and N to 12. Unique core allocations
change from (0,1,2,7,1) to (3,0,0,6,3).
Page 70, Theorem 2, characterizes Shapley by symmetry and strong monotonicity;
efficiency is part of the allocation-procedure definition. Page 72 discusses
an efficient Aumann-Shapley analogue. No subsidized funding-factor theorem is
stated in this article.

OUR DIRECT COMPARISON
I compared the scanned Young coalition table against every coalition value
reconstructed from Csoka-Pinter Table 3 by the already-existing
root/check_csoka_pinter_antecedent.py. All 31 nonempty coalition values agree
exactly at BOTH profiles. Young's low game corresponds to their Y profile;
Young's high game corresponds to their X profile. Their variable names for the
games differ; comparing the numeric profiles avoids an attribution mix-up.

Thus the old five-player core conflict is an exact antecedent, not merely a
similar example. Young already gives the individual own-local
characteristic-function monotonicity conflict. Csoka-Pinter supply the
explicit fixed-owner state-payoff report realization, which embeds as
F(c)=min_{r<=19}c_r on our nonnegative resource domain. Restoring owner 2's
resource forces its efficient payment from 1 down to 0. The basic incompatibility
must not be presented as an overnight or project-original discovery.

Young's use of "coalitional monotonicity" does NOT denote our TCWM. His
equation (5) protects an individual's allocation under specified changes of
coalition worths. TCWM instead protects the TOTAL payment of a fixed reporting
coalition under feasible restorations of its owned bundles, including its
unchanged members. Full OUT additionally requires compensation for production
from the withdrawn bundle. A name match is not a theorem transfer.

The article's core and allocation-procedure conditions impose exact efficiency.
Our funded covers permit payments above production and ask for quantitative
upper/lower factors under explicit report contracts. The inspected Young
results therefore do not themselves supply our universal TCWM/full-OUT sharp
factors. This is a statement about this source's actual theorem scope, not a
claim that those factors are historically new or absent from other literature.
