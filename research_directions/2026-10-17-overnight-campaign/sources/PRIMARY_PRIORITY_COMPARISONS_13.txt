PRIMARY PRIORITY COMPARISONS13 — RISK AND POPULATION CONTRACTS
17 October2026. Root-verified corrections to the bounded literature delegation.

1. Immorlica, Mahdian, Mirrokni, Limitations of cross-monotonic cost-sharing
schemes, author-hosted original (SODA2005 / journal version).
https://immorlica.com/pubs/costshare.pdf
Root read Definitions2.1,2.4,3.3, the Section3.5 scope statement, and the
complete Theorems3.6–3.7 arguments. The profit contract has coalition coverage
and a multiplicative payment cap, but cross-monotonicity protects each
continuing owner's payment when OTHER owners join. The matching example on
a complete bipartite graph has value min(number-left,number-right) and forces
a linear factor. TCWM instead protects the changing coalition's own total
under its resource restoration with outsiders fixed. These are different
axioms. The published theorem's short sentence omits 'cross-monotonic', but
the section's explicit scope and proof include it. Do not quote that sentence
as an unrestricted mechanism impossibility. The source supplies a particularly
close same-technology, different-contract comparison; it does not imply the
proposed sharp3/2 or1+c withholding bounds.

2. Csoka, Herings, Koczy, Stable allocations of risk, GEB67(2009),266–276.
https://cris.maastrichtuniversity.nl/ws/portalfiles/portal/64776577/Herings_2009_stable_allocations_of_risk.pdf
Root read Definition2.10 and Proposition3.1 with its full proof. Definition
2.10 defines v(C)=-rho(X(C));2.11 is the separate no-aggregate-uncertainty
condition. Proposition3.1 proves total balancedness by subadditivity and
positive homogeneity. The same proof adapts to F(t,x)=t-||x|| for ANY norm,
establishing fixed-profile core existence. Calling ||x||-t a coherent risk
measure on their ordered state-payoff domain requires a representation; it
is not supplied merely by naming the expression. Their fixed-profile theorem
does not compare allocations recalculated after reports change. Efficient
static coverage is established prior work, not the proposed quantitative
incentive contribution. Their theorem also cites Denault's earlier balancedness.

3. Thomson, New variable-population paradoxes for resource allocation,
RCER575,2012, author-hosted original.
https://www.sas.rochester.edu/eco/rcer/papers/rcer_575.pdf
Root read Section3's formal withdrawal condition and surrounding model. One
agent exits with its whole endowment; the allocation rule is recalculated on
the smaller population; the exiting owner may divide its endowment plus a
remaining owner's new allocation with that owner. Feasibility concerns goods
and preferences, with no multiplicative TU subsidy theorem. This is a useful
population-exit comparison, not an implication of or substitute for fixed-owner
partial-resource TCWM or outside-production protection.
