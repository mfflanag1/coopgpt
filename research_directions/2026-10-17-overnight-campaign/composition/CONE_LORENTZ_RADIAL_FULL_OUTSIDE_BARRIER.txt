A VARIATIONAL STOPPING BARRIER FOR CONSTANT-RESERVE RADIAL FULL-OUT RULES
17 October 2026. Exact restricted-architecture consequence; novelty unconfirmed.

CLASS
In exposure dimension d>=2, consider the same all-profile fixed-owner Lorentz
model, with at least two actual owners and payments

 y_i=lambda[t_i-a(s)<x_i,e>],  s=||X||/T, e=X/||X||.

Here lambda>0 is a public constant, a is continuous on[0,1], locally absolutely
continuous in(0,1), a(0)=0, and C^1 near1. The rule has every-coalition coverage,
full coalition outside-production protection, and finite uniform total funding
factor kappa. This includes the refined curve, but does not include variable
reserve coefficients, arbitrary nonradial fields, or owner-dependent rules.
No monotonicity assumption on a is needed for the lower bound.

Then, with phi=(1+sqrt5)/2,

 kappa >= 24 phi log(18)/(4 phi+24log(18)-13) > 1.786.   (1)

Thus the currently proved upper below1.836 and this lower enclose a genuine
scalar feasibility problem. This is not an unrestricted mechanism lower.

PROOF
Full OUT implies TCWM, since all withdrawal values are nonnegative. Coverage
forces |a(s)|<=1 in interior totals by inserting arbitrarily small boundary
coalition bundles. Finite funding forces a(1)=1. The regular radial TCWM
boundary proof in CONE_LORENTZ_PHI_COALITION_THEOREM.txt gives a'(1)>=c,
c=(sqrt5-1)/2, and hence

                    kappa>=lambda phi.                (2)

At the forward ellipse endpoint h=(1+s)/2, take a boundary restoration
parallel to the total exposure. The TCWM derivative criterion gives

 1-a(s)-(1-s^2)a'(s)/2 >=0.

Gronwall integration from a(0)=0 yields

                    a(s)<=2s/(1+s).                   (3)

At the opposite ellipse endpoint h=(s-1)/2, restore pure reserve (dt,dx)=(1,0).
Its outside value is1. Full OUT therefore necessarily gives

 lambda[1-s(1-s)a'(s)/2]>=1,
 a'(s)<=2(1-1/lambda)/[s(1-s)].                        (4)

This is a necessary inequality even if a' is negative. At a central total,
pure-reserve restoration also gives lambda>=1. Integrate (4) from1/7 to3/4:

 a(3/4)-a(1/7)<=2(1-1/lambda)log(18).                 (5)

Equation(3) gives a(1/7)<=1/4. The funding bound at s=3/4 gives
 a(3/4)>=4/3-kappa/(3lambda).
Substitution into(5) yields

             kappa>=6log(18)-lambda[6log(18)-13/4].    (6)

The increasing affine lower bound (2) and decreasing affine lower bound (6)
intersect at lambda=6log(18)/(phi+6log(18)-13/4). Their maximum is at least
the intersection value, which is exactly(1). Every derivative test above
is realized by two fixed cone owners on the indicated ellipse boundary;
restoration by any cone direction is feasible from their existing report.
No changing owner identity or null-payment hypothesis is used.

The logarithm arises from an integrated necessary incentive constraint, not
a numerical fit. An exact rational atanh-series bracket for log18 together
with arithmetic in Q(sqrt5) verifies the displayed lower1.786. Optimizing
endpoints1/7 and3/4 could modestly improve this architecture bound but would
not change its scope, and is not pursued here.
