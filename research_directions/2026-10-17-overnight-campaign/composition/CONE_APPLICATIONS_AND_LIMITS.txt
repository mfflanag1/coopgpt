1. SIGNED RESERVE ENTITLEMENTS
For m public periods let
 K={ (r,e) in R x R^m : r>=||e||_infinity },
 F(r,e)=r-||e||_infinity=min_{j,sign}(r+sign*e_j).
This is a closed pointed polyhedral cone of dimensionm+1, with2^m extreme
rays when m>=1. F is finite, nonnegative, concave and homogeneous. A bundle
can be read abstractly as reserve r backing a signed vector of net delivery
obligations e, and F as remaining uniform reserve after the largest absolute
net obligation. Opposite signs may cancel when bundles pool. For example,
(1,+1) and(1,-1) have standalone values0 but pooled value2.

A binding withdrawal is a pair d,b-d both in K. It may change signed delivery
coordinates in either direction, but must leave both pieces reserve-backed.
This is NOT coordinatewise withholding of each signed number. The theorem's
dimension is m+1, never the exponential number of extreme rays. Here F also
has2m explicit linear forms, so the separately available finite-form bound
can be stronger and should be compared before choosing the public rule.

This is a precise abstract entitlement model. It does not validate prices,
physical dispatch, intertemporal storage, private opportunity costs, or the
right to offset different parties' contractual obligations. Those institutional
and engineering assumptions must be supplied before calling it an energy-market
mechanism. Arbitrary signed reports without the fixed cone/deviation constraint
are not covered.

2. POSITIVE-SEMIDEFINITE INFORMATION BUNDLES
Let K=S_+^m be the cone of real symmetric positive-semidefinite matrices, with
dimension D=m(m+1)/2. Owner i reports an additive information contribution X_i.
A binding withdrawal D_i satisfies0<=D_i<=X_i in the Loewner order. Actual
feasible data deletions may be a subset of this larger mathematical domain.
The theorem therefore protects those deletions provided the matrix reports
are truthful, additive, verified contributions in a fixed statistical model.

Take F(X)=det(X)^(1/m), with value0 at singular matrices. Its required
properties have a short self-contained proof:

 det(X)^(1/m)=(1/m) inf{tr(A X): A positive definite, det(A)=1}.

AM-GM applied to the eigenvalues of A^(1/2) X A^(1/2) proves the lower bound.
For X positive definite equality holds at A=det(X)^(1/m) X^(-1). For X of
rankr<m, diagonalize X and set A=epsilon on its range and
epsilon^(-r/(m-r)) on its kernel; det A=1 and the trace tends to0. Thus F is
an infimum of nonnegative linear forms, hence finite, concave, homogeneous,
and Loewner-monotone. The cone theorem gives gain-only funding coefficient
min{D,n sqrt(2/(n+1)),1+2sqrt(D+1)} with individual outside protection.

This is genuinely outside finite orthant resource coordinates: S_+^m has
infinitely many extreme rays for m>=2, whereas a fixed finite positive linear
image of an orthant is polyhedral. The proof uses matrix-space dimension,
not a discretization of rank-one rays or of supporting eigenvectors.

D-optimal experimental design maximizes the determinant of an information
matrix; the positive1/m power has the same maximizers for fixed m. NIST's
official design handbook states that determinant criterion and emphasizes
its dependence on the chosen statistical model:
 https://itl.nist.gov/div898/handbook/pri/section5/pri521.htm
The page was directly read17October2026. This supports the statistical
connection, not a new strategic or monetary theorem.

The concrete economic contract remains assumed: transferable payments measured
in a public value scale proportional to F, verified additive matrix ownership,
and binding withholding only. Determinant information is not automatically
money, data cannot automatically be sold or shared, and the result establishes
neither privacy, model robustness, identification, nor empirical learning gain.
No incentive protection against falsified matrices or strategic identities is
asserted.

3. E-OPTIMAL / WORST-DIRECTION INFORMATION
The related F(X)=lambda_min(X) also fits the cone theorem. It has a stronger
matrix-order refinement in CONE_PSD_MIN_EIGENVALUE_GAIN_BOUND.txt: the gain
coefficient depends on m through min{m,n sqrt(2/(n+1)),1+2sqrt(m)}, rather than
on ambient dimensionD. The key extra property is the exact scalar-identity
shift lambda_min(X+tI)=lambda_min(X)+t. General concave matrix criteria such
as determinant need not have that residual decomposition. Noncommuting owner
matrices are allowed; no common eigenbasis is assumed.
