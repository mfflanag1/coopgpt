1. EXACT CONTRACT AND FIXED-MASK FOUNDATION

For a closed pointed cone K of dimension d=dim(span K), F is finite,
nonnegative, concave and homogeneous of degree one. Superadditivity implies
cone-order monotonicity. Boundary continuity is not assumed. Fixed actual
owners report b_i in K; an individual withdrawal d_i must satisfy both
d_i in K and b_i-d_i in K. Let T=F(sum b_i), f_i=F(b_i), S=sum f_i, G=T-S.
Superadditivity gives G>=0.

For each fixed owner mask B, open owners receive the marginal
F(C_B)-F(C_B-b_i). Closed owners receive
min{L_(C_B)(b_i), T-sum_(h!=i)f_h}, where L is a support chosen once on the
minimal face of C_B and extended as infinity outside that face. The cap is
f_i+G. Supports majorize F on their faces, and every retained bundle is in
the chosen face by its facial property.

The coalition-coverage argument checks: if a closed coalition member hits
its cap, the other members' standalone floors supply coverage by
superadditivity. Otherwise all closed prices are finite; the open marginals
dominate the same support prices, so summing them covers coalition worth.

Individual OUT also checks directly in ORIGINAL reports. An open owner's
off term is fixed. A closed owner's selected support excludes it and remains
fixed; both its finite raw price and cap increase by at least F(d_i).
Their minimum therefore does too. If an owner is outside the selected face
at its low report, it remains outside at the high report: a sum in a face
would force its summands into that face. Thus both payments use the cap.
If only the high report leaves the face, comparing the high cap with the
low cap gives the same inequality. These cases require neither global finite
supports nor continuity at a zero face.

The result protects individual deviations while covering EVERY coalition.
It does NOT protect coordinated coalition reports: another changing owner
could alter a closed owner's training support. This distinction must remain
explicit in the headline and comparison with stronger coalition theorems.

2. EXACT TRAINING IDENTITY AND DIMENSION d+1

At one fixed profile, define w(A)=F(b(A))-sum_(i in A)f_i. It is monotone,
nonnegative, with w(empty)=0 and w(N)=G. For finite supports, the selected
residual vector is a_i(B)=L_(C_B)(b_i)-f_i, with

 a(B)>=0,       sum_(j in B)a_j(B)=w(B).

For extended supports the cone proof supplies a finite surrogate preserving
this EXACT equality and all capped closed entries. I checked its separation
step: for the minimal face H and W=span H, K intersect W=H. The finitely
many outside-face owner bundles have nonzero projections whose convex hull
does not contain zero. A functional vanishing on W is strictly positive on
all those projected bundles. Adding a sufficiently large multiple to an
extension of the face support makes their residuals at least G, while leaving
all in-face scores and the training equality unchanged. No global exposed-
face assumption or positive functional on every point of K is needed.

All such vectors belong to the row space of the d-by-n owner-bundle matrix
augmented by the standalone vector f. Hence one common profilewise subspace
has dimension at most d+1. The extra vector cannot simply be discarded.
This surrogate is for FUNDING ANALYSIS ONLY; it is not a new, report-selected
support used in the incentive rule. Own-bit conditioning needs a vector for
each mask at the fixed profile, not statistical independence of its entries.

3. REFINED CAPPED TRACE LEMMA

Retain each owner with probability p=1-q. Closed excess C(q) satisfies the
exact conditioning identity

 C(q)=(q/p) E_B sum_(i in B) min{a_i(B\i),G}.

For a fixed retained B, put x_i=a_i(B\i) and
h_i=sum_(j in B\i)a_j(B\i)=w(B\i)<=w(B). For nonnegative x,h,G,

 min{x,G}<=(G+h)x/(x+h),

with zero value when x+h=0. If x<=G, subtracting x gives
x(G-x)/(x+h)>=0; if x>=G, subtracting G gives h(x-G)/(x+h)>=0.
Normalize each nonzero column a(B\i)|_B by x_i+h_i. The matrix is nonnegative,
column-substochastic, and of rank at most D. Thus its eigenvalues have modulus
at most1, the number of nonzero eigenvalues counting algebraic multiplicity
is at most its rank, and its nonnegative trace is at most D. Consequently

 sum_(i in B)min{x_i,G}<=[G+w(B)]D,
 C(q)<=D(q/p)[G+g(q)],             g(q)=E w(B).

The exact equality h_i=w(B\i) is load-bearing. The earlier lemma with only
training sum<=G gives2DGq/p; by itself it cannot produce the new g(q) term
or the density cancellation. The refined note correctly uses the stronger
identity supplied by its actual mechanism.

4. PSD SPECIALIZATION: WHY THE INDEX IS m

For real PSD matrices X_i of order m, set f_i=lambda_min(X_i) and
U_i=X_i-f_i I>=0. Every coalition has the exact residual identity
F(sum_A X_i)=sum_A f_i+lambda_min(U_A). A fixed minimum-eigenspace selector
invariant under scalar identity shifts selects the same support on the
original and residual masked sums. This equality is used only at the fixed
profile for funding. No Loewner monotonicity of X_i->U_i is assumed.

For i in B, let P_i be the selected PSD unit-trace test matrix on U_(B\i).
Then x_i=Tr(P_i U_i), h_i=lambda_min(U_(B\i)), and
x_i+h_i=Tr(P_i U_B). Since0<=U_i<=U_B,

 x_i/(x_i+h_i)
 <=lambda_max(U_B^(dagger/2) U_i U_B^(dagger/2))
 <=Tr(U_B^(dagger/2) U_i U_B^(dagger/2)).

The inverse square roots act on range(U_B). Zero denominator implies zero
numerator; it contributes0. The range inclusion follows from U_i<=U_B, so
singular masked sums cause no loss. Summing the traces gives rank(U_B)<=m,
even though the selected P_i differ and the matrices need not commute.
The refined trace argument therefore uses D=m, not m(m+1)/2 or m+1.

This does not identify m with the number of supporting forms; the eigenvector
support family is infinite. The diagonal finite-form logarithmic bound cannot
be imported by declaring that there are only m eigenvectors across all reports.

5. NORMALIZATION AND THE FACTOR OF TWO

The open excess expectation is exactly -p g'(q), by differentiating the finite
Bernoulli-mask polynomial g. Hence R(q)=C(q)-p g'(q). Define

 h_D(q)=p^(-D-1)exp(-Dq/p),    Z_D=integral_0^1 h_D(q)dq.

Direct differentiation gives h_D'/h_D=1/p-Dq/p^2 and
(p h_D)'=-D(q/p)h_D. Thus integration by parts yields

 integral h_D R <= D G integral(q/p)h_D -[p h_D g]_0^1
                  +integral[(p h_D)'+D(q/p)h_D]g
                =G[1+D integral(q/p)h_D].

The boundary contribution is G: g(0)=G,h_D(0)=1, while p h_D tends to0 at1.
The finite polynomial g is bounded; exponential endpoint decay justifies all
integrals. The substitution t=q/p gives

 Z_D=integral_0^infinity(1+t)^(D-1)e^(-Dt)dt,
 D integral(q/p)h_D=D integral_0^infinity t(1+t)^(D-1)e^(-Dt)dt=1.

The last integrand is minus the derivative of(1+t)^D e^(-Dt), with endpoints
1 and0. Therefore the UNNORMALIZED excess integral is<=2G and the normalized
bound is S+(2/Z_D)G. The standalone S is not multiplied by2/Z_D, because the
probability density is h_D/Z_D and all masks share the same standalone total.

For integer D, expanding(1+t)^(D-1) gives the asserted rational formula
Z_D=sum_(k=0)^(D-1)binom(D-1,k)k!/D^(k+1). A focused exact arithmetic check
gave(D,Z_D,K_D)=(1,1,2),(2,3/4,8/3),(3,17/27,54/17),(4,71/128,256/71),
with the t-moment exactly1 in each case. No broad preexisting test suite was
rerun. Rational K_D does not imply rational individual mask weights.

6. ASYMPTOTIC CONSTANT

With t=s/sqrt D, the exponent is
(D-1)log(1+s/sqrt D)-s sqrt D, tending to-s^2/2. On t<=1,
log(1+t)<=t-t^2/4 gives a dominating e^(-s^2/8) for D>=2 after substitution.
On t>=1, log(1+t)<=t log2 bounds the remaining integral, before multiplication
by sqrt D, by e^(-D(1-log2))/[D(1-log2)]. This tail vanishes after that
multiplication. Dominated convergence therefore gives
sqrt D Z_D->sqrt(pi/2), so K_D~sqrt(8D/pi).

This proves asymptotic equivalence. It does not prove an additive O(1) error,
a direction of approach, an optimal density, or a sharp all-mechanism constant.
The current refined note correctly avoids those stronger statements.

7. ALL PROFILES, ZERO GAINS, AND PUBLIC RULE CHOICE

For fixed public D, average each fixed-mask mechanism with weight
integral_0^1(h_D/Z_D)p^|B|q^(n-|B|)dq. The finitely many weights are nonnegative,
sum to1, and are independent of every report. Thus every coverage and
individual OUT inequality survives directly. The rule is deterministic;
random masks are an evaluation identity, not private random outcomes.

At G=0, monotonicity of w gives w(A)=0 for EVERY mask. All open residual
marginals and closed capped excesses are zero, including infinite raw support
cases. Every owner therefore receives exactly f_i. No ratio by G, density
limit, or report-dependent fallback is used in the actual mechanism. The
fixed-mask OUT proof also covers comparisons crossing from G=0 to G>0.

The valid public choices are D=d+1 for a general cone, D=m for PSD order m,
and a public choice among complete mechanisms giving coefficients

 general cone: min{d,n sqrt(2/(n+1)),K_(d+1)},
 PSD lambda_min: min{m,n sqrt(2/(n+1)),K_m}.

Observed report rank can sharpen an ANALYSIS inequality, but cannot be inserted
into the density or used to switch mechanisms after seeing reports without a
new incentive argument. The zero-dimensional cone is the separate trivial
zero-production case. The PSD grand-marginal order-m bound and the general
cone grand-marginal dimension bound are compatible baselines.
