FIXED SEPARABLE PRICE CURVATURE CANNOT BEAT TWO ON WEIGHTED GRAPHS
17 October 2026. Analytic architecture obstruction, independently derived
from the asymptotic suggested by root. Historical novelty not assessed.

SCOPE
Normalize public edge profits by their maximum, so max w_e=1. Fix ONE common
regularizer h, independent of the graph, individual edge profits and reports.
Assume h is convex and continuously differentiable on[0,1], is strictly
increasing on(0,1], h'(0)=0, and h is twice continuously differentiable near
zero with L=h''(0) strictly positive and finite. Price vectors minimize
  sum_v c_v h(p_v)
over p>=0, p_u+p_v>=w_uv; pay actual owners their report dot p.
Only the funding performance is considered here; no incentive hypothesis is
needed. Global public rescaling of a fixed h is included after normalization.
Graph-dependent or vertex-dependent h_v, nonseparable objectives, and mechanisms
not of this price form are NOT ruled out by the result.

THEOREM
The supremum funding factor of this architecture is at least2, even on
connected weighted four-vertex paths with all profits and capacities positive.
Thus no choice of fixed smooth finite positive curvature at zero can obtain a
uniform factor strictly below2 for arbitrary public weighted activities.

PROOF ON AN ISOLATED WEAK EDGE
For profit w>0 sufficiently small and endpoint capacities(A,1), monotonicity
of h makes the dual constraint tight. The price at the abundant endpoint is
the unique minimizer p_A in[0,w] of
  A h(p)+h(w-p).
For A>1 it lies in(0,w/2) and satisfies
  A h'(p_A)=h'(w-p_A).                                (1)
Strict convexity holds throughout[0,w] by continuity of h'' and L>0. Both
endpoint signs in the derivative are strict, so(1) is necessary and sufficient.
As A tends to infinity, p_A tends to zero: otherwise the left side of(1)
diverges while the right side is bounded. Since h'(p)/p tends to L,
  A p_A = h'(w-p_A) / (h'(p_A)/p_A) --> h'(w)/L.        (2)
The true production is F=w and funding is Y=A p_A+w-p_A. Hence
  lim_{A->infinity} Y/F = 1+h'(w)/(wL).                (3)
As w decreases to zero, h'(w)/(wL) tends to1. For every kappa<2, first choose
w small enough that(3)>kappa, then A large enough to retain the strict gap.

CONNECTED NORMALIZATION ANCHOR
Use the path0--1--2--3, profits(w,delta,1), capacities(A,1,epsilon,epsilon),
where0<delta<1/2 and0<w<min(delta,1/2). The candidate prices are
  (p_A,w-p_A,1/2,1/2).
The connecting constraint is strictly slack because its left side is at
least1/2>delta. The first edge minimizes its separate objective as above.
For the last edge Jensen's inequality makes(1/2,1/2) an optimizer; when h
is not strictly convex there, every optimizer still has price sum1 and total
last-edge payment epsilon. Thus the combined vector is a global optimizer.
More strongly, every global optimizer attains the sum of the separate edge
minimum objectives, so its first two prices equal the unique weak-edge pair,
and its last two prices sum1. No tie-breaking assumption affects total funding.

Production is exactly w+epsilon: primal quantities(1,0,epsilon) attain it;
the dual vector(0,w,1,0) covers all three edges and has capacity costw+epsilon.
Total mechanism funding is A p_A+w-p_A+epsilon. Let epsilon decrease to zero
after the choices of w and A. The same ratio gap survives. All four capacities
and all three public edge profits remain strictly positive, and maximum
profit is exactly1 throughout, so the global normalization cannot remove it.

BOUNDARY OF THE ARGUMENT
The assumptions h'(0)=0 and0<L<infinity isolate the smooth Huber-like family.
The proof does not claim a complete classification of singular/flat/positive-
slope objectives. Nor does it establish an unrestricted weighted lower bound.
The normalized Huber rule supplies a matching upper2 within its own family;
this note supplies a broader reason that retuning a fixed quadratic knee is
not a route to a strict improvement.

NEXT GATE
A weighted improvement must use additional structure: graph/edge-dependent
regularization with a valid mixed-owner derivative invariant, a nonseparable
price objective, or an owner-aware mechanism. Local maximum-profit knees and
independent uniform resource thinning already have exact incentive failures
in this directory, so they cannot serve as that new invariant.
