UNIT CONSUMPTION ARITY THREE DOES NOT BOUND TCWM FUNDING
17 October 2026. Frozen candidate for independent mathematical audit.
Historical novelty and external significance remain unconfirmed.

MAIN THEOREM -- EXPLICIT FINITE ACTUAL OWNERS
Fix integers B>=1 and K>=2. There is a unit-profit, unit-consumption,
three-sparse fractional production technology with
 d=4K+1 resource rows and n=(4+2B)K fixed actual owners
such that a nonnegative, coalition-covering, all-profile TCWM mechanism with
funding factor at most B can exist only if
 B^2 >= (H_K-1) / [16(1+H_{2K-1}-H_K)],                         (1)
where H_s=sum_{r=1}^s 1/r. Thus there is NO finite funding bound depending
only on consumption arity, even when the arity is exactly three, the public
technology has the very special common-cap form below, all profits and
nonzero coefficients are one, and every actual owner supplies just one resource.
All deviations used in the proof keep owner identities and eligibility fixed.
The same impossibility applies to full coalition outside-production incentives,
since they imply TCWM. It does NOT establish the analogous impossibility for
individual incentives alone; the standard factor-three grand-marginal rule
remains available in that weaker model.

TCWM means that for every fixed coalition S, increasing any of its members'
reported resource quantities while holding all outsiders fixed cannot decrease
SUM_{i in S} y_i. Equivalently a coalition cannot gain total payment by binding
underreports. The result allows randomized mechanisms interpreted through
expected payments, arbitrary report dependence, and arbitrary computation.
No price form, continuity, homogeneity, null-owner axiom or anonymity is assumed.

1. FIXED TECHNOLOGY AND FIXED FINITE OWNER POPULATION
Set M=2K and R=2BK. The resource rows are P and a_j,b_j,1<=j<=M. There is one
activity (P,a_j,b_j) for each j, consuming one unit of all three rows and
producing one unit of value. The continuous packing optimum is exactly
 F(P,a,b)=min(P, sum_{j=1}^M min(a_j,b_j)).                       (2)
Indeed each activity can run up to min(a_j,b_j), and the only shared constraint
is the sum of activity quantities<=P.

There are R fixed power owners, each eligible only for P and with report bound
1/R, and 2M fixed branch owners A_j,B_j, eligible only for a_j,b_j respectively,
each with report bound1. Every comparison below keeps all power-owner reports
at1/R, hence aggregate P=1. Branch reports lie in[0,1]. No owner is introduced,
removed, split, merged, or reidentified during a deviation. A zero report is
still an existing actual owner and may receive nonnegative payment.

2. SYMMETRIZATION WITHOUT AN EXTRA MECHANISM ASSUMPTION
Suppose a factor-B mechanism exists for this one fixed owner model. Average it
over the finite group generated by permutations of the R power owners, by
permutations of branches (simultaneously permuting their two resource labels
and actual owners), and by swaps A_j<->B_j within any branch together with the
corresponding a_j<->b_j resource swap. Each is an isomorphism of this same fixed
public technology, owner eligibility, report bounds and deviation domain.
For each group element, relabel the reports, apply the original rule, and undo
the relabeling on its payments. The resulting finite average preserves every
coverage, nonnegativity, funding and TCWM inequality, since those inequalities
are linear in payments. Hence it is enough to derive a contradiction for the
symmetric averaged rule. This does not assume the original rule anonymous.

3. THE MAIN PROFILES AND NOTATION
For m=1,...,K, let C_m have all R power reports1/R; branches1,...,m report(1,1),
and all remaining branches report(0,1). Then F(C_m)=1. Define:
 W_m = total payment to ALL2M branch owners at C_m;
 T_m = total payment to the2m owners of its active branches;
 q_m = payment to any one active branch owner, so T_m=2m q_m;
 z_m = payment to any one B_j owner in an inactive branch.
The equalities follow from the profile's stabilizer under the symmetry group.
In particular, each active pair may be swapped while fixing C_m. Inactive
zero-report A_j payments are not assumed zero: they are INCLUDED in W_m.
By nonnegativity and funding,
 0<=W_m<=B, and 0<=z_m<=B/(M-m).                               (3)
The latter only counts the M-m inactive B_j owners; additional nonnegative
payments, including to zero-report owners, make the bound no weaker.

4. A UNIFORM POSITIVE LOWER BOUND ON ACTIVE-BRANCH PAYMENTS
Fix m<=K. Put k_m=floor(K/m) and epsilon_m=k_m/R. Consider an auxiliary profile
D_m with the same power reports and the same inactive branches as C_m, but
all m active branches report(epsilon_m,epsilon_m). Its value is
 F(D_m)=m epsilon_m<=K/R=1/(2B).
Therefore total payment Y_P to the R power owners is at most1/2. Symmetry makes
each power owner's payment exactly Y_P/R, without any linear-price assumption.

For each active branch j, take the coalition consisting of A_j,B_j and ANY
k_m power owners. Their power stock is k_m/R=epsilon_m and their two branch
stocks are also epsilon_m; thus their coalition worth is epsilon_m. Coverage
implies
 y_{A_j}(D_m)+y_{B_j}(D_m)
 >=epsilon_m - k_m Y_P/R
 =epsilon_m(1-Y_P) >=epsilon_m/2.                              (4)
Sum(4) over the m branches. Next let the ONE coalition of all2m active branch
owners increase their reports from epsilon_m to1, with every outsider fixed.
TCWM implies
 T_m >= m epsilon_m/2 = m floor(K/m)/(2R) >=1/(8B).             (5)
The last inequality uses floor(x)>=x/2 for x>=1, so m floor(K/m)>=K/2, and
R=2BK. The repeated use of power owners in different coverage coalitions is
legitimate: coverage is a separate inequality for every coalition, not a
simultaneous physical allocation to all coalitions.

5. THE HARMONIC ACCUMULATION OF REQUIRED PAYMENTS
For m=2,...,K, C_m is obtained from C_{m-1} solely by increasing A_m's report
from0 to1. Apply TCWM to the fixed coalition S_m of ALL branch owners EXCEPT
B_m. It contains the changed owner A_m, every power owner is outside, and the
excluded B_m keeps its report1. The coalition's low payment is W_{m-1}-z_{m-1};
its high payment is W_m-q_m. Thus
 W_m-W_{m-1} >=q_m-z_{m-1}
              >=1/(16Bm)-B/(M-m+1),                          (6)
by(3),(5). All zero-report owners remain present in both totals; none has been
silently dropped from this transition inequality.

Sum(6) from2 throughK, and use W_1>=0, W_K<=B:
 B >= (H_K-1)/(16B) - B sum_{m=2}^K 1/(2K-m+1).
The final sum equals H_{2K-1}-H_K. Rearrangement gives(1), proving the theorem.
Also H_{2K-1}-H_K<log2, whereas H_K-1 grows like logK. Hence(1) fails for K
large enough at any prescribed integer B. An explicit, deliberately loose
choice K=2^(128B^2) already contradicts(1): log2>=1/2 gives H_K-1>=64B^2-1,
while16(1+H_{2K-1}-H_K)<32.
For an arbitrary proposed finite real factor kappa, take integer B>=max(1,kappa)
and use the corresponding fixed construction. A factor-kappa rule would also
be a factor-B rule and is therefore impossible. No assumption that an infimum
funding factor is attained is needed.

6. WHAT THE BOUND SAYS QUANTITATIVELY
The finite obstruction is explicit: d=4K+1, n=(4+2B)K, all report bounds and
all displayed profiles are rational. It gives a square-root-logarithmic lower
bound on necessary funding along growing unit-three-sparse technologies and
fixed finite owner populations. For this parameterized finite instance, (1)
is a NECESSARY condition for a factor-B mechanism. It is not an assertion that
this lower rate is sharp, nor a claimed formula for the optimal factor.
Pure-role ownership suffices; allowing arbitrary fixed mixed owners includes
this domain and cannot evade the lower bound. The assumption load-bearing here
is transferable coalition underreport protection. It is strictly stronger than
ordinary individual truthfulness.

7. SHARPER CONSTANT VIA THE SEPARATELY AUDITED PRICE-EXISTENCE REDUCTION
This paragraph is optional and NOT required for the main finite theorem.
For a fixed M, if factor kappa is feasible uniformly over every finite owner
model, the aggregate-price existence theorem in energy/
COALITION_PRICE_REDUCTION_CANDIDATE.txt supplies a nonnegative all-profile price
rule with the same guarantees. Average it over branch automorphisms. The same
C_m argument applies, now with active resources rather than particular owners.
At the auxiliary profile with active quantities epsilon=1/(2kappa m), funding
gives p_P<=1/2. Coverage of arbitrary small bundles supported on any active
triple implies p_P+p_a+p_b>=1. Hence active payment is at least1/(4kappa),
and TCWM preserves this when active coordinates grow to1. The recurrence has
1/(8kappa m) in place of1/(16Bm), yielding
 kappa^2 >= (H_K-1)/[8(1+H_{2K-1}-H_K)] for M=2K.              (7)
This sharper constant uses uniform owner-population feasibility. The elementary
main theorem instead gives explicit finite owners and avoids that reduction.

8. STATUS, PRIORITY AND CONSEQUENCE
The preceding result is an unrestricted-mechanism lower bound for the stated
TCWM model. It is NOT another stopped potential, scalar-wrapper or allocation
architecture. It establishes a sharp qualitative consumption-arity boundary:
all two-sparse technologies have the independently audited sharp3/2 TCWM upper
bound, while some unit three-sparse technologies require arbitrarily large
funding as resource and owner counts grow. It does not settle individual-IC
funding, real cluster welfare, private costs, integral scheduling, or historical
novelty. Exact checker and independent review must precede promotion.

9. INDIVIDUAL-INCENTIVE COMPARATOR: ELEMENTARY FACTOR-r GRAND MARGINALS
For completeness this is a short independent proof, not a historical novelty
claim. Let F(C)=max{sum_e w_e x_e:Ax<=C,x>=0}, with nonnegative coefficients
and profits, each column using at most r resource rows. Actual owner i reports
b_i, with C=sum_i b_i. Pay
 U_i=F(C)-F(C-b_i).
This is nonnegative and coordinatewise own-report monotone: the second term
holds all other owners fixed and does not depend on i's report.
A nonnegative optimal dual price p satisfies F(C)=p.C. Since the same p is
feasible for every capacity vector, F(C-b_i)<=p.(C-b_i), so U_i>=p.b_i.
Consequently sum_{i in S}U_i>=p.sum_{i in S}b_i>=F(sum_{i in S}b_i), proving
coalition coverage.

Fix an optimal primal plan x. Write s_iv=b_iv/C_v when C_v>0, and s_iv=0 when
C_v=0 (then b_iv=0). For each owner i define
 x_e^i=x_e[1-max_{v:A_ve>0} s_iv],
with maximum zero for an empty support. Positive-profit zero-use activities
are excluded by finiteness. This plan is feasible at C-b_i: in row v, every
used activity is reduced by at least the factor s_iv, hence row use is at most
(1-s_iv)C_v=C_v-b_iv. Rows with C_v=0 already have zero use. Thus
 U_i<=sum_e w_e x_e max_{v:A_ve>0}s_iv.
Sum in i and use max<=sum and sum_i s_iv=1 on each positive-capacity row:
 sum_i U_i<=sum_e w_e x_e sum_{v:A_ve>0}sum_i s_iv
          <=r sum_e w_e x_e=rF(C).
This is a column-arity argument, independent of owner counts or how mixed their
bundles are. It is consistent with the project's prior SPARSE_PRODUCTION_NOTE
and the existing MPH-r comparator; the present proof needs no imported theorem.

For r=3 this gives coalition coverage plus INDIVIDUAL truthfulness with total
funding at most3F for every technology in the main lower-bound family, while
TCWM has no uniform finite factor across that family. The failure is therefore
a substantive separation in incentive strength, not an inability to stabilize
three-sparse production or an unrestricted individual-IC lower bound.
