PARTICIPATION FLOORS CHANGE THE COMMON-CAP FUNDING EXPONENT
17 October 2026. Frozen candidate for independent review; novelty unconfirmed.
Dependencies: COMMON_CAP_GENERAL_ARITY_RATE.txt and COMMON_CAP_FULL_OUTSIDE_RATE.txt.

MODEL
Use F(P,x)=min(P,sum_{j=1}^M min_{l<=k}x_jl), fixed mixed actual owners, and the
full coalition outside-production incentive from the cited theorem. In addition,
fix public theta in[0,1] and require each owner i to receive the participation floor
 y_i >= theta [F(C)-F(C-b_i)].                               (1)
The same public theta is used at every report profile. This is an additional
contract requirement, not a consequence of outside-production protection.

RESULT
For each fixed k>=2, write alpha=(k-1)/k. Uniformly over theta in[0,1], the optimal
funding order in the WORST CASE over fixed finite actual-owner models is
 Theta_k(M^(alpha/2)+theta M^alpha).                         (2)
The worst-case quantifier is essential: a fixed institution with one owner
controlling the entire common resource can have efficient floor-free payments;
that special owner model is not assigned the worst-case lower.
In particular full floors(theta=1) double the exponent from alpha/2 to alpha.
For unit three-sparse common-cap production(k=2), the growth changes from
Theta(M^(1/4)) without floors to Theta(M^(1/2)) with full floors. A partial floor
begins changing the order when theta is of order M^(-alpha/2). Leading constants
and finite optimal factors are not settled by this statement.

1. A FULL-OUT MECHANISM THAT ALSO SATISFIES FULL GRAND-MARGINAL FLOORS
Take the full-OUT rule with public C=1:
 g_j=min(P,x_j1,...,x_jk), G=sum_j g_j, r=P/G,
 h=min(1,1/r), f=min(1,r^(1/k)),
 p_P=h, p_jl=f g_j/x_jl,
with the usual zero conventions. The preceding full-OUT proof applies verbatim.
Its funding bound is
 total <=[1+k M^alpha]F,                                   (3)
because max{1+k,1+k M^alpha}=1+k M^alpha for M>=1.

Here F=min(P,G) EXACTLY, since the cap is P. A maximum feasible activity plan is
 z_j=lambda g_j, lambda=min(1,P/G),
when P,G>0; take the zero plan otherwise. Indeed sum z_j=min(P,G), and each
private-row use z_j is bounded by g_j<=x_jl. Its normalized utilization prices are
 u_P=min(1,G/P)=h,
 u_jl=lambda g_j/x_jl.
Because f=min(1,r^(1/k))>=min(1,r)=lambda, the mechanism satisfies p>=u componentwise.

For completeness, normalized utilization of ANY maximum production plan bounds
every owner's grand marginal from above. For owner i write s_iv=b_iv/C_v on
positive rows and0 on zero rows. Reduce activity z_j to
 z_j^i=z_j[1-max_{v in its support}s_iv].
This plan is feasible at C-b_i: in row v, every contributing activity is reduced
by at least s_iv, leaving use at most(1-s_iv)C_v=C_v-b_iv. Hence
 F(C)-F(C-b_i) <=sum_j z_j max_{v in activity j}s_iv
 <=sum_j z_j sum_{v in activity j}s_iv
 =sum_v u_v b_iv <=sum_v p_v b_iv=y_i.                        (4)
No optimal-flow selection monotonicity is inferred: utilization only proves
floors; the separate explicit-price proof establishes full OUT. This distinction
avoids the earlier all-optimal-selection TCWM obstruction.

2. MATCHING LOWER RATE WITH FLOORS
Use the fixed pure-owner model and profiles C_m from the general-arity lower:
R pure power owners at1/R, first m branches all private rows1, other branches
(0,1,...,1). Every C_m has worth1. Symmetrize over power owners, branches and
private-row permutations; full owner floor(1) transforms equivariantly and is
preserved by this averaging, as are all other requirements.
At C_1 each of its k active private owners is individually indispensable: removing
that owner reduces production from1 to0. Thus active total T_1>=k theta.
The same active-only transition coalition gives
 [1-(k-1)/(km)]T_m>=T_(m-1),
so
 T_M>=k theta Q_M,k,
 Q_M,k=product_{m=2}^M km/[k(m-1)+1].                        (5)
Funding therefore requires kappa>=k theta Q_M,k. The optional stronger finite
bound is
 kappa>=1+(1-1/M)k theta Q_M,k.                              (6)
To see it, let Y_P be total power payment at C_M. The coalition of ALL power
owners plus one active branch has worth1 and receives Y_P+T_M/M. Hence
Y_P>=1-T_M/M, and total payment Y_P+T_M>=1+(1-1/M)T_M.
The right side remains valid if the intermediate lower on Y_P is negative.

The independent no-floor lower also applies:
 kappa>=Q_M,k R/(R^2+Q_M,k)                                  (7)
for any chosen finite R, because adding floors does not remove the original
nonnegative/coverage/TCWM constraints. Choose R near sqrt(Q_M,k) for order
M^(alpha/2). Since Q_M,k~Gamma(1+1/k)M^alpha, (5),(7) give the lower half of(2).
The lower uses only pure owners, no price architecture, no continuity and no
infimum-attainment assumption. All identities and eligibility stay fixed.

3. THE PARTIAL-FLOOR UPPER
Let y^0 be the full-OUT sharp-rate rule from COMMON_CAP_FULL_OUTSIDE_RATE.txt,
with funding
 K0=k+max{1,sqrt(k)M^(alpha/2)}.
Let y^1 be the C=1 full-floor rule above, with K1=1+kM^alpha. Define
 y^theta=(1-theta)y^0+theta y^1.                              (8)
This is a fixed positive convex combination at every report profile. Its funding
is at most[(1-theta)K0+theta K1]F. Its full-OUT inequality is the same convex
combination of two inequalities with identical right side F(d), hence remains
exactly F(d). Coverage and nonnegativity are preserved. Since y^0>=0 and y^1_i
satisfies the full floor, y^theta_i>=theta[F(C)-F(C-b_i)]. Thus (8) proves the
upper in(2) uniformly for theta in[0,1]. No incentive claim relies on rounding
or approximate evaluation of these prices.

4. SCOPE
This frontier concerns the stated common-cap hyperstar and complete owner-by-owner
grand-marginal floors. It is not a theorem about arbitrary three-sparse production,
private reservation utilities, public floor constants, or individual rather than
coalition incentives. The fixed-positive weighted-hyperstar layer identity also
preserves grand marginals, because F(C)-F(C-b_i) is exactly the sum of the layer
marginals; consequently the upper extends to the public heterogeneous corollary.
The unit-valued subclass supplies the same worst-case lower order.

The theorem is useful economically because it separates two contract choices:
preventing coalitions from hiding resources for independent production, and
promising every contributor its full incremental value. The former alone has the
smaller exponent; the latter has a quantitatively higher funding requirement.
No claim that either contract is institutionally appropriate or empirically
cost-effective is made without a concrete application and data.
