POSITIVE RADIAL MEASURES ALSO FAIL: FINITE FUNDING FORCES A DENSITY
17 October 2026. Corollary to TWO_COMMON_FRESH_RADIAL_PRIVATE_OBSTRUCTION_v2.txt.
Proposed and independently checked algebraically by the composition/root lanes;
external priority and specialist review remain unconfirmed.

Replace the density h(t)dt in the v2 architecture by any finite positive
Borel measure nu on [0,1]. Keep arbitrary nu-measurable (in particular, Borel-measurable) optimal
private dual choices at all ties, so every measure integral is defined. Then no such rule is both TCWM, coalition covering,
and uniformly finitely funded over all finite fixed owner populations.
For nonzero nu, TCWM plus finite funding already contradict each other on
the fixed five-owner technology of v2.

PROOF
Use the same one-active-private-job axis, P=Q=1, c=(r,0,0). For t<1/r the
charged coordinate's unique dual price is3/2, and for t>1/r it is0; at the
single kink t=1/r it can be any value in [0,3/2].

First, mass m0 at t=0 contributes at least (3/2)r m0 to the private owner's
payment. Grand production is3/2 for r>=1, so finite funding forces m0=0.

Next suppose nu has an atom m>0 at t=x in (0,1]. Let r0=1/x. At every r,
including reports whose threshold1/r is another atom, the private payment Y(r)
satisfies

 (3/2)r nu([0,1/r)) <= Y(r) <= (3/2)r nu([0,1/r]).

For r approaching r0 from below, measure continuity gives
liminf Y(r)>=(3/2)r0 nu([0,x]); from above it gives
limsup Y(r)<=(3/2)r0 nu([0,x)). The two bounds differ by(3/2)r0 m>0.
Thus there are nearby feasible reports on opposite sides with a strict
payment loss. These liminf/limsup bounds handle accumulating atoms and all
measurable tie selections; no regularity of Y or chosen kink payments is
assumed. This contradicts TCWM. For x=1, use r just below and above1;
intervals extending past1 are interpreted relative to[0,1], and the same
bounds apply. Hence nu is atomless on[0,1].

Write H(x)=nu([0,x]); it is continuous. The axis payment for r>=1 is exactly
(3/2)r H(1/r). As in v2, A(x)=H(x)/x is nonincreasing in x on (0,1], and
finite funding gives A(x)<=kappa. For 0<a<b<=1,

 0<=H(b)-H(a)<=b A(a)-a A(a)<=(b-a)kappa.

For a=0 the same inequality follows from H(b)<=kappa b and H(0)=0. Thus H
is kappa-Lipschitz on [0,1], so nu is absolutely continuous with a bounded
nonnegative density h. If nu is nonzero, h is nonzero a.e. on some set of
positive measure, and v2 supplies the contradiction. If nu=0, the fragmented
common-owner coverage argument in v2 Section4 forces unbounded funding.

SCOPE
This corollary closes arbitrary fixed positive radial averaging measures;
it does not exclude signed measures, report-dependent kernels, additional
private-owner compensation, or mechanisms outside the price architecture.
The density conclusion is a necessary consequence of axis TCWM and finite
funding, not an assumption of the theorem. No numerical checks are used.
