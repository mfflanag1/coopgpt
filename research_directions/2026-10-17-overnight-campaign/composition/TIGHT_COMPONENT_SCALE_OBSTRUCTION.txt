TIGHT-COMPONENT HUBER NORMALIZATION FAILS AT COMPONENT SEAMS
17 October 2026. Exact rule-class counterexample. No novelty assertion.

CANDIDATE BEING STOPPED
For public weighted resource graphs, choose a feasible resource-price vector.
On EACH connected component of its tight-edge graph, normalize the Huber knee
by W_C=max public edge profit in that component, and require its restriction
to minimize sum_v c_v h_{W_C}(p_v) over the component's own edge constraints.
Here h_W(p)=p^2/W below W/2 and p-W/4 above W/2. An isolated component has
price zero. Pay actual owner i its report dot p. This is a self-consistency
proposal, not a single global convex program. It would repair the weak-edge
anchor responsible for the global normalization factor2 boundary.

COUNTEREXAMPLE
Path0--1--2--3 has public edge profits(1,14/5,4). Four fixed actual owners
each own one vertex. Owners1,2,3 report capacity1; owner0 reports A.
At A=2 the UNIQUE self-consistent price is
  p=(3/10,7/10,21/10,19/10).                         (1)
All three edges are tight, and W_C=4. Exact KKT edge multipliers
  lambda=(3/10,1/20,19/20)
satisfy degree_lambda(v)=c_v min(2p_v/4,1). The low-price coordinates0,1,3
and a tight neighbor of vertex2 force uniqueness, as in the weighted note.
Owner0 receives2*(3/10)=3/5.

At A=5 the UNIQUE self-consistent price is
  p=(1/10,9/10,2,2).                                 (2)
Only the outer edges are tight; their separate scales are1 and4. Multipliers
are lambda01=1, lambda23=1. The middle edge is strictly slack because
9/10+2=29/10>14/5. Owner0 receives5*(1/10)=1/2.
Thus withholding capacity5 down to2 increases owner0's payment by1/10.
The failure already uses one resource per actual owner; no mixed bundle or
coalitional deviation is needed.

EXHAUSTION OF OTHER SELF-CONSISTENT PATTERNS
There are eight possible subsets of the three tight edges. Patterns missing
edge23 and with vertex3 isolated force p3=0. If edge12 is in its other
component, that component's maximum profit is at most14/5, hence every price
there is at most14/5<4; if edge12 is absent then p2=0. In either case edge23
cannot be covered. Therefore edge23 must be tight.

If edge01 is absent but edge12 is tight, the component on vertices1,2,3 has
scale4, capacities(1,1,1), and unique minimizing prices(2/5,12/5,8/5).
Their exact edge multipliers are(1/5,4/5). Isolated vertex0 has price0,
so edge01 is not covered. If both edge01 and edge12 are absent, vertices0,1
have zero price and again fail edge01. Therefore edge01 must also be tight.
Only patterns {01,23} and {01,12,23} remain.

For the disconnected pattern {01,23}, A>=1 gives the unique pairwise prices
  (1/(2A),1-1/(2A),2,2).
They cover the middle edge iff A>=5/2. Thus the pattern is impossible at A=2
and valid at A=5.

For the connected pattern {01,12,23}, scale is4. At A=2, certificate(1)
establishes the required optimizer. At A=5 the unique GLOBAL scale4 optimizer
is instead
  (1/6,5/6,2,2), lambda=(5/12,0,1).
The exact KKT identities hold, and its middle constraint is strictly slack
because5/6+2=17/6>14/5. Therefore no optimizer with all three tight edges exists
at A=5; a self-consistent connected component is impossible there.
This proves uniqueness of the asserted self-consistent choices at BOTH
profiles, so no tie-breaking or choice among fixed points repairs the failure.

CONSEQUENCE
Component-specific normalization has a valid derivative invariant only while
the component structure and its scales remain fixed. That local property
does not extend across component seams. This exact two-profile example stops
the candidate before any unrestricted claim. It is separate from the earlier
per-vertex local-knee obstruction and the global fixed-curvature funding bound.
