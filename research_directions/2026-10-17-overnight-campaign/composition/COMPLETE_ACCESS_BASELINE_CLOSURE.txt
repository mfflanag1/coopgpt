SETTING

Every resource is owned in arbitrary fractions by fixed actual owners; whole-

EXACT PRODUCTION IDENTITY, ALL REAL CAPACITIES
LetT(c)=min(sum_L c_u,sum_R c_v), and letF_q(c) be production with residual
profitsq, discarding its zero-profit edges if desired. Then
  F_{m+q}(c)=m T(c)+F_q(c).                            (1)
The equality holds for every real nonnegative capacity vector, and therefore
for every coalition's bundle, not only the grand profile.

PROOF
Every feasible plan has total throughput<=T and residual profit<=F_q, proving
the <= direction. Choose a residual-profit maximizing plan. If its throughput
is belowT, there is an unsaturated left resourceu and an unsaturated right
resourcev. Completeness provides edgeuv. It must haveq_uv=0, because otherwise
adding a positive amount on it would strictly improve the supposedly optimal
residual profit. Add flow on this zero-profit edge until at least one endpoint
becomes saturated. This preserves residual optimality and never reduces an
existing edge quantity. Repeat. At most|L|+|R| such augmentations are needed;
the final plan has throughputT and residual profitF_q. It attains the right
side of(1), proving equality.

MECHANISM TRANSFER
SupposeF_q has a coalition-covering, coalition-total-underreport-monotone
mechanism with funding<=kappa F_q. Add its payments to m times the unit-profit
complete-bipartite3/2 payments, with exactly the same actual owners and report
bundles. Each summand is nonnegative and coalition-total monotone. Coalition
coverage follows from(1), and funding is bounded by
  (3/2)m T+kappa F_q <= max(3/2,kappa)F_{m+q}.
The baseline and residual technologies and coefficients are public and fixed;
none depends on the reports or on a report-selected component decomposition.

EXACT GRAPH-THEORETIC SCOPE OF UNIVERSAL BASELINE CLOSURE
For a finite simple resource graphG, the identity
  F_{m*1+q}(c)=m F_unit,G(c)+F_q(c)
holds for EVERY m>=0, EVERY nonnegative public residualq onE(G), and EVERY
real nonnegative capacityc, if and only if each nontrivial connected component
ofG is complete bipartite. Sufficiency follows by summing(1) over components.

For necessity, an inducedP4 violates the identity at m=1, capacities1 on its
four vertices and0 elsewhere, and residualprofit1 on its middle edge only:
the three quantities areF_{1+q}=2,F_unit=2,F_q=1. An induced triangle violates
it at capacities1 on those vertices and0 elsewhere and residualprofit1 on
one edge only: F_{1+q}=2,F_unit=3/2,F_q=1. The fractional triangle value3/2
has the familiar half-unit flow on each edge; weighted value2 is certified
by prices1,1,0 at the heavy edge's two endpoints and the remaining vertex.

It remains to show that absence of both induced configurations implies the
claimed graph form. In a connected graph choose vertexu and its neighbor setN.
Triangle-freeness makesN independent. Every other vertex has distance2 fromu,
because the first four vertices of a shortest longer path would induceP4.
WriteM for these other vertices. Eachv inM has a neighborw inN. If some x inN
were not adjacent tov, then v-w-u-x would be an inducedP4: v-u andw-x are
absent, the latter by triangle-freeness. Thus eachv inM is adjacent to allN.
No two vertices ofM can be adjacent, since they have a common neighbor inN
and would form a triangle. Consequently the whole component is the complete
bipartite graph with sidesN and{u} unionM. Isolated vertices are harmless.

This is a characterization of the UNIVERSAL shift identity. A particular
non-complete graph and particularq may still satisfy that identity or admit
another3/2 mechanism; the theorem makes neither negative claim.
