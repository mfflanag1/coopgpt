FINITE NECESSARY-CONDITION LP FOR AGGREGATE LINEAR PRICES
17 October 2026. Search instrument, not a theorem inferred from numerical output.

The public technology is one fixed weighted resource graph. Exactly TWO actual
owners are fixed and eligible at every resource coordinate. A mechanism in the
tested architecture uses one price vector p(c) depending only on total reports
c and the fixed technology/owner set. Its owner payments are b_i.p(c).
Changing how the same aggregate c is distributed between these two owners does
not change p(c); this is an explicit restriction of the architecture.

All aggregate test profiles have strictly positive coordinates. For every edge
uv, choose epsilon<=min(c_u,c_v), give owner1 exactly epsilon at u and v, and
give owner2 the remainder c-b_1. Owner1's singleton production is at least
epsilon*w_uv. Its coverage therefore forces p_u(c)+p_v(c)>=w_uv. Similarly
nonnegative payments for a singleton-coordinate owner force p_v(c)>=0.
The full budget is c.p(c)<=K F(c), where F(c) is computed exactly from the
weighted fractional-cover LP vertices.

For c'=c+delta*e_k, delta>0, and ANY resource subset S, give owner1 the bundle
  b_1=c*1_S
and owner2 the fixed remainder c-b_1. Compare owner1's larger report
  b'_1=b_1+delta*e_k.
Owner2 is unchanged, owner1's eligibility and identity are unchanged, and
b_1<=b'_1. Incentive monotonicity therefore forces
  delta*p_k(c') + sum_{v in S}c_v[p_v(c')-p_v(c)] >= 0.
Every such comparison is realized by the SAME two fixed eligible owners,
within endowment boxes large enough to contain all listed reports. Different
LP rows may use different report profiles, which is legitimate for one
all-profile mechanism. There is no owner cloning within any comparison.

These subset inequalities are the corners of the linear condition over all
0<=b_1<=c. Thus they impose the full aggregate-price own-monotonicity condition
for each selected aggregate transition. Consecutive coordinate levels suffice
for the grid by transitivity. They do not imply incentives outside the finite
grid, nor do finite LP feasible prices establish any general upper theorem.

The program minimizes K over these necessary constraints. A numerical optimum
is only a scouting result. To claim a rigorous lower bound, a nonnegative dual
combination must be recovered and checked in exact arithmetic, including every
price coefficient and the K coefficient. A lower bound for this architecture
would not automatically lower-bound arbitrary owner-aware mechanisms.

Implementation: weighted_price_lp.py. Exact resource-game values use Fraction
arithmetic and enumerated dual vertices. scipy.linprog/HiGHS solves the payment
LP numerically. Reports retain primal residual and dual-support diagnostics.
