GAIN-ONLY COOPERATION ON FINITE-DIMENSIONAL RESOURCE CONES
17 October 2026. Composition extension of root/energy gain-only theorem.
Frozen proof for independent audit. Historical novelty unconfirmed.

1. MODEL AND THEOREM
Let K be a fixed closed pointed convex cone in a finite-dimensional real
vector space V=span(K), dim V=d. Polyhedrality is NOT required. Let
F:K -> R_+ be finite, concave, and positively homogeneous of degree one.
Then F(0)=0 and F(x+y)>=F(x)+F(y), so cone-order monotonicity follows without
an additional assumption. F may have downward jumps at boundary faces.

There are n fixed actual owners. A report b_i lies in K. A feasible withheld
bundle d_i satisfies d_i in K and b_i-d_i in K. Fixed public eligibility can
restrict this domain further; the theorem protects all the specified cone
deviations and therefore any subset of them. Identities remain fixed.
Set C=sum_i b_i, T=F(C), f_i=F(b_i), S=sum_i f_i, G=T-S>=0.

There is a deterministic all-profile nonnegative payment rule satisfying:
 (a) sum_{i in A} y_i >= F(sum_{i in A} b_i), every coalition A;
 (b) y_i(b)-y_i(b_i-d_i,b_-i) >= F(d_i), every individual deviation;
 (c) total payments <= S+min{d,n sqrt(2/(n+1)),1+2sqrt(d+1)} G.

The choice among whole rules and their parameters uses public n,d before
reports. This is INDIVIDUAL outside-production protection, not a coalition
deviation theorem. It is efficient at every G=0 profile: y_i=f_i. No
polynomial-time evaluation or observable/verifiable-resource institution is
asserted for arbitrary K,F. At d=0 everything is zero. The rank bound below
may be sharper at a profile but is an analysis bound, not an adaptive switch.

2. SUPPORTS ON MINIMAL FACES
For x in K let H_x be its minimal cone face. Standard finite-dimensional
facial geometry gives x in relint(H_x). A finite concave function on that
relative interior has a supporting affine functional. Homogeneity makes the
support linear: choose ell_x in span(H_x)^* with
 ell_x(x)=F(x), ell_x(z)>=F(z)>=0 for all z in H_x.
To see the support extends to boundary points of H_x despite possible jumps,
apply concavity to (1-t)x+tz, t>0 small, which lies in relint(H_x), and compare
with the supporting inequality there. Cancellation of t gives the same
inequality at z. Homogeneity on the ray through x forces its constant term
to zero. At x=0 take H_0={0} and ell_0(0)=0.

Choose these supports once as a function of x (for example the unique minimum
Euclidean norm supporting functional on span(H_x)). Define the extended
price L_x(z)=ell_x(z) if z in H_x, and +infinity otherwise. It is additive on
K in the extended nonnegative sense: if z+w belongs to a cone face then both
z,w belong to that face. In particular it majorizes F wherever finite.

3. THE FIXED-MASK RULE AND ITS INCENTIVES
Fix a report-independent mask B of actual owners and C_B=sum_{h in B}b_h.
Every retained b_h belongs to H_(C_B), by facial heredity. Pay

 i in B:    y_i^B=F(C_B)-F(C_B-b_i);
 i notin B: y_i^B=min{L_(C_B)(b_i), T-sum_{h!=i} f_h}.

The cap is f_i+G. Every payment is at least f_i by superadditivity/support.
If a closed member i of a coalition A hits its cap, all other standalone
floors give y^B(A)>=T-sum_{h notin A}f_h>=F(b(A)). Otherwise every closed
member has a finite support price, and every open marginal dominates its
support price, since the support touches C_B and majorizes C_B-b_i.
Summing these finite prices covers F(b(A)). This proves coalition coverage.

For an open owner, restoring d changes only the first term of its marginal;
superadditivity makes the payment increase at least F(d). For a closed owner,
the selected support excludes it and is fixed. The cap increases by at least
F(d). If both its reports are in the selected face, the linear-price increase
is ell(d)>=F(d), so the minimum also increases by at least F(d). If its low
report is outside that face then its high report is outside as well, since
high=low+d with both in K; both payments use the cap. If only the high report
is outside, the high payment equals the high cap and the low payment is no
larger than the low cap. Thus individual OUT holds on all faces, without
assuming boundary continuity. Fixed averaging preserves both properties.

4. OWNER-COUNT BOUND: UNCHANGED RESIDUAL-GAME ARGUMENT
At a fixed profile define w(A)=F(b(A))-sum_{i in A}f_i. Superadditivity gives
0<=w(A)<=w(N)=G and monotonicity under adding owners. Open excess is
w(B)-w(B\{i}); closed excess is min{L_(C_B)(b_i)-f_i,G}. The infinity case
means excess G. Draw B with independent retention probability1-q and average
q uniformly on[0,a], with a fixed publicly. If g(q)=E w(B), expected open
excess is -(1-q)g'(q), closed excess is at most nqG, and g(q)>=(1-q)^n G.
Integration by parts gives exactly the orthant bound

 total <=S+B_n(a)G,
 B_n(a)=na/2+n[1-(1-a)^(n+1)]/[(n+1)a].

For a=sqrt(2/(n+1)), B_n(a)<=n sqrt(2/(n+1)); n=1 uses a=1 and exact factor1.
The finitely many mask weights are deterministic constants. At G=0 each
open/closed excess is zero, including every infinite raw support case.

5. GRAND-MARGINAL DIMENSION BOUND ON A POINTED CONE
Choose a finite support ell at C on H_C and put H(z)=ell(z)-F(z) there.
It is nonnegative, convex, homogeneous, subadditive, and H(C)=0. All b_i and
their subset sums lie in H_C. Discard zero bundles. Let k=rank([b_1 ... b_n]).
The polytope P={alpha>=0:sum_i alpha_i b_i=C} is compact. For example, a
strictly positive linear functional on the closed pointed cone K bounds each
alpha_i by ell_+(C)/ell_+(b_i). Each extreme point of P has at most k positive
coordinates, by the usual linear-dependence perturbation argument.

For alpha in P define Phi(alpha)=sum_i H(C-alpha_i b_i), a convex function,
and Psi(alpha)=sum_i alpha_i H(b_i), a linear function. All arguments lie in
H_C because C-alpha_i b_i is the sum of the other nonnegative multiples.
At an extreme point with m<=k positive entries, zero-coordinate terms vanish
and subadditivity gives Phi(alpha)<=(m-1)Psi(alpha)<=(k-1)Psi(alpha).
Express alpha=1 as a convex combination of extreme points. Therefore

 sum_i H(C-b_i)<=(k-1)sum_i H(b_i).

Since G=sum_i H(b_i) and the grand marginal m_i=T-F(C-b_i) satisfies
m_i-f_i=H(b_i)+H(C-b_i), total grand marginals <=S+kG<=S+dG. These marginals
cover all coalitions via the support at C and satisfy individual OUT by
superadditivity. C=0 and zero bundles cause no exception.

6. FINITE-PROFILE SEPARATION: NO EXPOSED-FACE ASSUMPTION
The sharper dimension bound needs finite vectors even where L_x is infinite.
For a fixed profile and mask let H=H_(C_B), W=span(H), and extend ell_(C_B)
arbitrarily to a linear functional ell on V. The elementary identity

                         K intersect W = H

holds even if H is not exposed. Indeed any x in W is u-v with u,v in H;
if x is also in K then x+v=u in H, so facial heredity gives x in H.

Project the finitely many outside-face b_i into V/W. None of their projected
vectors is zero, and zero is not in their convex hull. Otherwise a nontrivial
nonnegative combination of outside bundles would lie in K intersect W=H,
forcing each contributing bundle into H, a contradiction. Strictly separate
that finite compact convex hull from zero. Equivalently there is a linear
functional psi on V, vanishing on W, with psi(b_i)>0 for EACH outside owner.
One elementary construction uses the closest point of that convex hull to
zero and its inner product. psi need not be nonnegative on all K and need
not expose H globally. Neither condition is needed here.

For sufficiently large finite M set
 a_i(B)=(ell+M psi)(b_i)-f_i.
Inside H these residuals equal the original finite nonnegative residuals.
Outside H choose M so a_i(B)>=G. Thus all a_i(B)>=0, their capped values
min(a_i(B),G) equal the extended-price closed excesses EXACTLY, and
 sum_{i in B}a_i(B)=w(B)<=G,
because retained bundles lie in H and psi vanishes there. There are finitely
many masks, so choose one common M after fixing all these finite surrogates,
or choose one for each mask; both give the same row-space conclusion.

Every vector a(B) lies in the row space of the d-by-n matrix whose columns
are the owner bundles, together with f=(f_1,...,f_n). Equivalently, it lies
in the span of the d coordinate vectors in R^n and f. This space has
dimension D at most d+1.
This construction is ONLY a fixed-profile funding proof. The mechanism still
uses the original extended face supports, so no profile-dependent surrogate
selection is used to infer incentives. Polyhedrality and exposed faces have
not been assumed, and the number of extreme rays never enters.

7. LOW-RANK CAPPED SAMPLING AND THE SQUARE-ROOT DIMENSION TERM
Apply root/LOW_RANK_CAPPED_SAMPLING_LEMMA.txt to these a(B). For completeness,
if each coordinate is retained independently with p=1-q, own-bit conditioning
rewrites the expected closed capped sum as q/p times an expected diagonal sum
over retained coordinates. For fixed retained B, column i is a(B\{i})|_B.
Its off-diagonal sum is <=G, so its capped normalized diagonal is at most
twice its diagonal divided by its column sum. Normalize the columns. The
resulting nonnegative column-substochastic matrix has spectral radius<=1
and rank<=D; its trace is at most D. Therefore

 E closed excess <=2D q/(1-q) G <=2(d+1)q/(1-q)G.

Average q on[0,a]. The open-excess endpoint integral is at most G, yielding

 total <=S+[1+2(d+1)(-a-log(1-a))]/a *G.

Using -a-log(1-a)<=a^2/[2(1-a)] and a=1/(1+sqrt(d+1)) gives
 total <=S+[1+2sqrt(d+1)]G.
The minimum in the theorem is obtained by a public choice among this fixed
rule, the owner-optimized fixed rule, and the grand-marginal rule. It is not
the pointwise minimum of individual payments or report-selected mechanisms.

8. LINEALITY AND SCOPE
If desired, closed cones with lineality L=K intersect(-K) reduce to the pointed
quotient K/L. It is a closed pointed cone: after fixing an inner product,
its orthogonal representative is exactly the closed cone K intersect L^perp,
since adding or subtracting elements of L preserves K. Nonnegativity and
superadditivity force F(l)=F(-l)=0 for l in L,
and then F(x+l)=F(x). Thus F descends to the quotient, as do all feasible
withdrawals and fixed owners. The theorem applies with public dimension
dim(span K/L). This is a mathematical quotient, not changing owner identities.

The extension is a consequence of the already established mask construction
plus cone facial geometry and the finite-profile separation lemma. The latter
is the needed boundary step beyond the orthant proof. No historical priority
claim is made. See CONE_APPLICATIONS_AND_LIMITS.txt for precise signed-resource
and positive-semidefinite examples, and CONE_CHECKS.json for finite exact tests.
