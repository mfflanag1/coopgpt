UNBOUNDED POSITIVE-BINARY LIFT DISTORTION IN TWO-SPARSE RESOURCE GRAPHS
17 October 2026. New structural theorem candidate; historical priority open.
Analytic proof below. Exact finite checks are supplementary, not the theorem.

THEOREM AND SCOPE
Let G be a finite simple graph, with unit-profit fractional matching production
 F_G(c)=max{sum_e x_e: x>=0, sum_(e incident v)x_e<=c_v}.
At a fixed report profile, a positive binary coalition majorant has the form

 H(S)=sum_(i in S) f_i + integral min(sum_(i in S)a_i(w),
                                    sum_(i in S)b_i(w))dmu(w),        (1)

where f_i>=0, a_i,b_i are nonnegative measurable functions, mu is positive,
and every displayed coalition integral is finite. These auxiliary holdings may depend
arbitrarily and nonlinearly on the ENTIRE profile. They need only be fixed
across coalitions at that profile. Infinite positive layers are permitted.
Each actual owner retains its own auxiliary holdings; no new strategic owner
identities, free capacities, or signed cancellation terms are permitted.
Assume the coalitionwise comparison

                   F_G(c_S)<=H(S)<=rho F_G(c_S)       (2)

for every coalition S, including zero-value coalitions. This is a VALUE
representation condition, not an incentive property.

A. If G is triangle-free, C4-free and d-regular, d>=1, take one actual pure
unit owner at every vertex. Then EVERY majorant(1)-(2) has

                              rho>=(d+1)/2.          (3)

B. For EVERY graph of maximum degree Delta>=1, the fixed public-linear
positive binary function

 H_star(c)=(1/2)sum_v min(c_v, sum_(u adjacent v)c_u)                  (4)

satisfies, for ALL nonnegative real capacities,

                     F_G(c)<=H_star(c)<=(Delta+1)F_G(c)/2.           (5)

Consequently(3) is sharp for every triangle/C4-free regular graph, even though
the lower permits arbitrary nonlinear profile-dependent positive integrals.
There exist explicit such graphs for arbitrarily large degree. Projective
plane incidence graphs give n=2(q^2+q+1) actual owners/resources, degree q+1,
and the lower rho>=(q+2)/2=Omega(sqrt(n)) for every prime q.
A separate elementary circulant construction supplies every integer degree d.

This is NOT a lower bound for arbitrary truthful graph mechanisms. The direct
all-profile graph full-OUT mechanism already has funding factor2 independent
of degree and owner count. The theorem proves that no constant-distortion
positive binary lifting route can explain that theorem, much less improve it.

1. ZERO COALITION VALUES FORCE STAR SUPPORTS
Fix the pure unit-owner profile, so c_S=1_S. Every singleton and nonedge pair
has production0. By positivity in(1) and the upper bound in(2), f_i=0 and,
outside one common null set, no owner has both a_i>0 and b_i>0; every pair
from opposite positive supports is an edge. There are finitely many zero
coalitions, so a common null set is valid even for an infinite measure space.
Thus every nonzero layer's two supports span a complete bipartite subgraph.
Because G has no C4, at least one support has size1: the layer is a star.
A layer with only one nonempty support has zero coalition value and is dropped.

Assign every remaining layer a center v, the vertex in its singleton support.
If both supports are singletons, choose one endpoint by a fixed ordering.
This choice is measurable: there are finitely many vertices and support
patterns, and positivity of each measurable holding defines measurable sets.
All positive leaves of a layer assigned to v lie in N(v).

Let R_v be the TOTAL grand-coalition value of layers assigned to v. For an
edge uv let E_uv^v be their total value on the pair{u,v}. These are finite
nonnegative quantities because H(V) is finite. Then

 H(V)=sum_v R_v,   H({u,v})=E_uv^u+E_uv^v.            (6)

2. THE CLOSED-NEIGHBORHOOD IDENTITY
Write N[v]={v} union N(v). Since G is triangle-free, for a neighbor u of v,
its assigned layers have only one possible leaf inside N[v], namely v.
Layers centered outside N[v] have no center in that coalition and contribute0.
Layers centered at v have ALL their positive leaves in N[v]. Therefore

                  H(N[v])=R_v+sum_(u in N(v)) E_uv^u.

Summing over v and using(6) yields the EXACT identity

             sum_v H(N[v])=H(V)+sum_(uv in E)H({u,v}).                (7)

No continuity, finite number of layers, linear dependence on capacities, or
incentive assumption is used. There are only finitely many coalitions and
vertices in each graph, so linearity of the finite integral sums suffices;
all displayed quantities are finite under(2).

At the unit profile, F_G(N[v])=1: the induced graph is a star with unit center,
and at least one incident edge can be produced. Each edge pair has value1.
For d-regular G, assigning flow1/d to every edge saturates every unit vertex;
its total is |E|/d=n/2. No feasible flow exceeds half the total capacity, so
F_G(V)=n/2. Now(2) and(7) give

       n rho >=sum_v H(N[v])=H(V)+sum_e H(e)
              >=n/2+|E|=n(d+1)/2,

proving(3). More generally the same identity gives
rho>=[F_G(1_V)+|E|]/n for triangle/C4-free graphs with no isolates.

3. A MATCHING ALL-CAPACITY UPPER FOR MAXIMUM DEGREE
Each term of(4) uses fixed nonnegative linear input maps, so actual-owner
holdings transfer directly and (5) automatically applies to every coalition.
For any feasible matching flow x, let l_v=sum_(e incident v)x_e. Both c_v
and sum_(u adjacent v)c_u are at least l_v. Hence
 H_star(c)>=(1/2)sum_v l_v=sum_e x_e.
Maximize over x to obtain F_G<=H_star.

For the upper, the fractional matching LP dual is
 min sum_v c_v p_v subject to p_v>=0, p_u+p_v>=1 on every edge.
It has an optimal solution with p_v in{0,1/2,1}. Here is a short proof of the
half-integral ingredient. Truncate prices to[0,1], which preserves every
edge cover and weakly lowers cost, and choose an extreme optimal solution
of this bounded polytope. On the set0<p_v<1, consider the tight-edge graph.
A tight edge cannot join a free vertex to a0 or1 vertex. A bipartite free
tight component permits a sufficiently small alternating positive/negative
perturbation in both directions, preserving all slack constraints, contrary
to extremality. Every free component therefore has an odd cycle, whose
alternating tight equations force all its prices to1/2.

Partition vertices into V0,Vh,V1 by these prices. Every neighbor of V0 lies
in V1. Using min(c_v,N_v)<=c_v for v in Vh union V1, and <=N_v for v in V0,

 2H_star <=sum_(Vh)c_v+sum_(V1)c_v+sum_(V0)sum_(u~v)c_u
          <=sum_(Vh)c_v+(Delta+1)sum_(V1)c_v
          <=(Delta+1)[(1/2)sum_(Vh)c_v+sum_(V1)c_v]
           =(Delta+1) F_G(c).

The penultimate inequality uses Delta>=1. Isolated vertices have zero terms
and may be discarded. This proves(5). The LP duality and half-integral vertex-
cover structure are established ingredients, with an elementary proof supplied;
no historical novelty is claimed for them.

4. EXPLICIT LARGE-DEGREE FAMILIES
For a prime q, use projective points and projective lines over F_q. Represent
each by a nonzero vector in F_q^3 normalized so its first nonzero coordinate
is1. There are q^2+q+1 vectors: (1,a,b), (0,1,b), and(0,0,1). Make disjoint
point and line vertex classes. A point p and line l are adjacent exactly when
p dot l=0 modulo q.

For any nonzero line vector, its nullspace has q^2-1 nonzero vectors, yielding
(q^2-1)/(q-1)=q+1 normalized points. The same argument counts lines through
each point. Two distinct normalized points are linearly independent, so their
common orthogonal space is one-dimensional and gives exactly one projective
line. Thus two distinct point vertices cannot have two common line neighbors:
there is no C4. Bipartiteness excludes triangles. This is a(q+1)-regular
triangle/C4-free graph on n=2(q^2+q+1) vertices, proving the stated growing
lower. This classical incidence construction is an established ingredient;
the graph-star identity and its lifting consequence are the proposed claim.

For completeness, every integer degree d>=1 has an elementary explicit family.
Let m=2^(d+1), with left and right copies of Z/mZ, and join left x to right
x+s for s in{1,2,4,...,2^(d-1)}. This graph is d-regular and bipartite. Ordered
nonzero differences of distinct powers of2 are unique as integers: their
2-adic valuation determines the smaller exponent, then the larger exponent.
Their absolute values are below2^(d-1), so congruence modulo m cannot identify
two unequal such differences. A C4 would give two different ordered pairs
of shifts with the same difference, impossible. Thus(3) is sharp in maximum
degree for every d, although this simpler family has exponentially many
vertices and gives a weaker owner-count relation than the projective family.

5. INTERPRETATION AND LIMITATIONS
The failure is present already at one pure-owner report profile; no changing
owner identities or cross-profile incentive reduction is involved. It permits
arbitrary mixed-owner eligibility because the selected pure reports are an
allowed subdomain. A single value identity/majorant must work simultaneously
for all coalitions, which is exactly what an independent positive binary
transfer needs to preserve coalition coverage and pooled outside production.

The lower does NOT exclude signed decomposition, different multiresource
primitives, joint auxiliary constraints, or a coupled direct payment rule.
It does not say that an approximation only at the grand coalition is bad:
condition(2) is explicitly coalitionwise, including zero-worth coalitions.
It does not reclassify numerical scouts as proofs. For these unit resource
graphs, the established direct bounds remain3/2 for TCWM
(HUBER_PRICE_THREE_HALVES_v2.txt) and2 for full OUT
(../root/GRAPH_OUTSIDE_TWO_CANDIDATE.txt).
The unbounded distortion shows a genuine architectural separation between
those direct mechanisms and all positive binary game-lifting explanations.
