INDEPENDENT UNIFORM RESOURCE THINNING FAILS MIXED-OWNER INCENTIVES
17 October 2026. Exact method obstruction on a unit-profit resource tree.

CANDIDATE TESTED
Independently draw U_v uniform on[0,1] for each resource vertex. At the thinned
aggregate capacity profile c_v U_v choose an efficient optimal dual resource
price p*(c U), and pay owner i her FULL reported bundle dotted into E[p*(c U)].
The average is dual feasible, hence coalition-covering. On a single resource
edge it agrees with the3/2 Huber-price rule. This suggested a nonseparable
extension to weighted graphs. It fails already on an unweighted four-vertex
path with two fixed actual owners.

TECHNOLOGY / OWNERS
Resources0--1--2--3 form a unit-profit path, every edge consuming one unit at
each endpoint. Actual owner A holds vertices0 and2; actual owner B holds1 and3.
Their eligibility remains fixed. Compare actual aggregate capacities
  c=(1,1,1,1) and c'=(2,1,2,1),
where only A increases reports. A rule satisfying whole-bundle underreport
monotonicity must give A at least as much at c' as at c.

MINIMUM-COVER EVENTS
With positive continuously random capacities the optimum cover is uniquely one
of the three minimal vertex covers
  X={0,2}, Y={1,3}, Z={1,2}.
Ties have probability zero. For general capacities(t,1,t,1), let independent
uniform variables for vertices0,1,2,3 be x,y,z,w. Cover X is optimal exactly if
  t x < y and t(x+z)<y+w.
Write f(t) for this probability. Reflection exchanging the two bipartition
sides gives P(Y optimal)=f(1/t); the remaining probability belongs to Z.

EXACT INTEGRATION
Set r=t x, s=t z. Then
  f(t)=t^(-2) integral_{r=0}^{min(t,1)} integral_{s=0}^t g(r,s) ds dr,
where g(r,s)=P[y>=r,y+w>=r+s]. For0<=r<=1:
  g=1-r-s^2/2                       when0<=s<=1-r;
  g=(1-s)(1-r)+(1-r)^2/2            when1-r<=s<=1;
  g=(2-r-s)^2/2                    when1<=s<=2-r;
  g=0                              when s>=2-r.
These pieces follow by integrating y from r to1 and the remaining interval
for w; there is no numerical probability approximation.

At t=1/2 the square r,s<=1/2 lies in the first region, and
  f(1/2)=1-(1/2)/2-(1/2)^2/6=17/24.
For t=1, put a=1-r. Integrating s from0 to1 gives
  a/2+a^2/2-a^3/6.
Its integral over a in[0,1] is3/8, so f(1)=3/8.
For t=2, the additional s in[1,2-r] contributes a^3/6. Hence the full s
integral is a/2+a^2/2, whose a integral is5/12; divide by t^2=4 to get
  f(2)=5/48.

PAYMENTS AND PROFITABLE WITHHOLDING
At t=1 the cover probabilities are(3/8,3/8,1/4), giving expected prices
  (3/8,5/8,5/8,3/8).
Owner A receives1.
At t=2 the cover probabilities are(5/48,17/24,3/16), giving expected prices
  (5/48,43/48,7/24,17/24).
Owner A receives2*(5/48+7/24)=19/24.
Thus at true capacities c', A gains5/24 by hiding half of both her resource
stocks. The owner set and all other reports remain unchanged. This disproves
even individual whole-bundle incentives; no coalition manipulation is needed.

WHY THE EASY IDENTITY DOES NOT REPAIR IT
For each single resource v, integration in U_v gives
  c_v E[p*_v(c U)]
   =E_{U_-v}[F(c_v,c_-v U_-v)-F(0,c_-v U_-v)].
This proves own-capacity monotonicity of that resource's payment while all
other resources are fixed. An actual owner can control several resources;
their price spillovers invalidate the aggregation step, exactly as above.

SCOPE
This stops independent uniform thinning of resource rows followed by efficient
dual prices. It is not the previously blocked fixed owner-mask architecture,
and it does not affect the deterministic Huber-price theorem. On this path
the latter uses a different global convex optimizer rather than an average
of efficient covers of independently thinned capacities.
