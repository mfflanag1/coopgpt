1. DOMAIN: BOTH REMOVED AND RETAINED BUNDLES ARE FEASIBLE

The cone is K={(t,x):t>=||x||}. Actual owner i has a retained report b_i^0 in K
and restores D_i=(dt_i,dx_i) in K; the high report is b_i^0+D_i. Thus
dt_i>=||dx_i||, and the entire affine restoration path lies in K. Conversely,
every allowed withdrawal in the stated theorem has this representation.
The proof does not silently allow a norm-boundary owner to remove pure reserve
while leaving an infeasible retained bundle. Removing AND retaining cone
bundles is essential; a statement allowing arbitrary aggregate-cone changes
without individual-owner feasibility would be a different contract.

There are finitely many original owners, held fixed throughout. The scalar
positive/negative quantities are evaluated for those owners; no extra agents
are introduced. A coalition may contain unchanged owners, whose holdings enter
the scalar payment W and whose D_i is zero. All estimates remain valid.

2. VALUE IDENTITY, NONNEGATIVITY, COVERAGE, AND FUNDING

Write u_i=Lx_i, a_i=u_i^+, b_i^-=u_i^-, and f_i=t_i-||x_i||. For every actual
coalition S, linearity of L and a+b-|a-b|=2min(a,b) give exactly

 F(sum_S(t_i,x_i))=sum_S f_i
                  +2 integral min(sum_S a_i,sum_S b_i^-)dmu.

This holds for EVERY coalition, not just the grand aggregate. The rule
y_i=kappa f_i+2 integral pi_i is therefore nonnegative. Scalar coverage and
kappa>=1 imply actual coalition coverage; scalar total funding<=kappa min(A,B)
implies total actual funding<=kappa F(C). These are total-production funding
factors, not bounds of the form standalone value plus a constant times gains.

Both scalar price pairs are nonnegative and at most1, so
0<=pi_i<=a_i+b_i^-=|u_i|. Each payment integral is finite. There is no integral
of an unweighted constant reserve over an infinite measure space: kappa f_i
is outside the integral. At a zero scalar input the continuous scalar payments
are zero; arbitrary uncharged price conventions do not affect any receipt.

3. FINITE SIGNED TCWM COMPENSATION

The positive/negative maps do NOT preserve the original cone order. The proof
properly controls their signed changes instead of applying ordinary scalar
underreport monotonicity to a false coordinatewise comparison.

For a coalition, let da,db denote its finite aggregate changes in the positive
and negative scalar holdings. Pointwise triangle inequalities give

 integral(|da|+|db|)<=sum_S ||dx_i||<=dt:=sum_S dt_i.

Its standalone-reserve change is exactly dt-integral(da+db). The scalar
derivative bounds0<=W_a,W_b<=kappa imply
Delta W>=-kappa[(-da)^++(-db)^+]. Consequently

 Delta Y>=kappa[dt-integral(|da|+|db|)]>=0.

I checked the factors of2 and both signs here: the compensation would fail
if the canonical scalar reports were simply treated as monotone reports or
if the standalone term were omitted. Neither mistake occurs in the proof.

4. FULL OUT: THE OUTSIDE VALUE IS POOLED, NOT OWNERWISE

Along the affine restoration, let r=a'_S and v=(b^-_S)' at a scalar feature.
Then r-v=L(sum_S dx_i), whereas
integral(|r|+|v|)<=sum_S ||dx_i||<=dt. With alpha=W_a,beta=W_b, the asserted
signed inequality is

 (kappa-1)(|r|+|v|)+(2alpha-kappa)r+(2beta-kappa)v+|r-v|>=0.

I rederived its cases. For r>=v>=0 the expression is
2alpha(r-v)+2(alpha+beta-1)v. For r>=0>=v it is
2alpha r+2(kappa-beta)(-v). For0>=r>=v it is
2(kappa-beta)(r-v)+2(2kappa-1-alpha-beta)(-r).
The other three regions exchange r,v and alpha,beta. Nonnegativity follows
from0<=alpha,beta<=kappa and1<=alpha+beta<=2kappa-1.

After integration, the actual derivative is bounded below by

 kappa dt-(kappa-1)integral(|r|+|v|)-integral|r-v|
 >=dt-||sum_S dx_i||=F(sum_S D_i).

The right side is constant along the path. Integrating it proves the full
coalition OUT inequality. It can exceed sum_i F(D_i); the proof retains the
cancellation between different owners' withdrawn signed features rather than
discarding it through a triangle inequality in the wrong direction.

5. SCALAR INPUTS: DERIVATIVES RECHECKED

For the Huber pair on A>=B, r=B/A, the derivative formulas in Section3 of
the theorem are correct. Over the complete holding rectangle0<=a<=A,

handles the other region. Coverage follows from prices summing to1 and
funding is B(3/2-r/2). Thus kappa=3/2 meets the signed TCWM hypothesis.

For the logarithmic pair, put kappa=1+c and t0=1/c-1. I differentiated its
two branches independently and recovered equation(9). On r<=t0, the minimum
of alpha+beta is attained at normalized holdings(u,v)=(0,1) and equals1;

c(1+r)<=alpha+beta<=c+c/r<=kappa, with c(1+t0)=1. Individual derivatives
are nonnegative and at most kappa. Hence the stronger interval
1<=alpha+beta<=kappa<=2kappa-1 holds.

The values and first derivatives of q agree at t0. At A=B the swapped
payment formulas agree; in the logarithmic case their adjacent expressions
even coincide in a neighborhood. At a vanishing scalar input, payments tend
to zero under the total funding bound. Bounded one-sided derivatives imply
global Lipschitz bounds for W with outsiders fixed, uniformly over the scalar
outsider holdings. This justifies the finite coordinate comparisons above.

6. CONTINUUM EMBEDDING, ZERO FEATURES, AND INTERCHANGE OF INTEGRALS

The fixed basis-representative convention is sufficient: choose measurable
representatives of the finitely many embedded basis vectors once and define
every Lx pointwise linearly. This makes all path identities simultaneous;
there is no report-dependent exceptional null set or embedding selection.

For each feature and each owner, u_i(s) is affine. Its positive and negative
parts are absolutely continuous, with combined derivative magnitude bounded
by|Ldx_i|. Finite owner count and the uniform scalar Lipschitz bound dominate
the payment derivative by an integrable multiple of sum_i|Ldx_i|. Scalar
crossings create only finitely many pieces per feature; ratio seams are
crossed finitely or followed tangentially, with matching path derivatives.
If a feature is identically zero, it contributes zero throughout.

Minor measure clarification: sigma-finiteness of mu is not stated. It need
not be added. All image functions are supported, modulo null sets, on the
union over finitely many basis images h_j and positive integers n of
{|h_j|>1/n}. Each set has measure<=n integral|h_j|<infinity. Thus this common
support is sigma-finite, and the integrands vanish off it. Restricting there
justifies the stated Fubini/dominated-differentiation argument even for an
originally non-sigma-finite measure space. The proof is not claiming an
infinite-dimensional extension, where the finite-basis reasoning would need
replacement.
