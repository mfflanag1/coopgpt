RESULT AND SCOPE

No inspected statement directly implies the proposed universal TCWM factor
(d+1)/2, universal full coalition OUT factor1+c(d-1), or common/private-DR
factor3/2 theorem. This is a narrow nonimplication finding about the statements
identified below, not evidence that the results have never been proved.

I first read the existing source comparisons03-07 and13-14 and the classical
reserve comparison. I did not repeat the already-completed Moulin--Sprumont2004
review, reopen inaccessible Moulin1999, or pursue another general search after
the three-text gate. One new direct comparison is Basteck--Huettner's coalition-
manipulation characterization. The other two texts locate the serial-sharing
boundary. The author-written survey is identified as a survey, not substituted
for an uninspected original proof.

OUR EXACT COMPARISON TARGET

For a public finite monotone concave degree-one F on R_+^d, actual owners i have
fixed eligibility and report bundles b_i. The same nonnegative rule y_i(b) must
cover every coalition S: sum_S y_i(b)>=F(sum_S b_i). For any fixed coalition S
and componentwise restorations d_i>=0 by its members (zero allowed), TCWM is
sum_S[y_i(b+d)-y_i(b)]>=0; full OUT replaces zero by F(sum_S d_i). Funding is
sum_i y_i<=kappa F(sum_i b_i). Owner count and resource dimension are distinct.
The common/private result additionally assumes continuous F(P,c), vanishing
block axes, and private DR; its conclusion is TCWM, not full OUT.

1. BASTECK--HUETTNER: AN EXACTLY DIFFERENT COALITION-MANIPULATION DOMAIN

Christian Basteck and Frank Huettner, Coalitional Manipulations and Immunity of
the Shapley Value, arXiv2310.20415v1, dated1 November2023.
Original: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.20415v1
Read Section2.2-2.3, printedpp7-10, Proposition1 and Theorem2, printedp11;
also the surrounding explanation of the superadditive domain.

Their W axiom compares two characteristic-function games differing only in
v(M), and requires monotonicity of the total assigned to M. Their R axiom fixes
v(M) and v(S union T)-v(S) for every S subset M and T subset N\M. Theorem2
characterizes Shapley by W, R, symmetry, null-player treatment and efficiency,
for |N|!=2, also on superadditive games. Coalition coverage is not its conclusion.

Our comparison/inference: resource restoration changes many coalition worths
and may change outsiders' marginal contributions even though their bundles are
fixed. Neither W nor R therefore applies to every required report comparison.
There is also no pooled restored-bundle OUT term or subsidy factor in this
theorem. Credit the existing coalition-total manipulation language; do not
mistake it for the fixed-resource contract or assert a contradiction.

2. SPRUMONT: NEARLY SERIAL OUTPUT SHARING

Yves Sprumont, Nearly Serial Sharing Methods, CIREQ17-2004 / Montreal2004-14,
5 November2004; later IJGT37(2008),155-184.
Original retrieved and read:
https://www.cireqmontreal.com/wp-content/uploads/cahiers/17-2004-cah.pdf
Read Section2 printedp3, Theorem1 p5, Group Monotonicity and Theorem2 p9,
and the pair-monotonicity/continuous-domain qualifications p13.

Each owner supplies one personalized integer input; nonnegative output shares
sum exactly to F(x). Group Monotonicity says that when every member of a group
strictly increases its input, at least one member's share does not fall. Theorem2
characterizes nearly serial methods using this axiom, additivity, zero-input
treatment, dummy-change invariance and anonymity. The text explicitly leaves
the corresponding continuous characterization conjectural.

Our comparison/inference: an existential member inequality is weaker than
monotonicity of a coalition's total with transfers; unchanged coalition members
are also relevant to our test. Exact balance, one scalar input per identity,
and this theorem's axioms do not supply mixed-resource coverage, the OUT term,
or either sharp funding constant. No claim is made about a later resolution
of its continuous conjecture or unread later theorem versions.

3. MOULIN--SPRUMONT: SERIAL BOUNDS AND THE MEANING OF SUBSIDY

Herve Moulin and Yves Sprumont, Fair Allocation of Production Externalities:
Recent Results, author survey dated7 December2005.
Original author manuscript:
https://www.ruf.rice.edu/~econ/papers/2005papers/moulin%2009.pdf
Read model Sections1-2, Section7.1-7.3, especially printedpp21-26, formula(4),
Proposition2, and Theorems9-10. This is not a new check of their2004 paper.

The model balances shares exactly. Its Universal Upper Bounds constrain each
cost share by C(x_i 1), while its unanimity bound uses C(x_i 1)/n on a restricted
class. These are liability bounds indexed by user demands, not multiplicative
external-funding bounds on aggregate production. Section7.2.3 explicitly states
that both serial variants fail Strong Group Monotonicity while meeting the
weaker group condition. Theorem9 concerns scalar-demand serial characterization;
Theorem10 reports the preceding nearly-serial result.

Our comparison/inference: cross-subsidizing and subsidy-free in this literature
must not be equated to our externally funded coalition cover. Its bounds do
not establish (d+1)/2 or the common/private3/2 coupling. The survey also flags
that its output-sharing interpretation does not automatically inherit the
cost-side upper-bound characterizations. Its citations are attribution leads,
not independently verified additional originals in this gate.

CONCRETE LOGICAL SEPARATIONS (OUR ANALYSIS)

The incentive distinction is substantive: a coalition change from receipts
(10,10) to(11,0) passes a condition that at least one member does not lose,
but fails a coalition-total comparison. This is only an illustration of the
logical quantifiers, not an alleged example from a cited mechanism.

For the characteristic-function distinction, take F(A,B)=min(A,B) and three
fixed owners with bundles (0,0),(0,1),(1,0). Restoring (1,0) to the first owner
changes v({1,2}) from0 to1 while leaving v({1})=0 and v(N)=1. With M={1}, the
W comparison does not apply because another coalition's worth changes; the R
comparison does not apply because owner2's marginal relative to {1} changes.
Thus a formal reduction from their axioms to our resource comparisons is absent
even on a technology used by our sharp lower bounds. No new theorem is claimed.

OPEN PRIORITY GATE AND HUMAN REVIEW QUESTION

The open question is not whether coalition monotonicity, serial allocation,
Aumann--Shapley averaging, or subsidized stability have prior literature: they
do. It is whether an existing theorem gives ONE all-profile nonnegative payment
field, for arbitrary fixed mixed owners and homogeneous concave production,
that simultaneously satisfies all coalition covers, aggregate resource-report
monotonicity (or the pooled-outside increment), and the stated resource-dimension
funding factor. For the3/2 theorem, the exact additional target is one common
resource and a private DR block, with arbitrary mixed ownership across blocks.

Focused question for a specialist: Is the common/private product-ray rule
q(P,c)=integral_0^1 integral_0^1 grad F(tP,s c) ds dt, or a result implying its
coalition-covering3/2 contract, already present under another allocation name?
If a universal predecessor is proposed, please match its incentive quantifiers,
coalition coverage direction, external-funding benchmark, and resource-versus-
owner dimension explicitly. An efficient scalar-demand rule, a static core
selection, or a one-worth-at-a-time monotonicity axiom alone is insufficient.
