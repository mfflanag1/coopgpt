A THREE-HALVES PAYMENT RULE FOR UNIT-PROFIT RESOURCE GRAPHS
17 October 2026. Version2 after independent Fable audit and root algebra/replay.
Version1 is preserved verbatim in HUBER_PRICE_THREE_HALVES_CANDIDATE.txt.
Root retains authority over final promotion and historical priority assessment.
Historical novelty is unassessed. The proof is stronger in topology than the
forest target: finite arbitrary undirected graphs, with fractional production.

MODEL
Fix a finite simple resource graph G=(V,E). Activity uv consumes one unit at
each of u,v and earns one unit. F(c)=max sum_e x_e subject to x>=0 and
sum_{e incident v}x_e<=c_v. This is fractional matching, not integral matching.
Fixed actual owners report bundles b_i>=0, c=sum_i b_i. Their eligibility sets
are fixed. Deviations are arbitrary coordinatewise binding underreports of
the whole bundle; utility is payment only. No ownership is split strategically.
Every coalition S can use exactly the aggregate bundle sum_{i in S}b_i.

CONSTRUCTION
Let h(t)=t^2 for 0<=t<=1/2, and h(t)=t-1/4 for 1/2<=t<=1.
For strictly positive c, minimize
  J_c(p)=sum_v c_v h(p_v)
over 0<=p_v<=1 and p_u+p_v>=1 for every edge uv. Set
  y_i(b)=sum_v b_iv p_v(c).                                (1)
For c with zeros, solve the same optimization on the induced graph of positive
capacity vertices, use its unique minimizer there, and set p_v=1 at zero
capacity vertices. Zero-capacity prices make every edge incident to zero
feasible, while contributing zero to every payment.

CLAIM
The prices on positive-capacity vertices are unique. Rule (1) is nonnegative,
all-profile own-bundle monotone, covers every actual-owner coalition, and
  sum_i y_i <= (3/2) F(c).                                (2)
The factor 3/2 is the exact supremum for this rule, already on a single edge.
No claim of optimality over all eligible mechanisms is made.

LEMMA 1: UNIQUENESS AND INTERIORITY AT POSITIVE CAPACITIES
The feasible set is nonempty compact convex and h is convex C1, so an optimum
exists. If p_v=0 at a nonisolated v, all neighbors have price1. Raise p_v by
epsilon and lower all those neighbors from1 to1-epsilon. Constraints involving
v stay feasible; constraints between lowered neighbors stay feasible for
epsilon<=1/2, and constraints to other vertices are feasible unless a neighbor
has another zero-price neighbor. In that case raise ALL zero-price vertices
and lower ALL price-one vertices. Edges of types zero/one remain exactly tight,
one/one remain feasible, and one/interior edges remain feasible for sufficiently
small epsilon (finiteness). The objective increases only O(epsilon^2) on zero
vertices and decreases linearly on at least one positive-cost price-one vertex.
This contradicts optimality. Therefore p_v>0 for nonisolated vertices.
If p_v=1, lowering it slightly is feasible because all neighbors are positive,
again contradicting positive cost. Thus 0<p_v<1 at nonisolated vertices.
Isolated vertices uniquely have p_v=0.
Every positive-capacity nonisolated vertex has at least one tight incident
edge: otherwise all its finitely many incident inequalities have positive
slack, so its positive price can decrease slightly, reducing the objective.

For uniqueness, let p,q both minimize. By convexity their midpoint also does.
Strict convexity of h below1/2 implies: if p_v!=q_v, both p_v,q_v>=1/2.
At any optimum, a vertex with p_v>1/2 has a tight neighbor u with p_u<1/2;
otherwise its own price could decrease slightly, reducing the objective.
All coordinates below1/2 are identical in p and q by the strict-convexity
observation. If p_v>q_v, take a tight low neighbor u of v under p. Then
q_u=p_u and feasibility requires q_v>=1-p_u=p_v, a contradiction. Interchange
p,q for the opposite inequality. Thus the optimizer is unique.

LEMMA 2: CONTINUITY AND ACTIVE COMPONENT FORMULA
Uniqueness and compactness imply that p(c) is continuous for c>0: every
subsequential limit along c_k->c minimizes J_c, and so equals its unique optimum.
Consider the graph of TIGHT edges p_u+p_v=1. Its connected components are:
(a) a strict-low/high bipartite component, all low vertices having one common
    t in(0,1/2), all high vertices having1-t;
(b) a component with every price1/2; or
(c) an isolated resource vertex with price0 (isolated in G).
An active component that is bipartite but has price1/2 is included in (b).

KKT gives nonnegative edge multipliers lambda_e, zero on non-tight edges, with
  sum_{e incident v}lambda_e = c_v h'(p_v)
at every nonisolated vertex, by Lemma1 interiority. For a component (a), let
A=sum_{low v}c_v and B=sum_{high v}c_v. Sum KKT on its two sides:
  2t A = B, so t=B/(2A), A>B>0.                          (3)
Components of type(b) have saturated incident flow at every vertex.
Inactive cross-component edges carry zero lambda and impose no derivative
equation inside the component. They remain part of the feasibility constraints.

LEMMA 3: COVERAGE AND FUNDING
Every p is a feasible dual fractional vertex cover, so for any S,
  F(sum_{i in S}b_i)<=sum_v p_v sum_{i in S}b_iv=sum_{i in S}y_i.
Define r_v=0 for p_v<1/2, r_v=1 for p_v>1/2, and r_v=1/2 otherwise.
Every graph edge obeys r_u+r_v>=1: a strict-low endpoint requires a strict-high
neighbor, and an edge between half-price vertices has rounded sum1.
The KKT lambda is a feasible primal packing since h'(p)<=1. On every positive
lambda edge the rounded price sum is1. Every vertex with r_v>0 is saturated
because h'(p_v)=1. Consequently complementary slackness gives
  F(c)=sum_e lambda_e=sum_v c_v r_v.                      (4)
On each strict-low/high component its contribution to F is B, while payment
is tA+(1-t)B = (3/2-t)B <=(3/2)B, using(3).
On each half-price component payment equals its production-flow contribution,
namely one half the sum of its capacities. Summing proves(2).

LEMMA 4: OWN-REPORT MONOTONICITY AT POSITIVE AGGREGATE CAPACITIES
Fix an actual owner i and vary ONE coordinate of her report while all others
stay fixed. On an interval with a fixed tight-edge graph, all other components'
prices are constant. If the varying coordinate lies in a strict-low/high
component, let a=sum_{low}b_iv and b=sum_{high}b_iv. Her payment from that
component is a t+b(1-t), with t=B/(2A), a<=A, b<=B.
For a low-side coordinate increase (a and A each increase at unit rate),
  derivative = t-(a-b)t/A = t(1-a/A+b/A)>=0.              (5)
For a high-side coordinate increase (b and B each increase at unit rate),
  derivative = 1-t+(a-b)/(2A)>=1-2t>=0.                 (6)
For a half-price component the derivative is1/2; all component prices remain
constant on that fixed-pattern interval. For an isolated vertex it is0.

To justify passing through changes of pattern, there are finitely many edge
subsets and their component bipartitions. Each positive-capacity optimizer
belongs to one of finitely many rational formulas: (3), its reverse-orientation
version, the constant1/2 on a component, or zero for a graph-isolated vertex.
Along the chosen line segment the formulas are rational with nonvanishing
positive denominators. Identify formulas that agree identically. Each pair
of remaining distinct vector formulas can agree at only finitely many points:
at least one coordinate difference is a nonzero rational function, whose
numerator has finitely many roots. Remove their finite collection of agreement
points. On each resulting open interval the candidate vectors are pairwise
distinct. The set where the continuous optimizer equals a particular candidate
is relatively closed. Its complement, a finite union of the corresponding
closed sets for the other candidates, is also closed. Connectedness therefore
forces the optimizer to follow just one candidate throughout the interval.
Refine these intervals at all nonidentical roots of p_u+p_v-1 and p_v-1/2;
there are finitely many. Each final interval has a fixed tight graph and fixed
strict-low/high classification (identically zero functions remain tight).
On each interval (5) or(6) applies, and continuity joins them without downward
jumps. Hence each
owner's payment is nondecreasing in every own coordinate, and therefore in
arbitrary finite whole-bundle coordinatewise increases.

LEMMA 5: ZERO CAPACITIES
At a zero-capacity profile, zero vertices can be assigned price1 at no objective
cost. Thus minimization reduces to the positive-capacity induced graph, where
Lemma1 gives unique prices on all positive vertices (including isolated ones).
As c^k>0 tends to c, compactness says every optimizer subsequential limit is a
minimizer of the limiting objective. Its prices on positive vertices are the
same unique induced-graph minimizer; on zero vertices prices may vary but
the corresponding total owner report goes to zero. Thus all owner payments
converge to(1), regardless of the zero-price convention.
For complete incentive rigor, regularize the objective's capacities to c+eps*1
but keep payments weighted by the actual b_i. The positive-capacity derivative
proof still holds because a<=A and b<=B remain true with this artificial
positive slack. Therefore each regularized payment is own-monotone. Taking
eps down to zero proves own-monotonicity at every boundary profile. This is
only a proof device; no new owner, public resource, or change of coalition
opportunities is introduced by the actual rule.
Coverage at zero follows from dual feasibility; the funding statement follows
by direct application to the positive induced graph, or by a limit.

RULE-SPECIFIC SHARPNESS
One edge with capacities A>=B>0 has p_low=B/(2A), p_high=1-B/(2A).
Its payment ratio is 3/2-B/(2A), tending to3/2 as A/B grows without bound.
This is only sharpness of the constructed Huber-price rule.

COROLLARY FOR INDEPENDENT REVIEW: COALITION TOTAL-PAYMENT MONOTONICITY
Fix any actual-owner coalition S and hold all outside reports fixed. Its total
payment is sum_v (sum_{i in S}b_iv) p_v(c). The proof of Lemma4 applies verbatim
with a,b equal to S's entire low/high holdings: still a<=A and b<=B. Thus every
coordinatewise increase in S's aggregate reported bundle increases or preserves
S's total payment. The same boundary argument applies. In particular, any joint
binding underreport by S cannot increase the sum of its members' payments.
This rules out profitable withholding even when members can transfer money
among themselves, under the stipulated payment-only utilities. It does not
address private opportunity costs, overreports, or other changes to the model.
Furthermore, splitting or merging identities while preserving the aggregate
bundle leaves their total payment unchanged, since prices use only c and the
payment is linear in each bundle. This is a consequence of the actual rule;
no change to the fixed owner set is used within the main proof.

COROLLARY: THE PRIOR HARDWARE / LOCAL-POWER / SHARED-POWER MODEL
Take resource vertices P, h_1,...,h_m, L_1,...,L_m and activity edges P--h_j
and h_j--L_j, each consuming one unit of each endpoint and earning one unit.
The unit-profit production value is
  F=sum_j min(h_j,L_j)+min(P,sum_j(h_j-L_j)_+)
   =min(H, P+sum_j min(h_j,L_j)), H=sum_j h_j.
All quantities here are total reported capacities; an actual owner may hold
any mixed bundle among all these vertices. H is an aggregate, not an additional
resource row or ownership identity. Therefore the new rule gives a3/2 funding
existence theorem on exactly the prior mixed-owner physical model, improving
the earlier323/200 existence upper bound there. It is a DIFFERENT mechanism.
The old323/200 bound, diffuse asymptotics, and sharp response/obstruction facts
remain valid facts about the older nested exponential envelope; this theorem
does not claim that older rule itself has funding at most3/2.

LIMITS
- Unit edge profits and unit consumption at both endpoints are essential to
  the proof as written. Arbitrary weighted edges are not covered.
- Production is the divisible fractional matching LP, even on odd cycles.
- No private cost, strategic participation, overreporting, or scheduling/
  energy-data claim is made. The joint-deviation corollary is confined to
  binding resource underreports and sums of payment-only utility.
- Exact algorithmic bit complexity is not yet assessed. The definition is a
  finite convex quadratic/linear program; floating-point evaluation alone
  does not certify exact incentives.
- Version1 passed an independent Fable proof audit with two clarifications,
  now incorporated. Root replayed the independent KKT certificates. The exact
  mixed-owner sweep passed20,528 profiles and62,080 incentive comparisons;
  501 KKT/primal-dual certificates passed a standalone exact replay. The new
  corollaries, final root promotion, and original-source comparison remain
  subject to their separate checks. Historical novelty remains unconfirmed.
