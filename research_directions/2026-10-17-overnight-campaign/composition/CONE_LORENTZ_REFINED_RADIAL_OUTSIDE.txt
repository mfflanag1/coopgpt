1. FUNDING AND COVERAGE
Both component functions s/[b+(1-b)s] and s are in[0,1] for s in[0,1].
Their positive mixture with weights c,b equals f(s), because 1-b=c and
b=c^2. Thus0<=f<=1, giving nonnegativity and coalition coverage.
The preceding phi curve f_0=s/[c+b s] satisfies the exact identity

 f(s)-f_0(s)=s(2-3c)(1-s)^2/[(c+s)(c+b s)] >=0.        (3)

Consequently total funding is no greater than that of the phi rule.
Moreover f(1)=1 and f'(1)=c, so the funding ratio still tends to phi at s=1.
This mixture observation proves only the scalar bound0<=f<=1; the first
component by itself need not be coalition-monotone. The needed monotonicity
is proved independently below.

3. EXACT FULL-OUT SCALING AND THE UNIQUE MAXIMUM
For any parameter a>0 define the scalar deficit
 d_a(s)=a s(1-s)/[2(a+(1-a)s)^2].
The square identity from the preceding full-OUT theorem gives d_a(s)<=1/8,
with equality only at s=a/(1+a). Here

                   d(s)=c d_b(s)+b d_1(s).

The two maximizing locations differ and c,b are strictly positive. Hence
max_[0,1] d(s)<1/8. The exact derivative of d is

                   d'(s)=H(s)/[2(c+s)^3].

Moreover
 H'(s)=b(c+s)^2(3-2c-8s)-c(1+2c)<0.
Indeed (c+s)^2(3-2c-8s) is decreasing on[0,1], because its derivative is
2(c+s)(3-6c-12s)<0. Its value at0 is c^2(3-2c). The positive part
b c^2(3-2c) is less than1, while c(1+2c)>1. Thus H is strictly decreasing;
H(0)>0 and H(1)<0. The unique s_* is the global maximizer of d.

For every coalition the temporal derivative is at least1-d_*. Scale by
lambda_*=(1-d_*)^-1. Then its temporal derivative is at least1 and remains

For a Lorentz direction(dt,dx), these two facts give derivative at least

Explicitly, with temporal coefficient alpha>=1 and spatial norm beta<=alpha,

Integrating along the same fixed coalition's restoration segment proves full

4. STOPPING GATE AND REMAINING QUESTION
This is a proved improvement to a general, owner- and dimension-independent
bound. It is not an assertion that repeated constant tuning alone warrants a
new research program. The normalized curve reaches the sharp phi boundary
within the regular coefficient-one radial TCWM class, but its full-OUT
scaling is not proved optimal. Improving further requires solving a genuinely
coupled differential feasibility problem or leaving this radial class.
For a general normalized regular curve a, the necessary all-profile conditions
are the ellipse gradient inequality and, after constant scaling lambda,

 lambda[1-s a'(s)(1-s)/2]>=1

when a' is nonnegative. These constraints must hold for every s, not just on
a finite grid. The present polynomial certificate supplies an exact feasible
point of that problem; it does not settle the variational optimum.
