TWO SHARED RESOURCES: AN EXACT BOUNDARY FOR POSITIVE THREE-POOL LIFTS
17 October 2026. Composition lane. Analytic proof plus exact rational check.
Historical novelty unconfirmed. This is an architecture obstruction, not an
unrestricted mechanism lower bound.

MODEL AND CLAIM
Let P,Q be common capacities and c_0,c_1,c_2 private activity capacities.
The three activities consume common resources and earn value as follows:

             P       Q       private row       profit
 activity0   1       1       c_0 (one unit)     3/2
 activity1   1       1/4     c_1 (one unit)     1
 activity2   1/4     1       c_2 (one unit)     1.

Write F(P,Q,c) for the corresponding fractional packing optimum. There is
no all-capacity identity expressing F as a nonnegative sum or nonnegative
integral of three-pool minima of fixed nonnegative linear capacity maps:

 F(C) = integral min(L_1^omega(C),L_2^omega(C),L_3^omega(C)) d mu(omega),

where C=(P,Q,c_0,c_1,c_2), all L have nonnegative coefficients, and mu is a
nonnegative measure. Positive scalar coefficients can be absorbed into L
or mu. Identically zero summands may be removed. This includes arbitrarily
many public three-pool components, each with arbitrary nonnegative copies
and linear mixing of original rows; it excludes affine free capacities,
signed cancellations, report-dependent maps, or a joint coupled lift.

PROOF: ZERO FACES FORCE SEPARATE COMMON POOLS
F is zero on each of P=0, Q=0, and c_0=c_1=c_2=0. Evaluate these faces at
the three vectors (0,1,1,1,1), (1,0,1,1,1), and (1,1,0,0,0).
Nonnegativity implies that almost every component is zero at each vector;
the intersection of the three full-measure sets still has full measure.
For any nonzero component, its first vanishing requires one L supported
only on P. Its second requires a distinct L supported only on Q: one row
cannot be supported only on both unless identically zero. The last
vanishing then forces its remaining L to be supported only on private
coordinates. Thus, after permuting its three rows, every nonzero component
has the form

              min(alpha P, beta Q, sum_j gamma_j c_j),
              alpha,beta>0, gamma_j>=0.

At fixed P,Q this is a concave scalar truncation of a nonnegative linear
function of c. It satisfies decreasing differences, hence the rectangular
submodular inequality in any two private coordinates. Nonnegative sums or
integrals preserve that inequality.

EXACT VIOLATION
Fix P=Q=1,c_0=1 and let c_1,c_2 be 0 or1. The values are

 F(0,0)=3/2; F(1,0)=3/2; F(0,1)=3/2; F(1,1)=8/5,

where only the last two private arguments are displayed. For neither/either
side activity, the baseline plan z_0=1 attains3/2. If only activity1 is
available, take common dual prices (p_P,p_Q)=(3/2,0), and private price
5/8 on the absent activity2 row, all other private prices zero. This is a
covering dual of cost3/2 because that absent row has zero capacity. The
symmetric dual works when only activity2 is available; either construction
also covers the profile with neither side activity. If both are available,
z_1=z_2=4/5
attains8/5, and p_P=p_Q=4/5 with all private prices zero is a dual cover:
baseline cover8/5>=3/2 and each side cover1. Thus all four optima are exact.

Consequently F(1,1)+F(0,0)-F(1,0)-F(0,1)=1/10>0, contradicting the forced
private submodularity. All common consumption coefficients are strictly
positive; this is not an artifact of a missing common-resource requirement.

CONSEQUENCE AND STOPPING BOUNDARY
One-common-resource fractional-knapsack layering and equal-ratio three-pool
compression cannot be extended to this two-common-resource class by any
positive fixed linear three-pool decomposition. The current logarithmic
ratio-range upper bound survives; whether a constant funding factor exists
independently of the ratio range remains open. This result does not rule out
approximation by such components, nonseparable mechanisms, coupled auxiliary
constructions, or owner-aware rules.
