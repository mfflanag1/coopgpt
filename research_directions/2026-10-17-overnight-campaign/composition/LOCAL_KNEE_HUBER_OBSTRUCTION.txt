LOCAL PROFIT KNEES DO NOT PRESERVE MIXED-OWNER INCENTIVES
17 October 2026. Exact method obstruction, not an unrestricted lower bound.

NATURAL REPAIR TESTED
The global-maximum-profit Huber knee gives a sharp factor2 on weighted graphs.
One tempting multiscale repair gives resource v its public local knee
  q_v=(1/2) max_{e incident v} w_e,
and minimizes sum_v c_v h_v(p_v) over weighted cover constraints, where
  h_v(p)=p^2/(2q_v) for p<=q_v, and p-q_v/2 for p>=q_v.
Payments remain linear actual-owner resource prices sum_v b_iv p_v.
This construction is NOT own-report monotone for arbitrary mixed owners.

EXACT TWO-PROFILE COUNTEREXAMPLE
Use resource path0--1--2, profits w01=5/4,w12=3. Its local knees are
  q=(5/8,3/2,3/2).
Fixed actual owner A holds vertices0 and2; owner B holds vertex1.
At the smaller report, capacities are
  c=(5/8,15/8,1),
and the unique convex optimum has prices
  p=(1/4,1,2),
with edge multipliers lambda=(1/4,1). Both edge constraints are tight.
Stationarity is degree_lambda(v)=c_v min(p_v/q_v,1), as direct fractions show.
At the larger report, owner A increases only vertex0 by1/8, so
  c'=(3/4,15/8,1).
The unique optimum is
  p'=(45/196,50/49,97/49),
with lambda'=(27/98,1). Again both edges are tight and exact KKT holds.

Uniqueness at both profiles does not require a general theorem: vertices0,1
lie strictly inside their quadratic pieces. Equality in convexity forces both
their prices to agree between minimizers; then positive cost and the tight
edge12 force vertex2 to its smallest feasible price3-p1. Thus both listed
prices are forced by the convex objective, not adverse tie-breaking.

Owner A's payments are
  y_A(c)=(5/8)(1/4)+2=69/32,
  y_A(c')=(3/4)(45/196)+97/49=1687/784,
and
  y_A(c)-y_A(c')=7/1568>0.
Therefore at true report c', owner A profits by hiding1/8 of vertex0 while
retaining the same vertex2 stock. The owner set and eligibility never change.
All capacities and activity profits are positive; this is already a tree/star.

CONSEQUENCE
Simply replacing a global knee by each vertex's largest incident edge value
does not extend the unit theorem. Public scale covariance and local incidence
are insufficient: the common curvature across a tight component was part of
the coalition-monotonicity proof. This does not rule out the positive-prefix
decomposition available for weighted stars or other multiscale constructions.
