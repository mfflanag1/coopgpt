OWNER STANDALONE COMPENSATION BY ONE NORM SUM DOES NOT ESCAPE THE RADIAL BARRIER
17 October 2026. Exact architecture obstruction; no unrestricted lower claim.

CLASS
Fix p>=2 and exposure dimension at least2. Let S=sum_i ||x_i||_p,
R=||sum_i x_i||_p, r=R/S, and e*(X)=J_p(X)/R^(p-1). Consider actual-owner
payments of the form

 y_i=lambda t_i-(lambda-1)||x_i||_p-a(r)<x_i,e*(X)>,   (1)

where lambda>=1 is any fixed finite constant (it may depend on p, dimension,
and the fixed owner count), and a is continuous and locally piecewise-C1, with finitely many seams on
each compact interior interval and bounded one-sided derivatives at those
seams. Require a to be C1 near r0=(p-1)/(p+3) and near1. These explicit
conditions imply local absolute continuity and justify the epsilon derivative
limit below. In particular, C1 on[r0,1] is sufficient for the tested interval. This is an OWNER-DEPENDENT class:
it uses each owner's standalone exposure norm and the total of those norms.

Here TCWM means that the TOTAL payment of every fixed coalition is
nondecreasing when a cone-feasible bundle is restored to any of its members,
with outsiders unchanged. Iteration gives simultaneous coalition restoration.

CLAIM
If(1) has nonnegative payments, coalition coverage, all-profile TCWM and
finite total-output funding, then it inherits the necessary derivative
condition of the coefficient-one norm-support-radial architecture. In
particular its worst-case funding factor is at least

                         1+(p+1)/8.                  (2)

Thus neither a fixed standalone reserve coefficient nor replacing aggregate
reserve by S can turn the preceding radial O(p) construction into O(sqrt p).
The sharp L1 lift escapes this class by preserving separate projection-level
positive/negative totals, rather than reducing them to one scalar norm sum.

1. REALIZING AN ARBITRARY NORM-CONE LENS WITH FIXED ACTUAL OWNERS
Fix a hypothetical coalition bundle(theta,x) and outside bundle(1-theta,z)
in the norm cone, with X=x+z nonzero and R=||X||=r. Thus ||x||<=theta,
||z||<=1-theta. Each vector x can be represented as the sum of TWO vectors
x_1+x_2=x having ||x_1||+||x_2||=theta. Indeed choose any unit vector v;
the continuous function
 t -> ||x/2+t v||+||x/2-t v||
starts at ||x|| and tends to infinity. The intermediate value theorem gives
the desired t. The same construction represents z with norm sum1-theta.
Use these four vectors as fixed owner reports with t_i=||x_i||, so S=T=1.
Zero vectors are allowed when a target norm sum is zero.

Add a FIFTH fixed actual owner to the coalition, initially reporting
(epsilon,epsilon u), where ||u||=1. All five owner identities and their full
cone eligibility are fixed. Changing epsilon changes reports, not identities.
As epsilon tends to0, the coalition and aggregate bundles approach the
chosen lens profile, while this owner's norm support e*(u) remains freely
choosable among all dual unit vectors. This last assertion follows directly
from ell_p/ell_q duality for1<p<infinity.

2. DIFFERENTIATING ONLY THAT SMALL OWNER'S RESTORATION
Restore a cone bundle(dt,dx), dt>=||dx||, to the fifth owner, holding all
other reports fixed. Write h=<x,e*(X)> at the limiting S=1 profile, and
let H_X be the Hessian of ||X||_p, which exists here for p>=2 at X!=0.
The coalition payment derivative in the epsilon->0 limit is

 lambda dt-[C e*(u)+V] dot dx,
 C=lambda-1-r a'(r)h,
 V=[a(r)+a'(r)h]e*(X)+a(r)H_X x.                     (3)

All expressions are continuous at the chosen positive-total smooth profile;
the limit is taken after applying the necessary derivative inequality at
each epsilon>0. For every epsilon, a restoration of a boundary retained
bundle is feasible. Thus TCWM implies

                      ||C e*(u)+V||_q<=lambda

for EVERY unit u. Choose e*(u) parallel or antiparallel to V according to
the sign of C. Then

 ||V||_q<=lambda-|C|
         <=1+r a'(r)h.                               (4)

This is exactly the temporal-versus-spatial gradient condition for the
ordinary radial payment t_i-a(r)<x_i,e*(X)> on the original norm-cone lens.
No aggregate-price representation theorem and no strategic owner splitting
are used; the five actual owners are fixed before every comparison.

3. FUNDING AND THE LINEAR p BARRIER
At any profile where each actual owner is on its cone boundary, T=S, and
summing(1) gives

                  sum_i y_i=S-a(r)R.

The production is S-R. Thus finite funding forces a(1)=1, and the boundary
ratio is1+a'(1). Nonnegativity for an aligned boundary owner implies a<=1.
Condition(4) is precisely the condition used in
P_NORM_GROWTH_RADIAL_LINEAR_BARRIER.txt. Its collinear and transverse tests
therefore give a'(1)>=(p+1)/8, proving(2).

4. A CONCRETE FAILED O(sqrt p) CANDIDATE
A tempting rule chooses lambda=K+1 and

                       a(r)=max(0,1-K+Kr).

It does satisfy nonnegativity, coalition coverage, and total <=(K+1)F:
when active, its total at T=S is (S-R)(1+Kr); the general T>=S case is
bounded by(K+1)(T-R), and the inactive case follows from r<=1-1/K.
But its boundary derivative is K, so TCWM would require K>=(p+1)/8.

For an exact numerical-free failure of the intended order, choose p=256,
K=16 and lambda=17. At r=(p-1)/(p+3)=255/259, the curve is smooth and
a(r)=195/259>1/2. The transverse lens from the preceding radial note has
h=0 and spatial dual norm at least2a(r)=390/259>1, contradicting(4).
The fifth-owner construction above converts this strict limiting violation
into a violation at some positive finite epsilon by continuity. Thus there
is an actual finite fixed-owner counterprofile, not merely an unsupported
failure of a numerical optimizer. The unbounded-p order cannot be repaired
inside this scalar compensation architecture.

5. SCOUT STATUS AND WHAT REMAINS OPEN
P_NORM_GROWTH_OWNER_SCOUT.py and P_NORM_GROWTH_OWNER_FOCUSED_SCOUT.py were
numerical discovery probes. Random profiles missed some relevant nearly
aligned/tiny-owner cases; no passed numerical sweep was treated as a proof.
The analytic reduction(3)-(4), not those sweeps, closes the gate.
The unrestricted gap remains Omega(sqrt(p/log p)) versus O(p). A better
upper must retain more of the owners' directional cancellation information
than either a single aggregate support direction or one total norm sum.
Historical priority of this architecture obstruction remains unconfirmed.
