First inference-consortium pilot: results and next decision
11 October 2026

DECISION
Proceed to measured service/energy calibration and request-level replay before
expanding the economic mechanism. The pilot successfully connects the existing
CoopGPT payment rules to a physically consistent, deliberately narrow capacity
model. It exposes real mathematical differences between payment rules. It does
not establish a practical market, measured throughput gains, historical novelty,
or a field-level breakthrough.

A necessary modeling correction: this first implementation pools additive ENERGY
allowances (kWh). Its separate engineering peak-power envelope is safe and
nonbinding. The same GPU-hours LP cannot simply be relabeled as an allocation of
scarce instantaneous kW. Scarce peak power remains a substantive next extension.

1. What was built

Three fixed owners hold flexible one-hour reservations in two local hardware
bundle types and incremental energy allowances at one meter. Each hardware bundle
contains its GPU, local memory and complete warm model replica. No memory or power
is implicitly transferred between sites. Reported node time can be scheduled
anywhere in the window. Owners have already committed the underlying reservations;
their modeled utility is payment only, without concurrent own-use or outside rental.

Let c=(H,L,E) be pooled node-hours and incremental kWh. The synthetic technology is
  F(c)=max {6*x_H+2*x_L : 0<=x_H<=H, 0<=x_L<=L, 2*x_H+x_L<=E}
      =min {6H+2L, 2H+2E, 3E}.
Every coalition S has value F(sum_{i in S} b_i), using only its members' rights.
The numbers 6,2,2,1 are declared synthetic value/energy parameters, not benchmarks
for named GPUs. Public output values measure service-capacity points, not dollars
or completed requests. The production technology, proportional weights and hybrid
parameter were frozen before outcome computation in PROTOCOL.txt and its hash.

For each profile, a separate primal-vertex calculation agrees exactly with the
dual value. The optimal runtimes are assigned to named owner/type/node intervals.
Every interval respects reported and installed node-hour capacity; incremental
energy and peak-envelope accounting are checked. The peak bound is redundant by
construction: it is the draw of all installed nodes at maximum operation, so this
check does not test allocation under scarce power or validate an actual feeder.
Idle warm-node power is a
synthetic 0.1 kW per installed node and is accounted for separately as a committed
baseline. The resulting certificates validate the continuous capacity schedule.
They do not validate token-level completions, batching, startup or latency SLAs.

Installed inventory is now a mandatory, explicit schedule input and cannot be
inferred from a changed report. The design grid fixes six nodes throughout. Each
four-owner stress case has its own fixed inventory derived from the predeclared
true endowment; the energy-only case has none. Baseline energy is therefore not
comparable across different physical scenarios. It never enters the payment game.

2. The predeclared example

Owner bundles (H hours,L hours,E kWh):
  A=(1,0,1), B=(0,1,2), C=(1,1,1).
Operating separately produces 8 value points. Serving each owner's standalone
plan first and then pooling residual resources produces 11. Joint optimization
produces 12: a 50% improvement over autarky in THIS SYNTHETIC EXAMPLE.
The optimum runs two H nodes for one hour each and leaves L nodes idle. It consumes
4 incremental kWh, plus 0.6 kWh of committed idle baseline across the fixed six-node
inventory. Peak node load is 4.6 kW, below the declared safe 9.6 kW feeder envelope.
These are model calculations, not measured facility electricity consumption.

All payment comparisons below use the SAME jointly optimal coalition game:
  rule            total paid    external subsidy    largest coalition deficit
  proportional    12            0                   1/2
  dual prices     12            0                   0
  Shapley         12            0                   0
  grand marginal  14            2                   0
  sharp hybrid    83/6           11/6                0

The hybrid requires about 1.833 additional value points in this example, against
2 for grand marginals. It is not claimed to dominate marginals pointwise. A public
sponsor or an explicit relaxation of coalition coverage is needed to fund subsidy;
creating extra accounting entries is not a balanced-budget market implementation.
Comparing these value points with real subsidy dollars requires an economic model.

'Standalone-first sharing' is a feasible dispatch comparator, not a claim to
implement owner-priority policies at a real consortium. There are no owner-specific
private jobs in this model. Dispatch comparisons and payment comparisons are kept
separate so a scheduling difference does not masquerade as a payment advantage.

3. Exhaustive design-grid evidence

Enumerated 1,728 three-owner profiles, each owner's H,L in {0,1} and E in {0,1,2}.
Of these, 1,638 have positive production. For EACH payment rule, checks cover 13,824
coalitions and 18,144 strict componentwise-lower report pairs. All 1,728 capacity
schedules passed. Observed results:

  rule            profiles with       profiles with       maximum total
                  coalition deficit   withholding gain    payment / F
  proportional    1,335               0                   1
  dual prices     0                   138                 1
  Shapley         504                 0                   1
  grand marginal  0                   0                   2
  sharp hybrid    0                   0                   19/12

These counts are frequencies in a uniform ARTIFICIAL STRESS GRID, not estimated
probabilities of real-world failure. Maximum hybrid subsidy in ratio terms is
7/12, about 58.3% of production, versus 100% for marginals on this grid. The observed
19/12 is not a new universal upper bound and is not an optimal-mechanism claim.
For grand marginals the pre-existing sparse-production result gives the universal
2F total-payment bound under these hypotheses because every activity uses two
resource rows. The existing manuscript gives the hybrid's all-profile guarantees;
the grid tests its implementation rather than proving those guarantees anew.

Exact adverse witnesses are retained. One dual-price example has owners
  (0,0,1), (0,0,2), (1,1,0).
Truthful production is 8 and fixed-priority dual payments are (2,4,2). If the third
owner withholds its L reservation, production falls to 6, but its payment rises
to 6: an individual gain of 4. This is a failure of this fixed-priority price rule,
not a theorem that all efficient mechanisms fail in this specific three-form class.
Proportional allocation's worst deficit is 24/7; Shapley's is 1. Full exact reports,
coalitions and payments are in RESULTS.json; every design-profile payment vector
and diagnostics are in PAYMENTS.csv.

4. Validation beyond the design grid

Six predeclared four-owner stress profiles cover fractional ownership, redundant
hardware, balanced ownership, energy without hardware, hardware without energy,
and separated complementary endowments. Each checks all 16 coalitions and every
unilateral report with coordinates zero, half or all of that owner's true quota:
96 coalitions and 110 strict report pairs per rule across these cases. All intended
mechanism checks and all six base capacity schedules passed. These are structural
holdout checks, not a statistical test of performance on sampled market data.

The exact LP oracle evaluated 368 distinct aggregate inputs; the driver cached
9,220 payment vectors. The run takes about 4-5 seconds on this machine. Hybrid and
Shapley enumerate subsets and are exponential in owner count; this prototype does
not demonstrate scalable deployment. The grand-marginal payment formula needs
n+1 production values, but this tiny oracle uses vertex enumeration rather than a
production-grade polynomial LP solver. Normal and optimized Python runs reproduce
identical mathematical results and CSV; runtime is intentionally variable.

5. What the real trace does and does not add

A bounded download from the official Azure LLM 2024 Code trace provided the first
source minute: 1,537 request records, timestamps and input/output token lengths.
The selected minute has 25.62 arrivals/second, context-token median 1,767 and p99
7,685, generated-token median 9 and p99 259. This is a chronological convenience
sample, not representative of all Azure service and not a held-out serving test.
Source, CC BY 4.0 attribution, selection, unchanged-row sample hash and transport
metadata are retained. The acquisition reads only 512 KiB of the large source.

Crucially, the trace does not measure our hardware service rates, energy, owners,
values, costs or private preferences. It does not enter or calibrate the economic
results above. It supplies a real workload-shape input for the NEXT replay stage.
No empirical throughput, energy savings, revenue or carbon claim follows yet.
Dataset: https://github.com/Azure/AzurePublicDataset/blob/master/AzureLLMInferenceDataset2024.md
Accompanying paper: https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.00741

6. Recommended next milestone and stopping gates

First obtain an openly usable, measured serving-mode table for two local hardware
configurations on the SAME model, recording memory fit, throughput versus input/
output length and batch size, incremental and idle power, and latency conditions.
A paper figure can motivate scenarios, but an incompatible pair of benchmark
numbers must not be sold as one calibrated system. If no coherent free data are
available, retain the synthetic label and specify the minimal local experiment.

Before inspecting replay outcomes, freeze hardware configurations, workload split,
public objective, available capacities, deadline definition, and validation gates.
Use additional chronological windows for evaluation rather than tuning to this
one-minute descriptive prefix. A reasonable proposed promotion gate is: zero
resource/deadline violations for admitted requests, at most 10% throughput and
energy prediction error on declared held-out windows, and a stated procedure for
rejected requests. Those tolerances are a prospective engineering choice, not a
result already established or a universal statistical standard.

Then evaluate whether feasible pooling gains survive queueing, request granularity,
heterogeneous memory fit, and the subsidy required by a credible committed-rights
contract. Do not claim payment-only incentives for a market where participants can
use withheld capacity privately. Private opportunity costs, scarce peak power,
and buyer demand require explicit extensions to the model or its theorem.

The most promising new mathematical target after this gate is a mechanism for
validated simultaneous serving configurations under a genuinely scarce shared
power limit, with explicitly owned rights and fixed-owner incentives. The current
linear capacity model provides a baseline and falsification harness; it does not
resolve that target. Owner-count scaling, private flexibility costs, and temporal
energy shifting are follow-ons rather than parameters silently added here.

7. Contribution and review status

This is an application prototype and reproducible comparison of existing payment
rules. LP duality, linear-production core reasoning, Shapley allocation, and the
grand-marginal baseline have established antecedents (see the prior scoping source
ledger). The sharp hybrid comes from the existing CoopGPT manuscript. No new
mathematical theorem or historical novelty is asserted by this pilot. A completed
Fable 5.1 audit found no consequential errors in its supplied scope, with two low-
severity physical-accounting clarifications now addressed. The sparse-production
bound was outside that packet; its written proof was independently checked here.
Verification and review limitations are recorded in REVIEW.txt. Internal AI review
is not external human specialist endorsement.
