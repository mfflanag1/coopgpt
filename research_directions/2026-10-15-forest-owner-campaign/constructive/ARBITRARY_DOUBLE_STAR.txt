All weighted double stars: positive decomposition into three-input components
15 October 2026. Candidate structural lemma; historical novelty unconfirmed.

Fix centers u,v of capacities U,V, central edge profit a>0, leaves at u of
capacities s_j and edge profits b_j>0, and leaves at v of capacities t_k and
edge profits c_k>0. Endpoint consumption coefficients are initially one.
Define threshold capacities
  B(theta)=sum_{j:b_j>=theta}s_j,
  C(theta)=sum_{k:c_k>=theta}t_k.
For all nonnegative real capacities, the production value satisfies

F = integral_0^a min(U+C(a-theta), V+B(theta), U+V) dtheta
    + integral_a^infinity min(U,B(theta)) dtheta
    + integral_a^infinity min(V,C(theta)) dtheta.             (ADS)

Integrals are finite step-function sums. Their breakpoints depend only on the
fixed technology profits, never on capacity reports. There are O(J+K) terms.
All weights are nonnegative and every term is a minimum of at most three fixed
nonnegative linear combinations of actual resource capacities. No dominance
condition such as a>=max b+max c is imposed.

PROOF. Fix a feasible plan, with central quantity z and quantities on each
leaf edge. Integrating its selected-edge quantities over the three ranges in
(ADS) recovers exactly its weighted objective: the central edge is counted for
length a; a u-leaf for length min(b_j,a)+(b_j-a)_+=b_j, and a v-leaf similarly.
For a theta in (0,a), after aggregating the selected leaves the corresponding
unweighted three-edge path has optimum
  min(U+C(a-theta), V+B(theta), U+V).
This follows either from the three minimal vertex covers or from the direct
one-dimensional calculation below. The tail components are unweighted stars.
Thus every plan has value at most the RHS of (ADS).

We construct one plan attaining all component optima simultaneously. Put
m=min(U,V). For a given central quantity z in [0,m], allocate remaining center
capacity greedily to its incident leaves in descending original profit order.
For the central theta component, the value of this plan is
  z+min(U-z,B(theta))+min(V-z,C(a-theta)).
As a function of z, this is maximized exactly on the interval between
  L(theta)=clip(U-B(theta),0,m),
  R(theta)=clip(V-C(a-theta),0,m),
where clip(x,0,m)=max(0,min(x,m)). This follows from slope +1 before both
unclipped thresholds, 0 between them, and -1 after both, with endpoints clipped.

L is nondecreasing in theta, R is nonincreasing. All intervals I(theta) between
L(theta),R(theta) intersect pairwise: if theta<eta and I(theta) lay strictly
left of I(eta), this would force R(theta)<R(eta), a contradiction; if it lay
strictly right, this would force L(theta)>L(eta). A finite family of intervals
with pairwise intersections has common intersection. Only finitely many open
threshold cells occur, so choose
  z*=max_theta min(L(theta),R(theta)),
which lies in every I(theta). The greedy-leaf plan with this z* attains all
central components.

Let B_>a=sum_{b_j>a}s_j and C_>a=sum_{c_k>a}t_k. Every central theta includes
all these high-profit leaves. Consequently, for every theta,
  min(L(theta),R(theta)) <= clip(U-B_>a,0,m),
  min(L(theta),R(theta)) <= clip(V-C_>a,0,m),
and the same bounds hold for z*. For any theta>a, this implies
  min(U-z*,B(theta))=min(U,B(theta)),
  min(V-z*,C(theta))=min(V,C(theta)).
Indeed if high-profit leaf capacity exceeds its center capacity, z*=0;
otherwise the remaining center capacity suffices for every high-profit leaf.
The same greedy-leaf plan therefore attains every tail star too. This proves
(ADS). Zero capacities, absent leaf sides, tied values and m=0 are included;
changes on finitely many integration endpoints do not affect the formula.

PAYMENTS. Give each actual owner its contribution to the two or three linear
arguments in each term of (ADS). Apply the proved mixed-owner three-pool softmax
rule (using the proved mixed two-input rule for two-form terms),
then sum with the positive interval lengths. Every real coalition obeys the
same decomposition. Increasing an owner's whole resource bundle raises each
of its derived accounts and leaves all other owners' accounts fixed. The mixed
rule is proved on the full independent-account domain: coordinatewise increases
may be checked there even if intermediate vectors leave the linear image of
physical bundles. Thus the
result covers all coalitions and is truthful for binding underreports on ALL
nonnegative profiles, with total at most
  [1+W(2/e)] F, approximately 1.4630565 F.
The number of fixed owners and both leaf counts may grow without bound.
Owner identities do not change, and an owner may hold arbitrary resources on
both sides of the graph. Disjoint unions compose even when owners span them.

POSITIVE ENDPOINT COEFFICIENTS. On any forest with positive incident resource
coefficients A_re, choose a root in each component, assign positive row scale
s_root=1, and recursively choose s_neighbor so
  s_r A_re = s_neighbor A_neighbor,e =: lambda_e.
There is no consistency obstruction because the graph is acyclic. Set
z_e=lambda_e x_e. Multiplying each resource constraint by s_r converts it to
  sum_{e incident r} z_e <= s_r c_r,
and edge profit becomes p_e/lambda_e. Every owner bundle transforms by the
same fixed positive coordinate scalings. Thus arbitrary positive two-endpoint
coefficients, including the target's hardware coefficient 1 and power e_jk,
reduce to the unit-incidence model without affecting all-profile incentives,
coalition values or budget factors. The normalization does not solve a general
forest; it transfers (ADS) only to components with double-star topology.

LIMITATION AND NEXT EXACT FRONTIER. The proof uses one central decision z and
an interval intersection. A path with four edges already has three nonleaf
vertices and two interacting central edge quantities; the scalar argument does
not apply automatically. This is a structural stopping point, not an
impossibility certificate for four-edge paths or deeper trees.

PRIORITY BOUNDARY. The dominant-corner double-star condition has an exact
antecedent in Solymosi, arXiv:2210.17373, Proposition 8, for unit-capacity
assignment games and population monotonicity. Formula (ADS) removes that
dominance condition and treats arbitrary continuous capacities with fixed
mixed resource owners. The present argument alone does not establish that
this decomposition or payment consequence is historically novel.
