Weighted forests reduce to unweighted subforests by fixed positive sums
15 October 2026. Structural reduction; historical novelty unconfirmed.

First normalize all positive endpoint resource coefficients to one by the
fixed row/activity scaling in ARBITRARY_DOUBLE_STAR.txt. Consider a bipartite
forest G=(H union P,E), edge profits w_hp>0, and arbitrary capacities c_v>=0:
  F_w(c)=max {sum_e w_e x_e: x>=0, sum_{e incident v}x_e<=c_v}.

POTENTIALS. Choose a root and assign it an arbitrary real potential r_root.
Traverse each tree to solve
  w_hp=r_p-r_h
on every edge hp, h in H,p in P. Acyclicity guarantees consistency. Potentials
may be negative; their endpoint differences on all actual edges remain positive.
For theta not equal to a vertex potential, define the fixed active subforest
  E_theta={hp in E: r_h<theta<r_p}.
Let F_theta(c) be the unit-profit matching/production LP on E_theta, using the
same actual resources and capacities, with isolated rows harmless.

THEOREM. On every nonnegative real capacity profile,
  F_w(c)=integral_{-infinity}^{infinity} F_theta(c) dtheta.       (WF)
The integral has at most |V|-1 nonempty intervals between distinct potentials.
Every coefficient (interval length) is nonnegative and technology-dependent,
not report-dependent. The active graphs are unweighted subforests.

PROOF. Fix a weight-optimal feasible flow x, which exists by compactness. We
show that its restriction to E_theta is a maximum-cardinality flow there for
every theta avoiding the finite vertex-potential set.
Suppose otherwise. The usual residual network for the capacitated bipartite
matching LP yields an augmenting alternating path in E_theta, starting at a
hardware h with unused active-flow capacity and ending at power p with unused
active-flow capacity. Every hardware on this active path has potential below
theta; every power has potential above theta.

Increase flow along forward edges and decrease it along backward edges by a
sufficiently small delta>0. At either endpoint, if total flow already saturates
its full resource capacity, remove delta from one incident positive-flow edge
outside E_theta. Such an outside edge exists because that endpoint has unused
active-flow capacity. If the endpoint has positive total slack, simply choose
delta below that slack and remove no outside flow. All backward/path/outside
edge quantities being reduced can be kept nonnegative by taking delta small.

An outside edge at the low hardware endpoint h leads to a power p' with
r_p'<theta; otherwise the edge would be active. An outside edge at the high
power endpoint p leads to hardware h' with r_h'>theta. These outside endpoints
are not internal vertices of the active augmenting path. All internal active
resource usage stays fixed; endpoint usage is either offset or within slack;
outside endpoint usage only decreases. Thus the modified plan is feasible.

Telescoping w_hp=r_p-r_h along the alternating path gives a gain per delta of
r_p-r_h before outside removals. With neither, hardware-only, power-only, or
both outside removals, the net gain per delta is respectively
  r_p-r_h, r_p-r_p', r_h'-r_h, or r_h'-r_p'.
Every expression is strictly positive by the threshold inequalities. This
contradicts weight optimality. Therefore sum_{e in E_theta}x_e=F_theta(c).
Finally, integrate and exchange the finite edge sum with the integral:
  sum_e w_e x_e
    =sum_e (r_p-r_h)x_e
    =integral sum_{e in E_theta}x_e dtheta
    =integral F_theta(c)dtheta.
This proves (WF) directly for arbitrary real capacities; no splitting or
cloning of strategic owners is used even in the proof.

MECHANISM CONSEQUENCE. Fix any class of forests closed under edge deletion.
If every unweighted graph in that class admits an all-profile, mixed-owner,
truthful coalition-covering payment rule with uniform factor C, compose those
rules for the finitely many graphs E_theta using the fixed positive interval
lengths in (WF). Every coalition has the same decomposition, and component
reports are the unchanged actual resource bundles. Thus the composition has
identical factor C, whole-owner monotonicity and coalition coverage. Conversely
unit profits are a subclass. The optimal uniform factor for weighted and
unweighted versions of the class is therefore the same (use epsilon-optimal
rules if the infimum is not attained).

Including fixed positive endpoint coefficients does not change this equality:
row/activity normalization maps owner bundles by fixed positive diagonal
scaling before the threshold decomposition. Counts of owners, resources and
activities remain unrestricted; actual owners never change across reports.

The proof in fact applies to any bipartite graph whose positive edge profits
admit potentials w_hp=r_p-r_h. Forests guarantee this property automatically;
arbitrary cyclic weighted graphs need not. This does not solve the remaining
uniform subsidy bound for unweighted forests, but removes price and energy
heterogeneity as separate sources of difficulty in that entire class.

APPLICATION. Every active subgraph of an arbitrary weighted double star is a
disjoint union of stars/double stars with unit profits. Aggregate its leaf
capacities to obtain at most three linear vertex-cover forms. The mixed
three-pool mechanism then yields the already obtained uniform factor
1+W(2/e). Every active subgraph of a four-edge path is a disjoint union of
paths of at most four edges; each unit-profit component has at most four
minimal vertex covers. The mixed four-pool rule gives uniform factor
1+(10/9)W(3/e)<2 on arbitrary weighted four-edge paths as well.
