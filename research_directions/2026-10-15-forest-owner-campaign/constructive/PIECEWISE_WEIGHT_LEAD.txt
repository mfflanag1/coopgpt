EXPLORATORY LEAD, NOT AN ACCEPTED MIXED-OWNER THEOREM
Investigation bounded at about ten minutes; this file is excluded from proved-result claims.

For K pools put r=K-1>=3 and u in (0,1) solving u exp(-u)=1/r. Define l=1+u,h=1+1/u,A=(1+u)^2/(r u), and weights
 w(rho)=exp(1-rho)                         for 1<=rho<=l;
        A/rho-1/r                         for l<=rho<=h;
        (u/r)exp(h-rho)                    for rho>=h.
The pieces and their first derivatives agree at l,h. This is the fastest pointwise decay obeying the two exclusive-owner sufficient differential inequalities
  -w'(rho)<=w(rho),  r[-rho w'(rho)-w(rho)]<=1,
with w(1)=1. These inequalities establish long- and short-coordinate truthfulness for exclusive-role owners. They do NOT by themselves currently constitute a proof for mixed owners.

Budget calculation for K=5 (r=4): on the middle interval, maximize
 w(rho)(rho-C)=A+C/r-AC/rho-rho/r.
The maximizing rho is sqrt(r A C), and the sharp middle-case equation C-1=r max w(rho)(rho-C) gives
  C=(1+r A)^2/(4r A)=approximately1.8373344851,
with maximizer approximately3.0776826319 inside [1.3574029562,3.7979623075]. The tails must still be checked explicitly before stating a full sharp-budget result. For K=4 the analogous middle maximum falls just beyond the middle interval and therefore that naive formula is not the exact bound.

Mixed-owner derivative test: write rho_j=k_j+t_j, s_j=-w'(rho_j)t_j/w(rho_j), W=1+sum w_j,M=sum w_js_j/W. A sufficient (and essentially extremal) short-derivative numerator is
  1-M+sum_{s_j<M} w_j min(t_j,1)(s_j-M).
Script test_piecewise_weight.py tested eight million random configurations for each of K=4 and K=5, with k integers and t>=max(0,1-k); no negative values beyond floating roundoff were observed. This is only computational evidence, not proof. A proof or exact failure of this mixed-owner inequality is the unresolved gate. Even a positive answer only supplies another bounded-form primitive and does not defeat the fixed-K forest decomposition obstruction.

Tail check for K=5: the first interval lies below C, so its w(rho)(rho-C) is nonpositive. On the upper exponential tail the unconstrained maximizer would be rho=C+1<h, so that expression decreases throughout the tail; its boundary value is bounded by the middle-interval maximum. Thus the displayed C bound is valid for the exclusive-owner five-pool rule. No mixed-owner or unrestricted-forest improvement follows without the missing derivative proof.
