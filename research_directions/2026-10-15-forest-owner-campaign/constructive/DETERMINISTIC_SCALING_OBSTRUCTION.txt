FIXED OUTSIDE-SCALING MARGINAL MIXTURES CANNOT UNIFORMLY BEAT FACTOR TWO
Architecture-specific obstruction; does not rule out general truthful mechanisms.

Consider payment-only underreports with fixed actual owners. For t in [0,1] define the eligible-incentive building block
  D_i(t;b)=F(b_i+t sum_{j!=i}b_j)-F(t sum_{j!=i}b_j).
Each D_i is own-report nondecreasing and nonnegative. The architecture pays
  y_i=integral_[0,1] D_i(t;b) nu_n(dt),
where nu_n is any fixed nonnegative measure chosen independently of reports; it may depend on owner count n and the fixed technology. Assume mu_n=integral t nu_n(dt)<infinity so that balanced-profile payments are finite. Atoms, including at t=1, are allowed; hence mixtures with grand marginal U_i=D_i(1) are included.

THEOREM. On the single-edge two-resource production F(a,b)=min(a,b), if this architecture covers every coalition on every n-owner profile, its worst-case total-payment factor is at least
  2(n-3)/(n-1), n>=4.
Consequently no uniform factor below2 is possible as owner counts grow.

PROOF. Write m=n-2 and delta=2/(m-1). Take two left owners, each capacity1, and m right owners, each capacity delta. Total right capacity is 2+delta. For any right owner, the OTHER right capacity equals2, the full left capacity. Thus D_right(t)=0 for every t. For either left owner,
  D_left(t)=min(1,(1+delta)t).
Choose a coalition consisting of one left owner and ceil(1/delta) right owners. It is feasible since ceil((m-1)/2)<=m, and its value is1. Coverage therefore forces
  1<=integral min(1,(1+delta)t)nu_n(dt)<=(1+delta)mu_n,
so mu_n>=1/(1+delta)=(m-1)/(m+1)=(n-3)/(n-1).
Now keep the same n actual owners and let exactly two report one unit in opposite resources, while every other owner reports zero. Each active owner has D_i(t)=t, and every zero-report owner has D=0. Total payment is2mu_n>=2(n-3)/(n-1), while T=1. This proves the claim. All profiles use the same fixed owner count and the same single-edge technology; there is no cloning or owner insertion within a deviation.

For a single report-independent measure nu shared across owner counts, letting n increase directly forces integral t nu>=1 and gives factor>=2 already at the balanced two-active-owner profile. Owner-count-dependent measures only replace exact2 by an asymptotic lower bound tending to2.

The theorem does not cover report-dependent weights (which would need a separate incentive proof), different perturbations of different resources, negative combinations, or mechanisms outside this own-marginal scaling architecture. Historical priority of this elementary obstruction is unassessed.
