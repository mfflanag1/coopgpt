A mixed-owner four-input softmax rule below factor two
15 October 2026. Candidate extension; historical novelty unconfirmed.

Fix lambda=9/10. For arbitrary real owners with four input accounts c_ij>=0,
let T=min_j sum_i c_ij, d_ij=min(c_ij,T), rho_j=sum_i d_ij/T, and
  w_j=exp[-lambda(rho_j-1)], p_j=w_j/sum_h w_h,
  y_i=sum_j p_j d_ij.
At T=0 pay zero. The rule covers all coalitions, is nondecreasing in every
coordinate of every actual owner's full bundle, and pays at most
  C4 T, C4=1+(10/9)W(3/e)<2.
Here W denotes the principal real Lambert W function. It is not the weight sum
W used locally below. The mechanism has no identity changes or separate
strategic control assigned to one owner's accounts.

COVERAGE. Each capped input total for a coalition covers its min-input value.
A positive convex combination preserves coverage.

BUDGET. Put u_j=rho_j-1>=0, with at least one u_j=0. If b>0 solves
b exp(b)=3/e, the inequality
  (v-b)exp(-v)<=exp(-1-b), v>=0,
gives sum_j (lambda u_j-b)exp(-lambda u_j)<=-b+3exp(-1-b)=0.
Thus total/T<=1+b/lambda. To see this is less than two, it suffices that
b<lambda, equivalent to 3<lambda exp(1+lambda); this holds at lambda=9/10.

INCENTIVES, LONG COORDINATE. If an increased own account belongs to a long
input, T is fixed. Its own account derivative before c_ij=T is
p_j[1-lambda(d_ij/T)(1-p_j)]>=0 since lambda<=1; after the cap it is constant.
All other weights' normalized probabilities increase and their own-owner
holdings are fixed. So the actual owner's total cannot fall.

INCENTIVES, SHORT COORDINATE. Fix all other capacities and increase the actual
owner's coordinate x in uniquely short input 0; T=x+a. Work away from clipping
and tie points. For the other three pools j=1,2,3 write
  rho_j=k_j+t_j,
where k_j counts capped accounts and t_j is the sum of uncapped accounts /T.
Then w_j'=lambda w_j t_j/T. The short pool has w_0=1. Put
  W=1+sum_j w_j, m=sum_j w_j t_j/W, M=lambda m.
We first have M<1. Indeed each term w_j(lambda t_j-1) is at most
exp(lambda-2): for a positive bracket use rho_j>=t_j and maximize
(lambda t-1)exp(lambda-lambda t); a nonpositive bracket is immediate.
Thus sum_j w_j(lambda t_j-1)<=3exp(-11/10)<1, which is equivalent to M<1.
(The strict exponential inequality is certified by the Taylor polynomial
through degree five at 11/10, which already exceeds three.)

Let a_j=d_ij/T be this owner's normalized account and delta_j its derivative
with respect to T (1 if capped, 0 if uncapped). Its full payment derivative is
  D=p_0(1-a_0 M)+sum_{j=1}^3 p_j[delta_j+a_j(lambda t_j-M)].
Here a_0=x/T<=1. A capped term is nonnegative because a_j=delta_j=1 and M<1.
For uncapped terms a_j<=min(t_j,1). Call j bad if t_j<m, good otherwise.
For good j the bracket is nonnegative. Bad refers to ALL pools with t_j<m,
including a pool where this owner has a capped account. Its true contribution
is nonnegative, so it can safely be lower-bounded by the same artificial
negative quantity min(t_j,1)(lambda t_j-M). Thus, writing a(t)=min(t,1),
  D W >= 1-lambda H,
  H=m+sum_bad w_j a(t_j)(m-t_j).
At least one of the three pools is good, since m is their weighted mean with
an additional positive-weight zero. There are 0,1, or 2 bad pools.

Set S=sum_bad w, A=sum_bad w a(t), N=sum_bad w t,
B=sum_bad w a(t)t, and q=(1+lambda B)/(1+A)>0. The inequality lambda H<=1
is algebraically equivalent to
  sum_good w[lambda(1+A)t-(1+lambda B)] <= R,
  R=(1+lambda B)(1+S)-lambda(1+A)N.
Since a(t) is nondecreasing, weighted covariance gives BS-AN>=0. Hence
  R-(1+A)=sum_bad w(1-a(t))(1-lambda t)+lambda(BS-AN)>=0.
The first terms vanish for t>=1, and otherwise both factors are nonnegative.
Each good term is at most
  (1+A)exp(lambda-1-q),
by maximizing (lambda t-q)exp(lambda-lambda t); negative brackets cause no issue.

If there are no bad pools, A=B=S=N=0, q=1, R=1, and the sum is at most
3exp(lambda-2)<1.
If there are two bad pools, at most one is good, so its bound is strictly less
than 1+A<=R because lambda<=1 and q>0.
If there is one bad pool, at most two are good and q>=3/4. For t>=1 this follows
from q=(1+lambda w t)/(1+w)>=lambda. For t<1, w<=1 gives
  q=(1+lambda w t^2)/(1+w t)>=(1+(9/10)t^2)/(1+t)>=3/4,
since 18t^2-15t+5>0. Therefore their sum is at most
  2(1+A)exp(-17/20)<1+A<=R;
exp(17/20)>2 already follows from its quadratic Taylor lower bound.
This proves D>=0 in every case.

The formula is continuous at all clipping/tie boundaries; the total bound and
nonnegativity imply convergence to zero at T=0. Increasing coordinates in
sequence proves whole-owner binding-underreport monotonicity on all profiles.

COROLLARY. A production function given by the minimum of FOUR fixed nonnegative
linear forms admits this actual-owner rule with factor C4<2, by mapping each
owner's bundle to the four linear accounts. The account-space proof holds on
the full orthant, so sequential coordinate paths may leave a particular
physical-bundle image while proving monotonicity between its endpoints. In
particular the unweighted
four-edge path escapes the obstruction to positive sums of three-form
technologies. Positive sums and disjoint resource components compose. This
does not yet give a uniform bound for arbitrary-depth forests.

SHARP RATE WITHIN THIS FOUR-POOL SOFTMAX FAMILY. The proof also holds at
lambda_*=2-log(3), approximately 0.9013877113. At this rate M<=1 replaces
M<1; every needed inequality is non-strict. The single-bad-pool q>=3/4 bound
continues to hold because lambda_*>=9/10; the two-good estimate is at most
2 exp(1-1-3/4)=2 exp(-3/4)<1 since lambda_*<1. All other cases are unchanged.
The budget improves to 1+W(3/e)/(2-log 3), still below two.

For any lambda in (lambda_*,1], truthfulness fails even with exclusive owners.
Take one short-pool owner reporting x near 1. In each other pool put three
fixed owners of capacity 2/(3lambda). These accounts are uncapped near x=1
and every other pool total is 2/lambda>1. The short owner's payment is
  x/[1+3exp(lambda-2/x)].
Its derivative at x=1 has the sign of 1-3exp(lambda-2), which is negative
precisely for lambda>lambda_*. A sufficiently small strict underreport thus
raises payment. For lambda>1, an independent long-account failure also exists:
make its normalized probability p small via many same-pool companions and
choose its uncapped own quantity near T; derivative
p[1-lambda(d/T)(1-p)] is then negative. Thus lambda_* is the largest truthful
rate in this family, not just a rate chosen for a sufficient proof.

The total-factor bound is attained by this rule: take rho_0=1 and
rho_1=rho_2=rho_3=1+(1+b)/lambda, b=W(3/e), realizing each long total by enough
accounts individually below T. Therefore lambda_* minimizes the worst-case
factor within this exponential family. This is not an optimal-mechanism claim.
