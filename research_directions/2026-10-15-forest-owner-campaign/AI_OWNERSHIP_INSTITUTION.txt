owner. See AI_INSTITUTION_EFFICIENT_RULE.txt. The model motivation below was

A credible candidate institution: fixed compute pods with grid and local battery reservations
16 October2026 UTC. Model design only; no empirical or existence claim about a
particular operator, and no claim that this example requires subsidy.

THE FOUR ACTUAL OWNERS
Consider two fixed compute pods in one campus or behind one deliverable grid
connection. No GPU or memory moves between pods; requests assigned to pod j
run there. A precontracted neutral operator schedules already admitted work
and enforces electrical and hardware reservations during one fixed window.

A: one independent battery/energy-services owner holds the exclusive firm
   discharge reservations L_1 and L_2 at the two pods. Local power L_j can
   serve only pod j; it cannot be transferred between locations.
B: owns the exclusive usable hardware/service reservation H_1 at pod1.
C: owns an exclusive firm incremental grid-intake reservation P that the
   campus distribution system can allocate to either pod within the window.
D: owns the exclusive usable hardware/service reservation H_2 at pod2.

The resources are contractual reservations with identifiable owners. Physical
asset ownership may be separate, but each reservation in this game must be
unambiguously controlled by one actual owner. C is not an owner of all usable
power: local batteries make coalitions without C productive. A is not a veto
owner either, since C can supply grid power. Utility/interconnection operation
is a precontracted fixed service outside the game, rather than a hidden fifth
strategic participant whose consent is required for every coalition.

PRODUCTION AND THE TWO GRAPHS
There are two production routes at each pod: grid-powered service and locally
battery-powered service. For fixed public conversion coefficients e_j>0,
  x_j^G + x_j^L <= H_j,
  sum_j e_j x_j^G <= P,
  e_j x_j^L <= L_j.
Maximize sum_j(v_j^G x_j^G + v_j^L x_j^L), with fixed nonnegative PUBLIC NET
output values. The quantity x_j is divisible qualified service throughput
in the chosen window; it is not the number of raw, unrestricted GPU jobs.
For the unit-coefficient/unit-profit mathematical baseline put e_j=1 and
v_j^G=v_j^L=1. Fixed unequal coefficients and values can be kept explicitly.

The resource-activity graph is the depth-two tree
  L_1 -- H_1 -- P -- H_2 -- L_2.
Its edges are complementary input pairs, not cables or routes along which
GPUs travel. Describing this as the physical electrical topology would be
misleading. The electrical engineering condition is simply that P is truly
deliverable to both fixed pods while L_j is deliverable only locally.

After exclusive resource ownership is mapped onto the four actual owners,
the necessary bilateral interactions are
  A--B, B--C, C--D, D--A.
This is an owner-interaction four-cycle. Coalition AB can operate pod1 using
local power; AD can operate pod2 using local power; BC or CD can operate the
corresponding pod using C's grid reservation. Neither AC nor BD can produce
incremental service on its own in this baseline. Standby/idle electricity is
reserved outside the game and cannot secretly power these incremental jobs.

A cycle alone does NOT prove incentive subsidy. The earlier repeatedABCD path
lower family uses a different, longer resource path. The present five-resource
institution has not been shown to require positive subsidy. Its value is that
it is a concrete ownership-cycle model, with no universal veto owner, on the
exact depth-two resource class proposed for the next mathematical campaign.

HOW THE CLASS GROWS
Use m fixed pods, each with a distinct hardware reservation owner, the same
owner A of all local battery reservations, and owner C of the common grid
reservation. The resource graph is P--H_j--L_j for j=1,...,m; the owner graph
is K_{2,m}. There are m+2 actual owners and2m+1 resources, and both counts grow.
Every local battery remains tied to its own pod. Further mixed ownership is
allowed by the abstract theorem but need not be imposed on this first model.

BINDING REPORTS AND FEASIBLE DEVIATIONS
Each owner commits a coordinatewise smaller reservation than its verified
available right. A can withhold L_1 and L_2 independently; B and D can reduce
their local service reservations; C can reduce its shared grid reservation.
The operator enforces these commitments via admission limits and power controls.
Reported capacity is available to any coalition containing its owner, under
the same deliverability rules. There is no owner splitting, relabeling, entry,
or redistribution of the withheld capacity to a different coalition for free.
Overreporting is excluded by verification/contract enforcement, not by the
monotonicity theorem. The graph and production coefficients stay fixed as
capacities go to zero; owner identities are never deleted or merged.

For coalition comparison, a coalition may use only reservations controlled by
its members. It may not simultaneously assume access to all of P after C has
left. Nor may it obtain replacement grid power, transport a GPU, or discharge
a battery at the other pod unless those outside options are explicitly added.
The institution is a committed local window, not the entire cloud-energy market.

POWER, ENERGY AND COSTS
P and L_j should first mean firm instantaneous/steady-window power capacity,
e.g. kW, not an interchangeable mixture of kW and kWh. In this interpretation,
x_j and H_j are qualified service rates sustained over the chosen window, and
e_j converts service rate into incremental kW. Fixed-window output value is
the corresponding rate value multiplied by the common window length. A battery reservation
L_j must satisfy BOTH its discharge-power limit and verified usable-energy
limit over window length Delta: L_j*Delta <= available reserved energy. That
energy is committed before the game; charging, future state of charge and
multi-period arbitrage are outside this static model. H_j is a measured safe
service envelope compatible with that window and latency class. Linearity in
that envelope is a hypothesis to check, not a conclusion from naming a GPU.

The payment-only incentive theorem is credible only for already committed
rights with no private alternative-use payoff in this window, or for explicitly
net payments after verifiable usage costs are reimbursed. The neutral operator
can pay precontracted public energy/degradation charges as operating costs;
then v_j^G and v_j^L are service revenue minus those known marginal costs, and
the game's payments distribute the remaining surplus. Budget/subsidy statements
must be stated on that same NET-surplus basis; gross cash also includes costs.
If A privately values later battery discharge, C faces private electricity
purchase costs, or B/D can run valuable outside jobs after withholding, utility
is payment plus those outside gains minus costs. Own-payment monotonicity no
longer establishes truthfulness. Those are substantive future model changes.

Public output values are most defensible with fixed-price prebooked work or a
known admitted backlog that exceeds feasible service in the window. They are
less defensible with endogenous spot prices, private customer values, changing
request mixes, scarcity pricing, or scarce demand. Likewise fractional steady
service is an approximation to discrete jobs and scheduling. Availability of
an economically meaningful safe operating range must be measured separately.

Arbitrary mixed ownership is mathematically useful for robustness to vertical
integration: a firm may hold hardware and local/grid power reservations, possibly
at several pods. Such integration is plausible as a contract design, but it
requires explicit ownership records and feasible independent withholding rights.
It must not be manufactured by dividing one physical entitlement into strategic
pseudo-owners. The initial four-owner example avoids those extra complications.

RECOMMENDED USE
Use this institution as the formal applied motivation for the depth-two target,
with the owner-forest case as the classical baseline. First prove or refute the
subsidy claim for the defined committed-rights game. Separately test throughput,
latency, power deliverability and net-cost accounting. Do not use the ownership
cycle, the mathematical bounds, or the existing Mac experiments as evidence
that real GPU pooling has already produced a benefit.
