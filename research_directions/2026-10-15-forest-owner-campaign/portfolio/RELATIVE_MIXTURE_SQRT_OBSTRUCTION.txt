CANONICAL RELATIVE-SUPPORT MIXTURES: SHARP LEADING sqrt(2n) OBSTRUCTION
15 October 2026. New candidate proof; independent review and priority pending.

SCOPE
This concerns the canonical extended relative-support hybrid in the current
supplement, averaged against ANY common probability law mu on [0,1] fixed
independently of reports. The law may depend on n and the fixed technology.
It does NOT concern all truthful coalition-covering mechanisms, the finite-form
selector, owner-dependent or report-dependent mixing, or alternative support
extensions outside the current aggregate's face.

THEOREM
For n>=2 let m be the least integer with m(m-1)>=2(n-1). Then m<=n.
There is one fixed finite nonnegative min-linear technology on d=nm resources
and n fixed whole-bundle owners such that every rule in the scoped class has
worst-profile total-payment factor at least

 L_n=2n/(m+1)=(sqrt(2)-o(1))sqrt(n).

Together with OWNER_SQRT_BOUND.txt this proves the optimal leading constant
sqrt(2) in the owner-count worst case WITHIN THIS CANONICAL-RELATIVE-SUPPORT
MIXTURE CLASS. It does not close the unrestricted owner-count gap.

FIXED TECHNOLOGY AND PROFILES
For k=1,...,m define
 h_k(x)=sum of the k smallest coordinates of x in R_+^n
       =min_{A subset [n], |A|=k} sum_{i in A}x_i.
Use separate n-coordinate blocks x^(1),...,x^(m), and the single fixed technology
 F(x)=sum_{k=1}^m h_k(x^(k)).
This is finite, nonnegative, monotone, concave and degree-one homogeneous. It is
finite min-linear: expand the sum of minima over the product of finite choices.
All coefficients are zero or one; no capacity is public or supplied for free.

For each k take profile b^[k], where owner i reports the unit basis bundle on
coordinate i of block k, with every other coordinate zero. Owners and technology
remain fixed across these profiles. There is no population change or cloning.
Each profile is in the unrestricted nonnegative whole-bundle report domain.
Grand production at b^[k] is T=k.

EXACT HYBRID BUDGET
At any mask, an excluded owner's positive coordinate lies outside the aggregate's
support face. The canonical relative support is +infinity there, so the capped
own-excluded contribution is T: p_i(q)=T. This conclusion is independent of the
particular finite support chosen inside the face.

For owner i, let C count excluded other owners, C~Binomial(n-1,q). With i open,
production in the active block is (k-C)_+; with i closed it is (k-C-1)_+.
Their difference is 1 exactly when C<=k-1. Hence

 R_k(q)/T = n q + (n/k)(1-q) P(C<=k-1).                 (1)

All other blocks produce zero at every mask, so this is the exact budget for
our one fixed technology, not a comparison between different technologies.
The formula holds also at q=0,1 by polynomial continuation.

WEIGHTED PROFILE ARGUMENT
Let S=m(m+1)/2 and weight profile k by k/S. From (1),
 sum_{k=1}^m (k/S) R_k(q)/T_k
  = n q + (n/S)(1-q) sum_{k=1}^m P(C<=k-1)
  = n q + (n/S)(1-q) E[(m-C)_+]
  >= n q + (n/S)(1-q) (m-(n-1)q)_+ =: H(q),           (2)
using convexity of the positive-part function and E C=(n-1)q.

If 0<=q<=m/(n-1), expansion gives
 H(q)=(n/S)[m + (S-m-(n-1))q + (n-1)q^2].
By the definition of m, S-m=m(m-1)/2>=n-1. Thus H(q)>=nm/S=2n/(m+1).
If q>=m/(n-1), H(q)=nq>=nm/(n-1)>=2n/(m+1), since S>=n-1.
When m/(n-1)>1 the second case is empty; the first still proves the bound.
Consequently the left side of (2) is >=L_n for every q in [0,1].

Integrate against ANY mu fixed across reports. Its weighted average budget across
the finitely many b^[k] profiles is >=L_n. Some profile therefore has factor
at least L_n. Since m=sqrt(2n)+O(1), L_n=(sqrt(2)-o(1))sqrt(n).
No Jensen step needs any smoothness of the rule, support selection or mixing law.

WHY THE DIRECT SUM IS NECESSARY
If each h_k were a different technology, a designer could choose a different
mixing law for each and the weighted argument would not establish this scope.
The direct-sum F fixes all blocks before reports and supplies all witness profiles
inside one domain. This is the reason the lower permits technology-dependent
but not report-dependent mu.

CONTRIBUTION BOUNDARY
This obstructs reweighting the existing canonical relative-support selector as a
route to o(sqrt n). It does not obstruct choosing finite supporting forms outside
faces: that alternative does not necessarily pay T to every excluded unit owner.
It does not improve the existing unrestricted lower ~log n/loglog n.
The technology uses ordinary sums of order statistics; no novelty is claimed for
those functions or for the elementary weighted-profile method itself.
