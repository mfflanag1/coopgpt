FINITE GLOBAL SUPPORTS DO NOT REMOVE THE sqrt(n) BARRIER
15 October 2026. Candidate proof; independent review pending.
This concerns the support/endpoint MASK-HYBRID architecture, not all mechanisms.

THE MASK-HYBRID CLASS
For one fixed technology F and n fixed owners, fix ANY law on masks B subset[N],
independently of reports. For each mask select a common nonnegative global linear
support ell_B at x_B=sum_(i in B)b_i: ell_B(x_B)=F(x_B), ell_B>=F everywhere.
The selector may depend on F, B and the open owners' reports, and may randomize.
Closed owner reports may not affect its selection. At B=empty the support law
therefore remains fixed across profiles. Pay
 Y_i(B)=min{ell_B(b_i),T} if i notin B;
 Y_i(B)=F(x_B)-F(x_B-b_i) if i in B,
then average over the fixed mask and support laws. This includes the current
finite-form hybrid and arbitrary report-independent iid/cardinality mixtures.
Every such rule is eligible; the argument below bounds its worst budget.

THEOREM
For every n>=4, there is one fixed continuous finite nonnegative min-linear
technology F on nm resources, m=floor(sqrt n), such that EVERY rule above has
worst-profile total-payment factor >=m/16. Thus no choice of finite supports,
tie-breaking, mask correlation, or fixed mask weights makes this architecture
achieve o(sqrt n) uniformly in owner count.
The construction uses finite global supports; +infinity off a face is unnecessary.
The unrestricted owner-count lower remains the existing log n/loglog n bound.

TECHNOLOGY
Set A=n^2 and for k=1,...,m define on R_+^n
 f_k(x)=min{ (sum_i x_i)/n,
              [A/|S|^2] sum_(i in S)x_i : k<=|S|<=n-1 }.
On m separate n-coordinate blocks take F=sum_k f_k. A sum of finite minima
is again a finite min-linear function (expand the product choices).
The single F is continuous, nonnegative, monotone, concave and homogeneous.
All owners have the same unrestricted whole-bundle report domain throughout.

REGULAR WITNESS PROFILES
Let epsilon=1/(8n^4), t=1+(n-1)epsilon. At profile P_k, owner i reports
 e_i+epsilon sum_(j!=i)e_j in block k and zero in every other block.
Thus T_k=t. Fix a proper nonempty mask with l closed owners and r=n-l open.
The active block aggregate has entries r epsilon on closed coordinates and
1+(r-1)epsilon on open coordinates.

If l<k, every subset form contains an open coordinate and costs more than1;
the baseline form costs r t/n<1. It is the unique supporting gradient.
If k<=l<n, the unique minimizing subset is S=the closed set C. Its value is
 A r epsilon/l. A smaller subset of C has strictly larger value A r epsilon/s;
a subset meeting an open coordinate has value>1; the baseline is>=r/n,
whereas A r epsilon/l<r/n. This proves uniqueness, not only one tie choice.
At these positive active-block aggregates, ANY global linear supporting form
must restrict to the unique active-block gradient, even when inactive blocks
are zero. Randomization or extra redundant forms cannot change that gradient.

For a closed owner i when k<=l<n,
 ell_B(b_i)=A[1+(l-1)epsilon]/l^2 >=n^2/(n-1)^2 >t.
Its capped payment is exactly T. Hence R(P_k,B)/T_k>=l in that case.

If l=k-1, an open owner has endpoint difference
 Delta = r t/n - A(r-1)epsilon/k >=r/n -1/(8n)
       >=(n-k)/n >=(n-m)/n.
(The removed-owner mask remains nonempty since k<=m<=n/2.)
Since r>=n-m and t<2,
 R(P_k,B)/T_k >=(n-m)^2/(2n)>=n/8.
For B=N (l=0), the same endpoint calculation applies at P_1.

Consequently the uniform average over P_1,...,P_m satisfies:
 - if 0<=l<m: choose k=l+1; its contribution alone gives >=n/(8m);
 - if m<=l<n: every k<=m gives >=l>=m.
These statements hold for every deterministic proper mask, regardless of which
owners are open and regardless of the support selector. All other payments are
nonnegative and may safely be ignored.

ALL-EMPTY-MASK ANCHORS
At B=empty the selected support is a fixed nonnegative global majorant w,
possibly random. Restrict w to block1. Because F(1_block1)=f_1(1)=1,
s=sum_j w_j>=1. At most one index j can have sum_(e!=j)w_e<1/2: two such
indices would imply w_j+w_h>2s-1>=s, a contradiction.

Let delta=1/(4n^3). At anchor Q_j, every one of the n owners reports
 z_j=delta e_j+sum_(e!=j)e_e in block1, with other blocks zero.
Its grand production is T_j=n f_1(z_j)<=n A delta=1/4.
For at least n-1 indices j, w.z_j>=1/2>=T_j. Every owner at that anchor
therefore receives capped payment T_j under the all-empty mask.
The uniform average normalized budget across all n anchors is >=n-1.
This holds for EVERY selected support realization, so randomizing w cannot
avoid it. The anchor profiles and weights are independent of the selector.

COMBINED WEIGHTED PROFILE ARGUMENT
Give total weight1/2 to the uniform regular profiles and weight1/2 to the
uniform anchors. For each mask:
 - l<m: weighted normalized budget >=n/(16m)>=m/16;
 - m<=l<n: >=m/2>=m/16;
 - l=n: >=(n-1)/2>=m/16.
Integrate these pointwise inequalities over ANY fixed mask law and support law.
The weighted average of the rule's normalized budgets at this finite set of
profiles is at least m/16. Some profile attains that bound. The theorem follows.

ELIGIBILITY AND QUANTIFIER LIMITS
For a fixed mask, closed prices exclude the recipient's report; open endpoint
marginals also increase with the own bundle. For coverage, the endpoint marginal
of an open owner dominates its selected support contribution; the closed capped
support terms and open support terms cover every coalition. Hence each mask
vector is itself eligible; any fixed mixture remains eligible.
No claim is made for a selector using closed reports, profile-dependent mask
weights, owner-specific incompatible support forms, or another payment formula.
The profiles lie in one fixed F with fixed n owners and vary only their reports.
Within the active block every regular owner's bundle is strictly positive.
Inactive blocks are zero, but all selected prices are finite global forms; the
proof does not exploit an infinite off-face convention.

NEW VERSUS ESTABLISHED
The project-level result is the explicit finite-form, arbitrary-fixed-mask-law
barrier for the existing architecture. Linear-production support/core facts,
min-linear concavity, and weighted averaging are established ingredients.
Historical priority has not been confirmed. This does not prove a sharp
unrestricted sqrt(n) lower bound; a different mechanism may improve that rate.
