Finite report-transition lower certificates on cycles transfer to paths
15 October2026. Internal structural lemma; external novelty unconfirmed.

Fix a cycle of L resource vertices with nonnegative edge output weights p_e,
unit input consumption at each endpoint, and an exclusive assignment of its
vertices to a fixed set of actual owners. An owner may hold several separated
vertices. Reports are nonnegative resource capacities; underreporting changes
only the reporting owner's capacities coordinatewise.

For integer k, form a path on k consecutive copies of the L-vertex cycle word.
Every consecutive edge has the original weight of its edge type; the last
wraparound edge is omitted. The same ACTUAL owner owns every copy of her
original vertices. At a selected original report, give each copy of a vertex
its original capacity. This repeats a report, not an owner identity.
Let F(c) be cycle production and F_k(c) this repeated-profile path production.
For every capacity vector c,
  k F(c)-p_last min(c_first,c_last) <= F_k(c) <= k F(c).
The same inequalities hold for every coalition after zeroing absent owners.

Proof. For the upper bound, sum the k resource constraints of each vertex
type for any feasible path activity vector. Divide total activity of each
edge type by k. The resulting cycle activity vector is feasible, so its
objective, the path objective divided by k, is at most F(c). For the lower
bound, take an optimal cycle plan and put the same plan on all copies, with
the single final wraparound activity omitted. The omitted activity consumes
at most min(c_first,c_last), and deleting it preserves all resource constraints.
This proves the uniform O(1) deficit relative to kF(c). No integrality is used;
the argument includes odd cycles and fractional activities.

Transfer of exact finite lower certificates. A report-transition certificate
is a nonnegative weighted sum of budget, coalition-coverage and own-monotone
constraints whose aggregate payment coefficients are nonnegative, whose
factor coefficient is negative, and whose right side is negative. Writing
D=sum_budget lambda_b T_b>0 and R=sum_coalition lambda_s v_s, it proves C>=R/D.
Repeat each of its finitely many profiles on the k-copy path. Every transition
still changes only one fixed actual owner and increases her entire copied
bundle coordinatewise. Every coverage constraint remains meaningful. The
aggregate payment coefficients are unchanged because they depend only on
owner subsets and transitions, not worths. The new lower certificate proves
  C >= R_k/D_k,
where R_k/k -> R and D_k/k -> D by the production bounds above. Therefore
R_k/D_k -> R/D. Any certified finite cycle lower bound can be approached
arbitrarily closely on exclusively owned path resources with fragmented
holdings. The resulting path is an unbounded resource family; independent
product replication can also make actual owner counts grow when needed.

Product-replication justification. Aggregate budget weights by profile as
lambda_b>=0 and put L=sum_b lambda_b>0. Removing the budget rows from the
certificate shows sum_b lambda_b y_N(b)>=R for every eligible payment rule:
coverage and incentive rows supply the lower bound, and nonnegative payments
allow any nonnegative coefficient slack to be discarded. For disjoint copies,
fix all other components' reports and apply this inequality to the payments of
one component's owners. Every coalition and own-report row stays valid. Draw
each component's profile independently with probability lambda_b/L. Then its
expected payments are at least R/L, while its expected production is D/L.
Sum over components and average the global budget inequality to obtain C>=R/D.
Thus no separability of the payment rule is assumed. The same argument applies
to each transferred finite path certificate with R_k,D_k in place of R,D.

Scope. This is a lower-bound transfer, not an equivalence of all-profile
mechanism classes, and it yields no upper bound. It does not say every cycle
or every fragmented ownership pattern incurs subsidy. It justifies using
compact merged-owner cycles as discovery problems for exclusively owned
forest lower bounds. A source comparison is still needed before claiming
this reduction itself is new.
