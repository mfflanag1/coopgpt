Netting closure and the three-owner boundary
15 October2026. Internal derivations; no novelty claim.

1. A general mixed-owner lifting lemma
For every assignment of a fixed finite owner set to two exclusive pools,
assume a scalar-capacity payment rule z that is nonnegative, covers the
min(total left,total right) game, spends at most C times that value (C>=1),
pays a zero-capacity owner zero, and is own-capacity nondecreasing and
1-Lipschitz. No relation between rules for different role assignments is needed
except the same bound and these properties.
For actual mixed owner i with (a_i,b_i), put
  t_i=min(a_i,b_i), d_i=a_i-b_i.
Feed |d_i| to the exclusive-pool rule on the sign partition, assigning zero
net owners by any fixed convention, and set y_i=t_i+z_i.
Then y is own-bundle monotone, coalition-covering, and spends at most CT on
all mixed reports, with fixed actual owners.

Proof: for every coalition S,
 min(sum_S a_i,sum_S b_i)=sum_S t_i+min(sum_S(d_i)+,sum_S(-d_i)+).
Coverage and budget follow, since sum t_i+C*residual<=C*T.
When a_i increases while a_i<b_i, standalone value rises by delta and the
negative net capacity falls by delta. The 1-Lipschitz assumption prevents a
payment loss larger than delta. When a_i>b_i, standalone value is unchanged
and positive net capacity increases, so monotonicity applies. At a_i=b_i,
the net payment is zero on both sides. Other owners' signed reports stay fixed.
The b_i argument is symmetric. This proves direct incentive validity without
asserting a general strategic merger theorem.

The existing exclusive-pool exponential rule has own derivative in[0,1] in
the short region and in[0,1/2] in the long region, with continuous boundaries.
It therefore meets the lifting hypotheses. This is another simple route to
a mixed-owner exponential bound and should temper claims that mixed ownership
alone is a historically novel difficulty.

2. Exact efficient rule for at most three mixed owners
Pad to three with zero owners. At a report, net each owner as above. If all
nonzero d_i have the same sign, pay each t_i. If one sign has a single owner
and the other sign has two, give the entire residual value to the sole owner
of the first sign, plus each owner's t_i. If there is exactly one positive
and one negative owner (third zero), divide the residual equally between those
two, plus t_i. This rule is efficient and covers coalitions because the residual
game has a veto owner, or two necessary owners receiving its value between them.

For a fully explicit own-monotonic formula, fix the OTHER two net capacities:
- Both strictly positive, sum D: pay i=min(b_i,a_i+D).
- One strictly positive D and the other zero:
    pay i=1/2 min(a_i,b_i)+1/2 min(b_i,a_i+D).
- Both strictly negative, absolute sum D: pay i=min(a_i,b_i+D).
- One strictly negative -D and the other zero:
    pay i=1/2 min(a_i,b_i)+1/2 min(a_i,b_i+D).
- Opposite signs or both zero: pay i=min(a_i,b_i).
These expressions are coordinatewise nondecreasing with the other reports
fixed and agree with the surplus description. Thus there is no incentive
subsidy for at most three actual mixed owners in the two-input class. The
existing four-owner9/8 lower witness is a sharp boundary in owner count for
the existence of a positive worst-case subsidy, not a determination of the
optimal subsidy once four or more owners are allowed.

This bounded-owner observation is not the campaign's unbounded-class gate.
It explains the exact finite-grid pattern and identifies an owner-dependent
mechanism feature absent from the scalar-bias family.

Independent seam audit, 15 October 2026.
The lift needs a family indexed by fixed role assignments, with the same C
and 1-Lipschitz bound. Other owners retain their role assignment throughout
an own deviation. At a zero net report, the selected sign convention can
change the rule used by others, but their payments are irrelevant to the
deviating owner's monotonicity; her own residual payment is exactly zero
in either role by hypothesis. On either open sign region, the applicable
role assignment is fixed. Integrate monotone/Lipschitz comparisons up to
zero, cross that zero seam, and continue on the other side. This proves
arbitrary finite coordinate changes, not only infinitesimal derivatives.
At a_i=b_i=0, payment is zero and both coordinate directions are covered.
No cross-role continuity for other owners and no global merger theorem is
being assumed. Without own-zero payment, cross-role monotonicity can fail.
The phrase 'at most three' includes padding with zero owners, but the pure
rule in part 1 must be defined on the entire fixed owner set, including such
zeros; deleting/relabeling owners profile by profile is not justified.
