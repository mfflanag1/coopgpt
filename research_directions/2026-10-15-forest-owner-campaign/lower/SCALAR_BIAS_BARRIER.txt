Scalar-bias barrier for the two-input rule family
15 October 2026. Internal lemma; no novelty claim.

Fix a single continuous function p:[1,infinity)->[0,1] with p(1)=1/2.
On each fixed-owner two-input profile call the smaller aggregate input short,
T its aggregate, s_i and l_i the owner's short and long inputs, d_i=min(l_i,T),
and rho=sum_i d_i/T. Consider rules
  y_i=(1-p(rho))*s_i+p(rho)*d_i,
using the SAME scalar p across every finite owner population and all reports.
If the rule is own-coordinate monotone for all profiles, then
  p(rho)>=1/2 exp(1-rho), for every rho>=1.
Consequently its uniform budget factor is at least 1+1/(2e).
The exponential rule attains this bound within this family.

Proof without differentiability:
Normalize T=1. Fix r>=1 and h in (0,1), r-h>=1. Make one long-only owner's
contribution increase from 1-h to1. Choose the remaining long contributions,
each <=1, summing to r-1; this is feasible with sufficiently many owners.
Put the entire unit of short input with a different owner. At the initial and
final profiles rho=r-h,r. Own monotonicity requires
  p(r)>= (1-h)*p(r-h).
Fix target R>1. For integer k>R-1 take h=(R-1)/k. Apply this inequality at
r=1+h,...,1+kh=R, constructing valid profiles separately for each comparison.
The same scalar function p is used at all of them, so
  p(R)>= (1-h)^k p(1).
Let k increase to get the claimed exponential lower bound.
At rho=2 the total payment equals T*(1+p(2)), hence at least
(1+1/(2e))*T. Such a profile uses one short-only owner holding1 and two
long-only owners holding1 each.

This is a mechanism-class obstruction, NOT a lower bound for arbitrary
monotone covering rules. Continuity and p(1)=1/2 are explicit family assumptions;
the proof below uses p(1)=1/2 but does not otherwise need continuity. If p is
allowed to depend on the owner count, the proof still works for R<=n-1 using
one short-only owner and sufficiently many long-only owners, so R=2 needs n>=3.
The owner count can remain fixed throughout each chain by adding zero owners.
