Classical hierarchical core allocations give truthful efficiency on owner forests
15 October 2026. Restricted-class boundary, not a new core theorem.

Setup. Fix a forest H on the ACTUAL owner set. For every report profile b,
v_b(empty)=0, v_b is nonnegative and superadditive, and v_b(S) is the sum of
v_b(C) over the connected components C of the induced owner graph H[S].
Assume v_b(S) is independent of reports outside S and is coordinatewise
nondecreasing in reports of owners in S. The graph and roots do not depend
on reported capacities, including when a capacity becomes zero.

Choose a fixed root in each component. Let D_i be the subtree rooted at i
and child(i) its immediate children. The classical hierarchical allocation is
  y_i(b) = v_b(D_i) - sum_{h in child(i)} v_b(D_h).
It is nonnegative, exactly efficient, coalition-covering, and nondecreasing
in every coordinate of owner i's report on the entire report domain.

Proof. Nonnegativity follows from superadditivity on the disjoint child
subtrees and singleton {i}. Summing over a rooted component telescopes to
its worth; summing components gives v_b(N). For connected coalition C let
r be its member nearest the fixed root. Summing y_i over i in C telescopes
as follows: y(C) equals v_b(D_r) minus the sum of worths of the subtrees
hanging off C below its boundary. These subtrees and C partition D_r.
Superadditivity thus gives y(C)>=v_b(C). For arbitrary S, sum this result
over the components of H[S] and use component additivity. Finally, owner
i's report changes v_b(D_i) monotonically and changes none of the child
terms, because i lies in no D_h. This proves all-profile own monotonicity.
A fixed convex combination over roots retains every property; the classical
average-tree solution is therefore another valid choice. Dependence of the
root weights or tree on reports would require a new incentive argument.

Production corollary. Let a fixed physical resource forest have activities
on its edges. Activity x_e>=0 consumes fixed positive amounts at its two
endpoints and generates nonnegative fixed output p_e*x_e. Each resource
has additive capacity available only from its owner. Partition resource
vertices among actual owners, with each owner's block connected, and let
that owner report an arbitrary coordinatewise-smaller bundle of all its
capacities. Different owners' blocks are disjoint. Contracting each block
gives a forest on actual owners. At every profile, including zero reports,
coalition production decomposes over components of that contracted forest:
there are no activity edges between those components, and no shared resource
rows. Separate feasible activity plans can be added, so the game is
superadditive. Coalition worth is increasing in included capacities and
independent of absent owners' reports. The preceding allocation therefore
is efficient, coalition-covering and own-bundle monotone for arbitrarily
many resources and owners. Internal owner activities merely allow positive
singleton values and cause no problem.

Limits. Multiple actual owners sharing the capacity of one resource do not
form a vertex partition. A single owner's disconnected resource holdings
also do not meet the connected-block hypothesis; contracting disconnected
blocks may create cycles. These cases must not be claimed from this result.
Two resources with two suppliers each already induce the four-owner
substitution cycle K_{2,2} and admit the existing 9/8 incentive lower bound.
Thus physical branching alone is not the obstruction; ownership structure
can remove the owner-forest decomposition even on a single physical edge.
This does not prove that every disconnected/overlapping ownership pattern
requires a subsidy, only that the classical argument no longer guarantees
zero subsidy.

Broader formulation. Connected resource blocks are sufficient, not required.
For ANY fixed production system whose resources have exclusive actual owners
and whose activity columns each use resources from at most two owners, form
the fixed owner-interaction graph: connect distinct owners exactly when some
activity needs resources of both. If this graph is a forest and there are no
other shared constraints, coalition production decomposes over its induced
components. All the report-locality and superadditivity hypotheses above
hold, so the classical hierarchy gives efficient own-bundle-monotone payments.
An owner's resources may be physically disconnected. Activities internal to
one owner are allowed, including positive singleton production. If an activity
uses several owners, the same conclusion follows whenever its owner support
is connected in a fixed owner forest and absence of a required owner makes
the activity unavailable; this broader variant is not needed here.

The repeated A-B-C-D path family has an owner-interaction four-cycle, although
its physical resource graph is a path and resources have exclusive owners.
FRAGMENTED_FOREST_FAMILY.txt proves an incentive factor tending to9/8 there.
The precise contrast is acyclic owner interaction (classical zero subsidy)
versus some cyclic owner-interaction examples with positive subsidy. We do
not claim every owner cycle necessarily incurs a subsidy.
