ALGORITHMIC STATUS AND IMPLEMENTATION GATES

The new general bounds are existence and payment-rule theorems. For a finite
min-linear technology with supplied forms, each fixed-mask payment is directly
evaluable, but summing over all masks can take exponential time in owners.
A production value oracle alone does not automatically supply the selected
supporting-price oracle. The relative-face construction proves existence,
not a polynomial-time support-selection theorem for arbitrary concave f.

The owner-count harmonic construction uses a finite rational mask distribution
and thus supplies an exact finite formula on rational min-linear inputs. Its
number of masks can still be exponential. The uniformly mixed Bernoulli version
is an exact deterministic expectation, not a sampled implementation with
unverified realized coverage. Random samples, rounding and solver tolerances
need their own eligibility guarantees. The inherited finite-sample certification
method is a separate result; its preprocessing cost must not be omitted.

Forest weighted-to-unit threshold reduction is polynomial-size (at most one
interval per relevant vertex-potential breakpoint). The arbitrary double-star
formula has polynomially many two-/three-input components. Its exponential
primitive is mathematically exact, but finite-precision execution has not been
shown to preserve exact incentives. The separate rational two-input primitive
does not establish rational implementations of the three-/four-input rules.

A useful future algorithmic gate is an explicitly evaluable C<2 rule for the
unbounded depth-two class, with proved coalition coverage and whole-owner
monotonicity under its actual arithmetic. Merely optimizing dispatch or computing
F in linear time does not solve the payment problem. The graph's2^m supporting
forms do not make its production-value calculation hard: a direct O(m) formula
is already available. Computational and economic obstacles must stay separate.
