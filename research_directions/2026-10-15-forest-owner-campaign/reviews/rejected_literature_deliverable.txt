## Result: narrow frontier check for the bipartite-forest target

**Known facts incorporated (not re-proposed):** α>1 is settled by the project's 9/8 witness (PROOF.txt §4, four fixed owners, profiles a,b,c); the mixed-owner single-power star has uniform factor in [9/8, 1+1/(2e)] (PROOF §2–4); star minimax factor **equals** the mixed two-input factor (positive-sum composition, PROOF §4); disjoint stars compose; the generic ceiling for the forest class is 2T (Ezra et al. Lemma 6.2 + LP core, per SOURCES.txt #5); a report-independent greedy fails on the 3-edge path (PROOF §6). I did not search for "is α>1 open".

**Verdict:** No inspected source subsumes target (A), (B) or (C) of NEXT_CAMPAIGN.txt. Silence does not certify novelty; several originals are paywalled (listed below).

### Five closest free originals, with what was checked and what was not

**1. Huang–Mei–Yang–Cao, *Mechanism Design in Max-Flows*, arXiv:2502.08248v1** — https://arxiv.org/html/2502.08248v1 (already in ledger; new facts checked this pass)
- Checked (fetched text, §2.1, §2.2, §3.1, §5): N=E, *each player owns exactly one edge*; multi-edge ownership is explicitly deferred to future work (§5). Capacities private, underreport only, utility = allocation. Every mechanism is required to be efficient (Σ = max-flow); **no subsidy is ever considered**. The "no core-selection mechanism is truthful" claim is Example 1 (four edges, capacities (2,1,1,1)), proved for NEIC; it is not a numbered theorem. Networks: general s–t DAGs with parallel edges; **no edge weights, no per-edge consumption coefficients** (no generalized flow).
- Relation to forest target: the unit-value, unit-power (v_jk=e_jk=1) forest with one owner per node is a 3-layer max-flow game (s→H→P→t) — but it is a *near*-subclass only: their model has every edge owned by a reporting player, so our public, uncapacitated activity edges would need dummy owners (inference, not in their text). Our mixed-owner bundles are outside their model (deferred in §5). Their model gives no upper/lower bound on subsidized coverage; hence it does not subsume (A) or (B). Unread: proofs in §3–4 of the MC mechanism's properties; journal version (none located).

**2. Meir–Rosenschein–Malizia, *Subsidies, Stability, and Restricted Cooperation in Coalitional Games* (CoopMAS 2011 version)** — https://www.cs.huji.ac.il/~jeff/papers/coopmas11meir.pdf
- Checked (pp. 1–8 read): Def. of CoS (§2.2), RCoS = (v(N)+CoS)/v(N) with trivial tight bounds 1 ≤ RCoS ≤ n (§2.4); Theorem 2 (Bachrach et al.): RCoS ≤ √n for superadditive games, tight; Proposition 1: with deviating coalitions restricted to size ≤ k, RCoS ≤ min(k,√n), greedy gives a stable superimputation of cost ≤ 2v(N) for k=2 and the tight value is 1.5; single-cycle communication graph ⇒ RCoS ≤ 2.
- Difference: all bounds are for a *fixed* game whose core may be empty. In our model every fixed profile has CoS = 0 (B(b)=T, PROOF §1); the extra funding comes only from linking payments across report profiles by own-monotonicity. Their "2" for k=2 and "≤2" for cycles concern restricted deviating coalitions, not grand-marginal payments; no identification with the 2T baseline is warranted. Unread: §4 (CoS vs least-core ratio bounds) beyond p. 8, §5.

**3. Calleja–Rafels–Tijs, *The Aggregate-Monotonic Core* (2008 preprint of SCW 2009 paper)** — http://www.accessecon.com/pubs/SCW2008/GeneralPDFSCW2008/SCW2008-08-00085S.pdf — used as the free carrier of the Young (1985) / Housman–Clark (1998) statements
- Checked (pp. 1–6): p. 2 states coalitional monotonicity (Young 1985): "if the worth of a given coalition increases whereas the worths of all other coalitions remain the same, then the payoff of every member of that coalition should weakly increase"; Young gives a five-agent example showing incompatibility with core-selection; Housman–Clark (1998) show the same for four agents. Def. 2 aggregate monotonicity (only v(N) changes); Theorem 5: AC(v)=C(v_r)+(v(N)−v_r(N))Δ_N (core-selection + aggregate monotonicity are compatible); Theorem 8 coincidence iff root game has a large core.
- Relation: this is the closest classical impossibility to the project's incentive axiom at factor 1. Differences (both directions unproved): (i) our own-bundle increase raises the worths of *all* coalitions containing i simultaneously and only within the LP-game subclass, whereas coalitional monotonicity raises one coalition's worth with arbitrary games; (ii) Young/H–C require exact efficiency, we allow a subsidy up to C·T. Neither implies the other on our domain (intermediate single-coalition games may be unbalanced or non-LP). The project's 9/8 witness is the quantified LP-subclass analogue; the coincidence that it also uses four owners (H–C threshold) is noted, not claimed. Neither source gives any constant factor, nor anything forest-specific. Unread/unaccessed: Young 1985 IJGT 14:65–72 (https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/BF01769885, paywalled) and Housman–Clark IJGT 27:611–616 (https://ideas.repec.org/a/spr/jogath/v27y1998i4p611-616.html abstract only, paywalled); Calleja et al. pp. 7+.

**4. Thomson (2024), *Allocation rules are very generally vulnerable to the strategic withholding of endowments*, IJGT 53** — abstract via https://ideas.repec.org/a/spr/jogath/v53y2024i3d10.1007_s00182-024-00897-0.html (full text paywalled; no free version located; Postlewaite 1979 original not accessed)
- Checked (abstract only): withholding-proofness (agent gains nothing by keeping back resources, final bundle = assignment + withheld amount); impossibility of efficiency + withholding-proofness persists under homothetic preferences, arbitrarily small recovery fraction, and distributional axioms. Model is an exchange economy (inference from "trades/endowments").
- Difference: goods allocated and consumed vs. our payment-only utility with zero value for withheld capacity (the weakest withholding incentive); no coalition coverage, no subsidy, no quantitative factor. Does not subsume; it is the endowment-manipulation precedent to credit for the *qualitative* phenomenon.

**5. Abe–Liu, *Monotonic core allocation paths for assignment games* (WINPEC E1808, 2018; SCW 53, 2019)** — https://www.waseda.jp/fpse/winpec/assets/uploads/2018/11/No.E1808.pdf (fetch returned 403; abstract read via https://ideas.repec.org/a/spr/sochwe/v53y2019i4d10.1007_s00355-019-01197-3.html)
- Checked (abstract): MCAP = allocations along a player order, each in the core of the subgame, payoffs non-decreasing along the sequence; existence for every assignment game; terminals refine the core.
- Difference: this is **population monotonicity** (PMAS-type, like Solymosi 2024 in SOURCES.txt #4), bipartite but with one player per node and matrix-entry valuations, no reports, no subsidy. Not our own-bundle monotonicity; not subsuming.

Paywalled; working paper 9805 not verified free. Unresolved.

### One precise unresolved priority comparison
Whether Housman–Clark's four-player (or Young's five-player) counterexample games are realizable as forest/LP production games in which the game change is induced by one owner's bundle increase. If yes, the factor-1 impossibility on the LP subclass is literally in those originals (affecting only the already-known α>1 statement's priority, not the C<2 forest target); if no, the 9/8 witness stands as the first subclass statement known to this pass. Resolving it needs the two paywalled originals.

### Limitations
Free-only, no purchases/outreach; 2 PDFs read as page images (Meir pp. 1–8; Calleja pp. 1–6), one as extracted text (Huang v1); items 4–5 and the overlaps are abstract-level only. Absence of a subsidized, report-linked, forest/transportation result in searches is weak evidence. No acceptance checks were run.
