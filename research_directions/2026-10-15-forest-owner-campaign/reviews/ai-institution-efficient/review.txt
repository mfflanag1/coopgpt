## Findings

**1. (Low — wording error, not a counterexample) Line 5: "A+C+I … exact coalition worth."**
Coverage is only an inequality for proper subsets. Example: m=2, H=(2,2), L=(1,1), P=2 → a=(1,1), r=(1,1), R=2. For I={1}: worth = a₁ + min(P, r₁) = 1+1 = 2, payments = R + a₁ = 3 > 2. Equality holds only for I = all hardware owners. The inequality R ≥ min(P, Σ_I r_i) is correct (r_i ≥ 0, min(P,·) nondecreasing), so coverage still holds. Fix: replace "exact" with "≥; exact at I = full set."

**2. (Medium — missing hypothesis / interpretation) Line 6: "all-profile binding resource truthfulness."**
Verified: y_A ≡ 0, y_C = min(P, Σr) nondecreasing in P, y_Bi = min(H_i, L_i) nondecreasing in H_i, for all opposing profiles. Since underreports are binding and payments are monotone, truth is *weakly* dominant — but for A it is exactly indifferent at every profile. Consequence: A may report L = 0 at zero cost, which sets a_i = 0, r_i = H_i, and raises y_C from R to min(P, ΣH_i), while every B_i loses a_i. With side payments, the pair {A, C} strictly gains (concrete: H=(1,1), L=(1,1), P=2: truthful y_C = 0; A reports L=0 → y_C = 2, B's drop from 1 to 0). Line 7 acknowledges "weak incentive only," but the theorem should state explicitly that (a) no coalitional/group strategy-proofness is claimed and (b) B_i's payment depends on a report by an indifferent agent, so without a tie-breaking-toward-truth assumption the B_i guarantees are not self-enforcing.
