4: MODEL. The connected resource graph has central vertices u,v and an edge uv
5: of value a>0. Leaves attached to u have capacities s_j and edge values b_j>0;
6: leaves attached to v have capacities t_k and edge values c_k>0. The centers
7: have capacities U,V. Every edge consumes one unit at each incident endpoint.
8: All resource capacities are sums of arbitrary nonnegative bundles held by a
9: fixed real owner set. The owner count and both leaf counts are unrestricted.
10: Any owner can own resources on both sides and at several vertices.
11: 
12: Assume a >= b_max+c_max, with an absent side's maximum defined as zero.
13: Sort b_1>=...>=b_J>0 and c_1>=...>=c_K>0, put b_{J+1}=c_{K+1}=0,
14: B_j=sum_{h<=j}s_h, C_k=sum_{h<=k}t_h. Then
15: 
16: F = (a-b_1-c_1) min(U,V)
17:     + sum_j (b_j-b_{j+1}) min(U,V+B_j)
18:     + sum_k (c_k-c_{k+1}) min(V,U+C_k).                 (DS)
19: 
20: Missing-side sums and maxima are zero. All coefficients are nonnegative and
21: all arguments are nonnegative fixed linear combinations of resource capacities.
22: 
23: PROOF. Set m=min(U,V). Start with any optimal feasible edge quantities having
24: central quantity z<m. Increase z by delta<=m-z. At u, reduce incident leaf
25: quantities by max(0,delta-(U-z-L_u)), where L_u is their current total; this
26: amount is at most delta and at most L_u because z+delta<=U. Similarly at v.
27: This preserves all capacities (leaf capacities only become less used), while
28: lost leaf value is at most delta(b_1+c_1) and central gain is a delta. Hence
29: there is an optimum with z=m. The two leaf systems then independently maximize
30: fractional knapsack value with center budgets U-m and V-m. Layer decomposition
31: gives
32: F=a m + sum_j Delta b_j min((U-V)_+,B_j)
33:           + sum_k Delta c_k min((V-U)_+,C_k).
34: Use min((U-V)_+,B)=min(U,V+B)-min(U,V), and its symmetric version; telescoping
35: sum Delta b=b_1 and sum Delta c=c_1 yields (DS).
36: 
37: PAYMENTS. Apply the proved mixed-owner two-input exponential rule to each
38: minimum in (DS), using each real owner's contributions to its two aggregate
39: arguments. Sum with the fixed nonnegative coefficients. Every coalition obeys
40: (DS), so coverage adds. Own whole-bundle increases raise both derived accounts
41: coordinatewise without changing another owner's accounts, so all-profile
42: truthfulness follows from the mixed-owner lemma. The total is at most
43: (1+1/(2e))F. Zero capacities, zero-weight differences, ties, and zero output

20: final profiles rho=r-h,r. Own monotonicity requires

3: \subsection{Small-stage guarantees and failed exponential extensions}
4: \begin{proposition}\label{hist:softmax}
5: For $k=2$ or $3$, put $\rho_j=\sum_{i\in N_j}d_i/T$, $u_j=\rho_j-1$,
6: $w_j=e^{-u_j}$, $p_j=w_j/\sum_\ell w_\ell$, and $y_i=d_ip_j$.
7: This rule is own-report monotone, covers coalitions, protects $M_i/k$,
8: and has exact worst-case subsidy $b_k$, where
9: $b_ke^{b_k}=(k-1)/e$. The same unit-rate formula fails own-report
10: monotonicity when $k=4$.
11: \end{proposition}
12: \begin{proof}
13: Coverage is immediate from capping and $\sum p_j=1$.
14: For an owner with $M_i>0$, same-pool remainder $a<T$, and $m=d_i$, one
15: has $u_j=(m-M_i)/T$ and $\sum w_j\le k$. Hence
16: $y_i\ge m e^{-(m-M_i)/T}/k\ge M_i/k$ by the preceding monotonicity of
17: $t e^{-t/T}$. The assertion is immediate if $M_i=0$.
18: In the long region of an own report, $T$ is fixed, and differentiation
19: with respect to $m$ gives $p_j[1-(m/T)(1-p_j)]\ge0$.
20: In the short region, $T=x+a$, $u_j=0$, and
21: $0\ge\rho_\ell'\ge-\rho_\ell/T$ off clipping points. Therefore
22: \[
23:  \frac{d}{dx}(xp_j)
24:  \ge p_j\left[1-\sum_{\ell\ne j}p_\ell\rho_\ell\right]\ge0,
25: \]
26: since the last bracket is nonnegative precisely when
27: $\sum_{\ell\ne j}u_\ell e^{-u_\ell}\le1$, and each term is at most
28: $1/e$. For $k\le3$, $(k-1)/e<1$. Continuity handles clipping points
29: and the bottleneck seam; the zero boundary is handled by $0\le y_i\le x$.
30: 
31: The subsidy is $F=\sum_j u_je^{-u_j}/\sum_j e^{-u_j}$, with some
32: $u_s=0$. For $b=b_k$, the elementary maximum
33: $(u-b)e^{-u}\le e^{-1-b}$ implies
34: \[
35:  \sum_j(u_j-b)e^{-u_j}\le-b+(k-1)e^{-1-b}=0.
36: \]
37: Thus $F\le b$. Equality holds when $u_s=0$ and every other $u_j=1+b$:
38: use one unit owner in pool $s$ and three owners of capacity $(2+b)/3<1$
39: in each other pool. Here $b<1$ follows from $k\le3$.
40: 
41: For failure at $k=4$, use one owner reporting $x$ in the first pool and
42: three owners of capacity $2/3$ in each other pool. Near $x=1$ the first
43: owner's payment is
44: $y(x)=x/[1+3e^{1-2/x}]$, whose derivative at one is
45: $(1-3/e)/(1+3/e)^2<0$. Thus some strict underreport increases payment.
46: \end{proof}

48: $e^{-u_j}/k$ and the residual to the short pool is not truthful even for
49: three pools. At $(x\mid1/2,1/2\mid1/2,1/2)$, the first payment is
50: $1/3$ when $x=1$, but at $x=99/100$ it is
51: \[
52:  x\left[1-\tfrac23 e^{-(1-x)/x}\right]
53:  \ge x(1-2x/3)=1683/5000>1/3,
54: \]
55: using $e^{-q}\le1/(1+q)$. These are obstructions to the stated formulas,
56: not to all mechanisms with more pools.
