FILE EXTREME_RAY_OBSTRUCTION.txt
1: Connected unit-profit bipartite production has no nontrivial positive concave decomposition
2: 15 October 2026. Structural lemma; historical novelty unconfirmed.
3: 
4: Let G=(H union P,E) be a finite connected simple bipartite graph with at least
5: one edge. Its unit-profit resource-production function is
6:   F(c)=max {sum_e x_e: x_e>=0, sum_{e incident v}x_e<=c_v}, c>=0.
7: 
8: THEOREM. Suppose, on the whole nonnegative orthant,
9:   F(c)=sum_{l=1}^L f_l(c),
10: where every f_l is finite, nonnegative, concave and homogeneous of degree one.
11: Then every summand is proportional to F: f_l=lambda_l F, lambda_l>=0,
12: sum_l lambda_l=1. Thus F spans an extreme ray in this function cone.
13: Positive constant coefficients can be absorbed into the summands.
14: 
15: PROOF. Concavity and degree-one homogeneity imply superadditivity:
16: f_l(x+y)>=f_l(x)+f_l(y). For each edge e=uv let z_e be the vector with ones
17: at u and v and zero elsewhere, and define w_le=f_l(z_e)>=0. Since F(z_e)=1,
18:   sum_l w_le=1 for every edge e.
19: For any feasible edge plan x at capacities c, the residual vector
20: c-sum_e x_e z_e is nonnegative. Superadditivity, homogeneity and nonnegativity
21: give
22:   f_l(c)>=sum_e x_e f_l(z_e)=sum_e w_le x_e.
23: Maximizing the RHS over feasible plans yields f_l(c)>=F_{w_l}(c), the same
24: production LP with nonnegative edge profits w_l. Conversely,
25:   F(c)=F_{sum_l w_l}(c)<=sum_l F_{w_l}(c)<=sum_l f_l(c)=F(c).
26: All differences are nonnegative and the sum is zero, so
27:   f_l(c)=F_{w_l}(c) for every l and c.
28: 
29: Now take any pair of adjacent edges e=uv and f=vw. Since the graph is simple
30: and bipartite, u and w are distinct same-side vertices, with no edge uw.
31: Give capacity one to u,v,w and zero elsewhere. Only e and f can produce,
32: and their shared unit at v implies
33:   F(c)=1, F_{w_l}(c)=max(w_le,w_lf).
34: Therefore sum_l max(w_le,w_lf)=1, while both sum_l w_le and sum_l w_lf equal
35: one. The identity max(a,b)=(a+b+|a-b|)/2 forces w_le=w_lf for every l.
36: The edge-adjacency graph of a connected graph is connected. Hence for each l,
37: w_le is a constant lambda_l on all edges, and
38:   f_l=F_{w_l}=lambda_l F.
39: This completes the proof without strategic owner splitting or any finite-grid
40: inference. The statement is purely about production functions.
41: 
42: CONSEQUENCE FOR BOUNDED-FORM ROUTES. For a bipartite graph, F is the minimum of
43: the capacity sums over its inclusion-minimal vertex covers. Every such linear
44: form is exposed at a strictly positive capacity vector: give vertices in a
45: chosen minimal cover C capacity epsilon with 0<epsilon<1/|C|, and every other
46: vertex capacity one. C costs less than one. Any distinct minimal cover has a
47: vertex outside C (otherwise C would not be minimal), so costs at least one.
48: Thus a nonzero scalar multiple of F requires at least as many linear pieces
49: as there are minimal covers. The extreme-ray theorem implies that F cannot
50: be a finite positive sum of minima of at most K nonnegative linear forms
51: whenever its number of minimal covers exceeds K.
52: 
53: This blocks every fixed-K version of that route on all forests. For an even
54: path P_{2m}, the m+1 distinct minimal covers
55:   C_j={1,3,...,2j-1} union {2j,2j+2,...,2m-2}, j=0,...,m,
56: (using vertices 0,...,2m-1) already show unbounded cover count. Each selected
57: vertex has an adjacent unselected vertex, so these covers are inclusion-minimal.
58: Taking m>=K gives more than K exposed forms on a connected unit forest.
59: 
60: The four-edge path has four minimal covers, yielding the earlier specific
61: obstruction to positive <=3-form decomposition as a special case. The new
62: mixed four-form mechanism handles that particular path directly, but extending
63: composition to any fixed larger K cannot by itself settle every forest.
64: 
65: BOUNDARIES. This is not a lower bound on required subsidy. It does not rule
66: out direct nonlinear mechanisms, sums involving negative coefficients,
67: nonconcave intermediate constructions, or increasing-form mechanisms with a
68: new bound depending on graph structure. The nonnegative global function domain
69: and degree-one homogeneity are explicit hypotheses. For the particular
70: minimum-of-nonnegative-linear-forms components under discussion they hold
71: automatically. Disconnected graphs do decompose over their connected components.
72: 
73: The mathematical argument uses standard superadditivity, max-of-sum versus
74: sum-of-max, and connectivity facts. No historical novelty claim is made until
75: an exact original-source comparison is completed.
76: 
77: CLARIFICATION ON THE NUMBER OF LINEAR PIECES. At a positive capacity vector exposing one minimal cover uniquely, F agrees with that cover's linear form on a neighborhood. If F is represented as the minimum of finitely many other linear forms, any form active at that interior point majorizes F there and has zero linear difference there. Its difference from the exposed form is linear and nonnegative on a neighborhood, so it must vanish identically. Thus every exposed minimal-cover form must literally occur among the representing forms; the count cannot be avoided by different gradients or redundant terms.
78: 
79: DEPTH-TWO SPECIALIZATION. The obstruction already grows exponentially on the next infrastructure model, without increasing tree depth. For the subdivided star with branches p--h_j--l_j, j=1,...,n, the minimal covers are:
80: - all h_j, excluding p and every l_j;
81: - p together with exactly one of h_j,l_j on every branch, with at least one l_j selected (so p is not redundant).
82: There are1+(2^n-1)=2^n minimal covers. Each is exposed as above. Hence its unit-value function requires exactly2^n linear forms, and any nonzero concave homogeneous summand in a positive decomposition requires that many forms as well. Thus a fixed-K positive decomposition fails even on depth-two trees with unbounded branch count; arbitrary depth is not needed for this barrier.

1: Classical hierarchical core allocations give truthful efficiency on owner forests
2: 15 October 2026. Restricted-class boundary, not a new core theorem.
3: 
4: Setup. Fix a forest H on the ACTUAL owner set. For every report profile b,
5: v_b(empty)=0, v_b is nonnegative and superadditive, and v_b(S) is the sum of
6: v_b(C) over the connected components C of the induced owner graph H[S].
7: Assume v_b(S) is independent of reports outside S and is coordinatewise
8: nondecreasing in reports of owners in S. The graph and roots do not depend
9: on reported capacities, including when a capacity becomes zero.
10: 
11: Choose a fixed root in each component. Let D_i be the subtree rooted at i
12: and child(i) its immediate children. The classical hierarchical allocation is
13:   y_i(b) = v_b(D_i) - sum_{h in child(i)} v_b(D_h).
14: It is nonnegative, exactly efficient, coalition-covering, and nondecreasing
15: in every coordinate of owner i's report on the entire report domain.
16: 
17: Proof. Nonnegativity follows from superadditivity on the disjoint child
18: subtrees and singleton {i}. Summing over a rooted component telescopes to
19: its worth; summing components gives v_b(N). For connected coalition C let
20: r be its member nearest the fixed root. Summing y_i over i in C telescopes
21: as follows: y(C) equals v_b(D_r) minus the sum of worths of the subtrees
22: hanging off C below its boundary. These subtrees and C partition D_r.
23: Superadditivity thus gives y(C)>=v_b(C). For arbitrary S, sum this result
24: over the components of H[S] and use component additivity. Finally, owner
25: i's report changes v_b(D_i) monotonically and changes none of the child
26: terms, because i lies in no D_h. This proves all-profile own monotonicity.
27: A fixed convex combination over roots retains every property; the classical
28: average-tree solution is therefore another valid choice. Dependence of the
29: root weights or tree on reports would require a new incentive argument.
30: 
31: Production corollary. Let a fixed physical resource forest have activities
32: on its edges. Activity x_e>=0 consumes fixed positive amounts at its two
33: endpoints and generates nonnegative fixed output p_e*x_e. Each resource
34: has additive capacity available only from its owner. Partition resource
35: vertices among actual owners, with each owner's block connected, and let
36: that owner report an arbitrary coordinatewise-smaller bundle of all its
37: capacities. Different owners' blocks are disjoint. Contracting each block
38: gives a forest on actual owners. At every profile, including zero reports,
39: coalition production decomposes over components of that contracted forest:
40: there are no activity edges between those components, and no shared resource
41: rows. Separate feasible activity plans can be added, so the game is
42: superadditive. Coalition worth is increasing in included capacities and
43: independent of absent owners' reports. The preceding allocation therefore
44: is efficient, coalition-covering and own-bundle monotone for arbitrarily
45: many resources and owners. Internal owner activities merely allow positive
46: singleton values and cause no problem.
47: 
48: Limits. Multiple actual owners sharing the capacity of one resource do not
49: form a vertex partition. A single owner's disconnected resource holdings
50: also do not meet the connected-block hypothesis; contracting disconnected
51: blocks may create cycles. These cases must not be claimed from this result.
52: Two resources with two suppliers each already induce the four-owner
53: substitution cycle K_{2,2} and admit the existing 9/8 incentive lower bound.
54: Thus physical branching alone is not the obstruction; ownership structure
55: can remove the owner-forest decomposition even on a single physical edge.
56: This does not prove that every disconnected/overlapping ownership pattern
57: requires a subsidy, only that the classical argument no longer guarantees
58: zero subsidy.

67: to Demange (2004); Theorem 4.1 characterizes the stable average-tree solution.

70: this classical construction, not independent evidence of novelty.

75: Broader formulation. Connected resource blocks are sufficient, not required.
76: For ANY fixed production system whose resources have exclusive actual owners
77: and whose activity columns each use resources from at most two owners, form
78: the fixed owner-interaction graph: connect distinct owners exactly when some
79: activity needs resources of both. If this graph is a forest and there are no
80: other shared constraints, coalition production decomposes over its induced
81: components. All the report-locality and superadditivity hypotheses above
82: hold, so the classical hierarchy gives efficient own-bundle-monotone payments.
83: An owner's resources may be physically disconnected. Activities internal to
84: one owner are allowed, including positive singleton production. If an activity
85: uses several owners, the same conclusion follows whenever its owner support
86: is connected in a fixed owner forest and absence of a required owner makes
87: the activity unavailable; this broader variant is not needed here.
88: 
89: The repeated A-B-C-D path family has an owner-interaction four-cycle, although
90: its physical resource graph is a path and resources have exclusive owners.
91: FRAGMENTED_FOREST_FAMILY.txt proves an incentive factor tending to9/8 there.
92: The precise contrast is acyclic owner interaction (classical zero subsidy)
93: versus some cyclic owner-interaction examples with positive subsidy. We do
94: not claim every owner cycle necessarily incurs a subsidy.
