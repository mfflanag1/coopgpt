92: \begin{lemma}[Relative supporting forms]\label{ext:lem:support}
93: Assume $f$ is concave.  For $x\ge0$ let $J=\operatorname{supp}(x)$ and
94: $F_J=\{y\ge0:\operatorname{supp}(y)\subseteq J\}$.
95: If $J\ne\emptyset$, the set of $u\in\R_+^J$ satisfying
96: \[
97:  u\cdot x_J=f(x),\qquad f(y)\le u\cdot y_J\quad(y\in F_J)
98: \]
99: is nonempty, compact and convex.  Its unique minimum-norm element $u(x)$
100: therefore defines a choice depending only on $f,x$.
101: Set $\ell_x(y)=u(x)\cdot y_J$ on $F_J$ and $+\infty$ outside it.
102: For $x=0$, instead set $\ell_0(0)=0$, $\ell_0(y)=+\infty$ for $y\ne0$.
103: Then $\ell_x$ is componentwise nondecreasing, majorizes $f$ everywhere,
104: and touches it at $x$.  On its finite face it is an ordinary nonnegative
105: linear form.  No boundary continuity is required.
106: \end{lemma}
107: \begin{proof}
108: On the relative interior of $F_J$, finiteness and concavity supply a finite
109: supergradient $u$ at $x$~\cite{BoydSubgradients}:
110: $f(y)\le f(x)+u\cdot(y_J-x_J)$.
111: Putting $y=\lambda x$ with both $\lambda>1$ and $0<\lambda<1$ yields
112: $u\cdot x_J=f(x)$.  Monotonicity applied to $y=x+te_e$ gives $u_e\ge0$.
113: For any boundary point $y\in F_J$ and $0<\epsilon<1$, the point
114: $y_\epsilon=(1-\epsilon)y+\epsilon x$ is in the relative interior.
115: Concavity and the supporting inequality give
116: \[
117:  (1-\epsilon)f(y)+\epsilon f(x)\le f(y_\epsilon)
118:  \le(1-\epsilon)u\cdot y_J+\epsilon f(x).
119: \]
120: Cancel and divide by $1-\epsilon$ to obtain the boundary inequality.
121: The admissible set is an intersection of closed half-spaces and the touching
122: hyperplane.  It is bounded by $0\le u_e\le f(x)/x_e$, so is compact;
123: strict convexity of squared norm gives the unique choice.
124: The extension majorizes $f$ trivially outside its face.  Increasing an argument
125: within the face increases its nonnegative linear value; leaving the face
126: changes that value to infinity.  The zero-face convention has the same
127: properties.  In particular, no subtraction of infinite quantities is needed.
128: \end{proof}

139: \begin{theorem}[General concave homogeneous production]\label{ext:thm:general}
140: For every finite, monotone, concave, degree-one homogeneous $f$, the rule of
141: Definition~\ref{ext:def:general} is eligible at every real bundle profile and
142: \[
143:  0\le M_i(b)\le T,\qquad
144:  \sum_iM_i(b)\le\min\{n,C_j(2^n)\}T.
145: \]
146: A zero bundle receives zero, and every zero-value profile receives zero.
147: \end{theorem}
148: \begin{proof}
149: Both capped contributions and endpoint marginals belong to $[0,T]$, so the
150: same holds for their mixtures.  If $b_i=0$, then $\ell(0)=0$ and its on/off
151: aggregates coincide.  If $T=0$, monotonicity makes every masked value zero.
152: 
153: For incentives, fix $b_{-i},B_{-i}$ with $B_i=0$.
154: The aggregate $x_B$ and its selected form are independent of the entire own
155: bundle $b_i$.  Both $\ell_{x_B}(b_i)$ and $T$ increase weakly with $b_i$,
156: including finite-to-infinite transitions; their capped minimum is therefore
157: nondecreasing.  The off term of $m_i^q$ is fixed and the on term increases.
158: Averaging with report-independent nonnegative weights proves own-bundle
159: monotonicity on the whole domain.
160: 
161: For coverage, fix a selected form $\ell$ and coalition $S$.
162: If $\ell(b_i)\ge T$ for some $i\in S$, that capped term alone covers
163: $v_b(S)\le T$.  Otherwise all coalition terms are finite, uncapped and on
164: the same face, so their sum is $\ell(\sum_{i\in S}b_i)\ge v_b(S)$.
165: Thus every vector $a(B)$, and hence $z^q$, covers coalitions.
166: For a fixed $B_{-i}$ select $\ell=\ell_x$ at the on aggregate
167: $x=b_i+\sum_{h\ne i}B_hb_h$.  The bundles $b_i,x-b_i$ lie on its finite face.
168: Touching, majorization and finite additivity give
169: \begin{equation}\label{ext:eq:endpoint}
170:  f(x)-f(x-b_i)\ge\ell(x)-\ell(x-b_i)=\ell(b_i),\qquad
171:  0\le\ell(b_i)\le f(x)\le T.
172: \end{equation}
173: The middle term is the uncapped on contribution.  Averaging implies
174: $m_i^q\ge\E[a_i(B)\mid B_i=1]$, hence $r_i^q\ge z_i^q$ and coverage of $M$.
175: 
176: For the budget, every open bundle lies on the finite face of $\ell_{x_B}$.
177: Its nonnegative contributions sum to $\ell_{x_B}(x_B)=f(x_B)\le T$,
178: so \eqref{ext:eq:open-sum} holds.  At the fixed profile there are at most
179: $2^n$ capped vectors.  Lemma~\ref{ext:lem:budget} gives the $C_j(2^n)$ bound;
180: $M_i\le T$ gives the other bound.  All endpoint arithmetic above is finite.
181: \end{proof}

183: \begin{corollary}[Owner-count upper bound]\label{ext:cor:owner-upper}
184: Let $L_n=n\log2$ and $j_n=\max\{2,\lceil L_n/[\log(2+L_n)]^2\rceil\}$.
185: The rule with this report-independent parameter satisfies
186: \[
187:  \sum_iM_i(b)\le C_{j_n}(2^n)T
188:    =(\log2+o(1))\frac n{\log n}T,
189: \]
190: uniformly in dimension, technology and profiles.  For $n=1$ it is efficient.
191: \end{corollary}
192: \begin{proof}
193: Here $j_n\sim L_n/(\log L_n)^2$, $\alpha_{j_n}\sim\log n$, and
194: $j_n=o(n/\log n)$; substitute into $C_j$.
195: For $n=1$ and $b_1\ne0$, the off form is $\ell_0$, so its capped
196: contribution is $T$, and the endpoint marginal is also $T$.
197: Thus $r_1^q=M_1=T$; the zero bundle is immediate.
198: \end{proof}

200: The direct construction covers downward boundary discontinuities, for example
201: $f(x_1,x_2)=x_1$ when $x_2>0$ and zero otherwise.
202: It avoids limits of approximating technologies.  The canonical relative
203: support is a mathematical definition, not an efficient oracle; no regularity

207: \subsection{Owner-count and dimension lower bounds}
208: \begin{definition}[Total-payment factors]\label{ext:def:factors}
209: Let $\mathcal Y_n(f)$ denote eligible rules on all of $(\R_+^d)^n$ and put
210: \[
211:  \mathfrak b_n(f)=\inf_{y\in\mathcal Y_n(f)}\sup_{b:T(b)>0}\frac{y(b)(N)}{T(b)}.
212: \]
213: The inner supremum is zero if $f\equiv0$.  These are total-payment factors,
214: not subsidy quantities.  Let $\mathfrak M_d$ be finite nonnegative min-linear
215: technologies on $\R_+^d$, and $\mathfrak F_d$ all finite monotone concave
216: homogeneous technologies there.  Define
217: \[
218:  B_n^{\rm own}=\sup_{d\ge1,f\in\mathfrak M_d}\mathfrak b_n(f),\quad
219:  B_n^{\rm conc}=\sup_{d\ge1,f\in\mathfrak F_d}\mathfrak b_n(f),\quad
220:  B_d^{\rm dim}=\sup_{n\ge1,f\in\mathfrak M_d}\mathfrak b_n(f).
221: \]
222: Owners, resources and technology are fixed inside each infimum; different
223: problems need not share a rule.  The first two suprema allow dimension to
224: grow; the last fixes dimension and permits arbitrarily many owners.
225: \end{definition}
226: 
227: \begin{theorem}[Owner-count range]\label{ext:thm:owner-range}
228: As $n\to\infty$ along all integers,
229: \[
230:  (1-o(1))\frac{\log n}{\log\log n}\le B_n^{\rm own}
231:  \le B_n^{\rm conc}\le\min\{n,C_{j_n}(2^n)\}
232:        =(\log2+o(1))\frac n{\log n}.
233: \]
234: The lower bound is a parameter translation of the weighted hierarchy,
235: not a new construction; it permits dimension to grow with $n$.
236: \end{theorem}
237: \begin{proof}
238: The upper follows from Theorem~\ref{ext:thm:general} and
239: $\mathfrak M_d\subseteq\mathfrak F_d$.
240: For the lower, use $f_k(x)=\min_{1\le e\le k}x_e$.
241: Restrict $km$ owners to axis-supported bundles, with $m$ owners per axis.
242: An eligible all-bundle rule restricts to a valid scalar-report multi-glove
243: rule, and its restricted total-payment factor cannot be larger.
244: Thus Lemma~\ref{lem:weighted-hierarchy} applies to $\mathfrak b_{km}(f_k)$.
245: If $n\ge n'$, restricting an $n$-owner rule to zero reports for the last
246: $n-n'$ owners and discarding their nonnegative payments yields an eligible
247: $n'$-owner rule.  All original coalition constraints and unilateral comparisons
248: remain, so $\mathfrak b_n(f)\ge\mathfrak b_{n'}(f)$ without population incentives.
249: 
250: For an integer $h\ge2$ set
251: \begin{align*}
252:  R&=\lceil\log(h+1)\rceil, &s&=\lceil\sqrt R\rceil, &m&=hs,\\
253:  \alpha&=1/s, &A&=(h+1)m-h^2, &\lambda&=As,\\
254:  Z&=\sum_{r=0}^h\frac{\lambda^r}{r!}, &K_h&=\lceil2RZ\rceil, &\nu_h&=K_hm.
255: \end{align*}
256: Here $s\ge2$, so $m>h$, and $K_hp_0=K_h/Z\ge2R\ge2\log(h+1)$.
257: Equation~\eqref{eq:weighted-bound}, with hierarchy height $h$, yields
258: \begin{equation}\label{ext:eq:owner-lower-finite}
259:  \mathfrak b_{\nu_h}(f_{K_h})
260:  \ge\frac{h+1-h/s}{(1+1/s)(1+h/(h+1)^2)}=(1-o(1))h.
261: \end{equation}
262: Also $\log\lambda=2\log h+O(\log\log h)$ and $\lambda\ge h$.
263: The terms of $Z$ increase up to index $h$, so
264: $\lambda^h/h!\le Z\le(h+1)\lambda^h/h!$.
265: Stirling's formula gives $\log Z=(1+o(1))h\log h$ and hence
266: $\log\nu_h=(1+o(1))h\log h$.
267: For fixed $0<\epsilon<1$ choose
268: $h=\lfloor(1-\epsilon)\log n/\log\log n\rfloor$.
269: Then $\log\nu_h\le(1-\epsilon+o(1))\log n<\log n$ for all large $n$.
270: Padding to $n$ owners and \eqref{ext:eq:owner-lower-finite} give
271: $B_n^{\rm own}\ge(1-\epsilon-o(1))\log n/\log\log n$.
272: Taking the liminf and then $\epsilon\downarrow0$ proves the all-integer lower.
273: \end{proof}
