4: CLASS AND RESULTS
5: Fix owners, a finite nonnegative min-linear technology, and any report-independent
6: law on masks B. Select a common nonnegative global linear support at the open
7: aggregate x_B, using only B, technology and open reports, possibly at random.
8: Open owners receive their endpoint marginal f(x_B)-f(x_B-b_i). Closed owners
9: receive their support price capped either at T=f(sum b_i), or at
10:  V_i=max_{S subset[N]\{i}}[f(b_i+b(S))-f(b(S))].
11: Average the mask vectors. All caps and payments below are total-payment factors.

13: (A) For global-T caps, there are n-owner examples in dimension d=n on which
14: every such fixed-mask rule has worst-profile factor (sqrt(2)-o(1))sqrt(d).
15: (B) Even with the stronger V_i caps, there are n-owner examples in dimension
16: d_n=2n+ceil(n^(3/4)) on which every such rule has factor
17: (1/2-o(1))sqrt(d_n).
18: Thus the O(sqrt(d)) general dimension upper has the correct ORDER within both
19: fixed-mask architectures. This DOES NOT establish an unrestricted sqrt(d) lower.

22: COMMON FINITE SUPPORT CONSTRUCTION
23: Take n>=9 and a nonincreasing sequence delta_0,...,delta_(n-1)>=0, summing1.
24: Suppose delta_l=0 for l>=m, with 1<=m<n, and delta_0>=1/n+1/n^2.
25: Write r_l=n-l, v_l=sum_(j=l)^(n-1)delta_j, so v_0=1,v_n=0.
26: Then a_l=v_l/r_l decreases with l for1<=l<n: indeed
27: v_(l+1)<=(r_l-1)delta_l implies v_l/r_l>=v_(l+1)/(r_l-1).
28: It also follows that a_l<=a_1=(1-delta_0)/(n-1)<1/n.
29: 
30: Put epsilon=1/(100n^6), t=1+(n-1)epsilon, A=n^2. For every nonempty proper
31: subset S of[n], define w^S_j=A/|S|^2 for j in S and a_(|S|) otherwise.
32: Let
33:  f(x)=min{(sum x_j)/n, w^S.x:empty!=S!=[n]}.
34: It is finite, continuous, monotone, concave, and degree-one homogeneous.
35: At the regular profile P, owner i reports e_i+epsilon sum_(j!=i)e_j.
36: The full aggregate is t times the all-ones vector. The baseline is uniquely
37: minimal there, with T=t, since each subset form contributes at least At/|S|>t.
38: 
39: For a nonempty proper mask let C be its closed set, l=|C|, r=n-l.
40: Its aggregate equals r epsilon on C and1+(r-1)epsilon elsewhere. The unique
41: minimum is w^C. Here are all comparisons, so no tie priority is exploited:
42: 1. Any S meeting an open coordinate costs at least A/(n-1)^2>1.
43: 2. The C form costs v_l+epsilon E_l, where
44:    E_l=A r/l+(r-1)v_l, 0<=E_l<=2n^3.
45:    Its gap below the baseline r/n before the epsilon perturbation is
46:    r/n-v_l >=r(delta_0-1/n)/(n-1)>=1/n^3.
47:    Since2n^3 epsilon<1/n^3, it stays below r/n and hence below the actual
48:    baseline r t/n; in particular its value is below1.
49: 3. If nonempty S is strictly contained in C, its first-coordinate contribution
50:    A r epsilon/|S| is strictly larger than A r epsilon/l. Its off-S coefficient
51:    a_(|S|)>=a_l multiplies all open coordinates and additional nonnegative
52:    closed coordinates. Hence it is strictly more expensive than C.
53: These exhaust all subset forms. The active aggregate is positive in every
54: coordinate, so ANY global supporting form has this unique gradient; randomized
55: support selection or redundant forms cannot change it.
56: 
57: Consequently every closed raw support price is at least
58:  A[1+(l-1)epsilon]/l^2 >=n^2/(n-1)^2 >t=T.
59: The masked production values are exactly
60:  f(x_B)=v_l+epsilon E_l for1<=l<n,
61:  f(x_N)=t=v_0+epsilon(n-1), and f(x_empty)=0.
62: Each open endpoint marginal, divided by T, is therefore at least
63:  delta_l-eta, where eta=1/n^3.
64: Indeed its unnormalized error is at most2n^3 epsilon, normalization adds at
65: most(n-1)epsilon since delta_l<=1, and (2n^3+n)epsilon<1/n^3.
66: All endpoint marginals are nonnegative by monotonicity, even when this lower
67: bound is negative. The normalized maximum coalition marginal V_i/T is at least
68: delta_0-eta for every owner, by using the grand coalition's endpoint marginal.

70: PART A: ONE REGULAR PROFILE, GLOBAL-T CAP, d=n
71: Let m be the largest integer with
72:  S_m=sum_(l=0)^(m-1) (m-l)/(n-l)<=1.
73: For n>=9, 2<=m<n/2. The latter follows from
74: S_m>=m(m+1)/(2n) and sqrt(2n)<n/2 for n>8.
75: Set delta_l=(m-l)/[(n-l)S_m] for0<=l<m, zero afterward.
76: These sum1 and decrease, because (m-l)/(n-l) decreases for m<n.
77: Also delta_0=m/(nS_m)>=m/n>=2/n, satisfying the common construction.
78: 
79: For a nonempty mask with l<m, its normalized budget is at least
80:  l+(n-l)(delta_l-eta)
81:  =l+(m-l)/S_m-(n-l)eta >=m-1/n^2=:C_n.
82: For m<=l<n, closed payments alone give at least l>=m. At the full mask l=0
83: the same endpoint bound applies, so EVERY nonempty mask has budget >=C_n.
84: 
85: To handle the empty mask in this SAME f and SAME n-owner domain, make n
86: cyclic anchors indexed by j. Let S_j={j,...,j+m-1} modulo n, and let z_j
87: have coordinate tau=m/(4n^3) on S_j and1 elsewhere. At Q_j all n owners
88: report the identical bundle z_j. Because a_m=0,
89:  T_j=n f(z_j)<=n A tau/m=1/4; it is strictly positive.
90: Every empty-mask global support w has total mass s=sum w_e>=f(1)=1.
91: Uniformly over the n cyclic windows, expected scarce weight is (m/n)s.
92: Thus at least a fraction1-2m/n of windows have scarce weight<=s/2, and on
93: those windows w.z_j>=s/2>=1/2>=T_j. All n closed owners are then capped
94: at T_j. Uniform anchor average normalized budget is at least A_n=n-2m>0.
95: This holds for every realization of the empty support and so also randomized
96: support selection. The anchor list is fixed independently of the selector.
97: 
98: Mix P with probability A_n/(A_n+C_n) and uniform anchors with probability
99: C_n/(A_n+C_n). Every mask, empty or nonempty, has weighted normalized budget
100: at least L_n=C_n A_n/(C_n+A_n). Average over any fixed mask/support law;

102: Since S_m=m(m+1)/(2n)+O(m^3/n^2) at m=O(sqrt n), maximality gives
103: m=(sqrt(2)+o(1))sqrt(n). Hence L_n=(sqrt(2)-o(1))sqrt(n), with d=n.

105: PART B: V_i CAP, ONE REGULAR BLOCK AND ONE LEONTIEF BLOCK

107:  H=sum_(l=1)^(m-1) (m-l)/(n-l), a=1/(1+H),

128: For the empty-mask anchors, index Leontief resources cyclically modulo D.
129: For each j take the n-resource scarce window S_j={j,...,j+n-1}. Give owner i
130: one unit of scarce resource j+i-1, zero on the other scarce resources, and

FILE FOUNDATIONS.txt
1: SELF-CONTAINED FOUNDATIONS FOR THE NEW GENERAL BOUNDS
2: 15 October 2026. Established ingredients from the existing truthful-production
3: manuscript, restated and checked here. No priority claim.
4: 
5: Fix n actual owners, d resources, and a finite-valued monotone concave degree-one
6: homogeneous f on the nonnegative orthant. Thus f(0)=0 and f>=0. Reports b_i>=0
7: are binding available bundles; utility is the payment, with no private operating
8: cost. Set c=sum b_i, T=f(c), and v(S)=f(sum_(i in S)b_i). Requirements are
9: nonnegative payments, sum_(i in S)y_i>=v(S) for every coalition, and coordinatewise
10: nondecrease of y_i in b_i while every other owner remains fixed. A budget factor
11: C means total payment <=CT; the external subsidy is at most (C-1)T.
12: 
13: RELATIVE SUPPORTS, INCLUDING DOWNWARD BOUNDARY DISCONTINUITIES
14: For x!=0, restrict f to its coordinate face F_x={z>=0:z_e=0 if x_e=0}.
15: The point x is in the relative interior. A finite concave function has a
16: supergradient u there. Monotonicity forces u>=0: f(x+t e)>=f(x), whereas the
17: support inequality gives f(x+t e)<=f(x)+t u_e. Homogeneity, applied to scalar
18: multiples of x both above and below x, gives u.x=f(x). The support inequality
19: on the whole face follows from the interior inequality by the segment from
20: x to any boundary point z and concavity; this does not require continuity at z.
21: Consequently u.z>=f(z) for every z in F_x. The set of such nonnegative u with
22: u.x=f(x) is nonempty, closed, and convex; its minimum-Euclidean-norm element
23: defines one fixed unique choice. Extend ell_x(z)=u.z on F_x and +infinity outside.
24: At x=0 use ell_0(0)=0 and ell_0(z)=+infinity otherwise.
25: 
26: For a finite min-linear technology one may instead select a fixed-priority
27: minimizing nonnegative global form, producing finite ordinary linear supports.
28: The arguments below apply to either selection. Each selection is fixed before
29: evaluating deviations and depends only on its indicated aggregate/report data.
30: 
31: MASK ELIGIBILITY
32: For any fixed subset B of owners let x_B=sum_(i in B)b_i and A_i=min{ell_xB(b_i),T}.
33: The vector A covers every coalition. If any member's raw price is >=T (including
34: +infinity), that member's capped payment alone covers its coalition. Otherwise
35: all relevant prices are finite, add on the support face, and majorize v(S).
36: For i in B, ell_xB(b_i) is finite and
37:  f(x_B)-f(x_B-b_i)>=ell_xB(b_i),
38: by touching at x_B and majorization at x_B-b_i. This marginal is <=T. Therefore
39: replace the open owners' prices by these marginals and retain A_i for closed
40: owners; the resulting vector Y(B) still covers every coalition.
41: 
42: For a closed i, ell_xB excludes b_i, so its raw price and cap T are separately
43: nondecreasing in b_i. For an open i, x_B-b_i excludes b_i and the marginal is
44: nondecreasing in b_i. Thus Y_i(B) is own-bundle monotone. Every report-independent
45: distribution over masks, including any fixed mixture of Bernoulli missing
46: probabilities, preserves these pointwise properties. The expected rule is a
47: deterministic payment map; no approximation by a finite sample is being claimed.
48: 
49: POLYNOMIAL ENDPOINT IDENTITY
50: For independent missing probability q, g(q)=E f(x_B) is a finite polynomial.
51: Conditioning on each owner's bit and differentiating the multilinear polynomial
52: along its diagonal yields -g'(q)=sum_i E[f(b_i+x_(B without i))-f(x_(B without i))].
53: This identity does not differentiate f, move owner identities, or assume report
54: continuity. Every q integral at a fixed profile is an integral of a polynomial.
55: 
56: THE ELEMENTARY d BOUND
57: The grand marginal U_i=f(c)-f(c-b_i) is own-bundle monotone and dominates the
58: selected support price at c, hence covers every coalition. If T>0, let
59: alpha_i=max_(e:c_e>0)b_ie/c_e. Then c-b_i>=(1-alpha_i)c coordinatewise, so
60: U_i<=alpha_i T by monotonicity and homogeneity. Finally
61: sum_i alpha_i<=sum_(e:c_e>0)sum_i b_ie/c_e<=d.
62: Thus total grand-marginal payment is <=dT. If T=0 every term vanishes. This
63: explains the prior linear dimension baseline without relying on a finite-form
64: approximation or on continuity at the boundary.
65: 
66: DEPENDENCIES AND LIMITS
67: Only the standard finite-dimensional supporting-hyperplane theorem for a finite
68: concave function on a relative interior is used above. General evaluation may
69: require exponentially many coalitions or masks; no polynomial-time theorem is
70: asserted. Taking the minimum of independently valid fixed bounds means selecting
71: one entire rule from known n,d,technology parameters before reports; it does not
72: mean taking pointwise minima of payments or tuning a rule after seeing reports.

FILE DIMENSION_SQRT_BOUND_v1.txt
1: GENERAL RESOURCE-DIMENSION UPPER BOUND: (2+o(1))sqrt(d)
2: 15 October 2026. Candidate proof; independent review pending.
3: 
4: THEOREM
5: Let f:R_+^d ->R be finite, monotone, concave and homogeneous of degree one.
6: There are arbitrarily many but fixed n owners reporting whole bundles b_i>=0.
7: For every fixed 0<a<1, the existing relative-support/endpoint hybrid, averaged
8: uniformly over missing probability q in [0,a], is nonnegative, coalition-covering,
9: and nondecreasing in every owner's own bundle at every real profile. Its total
10: payment satisfies
11:  sum_i M_i(b) <= C_d(a) T(b),
12:  C_d(a)=[1+2d(-a-log(1-a))]/a.
13: In particular, taking a=1/(1+sqrt(d)) gives factor at most 1+2sqrt(d).
14: As d tends to infinity, a~1/sqrt(d) gives factor (2+o(1))sqrt(d).
15: Combining the existing dimension marginal bound and the new bound gives
16:  B_d^dim <= min{d, 1+2sqrt(d)},
17: and the same upper for arbitrary finite monotone concave homogeneous functions,
18: including boundary discontinuities. The bound is independent of n and the
19: number of supporting forms. It does not assert efficient oracle evaluation,
20: measurability of the full report map, or historical novelty.
21: 
22: THE UNCHANGED RULE
23: At each profile fix T=f(c), c=sum_i b_i. For a mask B, x_B=sum_i B_i b_i.
24: Choose the canonical relative support ell_(x_B) from the manuscript: it is
25: nonnegative linear on the coordinate face containing x_B, majorizes f there,
26: touches f at x_B, and equals +infinity outside that face. At x_B=0 it is zero
27: at0 and +infinity elsewhere. Put A_i(B)=min{ell_(x_B)(b_i),T}.
28: For independent B_i with Pr(B_i=0)=q, define
29:  p_i(q)=E[A_i(B)|B_i=0],
30:  m_i(q)=E[f(b_i+x_(B_-i))-f(x_(B_-i))],
31:  r_i(q)=q p_i(q)+(1-q)m_i(q),
32:  M_i=(1/a)int_0^a r_i(q)dq.
33: These are finite mask sums and integrals of finite polynomials; no random
34: sampling implementation or limit over technologies is needed. Eligibility is
35: the existing relative-support theorem: a closed support excludes its recipient,
36: both caps increase in the own report, endpoint marginals increase in the own
37: report, capped supports cover coalitions, and on-endpoint marginals dominate
38: on-support contributions. The proof works for every0<q<1.
39: 
40: SCALAR BERNOULLI RECIPROCAL LEMMA
41: Fix arbitrary u_1,...,u_n>=0 and let S_B=sum_i B_i u_i. Define
42:  R_i(B)=min{u_i/S_B,1}, taking R_i=0 if u_i=0 and R_i=1 if u_i>0,S_B=0.
43: Then, with p=1-q,
44:  E sum_(i:B_i=0) R_i(B) <= 2q/p.
45: Proof: for nonnegative u,S,
46:  min{u/S,1} <= 2u/(u+S),
47: with the same zero conventions. Conditioning on the own bit gives the exact
48: identity
49:  E sum_i (1-B_i) u_i/(u_i+S_B)
50:   = (q/p) E sum_i B_i u_i/S_B
51:   = (q/p) Pr(S_B>0) <=q/p.
52: The fractions with zero numerator and denominator are defined as zero.
53: The first equality holds because in either conditioned expression the ratio
54: is u_i/(u_i+sum_(j!=i) B_j u_j). Summing and applying the elementary inequality
55: proves the lemma. No lower bound on positive weights is used.
56: 
57: POINTWISE RESOURCE-COORDINATE DOMINATION
58: Assume T>0. For every mask and every owner i,
59:  A_i(B)/T <= sum_(e=1)^d min{b_ie/(x_B)_e,1},
60: with the scalar lemma's conventions coordinatewise.
61: If b_i has a positive coordinate outside the support of x_B, the right side
62: contains1 and the claim follows from A_i<=T. Otherwise the relative support
63: is an ordinary nonnegative linear form w on that face. Touching gives
64:  sum_e w_e (x_B)_e=f(x_B)<=T,
65: so for each positive coordinate (x_B)_e, w_e (x_B)_e<=T and
66:  w_e b_ie/T <= b_ie/(x_B)_e.
67: Use min{sum z_e,1}<=sum min{z_e,1} for nonnegative z_e to conclude. If x_B=0
68: and b_i=0, both sides are zero; the remaining zero-mask cases were handled
69: by the off-face argument. This also proves the same claim for any finite
70: nonnegative global support, regardless of minimizer ties.
71: 
72: EXPECTED CLOSED BUDGET
73: Apply the scalar lemma separately to each resource's weights u_i=b_ie.
74: The preceding pointwise inequality yields
75:  q sum_i p_i(q)=E sum_(i:B_i=0) A_i(B)
76:  <= [2d q/(1-q)]T.
77: Only d appears; owner count and support-form complexity have disappeared.
78: 
79: ENDPOINT BUDGET AND INTEGRATION
80: Let g(q)=E f(x_B), a finite polynomial. The elementary diagonal derivative
81: identity is sum_i m_i(q)=-g'(q). Therefore
82:  sum_i r_i(q) <= [2d q/(1-q)]T +(1-q)(-g'(q)).
83: Since g(0)=T and g>=0, integration by parts gives
84:  int_0^a (1-q)(-g'(q))dq
85:   =T-(1-a)g(a)-int_0^a g(q)dq <=T.
86: The integral of q/(1-q) is -a-log(1-a). Divide by a to obtain C_d(a).
87: At T=0 monotonicity makes all coalition worths, capped contributions and
88: endpoint marginals zero, so the same bound is immediate.
89: 
90: EXPLICIT PARAMETERS AND ASYMPTOTICS
91: For0<a<1,
92:  -a-log(1-a)=sum_(k>=2) a^k/k <=a^2/[2(1-a)].
93: Consequently C_d(a)<=1/a+d a/(1-a). The choice a=1/(1+sqrt(d)) gives
94: 1/a+d a/(1-a)=1+2sqrt(d). This a is at most1/2 for d>=1, so it stays inside
95: the old averaging interval range (although it need not be an inverse integer).
96: Alternatively choose j=ceil(sqrt(d))+1 and a=1/j to retain the literal old
97: integer parameter: C_d(1/j)<=j+d/(j-1)=(2+o(1))sqrt(d).
98: All parameters are chosen from the fixed resource dimension before reports;
99: there is no report-dependent retuning or deletion of zero owners/resources.
100: 
101: CONTRIBUTION BOUNDARY
102: The production model, relative supports, hybrid eligibility, and derivative
103: identity are established earlier in this project. The new ingredient is the
104: coordinatewise capped-price domination plus the scalar Bernoulli reciprocal
105: identity, giving an owner-independent closed-budget estimate. This changes the
106: previous general dimension upper bound from d to O(sqrt d). The existing lower
107: bound of order log d/loglog d remains unchanged. It does not prove a sharp
108: dimension rate or an unrestricted sqrt(d) lower. Priority is unconfirmed.
