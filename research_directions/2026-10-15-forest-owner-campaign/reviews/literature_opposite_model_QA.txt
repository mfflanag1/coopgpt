## Verdict: revise

The candidate correctly incorporates the project’s \(9/8\) lower bound and \(1+1/(2e)\) star upper, and generally distinguishes population monotonicity, physical allocation, and fixed-game cost of stability from report-linked monetary coverage. But it misses the closest known source already identified in the supplied project record and does not satisfy the “free originals with checked theorem pointers” requirement.

### Major findings

1. **The closest comparator is omitted.**  
   Amigo–Belzarena–Vaton (2016), *Revenue Sharing in Network Utility Maximization Problems*, is materially closer than several listed items. Its model has fixed service/resource coefficients, owners contributing resource stocks, core stability, and an explicit own-resource monotonicity desideratum. The supplied `SOURCE_COMPARISON.txt` gives checked pointers: model §§2.1–2.2, monotonicity §3.2, grand-minus-complement marginals §3.3, and core projection §§5.1–5.2. The paper’s universal result appears to be stability of the projection, while monotonicity is supported only by simulations, but that precise distinction is central to the requested frontier check. Its omission makes “No inspected source subsumes…” inadequately supported. A free author manuscript exists, and the abstract confirms resource ownership, stability, incentives, and projection into an economically stable set ([original manuscript](https://www.colibri.udelar.edu.uy/jspui/bitstream/20.500.12008/42701/1/ABV16.pdf), [bibliographic abstract](https://ideas.repec.org/a/kap/netnom/v17y2016i3d10.1007_s11066-016-9112-4.html)).

2. **Items 4 and 5 are not checked free originals.**  
   Thomson is abstract-only and paywalled; Abe–Liu is likewise supported only by a bibliographic abstract after the alleged original returned 403. These may be leads, but they do not meet the requested standard of “free originals with checked theorem pointers and unread portions.” Replace at least one—preferably both—with accessible full texts, especially Amigo et al.

3. **The five-source limit is not respected in substance.**  
   The report labels five sources as the main list, then adds three further literature comparisons and a ten-link source rollup. Since the task asks for “at most five closest free original sources,” paywalled/abstract-only leads should be separated into a brief limitations sentence, not treated as additional evidentiary comparisons.

4. **The unresolved comparison is precise but poorly targeted.**  
   Realizability of the Housman–Clark example would affect priority only for the already-established factor-\(1\) impossibility. The task explicitly says not to reopen \(\alpha>1\). A more relevant unresolved comparison is:

   > Does the Amigo–Belzarena–Vaton projection rule satisfy universal own-stock monotonicity on the linear, two-resource-per-activity forest subclass, or is their evidence only simulation-based even there?

   That comparison directly addresses the forest target and could reveal either a subsuming construction or a concrete gap. Housman–Clark itself only establishes an incompatibility for arbitrary four-player games; no reduction to this LP/forest domain is supplied ([abstract](https://ideas.repec.org/a/spr/jogath/v27y1998i4p611-616.html)).

5. **Some model relations are stated appropriately but should be tightened.**  
   The Huang comparison is mostly sound: its model has one player per edge, efficient payments, and an explicit underreporting failure for core selection ([model and Example 1](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.08248v1)). But the forest game is not literally a subclass of its cooperative game merely by inserting dummy owners: public activity arcs must be available to every coalition, whereas ordinary max-flow coalitions contain only their owned edges. Calling it a “near-subclass” is acceptable only if this coalition-domain mismatch is made explicit.

6. **The transportation-game statement is static and narrower than the target.**  
   The one-owner-per-node, unit-consumption case is a transportation-game overlap, but it does not cover arbitrary owners bundling several hardware and power nodes, nor report-linked monotonicity. The accessible record supports core nonemptiness and dual/core relations, not incentives or payment-factor bounds ([source record](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/matsoc/v41y2001i2p215-225.html)).

### What is correct

- The \(9/8\) witness is treated as known and not rediscovered.
- The star upper and equality with the mixed two-input minimax factor are incorporated.
- Disjoint-star composition is acknowledged.
- Fixed-profile cost of stability is correctly separated from cross-profile incentive funding.
- PMAS/MCAP is not confused with own-bundle report monotonicity.
- No unsupported novelty certification is made.

The revision should center Amigo et al., retain Huang and perhaps the transportation-game original, use no more than five genuinely accessible originals, and replace the Housman–Clark priority question with the forest-relevant monotonicity/projection comparison.
