21: \begin{lemma}[Fixed-vector Chernoff bound]\label{ext:lem:chernoff}
22: Let independent bits have $\Prob(B_i=0)=q\in(0,1)$ and put
23: $\alpha=-\log(q+(1-q)e^{-\lambda})$ for $\lambda>0$.
24: For every fixed $\delta\in[0,1]^n$,
25: \[
26:  \Prob\Bigl(\sum_iB_i\delta_i\le1\Bigr)
27:  \le e^{\lambda-\alpha\sum_i\delta_i}.
28: \]
29: If $\mathcal L\subseteq[0,1]^n$ has at most $H$ elements and a mask-dependent
30: choice $\delta^B\in\mathcal L$ always satisfies
31: $\sum_iB_i\delta_i^B\le1$, then
32: $\E\sum_i\delta_i^B\le(\log H+\lambda+1)/\alpha$.
33: For $q\le1/j$, $\lambda=2\log j$, one has $\alpha\ge\alpha_j$.
34: \end{lemma}
35: \begin{proof}
36: The fixed-vector exponential calculation in \eqref{prod:chernoff} uses only
37: $\delta_i\in[0,1]$ and independence, so applies verbatim.
38: The event $\sum_i\delta_i^B\ge x$ implies that some vector in the fixed
39: list with sum at least $x$ has sampled score at most one.
40: A union bound gives $\min\{1,He^{\lambda-\alpha x}\}$; its integral is
41: $(\log H+\lambda+1)/\alpha$.  Finally
42: $q+(1-q)/j^2\le(j^2+j-1)/j^3$ when $q\le1/j$.
43: The list must be fixed before sampling, but may depend on the full profile.
44: \end{proof}

91: \subsection{General concave homogeneous production}
92: \begin{lemma}[Relative supporting forms]\label{ext:lem:support}
93: Assume $f$ is concave.  For $x\ge0$ let $J=\operatorname{supp}(x)$ and
94: $F_J=\{y\ge0:\operatorname{supp}(y)\subseteq J\}$.
95: If $J\ne\emptyset$, the set of $u\in\R_+^J$ satisfying
96: \[
97:  u\cdot x_J=f(x),\qquad f(y)\le u\cdot y_J\quad(y\in F_J)
98: \]
99: is nonempty, compact and convex.  Its unique minimum-norm element $u(x)$
100: therefore defines a choice depending only on $f,x$.
101: Set $\ell_x(y)=u(x)\cdot y_J$ on $F_J$ and $+\infty$ outside it.
102: For $x=0$, instead set $\ell_0(0)=0$, $\ell_0(y)=+\infty$ for $y\ne0$.
103: Then $\ell_x$ is componentwise nondecreasing, majorizes $f$ everywhere,
104: and touches it at $x$.  On its finite face it is an ordinary nonnegative
105: linear form.  No boundary continuity is required.
106: \end{lemma}
107: \begin{proof}
108: On the relative interior of $F_J$, finiteness and concavity supply a finite
109: supergradient $u$ at $x$~\cite{BoydSubgradients}:
110: $f(y)\le f(x)+u\cdot(y_J-x_J)$.
111: Putting $y=\lambda x$ with both $\lambda>1$ and $0<\lambda<1$ yields
112: $u\cdot x_J=f(x)$.  Monotonicity applied to $y=x+te_e$ gives $u_e\ge0$.
113: For any boundary point $y\in F_J$ and $0<\epsilon<1$, the point
114: $y_\epsilon=(1-\epsilon)y+\epsilon x$ is in the relative interior.
115: Concavity and the supporting inequality give
116: \[
117:  (1-\epsilon)f(y)+\epsilon f(x)\le f(y_\epsilon)
118:  \le(1-\epsilon)u\cdot y_J+\epsilon f(x).
119: \]
120: Cancel and divide by $1-\epsilon$ to obtain the boundary inequality.
121: The admissible set is an intersection of closed half-spaces and the touching
122: hyperplane.  It is bounded by $0\le u_e\le f(x)/x_e$, so is compact;
123: strict convexity of squared norm gives the unique choice.
124: The extension majorizes $f$ trivially outside its face.  Increasing an argument
125: within the face increases its nonnegative linear value; leaving the face
126: changes that value to infinity.  The zero-face convention has the same
127: properties.  In particular, no subtraction of infinite quantities is needed.
128: \end{proof}

139: \begin{theorem}[General concave homogeneous production]\label{ext:thm:general}
140: For every finite, monotone, concave, degree-one homogeneous $f$, the rule of
141: Definition~\ref{ext:def:general} is eligible at every real bundle profile and
142: \[
143:  0\le M_i(b)\le T,\qquad
144:  \sum_iM_i(b)\le\min\{n,C_j(2^n)\}T.
145: \]
146: A zero bundle receives zero, and every zero-value profile receives zero.
147: \end{theorem}
148: \begin{proof}
149: Both capped contributions and endpoint marginals belong to $[0,T]$, so the
150: same holds for their mixtures.  If $b_i=0$, then $\ell(0)=0$ and its on/off
151: aggregates coincide.  If $T=0$, monotonicity makes every masked value zero.
152: 
153: For incentives, fix $b_{-i},B_{-i}$ with $B_i=0$.
154: The aggregate $x_B$ and its selected form are independent of the entire own
155: bundle $b_i$.  Both $\ell_{x_B}(b_i)$ and $T$ increase weakly with $b_i$,
156: including finite-to-infinite transitions; their capped minimum is therefore
157: nondecreasing.  The off term of $m_i^q$ is fixed and the on term increases.
158: Averaging with report-independent nonnegative weights proves own-bundle
159: monotonicity on the whole domain.
160: 
161: For coverage, fix a selected form $\ell$ and coalition $S$.
162: If $\ell(b_i)\ge T$ for some $i\in S$, that capped term alone covers
163: $v_b(S)\le T$.  Otherwise all coalition terms are finite, uncapped and on
164: the same face, so their sum is $\ell(\sum_{i\in S}b_i)\ge v_b(S)$.
165: Thus every vector $a(B)$, and hence $z^q$, covers coalitions.
166: For a fixed $B_{-i}$ select $\ell=\ell_x$ at the on aggregate
167: $x=b_i+\sum_{h\ne i}B_hb_h$.  The bundles $b_i,x-b_i$ lie on its finite face.
168: Touching, majorization and finite additivity give
169: \begin{equation}\label{ext:eq:endpoint}
170:  f(x)-f(x-b_i)\ge\ell(x)-\ell(x-b_i)=\ell(b_i),\qquad
171:  0\le\ell(b_i)\le f(x)\le T.
172: \end{equation}
173: The middle term is the uncapped on contribution.  Averaging implies
174: $m_i^q\ge\E[a_i(B)\mid B_i=1]$, hence $r_i^q\ge z_i^q$ and coverage of $M$.

176: For the budget, every open bundle lies on the finite face of $\ell_{x_B}$.
177: Its nonnegative contributions sum to $\ell_{x_B}(x_B)=f(x_B)\le T$,
178: so \eqref{ext:eq:open-sum} holds.  At the fixed profile there are at most
179: $2^n$ capped vectors.  Lemma~\ref{ext:lem:budget} gives the $C_j(2^n)$ bound;
180: $M_i\le T$ gives the other bound.  All endpoint arithmetic above is finite.
181: \end{proof}

215: technologies on $\R_+^d$, and $\mathfrak F_d$ all finite monotone concave
216: homogeneous technologies there.  Define
217: \[
218:  B_n^{\rm own}=\sup_{d\ge1,f\in\mathfrak M_d}\mathfrak b_n(f),\quad
219:  B_n^{\rm conc}=\sup_{d\ge1,f\in\mathfrak F_d}\mathfrak b_n(f),\quad
220:  B_d^{\rm dim}=\sup_{n\ge1,f\in\mathfrak M_d}\mathfrak b_n(f).
221: \]

227: \begin{theorem}[Owner-count range]\label{ext:thm:owner-range}
228: As $n\to\infty$ along all integers,

231:  \le B_n^{\rm conc}\le\min\{n,C_{j_n}(2^n)\}
232:        =(\log2+o(1))\frac n{\log n}.
233: \]
234: The lower bound is a parameter translation of the weighted hierarchy,
235: not a new construction; it permits dimension to grow with $n$.
236: \end{theorem}
237: \begin{proof}
238: The upper follows from Theorem~\ref{ext:thm:general} and
239: $\mathfrak M_d\subseteq\mathfrak F_d$.
240: For the lower, use $f_k(x)=\min_{1\le e\le k}x_e$.
241: Restrict $km$ owners to axis-supported bundles, with $m$ owners per axis.
242: An eligible all-bundle rule restricts to a valid scalar-report multi-glove
243: rule, and its restricted total-payment factor cannot be larger.
244: Thus Lemma~\ref{lem:weighted-hierarchy} applies to $\mathfrak b_{km}(f_k)$.
245: If $n\ge n'$, restricting an $n$-owner rule to zero reports for the last
246: $n-n'$ owners and discarding their nonnegative payments yields an eligible
247: $n'$-owner rule.  All original coalition constraints and unilateral comparisons
248: remain, so $\mathfrak b_n(f)\ge\mathfrak b_{n'}(f)$ without population incentives.

250: For an integer $h\ge2$ set
251: \begin{align*}
252:  R&=\lceil\log(h+1)\rceil, &s&=\lceil\sqrt R\rceil, &m&=hs,\\
253:  \alpha&=1/s, &A&=(h+1)m-h^2, &\lambda&=As,\\
254:  Z&=\sum_{r=0}^h\frac{\lambda^r}{r!}, &K_h&=\lceil2RZ\rceil, &\nu_h&=K_hm.
255: \end{align*}
256: Here $s\ge2$, so $m>h$, and $K_hp_0=K_h/Z\ge2R\ge2\log(h+1)$.
257: Equation~\eqref{eq:weighted-bound}, with hierarchy height $h$, yields
258: \begin{equation}\label{ext:eq:owner-lower-finite}
259:  \mathfrak b_{\nu_h}(f_{K_h})
260:  \ge\frac{h+1-h/s}{(1+1/s)(1+h/(h+1)^2)}=(1-o(1))h.
261: \end{equation}
262: Also $\log\lambda=2\log h+O(\log\log h)$ and $\lambda\ge h$.
263: The terms of $Z$ increase up to index $h$, so
264: $\lambda^h/h!\le Z\le(h+1)\lambda^h/h!$.
265: Stirling's formula gives $\log Z=(1+o(1))h\log h$ and hence
266: $\log\nu_h=(1+o(1))h\log h$.
267: For fixed $0<\epsilon<1$ choose
268: $h=\lfloor(1-\epsilon)\log n/\log\log n\rfloor$.
269: Then $\log\nu_h\le(1-\epsilon+o(1))\log n<\log n$ for all large $n$.
270: Padding to $n$ owners and \eqref{ext:eq:owner-lower-finite} give
271: $B_n^{\rm own}\ge(1-\epsilon-o(1))\log n/\log\log n$.
272: Taking the liminf and then $\epsilon\downarrow0$ proves the all-integer lower.
273: \end{proof}

30: \section{General production: a sharp complexity bound}\label{prod:section}
31: There are $n$ fixed owners and $d$ fixed resource types. Owner $i$ reports
32: $b_i\in\R_+^d$; write $c=\sum_i b_i$. Fix a nonempty finite family
33: $V=\{w^1,\ldots,w^K\}\subseteq\R_+^d$, with a report-independent priority,
34: and define
35: \[
36:  f(c)=\min_{w\in V}w\cdot c,\qquad
37:  v_b(S)=f\left(\sum_{i\in S}b_i\right),\qquad T=f(c).
38: \]
39: A rule is eligible if its payments are nonnegative, cover every coalition,
40: and are componentwise nondecreasing in each owner's whole bundle, with all
41: other reports fixed. These conditions hold on all of $(\R_+^d)^n$.
42: They express truthfulness for binding underreports and payment-only utility;
43: there are no operating costs, overreport, false-name, population-change or
44: coordinated-report guarantees. A factor $C$ bounds \emph{total payment} by
45: $CT$; external subsidy is at most $(C-1)T$. The family $V$ remains fixed
46: across reports. Its size may be exponential in a compact description. All logarithms are natural.

48: \begin{theorem}[General sharp-order upper bound]\label{prod:upper}
49: For every integer $j\ge2$, there exists an eligible deterministic rule with factor
50: \begin{equation}\label{prod:constant}
51:  C_j(K)=j+\frac{\log K+2\log j+1}{\alpha_j},\qquad
52:  \alpha_j=\log\frac{j^3}{j^2+j-1}>0.
53: \end{equation}
54: With $L=\log K$ and
55: $j=\max\{2,\lceil L/[\log(2+L)]^2\rceil\}$, this factor is
56: $(1+o(1))\log K/\log\log K$, uniformly over owner counts, dimensions,
57: technologies and reports. Zero-value profiles receive zero; for $K=1$ the
58: constructed rule is efficient.
59: \end{theorem}

61: \paragraph{The mechanism.}
62: For $q\in(0,1)$ let independent owner bits satisfy $\Prob(B_i=0)=q$.
63: Choose the first minimizer of the \emph{uncapped} objective,
64: \[
65:  W(b,B)=\operatorname*{first\,argmin}_{w\in V}
66:                \sum_i B_i(w\cdot b_i),\qquad
67:  a_i(w,b)=\min\{w\cdot b_i,T\}.
68: \]
69: Only the payment contribution $a_i$ is capped. Put
70: \begin{align}
71:  z_i^q&=\E a_i(W,b),&
72:  p_i^q&=\E[a_i(W,b)\mid B_i=0],\label{prod:p}\\
73:  m_i^q&=\E_{B_{-i}}\left[f\left(b_i+\sum_{h\ne i}B_hb_h\right)
74:                          -f\left(\sum_{h\ne i}B_hb_h\right)\right],
75:  &r_i^q&=qp_i^q+(1-q)m_i^q.\label{prod:hybrid}
76: \end{align}
77: The final payment is the deterministic finite expectation
78: \begin{equation}\label{prod:payment}
79:  M_i(b)=j\int_0^{1/j}r_i^q(b)\,dq.
80: \end{equation}
81: These quantities are finite polynomials in $q$ at a fixed profile; no
82: continuity or generic-position assumption about the reports is required.

84: \begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{prod:upper}]
85: Conditional on $B_i=0$, every score and the selected form are independent of
86: $b_i$. For that fixed nonnegative form, both $w\cdot b_i$ and $T$ are
87: nondecreasing in $b_i$, so their minimum is nondecreasing. In $m_i^q$ the
88: off term is independent of $b_i$ and the on term is nondecreasing. The
89: nonnegative fixed mixtures preserve this property and nonnegativity.
90: 
91: For any $w,S$, if some $i\in S$ has $w\cdot b_i\ge T$, then
92: $\sum_{i\in S}a_i(w,b)\ge T\ge v_b(S)$. Otherwise no term in $S$ is capped,
93: and the sum is $w\cdot\sum_{i\in S}b_i\ge v_b(S)$. Thus every capped form
94: covers coalitions. Fix $B_{-i}$ and let $w_1$ be the on minimizer. The off
95: optimum is at most the off objective of this same form, whence
96: \begin{equation}\label{prod:endpoint}
97:  f\left(b_i+\sum_{h\ne i}B_hb_h\right)-f\left(\sum_{h\ne i}B_hb_h\right)
98:  \ge w_1\cdot b_i=a_i(w_1,b).
99: \end{equation}
100: The equality holds because the contribution is a nonnegative summand of
101: the selected on value, which is at most $T$. Averaging proves $r_i^q\ge z_i^q$,
102: and hence coverage for $r^q$ and $M$.
103: 
104: For the budget define $g(q)=\E f(\sum_iB_ib_i)$, $Z(q)=\sum_i z_i^q$,
105: and $R(q)=\sum_i r_i^q$. Capping leaves every open contribution unchanged:
106: \[
107:  \sum_iB_i a_i(W,b)=f\left(\sum_iB_ib_i\right).
108: \]
109: Consequently $q\sum_i p_i^q=Z-g$. Differentiating the finite mask polynomial
110: gives $\sum_i m_i^q=-g'$, so $R=Z-g-(1-q)g'$. Since $g(0)=T$ and $g\ge0$,
111: for $0<\eta<1$ integration by parts gives the exact identity
112: \begin{equation}\label{prod:integral}
113:  \int_0^\eta R\,dq=\int_0^\eta Z\,dq+T-(1-\eta)g(\eta)
114:                          -2\int_0^\eta g\,dq
115:  \le\int_0^\eta Z\,dq+T.
116: \end{equation}
117: Both the sign and the factor two in this identity matter.
118: 
119: Suppose $T>0$. For each fixed form let
120: $d_i(w)=a_i(w,b)/T\in[0,1]$ and $\rho_w=\sum_i d_i(w)$.
121: The selected form satisfies $\sum_iB_id_i(W)\le1$. For $\lambda>0$ put
122: $\alpha=-\log(q+(1-q)e^{-\lambda})$. The convex function
123: $u\mapsto\log(q+(1-q)e^{-\lambda u})$ lies below its endpoint chord
124: $-\alpha u$ on $[0,1]$. Independence and Markov's inequality give
125: \[
126:  \Prob\left(\sum_iB_id_i(w)\le1\right)
127:  \le e^\lambda\prod_i(q+(1-q)e^{-\lambda d_i(w)})
128:  \le e^{\lambda-\alpha\rho_w}.
129: \]
130: A union over the fixed family and integration of the resulting tail yield
131: \begin{equation}\label{prod:chernoff}
132:  \Prob(\rho_W\ge x)\le\min\{1,K e^{\lambda-\alpha x}\},\qquad
133:  \frac{Z(q)}T\le\frac{\log K+\lambda+1}{\alpha}.
134: \end{equation}
135: Take $\eta=1/j$ and $\lambda=2\log j$. For $q\le1/j$,
136: $q+(1-q)e^{-\lambda}\le(j^2+j-1)/j^3$, so
137: \eqref{prod:integral}--\eqref{prod:chernoff} give \eqref{prod:constant}.
138: For the displayed choice of $j$, as $L\to\infty$,
139: $j\sim L/(\log L)^2$, $\alpha_j\sim\log L$, and
140: $j=o(L/\log L)$, proving the asserted asymptotic.
141: 
142: If $T=0$, all masked production values and capped contributions vanish.
143: If $K=1$, no contribution is capped, $p_i^q=m_i^q=w\cdot b_i$, and
144: $M_i=w\cdot b_i$. Finally each finite-mask term is
145: $q^{n-1-|S|}(1-q)^{|S|}[q a_i(W(b,1_S),b)+(1-q)(v_b(S\cup\{i\})-v_b(S))]$.
146: Its integral is a finite sum with rational coefficients when $j$ is integer.
147: This gives exact rational payments for rational input, without an efficiency
148: claim about enumeration of masks or forms.
149: \end{proof}
