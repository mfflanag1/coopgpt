1. FULL ACTUAL PROFILE
For rho>=3, n=floor(rho), s=rho-n, each productive branch has hardware T/k and
long local total rho*T/k. Its n full local coordinates and optional fractional
coordinate all lie at or below the short total. Thus the capped ratio really is
rho and q=exp(1-rho)/2. Each B owner receives epsilon=qT; the fractional owner
receives s*epsilon. A branch-specific hardware owner's payment is
(1-q)T/(k*m)<=epsilon/k because m>=ceil((1-q)/q). Grid-only owners receive zero
local payment. Hence max z_i=epsilon exactly, including every hardware owner.

The separate zero-local branch contributes hardware 1-T and no local payment,
so H=1 and M=T. Every owner included in the instance has a positive resource:
the B owners also supply productive local resources, the A owners supply active
productive hardware, and both grid reports are positive. The optional owner is
included only when s>0. This is legitimate variation between instances, never
variation of identities within one strategic report domain.

Z=T+epsilon(rho-1)<=CT<2, so both grid reports 1-Z/2 lie strictly between zero
and one. Also epsilon(rho-1)<=cT<1, proving P+M=2-epsilon(rho-1)>1. Thus F=H.
All full opposing accounts are below one and sum to exactly two. The outer
coefficient is c=1/(2e), and the raw sum is exactly C. No approximate equality
or clipped-account replacement is involved in this baseline.

2. ACTUAL DEVIATION, NOT A RESTORED AUXILIARY PROFILE
One B_i removes its entire local bundle on the same k branches and leaves its
hardware a=(1-T)/n unchanged. Other owners, all grids, and all identities remain
unchanged. Each productive capped ratio becomes rho-1>=2, so the local
coefficient becomes e*q. The deviator's local payment is zero. Summing the
remaining actual accounts gives
 Z'=T+e*epsilon(rho-2),
 Delta=epsilon[(e-1)rho-(2e-1)]>0.
The remaining full local accounts e*epsilon satisfy e*epsilon<=T/(2e)<1;
the fractional account is smaller, hardware payments decrease, and the two
grid accounts stay below one. Therefore the ACTUAL new outer sum is 2+Delta
with unchanged hardware total one. It has coefficient c*exp(-Delta).

The exact own raw gain is consequently
 c[a(1-exp(-Delta))-epsilon],
which verifies the source formula directly. Each B_i may select this deviation
separately in its own envelope. Summing the n corresponding payment lower bounds
and truthful payments of all other owners proves equation (4). The different
deviations are not required to occur simultaneously. Its lower bound remains
valid even if the displayed gain is negative at some finite parameters.

3. ALL-EPSILON RATE AND LEADING-COEFFICIENT SCOPE
For each fixed T>0, choosing rho=1+log(T/(2epsilon)) realizes every sufficiently
small positive epsilon exactly. The fractional owner handles nonintegral rho.
With L=log(1/epsilon), rho/L and n/L tend to one, Delta/(epsilon L) tends to
K=e-1, and Delta tends to zero. Thus the EXHIBITED aggregate gain divided by
epsilon L tends to c[(1-T)K-1]. The actual envelope inherits a liminf lower
bound; the source correctly does not assert equality for its actual premium.

At fixed T=1/4 this coefficient is c(3e-7)/4>0, proving a positive rate with
nonvanishing local output. Since the same liminf is bounded below by each
fixed-T coefficient, taking the supremum over T>0 gives c(e-2). This does not
exchange limits or require uniformity in T. Setting k=n grows the productive
branch count; m is finite in each instance and may grow exponentially.

The cited upper bound D=1+(A/2)exp(-B/A), with
A=exp(Kepsilon), B=exp(-Kepsilon[1+L]), expands as
 C+cKepsilon L+O(epsilon).
Indeed B/A=1-Kepsilon(L+2)+O(epsilon^2 L^2), and multiplication by A adds
only an O(epsilon) term. The upper coefficient c(e-1) therefore has the stated
direction. These facts establish Theta(epsilon log(1/epsilon)); they do not
determine the leading constant, prove unrestricted C^2 funding, or lower-bound
the best possible truthful mechanism.
