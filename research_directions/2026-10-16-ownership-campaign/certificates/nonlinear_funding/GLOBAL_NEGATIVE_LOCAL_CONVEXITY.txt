THEOREM
Under the physical-bottleneck, exact-restoration (ER), and opposing-local-supply
(LB) hypotheses of JOINT_RESPONSE_CONVEX_LOCAL_FRONTIER.txt, a negative full-gap
owner's exact envelope is given by ONE globally strictly convex minimization
on its entire branch-price box. No opponent cap enumeration, cap-count bound,
or effective-grid bound is needed for this negative-gap conclusion.

The positive-gap representation still uses separate concave programs for cap
regions; the low-effective-grid theorem bounds their number by four. Zero full
gap remains the direct case E_i=a_i. The theorem does not remove (ER) or (LB).

1. GLOBAL OBJECTIVE
Use the exact branch-price box Q and responses beta_k(q) from the earlier notes.
For a negative full gap, beta_i(q)<=beta_i(full)<a_i everywhere. Define
 w(q)=a_i-beta_i(q)>0,
 C(q)=sum_{k!=i}min(beta_k(q),H),
 S(q)=beta_i(q)+C(q),
                    L(q)=w(q)exp[-S(q)/H].              (1)
Every beta_k for k!=i is affine. Thus C is a continuous, concave, piecewise
affine function. The own response beta_i is smooth and concave on the positive
price box, and w is smooth and convex. The physical-output argument ensures
S(q)>=H at every point. The exact actual-envelope identity is
                    E_i=a_i-(e/2)*min_{q in Q}L(q).     (2)
Every q corresponds to an actual whole local bundle. There are no independent
opponent extrema or relaxed own accounts in (1)-(2).

2. CONVEXITY ACROSS CAP CHANGES
Fix any nonconstant line segment q(t)=q^0+t v in Q, 0<=t<=1. Each opponent's
beta_k(q(t)) is affine in t. It therefore crosses H at most once unless it is
identically H; an identically tied account contributes the constant H. There
are consequently finitely many breakpoints, with simultaneous crossings allowed.

On each open interval between breakpoints, C is affine and the regional
negative-loss theorem applies. With W=diag_j(T_j/q_j), it gives
 d^2 L(q(t))/dt^2
 >=exp[-S/H](1-1/e+w/H) v^T W v>0.                    (3)
All branch prices remain positive. If no branches vary, Q has one point and
there is no nonconstant segment to check.

At a breakpoint, w and w' are continuous, while the concavity of C along the
line implies C'_+<=C'_-. The same jump applies to S', because beta_i is smooth.
The exact first derivative away from the breakpoint is
                 L'=exp(-S/H)[w'-(w/H)S'].
Continuity of all undifferentiated quantities gives
       L'_+-L'_- = -(L/H)(C'_+-C'_-)>=0.                (4)
Thus every cap change produces an UPWARD derivative jump for the minimized
loss. Simultaneous crossings simply combine their nonpositive jumps in C'.
Persistent equalities have no jump and were already accounted for above.

Equations (3)-(4) show that L restricted to any line in Q has a nondecreasing
derivative, and a strictly increasing derivative between its finitely many
breakpoints. It is therefore convex, and strictly convex on every nonconstant
segment. Hence L is globally strictly convex on Q, not merely separately convex
within the cap regions. This establishes the theorem.

3. ATTAINMENT, UNIQUENESS, AND AN EXPLICIT SUBGRADIENT
The box Q is compact, nonempty, and bounded away from zero price coordinates.
The continuous L therefore attains its minimum, and strict convexity gives a
unique minimizing price vector. Canonical report recovery t_j=-T_j log(q_j/q0_j)
is feasible and exactly realizes (2). Original reports above a local cap can
remain redundant; uniqueness here concerns the price vector.

An explicit subgradient makes the nonsmooth convex program concrete. Let
 X=grad w, and for each actual opponent select
 theta_k=1 if beta_k(q)<H,
 theta_k=0 if beta_k(q)>H,
 any theta_k in [0,1] if beta_k(q)=H.
Then V=sum_{k!=i}theta_k grad beta_k is a supergradient of the concave C, and
       g=exp(-S/H)[(1+w/H)X-(w/H)V]                    (5)
is a subgradient of L on Q. To verify the direction of this statement, the
directional derivative of C is the minimum of V^T v over its supergradients.
Substituting it in the formula for L' makes the directional derivative of L
the maximum of g^T v over (5), as required for the globally convex function.
This also handles several simultaneous cap equalities without enumerating
their regions.

Evaluating (1) and (5) uses the actual affine opponent accounts, the concave own
response, and a scan of the cap comparisons. The representation is one convex
program in the number of variable branches, even with unbounded opponent count.
This is not a claim of polynomial bit complexity for its transcendental inputs,
or that an approximate numerical minimizer automatically preserves exact
truthfulness. The earlier fixed-lattice certified construction addresses a
different additive-approximation guarantee. Local pool switches and unjustified
hardware restoration remain outside the present structural theorem.
