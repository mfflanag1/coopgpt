MONOPOLY HARDWARE: EXACT COORDINATE RESTORATION AND ITS BOUNDARY
16 October 2026. Original symmetric exponential nested rule N and its actual
whole-owner envelope E. Counts of owners and branches are arbitrary and finite.
This is a structural calculation for this particular rule, not a priority claim.

RESULT
Fix an actual owner a, and suppose every hardware coordinate of every other
owner is zero. Owner a may also hold arbitrary local resources and grid supply;
all other owners may hold arbitrary local resources and grid supply. Then:

 (i) Increasing any hardware coordinate of a, with all other reports fixed,
     makes its raw nested payment N_a nondecreasing.
 (ii) Consequently its envelope can restore all hardware and grid coordinates
      to their full reports EXACTLY, without an upper-relaxation loss:
      E_a(h,l,p;b_-a)
          = max_{0<=x<=l} N_a(h,x,p;b_-a).              (1)
      Every candidate on the right remains a report by the same actual owner.
      In particular, if a's local reports are all zero, E_a=N_a, even when
      a holds grid supply and other owners hold arbitrary local/grid bundles.
 (iii) The corresponding assertion for a's local coordinates is false. There
       is an explicit unbounded family with every branch productive in which
       lowering its own local reports strictly increases N_a.

The theorem applies either to fixed eligibility giving a monopoly of hardware
on the entire domain, or pointwise whenever opponents' hardware reports are
zero. It does not assert hardware restoration for other ownership patterns.

1. A SOLE HARDWARE OWNER: ALL LOCAL PAYMENTS INCREASE WITH HARDWARE
Consider one branch with hardware h held entirely by a and fixed nonnegative
local reports l_k. Set L=sum_k l_k. The branch primitive is denoted Phi.
If h>=L, local resources are the short pool and the sole long hardware account
has capped total L. Its coefficient is exactly 1/2, and
 Phi_a=(L+l_a)/2,           Phi_k=l_k/2 for k!=a.        (2)
These quantities are independent of h. If 0<h<L, put
 d_k=min(l_k,h), rho=sum_k d_k/h>=1,
 q=exp(1-rho)/2.
Now hardware is short, and
 Phi_a=h-q(h-d_a),          Phi_k=q*d_k for k!=a.       (3)
On a cap cell let m be the number of k with l_k>h (the derivative convention
at equality is immaterial; continuity is checked below). As h increases,
 d_k'=1 on those m accounts and zero otherwise, and
                  q'=q(rho-m)/h>=0.                   (4)
Indeed rho-m is the sum of uncapped local shares l_k/h. Every Phi_k with k!=a
is therefore nondecreasing. If l_a>=h, Phi_a=h. If l_a<h, differentiating gives
 Phi_a'=1-q-q(rho-m)(1-l_a/h)
       >=1-q(1+rho)>=0.                                (5)
For the last inequality, (1+rho)exp(1-rho)/2 decreases on rho>=1 and equals
one at rho=1. This proves nonnegative derivatives on every cap cell.

Cap formulas agree at l_k=h. At h=L all local accounts are uncapped, rho=1,
q=1/2, and (3) agrees with (2). At h=0 all payments are zero, and (3) tends
to zero: each capped account is at most h, and the owner count is finite.
There are finitely many breakpoints h=l_k and h=L. Thus EVERY branch payment
is globally nondecreasing in the sole owner's hardware report, including all
zeros and equalities. In particular this property concerns opponents' local
payments too; it is stronger than the primitive's usual own-coordinate IC.

2. THE SOLE HARDWARE OWNER'S OUTER PAYMENT INCREASES IN EVERY ACCOUNT
Consider the same primitive with total hardware H entirely assigned to a,
and arbitrary opposing accounts b_k>=0, B=sum_k b_k. Its payment to a is
 W_a(H,b)=(B+b_a)/2                              if B<=H,
 W_a(H,b)=H-q(H-min(b_a,H)),
   q=exp(1-sum_k min(b_k,H)/H)/2                 if B>=H>0.  (6)
At H=0 it is zero. By Section 1 it is nondecreasing in H with b fixed.
It is also coordinatewise nondecreasing in EVERY b_k, not only b_a.
To see this, on B<H its derivative in b_a is one, and in any other b_k is
1/2. On B>H, if b_a>=H the payment is H and cannot vary with any account.
If b_a<H, its derivative in its own account is
                 partial W_a/partial b_a=q(2-b_a/H)>0. (7)
For k!=a an uncapped account b_k<H has derivative
                 partial W_a/partial b_k=q(1-b_a/H)>=0, (8)
and a capped account has derivative zero. The two orientation formulas agree
at B=H; cap formulas agree at b_k=H. Continuity at zero is immediate, or follows
from the primitive's output bound. Varying one coordinate crosses finitely
many such cells, so these derivatives prove global coordinatewise monotonicity.

3. COMPOSITION AND EXACT HARDWARE RESTORATION
Increase a single h_aj by a nonnegative amount, holding all other reports fixed.
Section 1 says every recipient's local payment on branch j weakly increases;
other branches are unchanged. Therefore EVERY aggregate local account z_k
weakly increases, as does the total outer hardware H. All grid reports p_k
are fixed, so every outer opposing account b_k=p_k+z_k weakly increases.
Section 2 then gives the claimed N_a monotonicity. Increasing several hardware
coordinates is a succession of these comparisons, including from zero.

Take any actual whole-bundle underreport (h',l',p') by a. Restore its hardware
one coordinate at a time; the preceding argument applies with the underreported
l' and the opponents fixed, so it weakly increases N_a. Restore p' to p next;
only its own outer account rises, so Section 2 again gives weak increase. Hence
 N_a(h',l',p';b_-a)<=N_a(h,l',p;b_-a).
The right-hand candidate remains an actual allowed underreport. Taking maxima
proves (1). The maxima exist by continuity of the raw rule on a compact report
box; equivalently one can state both sides as suprema without this fact.

This removes hardware variables, and the grid variable, from this particular
owner's exact optimization. It neither merges owners nor replaces the other
owners' responses by independent minima. There is no new funding bound here.

4. LOCAL-REPORT COUNTERFAMILY WITH GROWING COUNTS
For every integer r>=1 take r regular branches and one anchor branch. One actual
owner a holds ALL hardware: 1/r on each regular branch and 1 on the anchor.
On every regular branch it holds local resource 1/r at the full profile.
That branch additionally has three distinct local-only owners, each holding
1/r there and no resource elsewhere. These triples are disjoint across branches.
On the anchor, a alone holds local resource 1/2. All grid reports are zero.
Thus there are 3r+1 active actual owners and r+1 productive branches, with
                     H=2, M=3/2, F=3/2.

Let a report local s/r on each regular branch, with 0<=s<=1, retaining ALL
hardware and its anchor local resource. The regular branches remain hardware
short and have capped local ratio rho=3+s and q(s)=exp(-2-s)/2. Summing their
local outputs and a's own local payment, and adding the anchor's payment 1/2,
gives
 Z(s)=3/2+q(s)(2+s),
 z_a(s)=3/2-q(s)(1-s).                                (9)
For the entire interval, Z(s)<2: q(s)(2+s) is decreasing for s>=0 and at zero
equals exp(-2)<1/2. Thus the OUTER short pool is always opposing power, and
the sole long hardware owner has coefficient 1/2. Its raw payment is exactly
 N_a(s)=[Z(s)+z_a(s)]/2
       =3/2+(1+2s)exp(-2-s)/4.                        (10)
In particular,
 N_a(1)=3/2+3/(4e^3),
 N_a(1/2)=3/2+1/(2e^(5/2)),
 N_a(1/2)-N_a(1)=(2 sqrt(e)-3)/(4e^3)>0.             (11)
Strict positivity follows already from e>1+1+1/2>9/4. All owners and branches
remain active at both profiles, and only a's permitted local coordinates were
lowered. No owner, eligibility pattern, or branch is created by the deviation.
Equation (10) even shows the best report along this diagonal is s=1/2, since
its derivative has the sign of 1-2s. This last observation is not a claim that
the entire multivariate local-report envelope has been solved.

5. EXACT SIGN PATTERN FOR A NONHARDWARE OWNER'S LOCAL DEVIATION
Let j!=a reduce any or all of its local coordinates, with other reports fixed.
Then its own aggregate local payment z_j weakly decreases; z_a also weakly
decreases; and EVERY z_k for k not in {a,j} weakly increases. Grid reports have
no effect on these local quantities, so the same statement applies to the
local component of any whole-bundle underreport by j.

On a branch with L<=h, formula (2) shows that the other nonhardware payments
are constant, z_j=l_j/2, and z_a=(L+l_a)/2. On a branch with L>=h, lowering l_j
weakly lowers its cap d_j and hence weakly increases q. All other nonhardware
payments q*d_k increase, and a's payment h-q(h-d_a) decreases. The own payment
q*d_j increases as a function of l_j: its derivative is q(1-l_j/h)>=0 when
l_j<h and zero when l_j>h. The formulas agree at both cap and pool boundaries.
Summing over branches proves the claim, including simultaneous local reductions.

Thus a nonhardware owner's external positive loss has only ONE possible
recipient, a, even when the numbers of other owners and branches are unbounded.
For exact envelope analysis, the other nonhardware owners' full local accounts
are valid coordinatewise lower bounds throughout its deviation. Their responses
are still jointly constrained; this does not assert an exact independent-floor
optimizer or merge their fixed actual identities.

6. A SHARP FUNDING CONSEQUENCE IN A DOMINANCE CLASS
Suppose additionally
                    p_a+m({a})>=H,
where m({a})=sum_j min(h_aj,l_aj) is the sole hardware owner's standalone
local production at the full profile. Then E_k=N_k for EVERY owner k, and
                         sum_k E_k<=C F,
where C=1+1/(2e) and F=H. This bound is sharp for the original rule in this
class; it is not an optimal-mechanism lower bound.

First, branch coalition coverage gives z_a>=m({a}), and the same inequality
holds during any OTHER owner's underreport, since a's resources stay fixed.
Consequently a's outer opposing account stays capped at H during each such
deviation. At the full profile a receives exactly H; no own underreport can
yield more than its underreported hardware H'<=H. Hence E_a=N_a=H.

For an owner j!=a, restore its grid supply (only its own outer account rises).
By Section 5 its own local account weakly decreases under its remaining local
deviation, and every other nonhardware local account weakly increases. Set
d_k=min(p_k+z_k,H). The hardware owner's cap stays equal to H. The outer rule
therefore always has hardware as a short pool (possibly tied), and
 N_j= d_j exp(-sum_{k!=a}d_k/H)/2.
For fixed other caps this is nondecreasing in d_j in [0,H], since the derivative
of x exp(-x/H) has the sign of 1-x/H. It is nonincreasing in every other cap.
The sign pattern therefore implies that j's deviation cannot improve its
payment. This proves E=N, allowing ALL actual own underreports, including ones
that leave the displayed dominance class when a is the deviator.

If H>0, put r=sum_{k!=a}d_k/H. Then
 sum_k E_k=H[1+r exp(-r)/2]<=H[1+1/(2e)]=C F.
At H=0 neither local nor outer production is possible and all payments are zero,
so the same statement holds. To attain the bound with both counts growing,
take r0 productive branches, each with hardware and local 1/r0 owned by a,
grid p_a=1, and r0 additional actual owners each with grid 1/r0. Then H=M=1,
the other capped accounts sum to one, and total payments equal C exactly.

This extra condition makes a able to operate all hardware using its own local
and grid resources. Accordingly it is a dominance-class consequence, not a
resolution of the unrestricted C^2 problem or an optimal-subsidy lower bound.

7. INTERPRETATION
In this unbounded ownership class the monopoly owner's hardware and grid
reports may be restored exactly. The remaining local problem has real incentive
content: already the explicit family above defeats own local monotonicity.
This distinction avoids importing a local coordinate restoration into a place
where it would be false. Other owners' harmful local responses concentrate on
the monopoly hardware recipient, a useful further restriction on the remaining
optimization. The general C^2 funding question is unchanged.
