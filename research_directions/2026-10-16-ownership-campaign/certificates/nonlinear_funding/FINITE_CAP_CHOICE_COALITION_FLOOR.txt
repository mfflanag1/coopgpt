FINITE CAP-CHOICE COALITION FLOORS
16 October 2026. Constant-dimensional coverage relaxation for S<2.
Uses THREE_CAP_RECIPIENT_REDUCTION.txt. No global funding conclusion.

1. FIXED ACTUAL RECIPIENTS AND COALITIONS
Normalize full hardware H=1 and assume the full capped opposing total S<2.
Use the common ceilings U_k, baseline pi_k=min(p_k,1), and exceptional actual
recipient set J from THREE_CAP_RECIPIENT_REDUCTION.txt, so |J|<=3. Fix actual
deviator i. Let Q=J\{i}, O=N\(Q union {i}), and q_k=1-pi_k for k in Q.
For coalitions A excluding i, write m(A)=sum_j min(H_j(A),L_j(A)). All these
coalitions retain their full resource reports while i underreports.

At every i-underreport, write y_k=z_k^i. Every k in O is either uncapped at all
such reports or has y_k=0. Thus the opponents' capped total is exactly
 pi_{-i}+sum_{k in O}y_k+sum_{k in Q}min(y_k,q_k)
 =pi_{-i}+min_{A subset Q}
       [sum_{k in O union A}y_k+sum_{k in Q\A}q_k].    (1)
This is merely the distributive identity for a finite sum of minima. It does
not presume that the minimizing cap pattern is the same at different reports.

2. DIRECT CAP-CHOICE COVERAGE FLOOR
Local coverage applied to O union A in (1) proves the uniform floor
 R_i^choice=pi_{-i}+min_{A subset Q}
             [m(O union A)+sum_{k in Q\A}q_k].         (2)
At most eight actual coalition values are required. Unlike replacing the entire
opponents' account by one cap at one, (2) keeps each exceptional recipient's
own headroom q_k and allows the ordinary coalition to contribute above one.

For one exceptional opponent k this is
 R_i^choice=pi_{-i}+min{M_{-i},m(O)+1-pi_k}.            (3)
The floor need not dominate the weighted layer-cake floor; take their maximum.
Neither the cap-choice identity nor this elementary coverage implication is
presented as a historical-priority claim.

3. RETAINING THE EXCEPTIONAL PAYMENT CEILINGS
For any A subset Q and B subset Q\A, coverage and y_k<=U_k imply
 sum_{k in O union A}y_k
 >=m(O union A union B)-sum_{k in B}U_k.
Consequently the stronger, still explicit floor is
 R_i^upper=pi_{-i}+min_{A subset Q}
   [sum_{k in Q\A}q_k+
      max_{B subset Q\A}{m(O union A union B)-sum_{k in B}U_k}].  (4)
The inner maximum includes B empty. Therefore (4)>=(2). It requires the same
at most eight coalition values and at most 3^|Q|<=27 scalar combinations.
For one exceptional opponent k, (4) simplifies to
 R_i^upper=pi_{-i}+min{M_{-i},
       q_k+max(m(O),M_{-i}-U_k)}.                      (5)

4. A CONSTANT-DIMENSIONAL LINEAR-PROGRAM FORM
A stronger floor follows by retaining all union-of-groups coalition inequalities.
Introduce variables v_0 and (v_k:k in Q). The actual vector
v_0=sum_{k in O}y_k, v_k=y_k is feasible for the polytope P_i defined by
 0<=v_0<=sum_{k in O}U_k,  0<=v_k<=U_k (k in Q),
 sum_{k in A}v_k>=m(A)                    (A subset Q),
 v_0+sum_{k in A}v_k>=m(O union A)         (A subset Q). (6)
The constraints for the empty coalition are harmless. If O is empty then its
upper bound sets v_0=0. Feasibility follows from the actual full local payment
vector, so neither an infeasible LP nor a +infinity convention is needed.

Define
 R_i^LP=pi_{-i}+min_{A subset Q}
   [sum_{k in Q\A}q_k+
     min_{v in P_i}(v_0+sum_{k in A}v_k)].             (7)
Every counterprofile vector lies in P_i; (1) therefore proves its opposing
capped total is at least (7). There are at most eight linear programs, each in
at most four variables, with at most sixteen coalition inequalities plus box
bounds. The outer minimum over cap choices is essential: minimizing a concave
sum of capped coordinates is not equivalent to a single ordinary LP.

This LP floor dominates both (4) and the weighted layer-cake floor based on
these U_k. The first dominance follows because every inequality used in (4)
is a consequence of (6) and its upper bounds. For the second, every v in P_i
satisfies min(v_k,q_k)>=w_k v_k on 0<=v_k<=U_k, where
w_k=min(1,q_k/U_k), and the weighted sum of the nested coalition constraints
in (6) gives exactly the layer-cake floor. Its ordinary-group weight is one.
Thus the concave objective at every v in P_i is at least that weighted floor;
taking its minimum, equivalently (7), proves the claim.

The variable v_0 is an accounting sum of payments to a fixed actual coalition.
It is not a merged owner, is never assigned a report, and has no incentive
constraint. We retain all actual-owner deviations when invoking this floor.
We also do not assert that (6) contains every implied projection of all possible
actual coalition inequalities. Ignoring some constraints only lowers the floor.

5. CONSEQUENCE FOR THE ACTUAL ENVELOPE AND COMPUTATION
For a full positive-gap recipient i, g_i=d_i-H_i>0, the established restoration
argument now yields
                E_i<=H_i+g_i k(d_i+R_i^LP).             (8)
Alternatively keep the floor inside the own-loss scalar bound
             t_i(x)>=d_i-x+R_i^LP.
Formula (8) is a pointwise upper bound, not an exact envelope formula. The
largest of this floor and independent coalition/response floors is also valid.

All m(A) and m(O union A), for A subset Q, use original actual coalitions and
can be evaluated from branch totals and the at most three exceptional owners'
resource vectors. After O(n*m) aggregation, the arithmetic work for each i is
O(m) plus a bounded-dimensional LP calculation. The numbers U_k can contain
exponentials. These statements are finite-dimensional real-arithmetic reductions,
not a rational bit-complexity theorem or a new method for computing the true E.

The result is uniform in owner and branch counts. It resolves the unbounded
number of possible cap-sensitive recipients in the S<2 frontier, but does not
yet bound the sum of (8) by C^2. That funding inequality remains a separate gate.
