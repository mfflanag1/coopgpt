TWO INDEPENDENT RESPONSE BRANCHES IMPROVE THE GLOBAL ORDER-EPSILON LOWER
16 October 2026. Fixed actual owners; original exponential nested envelope.
Independent construction check of the root coordinator's proposed extension.

Let c=1/(2e), C=1+c, K=e-1, L=log(1/epsilon). Let W(epsilon) be the
supremum of sum_i E_i/H over physical hardware-bottleneck profiles with
max_i z_i/H<=epsilon, where E is the ACTUAL own-report envelope of the original
symmetric exponential nested rule. Counts and real reports are unrestricted.

Put v=1/5 and define the fixed positive constant

 B=(1-v)[(1-q_new)/q-1]
  =(4/5)[2exp(1/5)-exp(-19/5)-1],
 q=exp(-1/5)/2, q_new=exp(-4)/2.                         (1)

THEOREM. For every sufficiently small positive epsilon there is a finite
fixed-owner instance whose actual envelope funding is at least

 C+cK epsilon(L-log L)
   +c[2B-2e-K log 2]epsilon+o(epsilon).                  (2)

Consequently the corresponding centered liminf for W is at least
c[2B-2e-K log 2]. Because 2B>K, this strictly improves the earlier one-capped
response lower coefficient -c[e+1+K log 2]. It does not determine the optimal
order-epsilon coefficient, nor any lower bound against all possible mechanisms.
The productive branch count and active owner count both grow without bound.

1. THE EXACT RESPONSE BRANCH
Take hardware total t=epsilon/q=2exp(1/5)epsilon. Five separate actual owners
each hold hardware t/5 and no local supply or grid. A distinguished actual
local/grid owner J holds local resource t. One other actual owner D holds local
resource vt=t/5 and no hardware or grid. The full short side is hardware t,
and the capped long ratio is 1+v, giving coefficient q. Thus

 z_J=epsilon, z_D=v epsilon,
 each hardware local payment=(1-q)t/5,
 Z_tiny=t+v epsilon.                                   (3)

The elementary bound exp(1/5)<5/4 gives t<(5/2)epsilon. In particular every
hardware local payment is less than (3/10)epsilon. All full local payments on
this branch are at most epsilon.

Let J delete its entire local report, retaining its grid. The remaining local
total is vt=t/5, and every hardware fragment equals that short total. The new
capped long ratio is exactly 5, so q_new in (1) applies, including at these
hardware cap equalities. The new payments are

 z'_J=0, z'_D=(1-q_new)vt,
 each hardware local payment=q_new t/5.

The old minus new TOTAL local payment is
 (1-v)(1-q_new)t+v epsilon=(1+B)epsilon.                 (4)
After subtracting J's own epsilon loss, the sum of the OTHER recipients'
local-payment losses, with gains counted negatively, is exactly B epsilon.
This is a net response identity, not a sum of positive losses. Every changed
local account is below epsilon: z'_D<t/5<epsilon/2, and each hardware account
is below t/10<epsilon/4. This guarantees outer uncappedness in the uses below.

2. CORE, TWO DISJOINT TINY BRANCHES, AND FIXED HARDWARE
Take T=1/L and
 rho=1+log(T/(2epsilon))=L-log L+1-log 2,
 q_core=epsilon/T.
For sufficiently small epsilon, rho>=3. Choose k=floor L productive core
branches, each of hardware T/k. Choose finitely many actual local owners
B_j with coefficients d_j in (0,1], sum_j d_j=rho, and d_1=1: floor(rho)
full coefficients and, if nonzero, one fractional remainder suffice. Owner
B_j holds local resource d_j T/k on every core branch and no grid.

Fragment each core branch's hardware among m pure hardware owners, where
 m=max(2,ceil((1-q_core)T/(k epsilon))).
Each fragment holds T/(km), and no other resources. Its full local payment
is at most epsilon. The full core accounts and their sum are exactly

 z_Bj=d_j epsilon,  Z_core=T+epsilon(rho-1).             (5)

Adjoin TWO copies of the tiny branch from Section 1. Their distinguished
owners are two DIFFERENT actual owners G and A; their hardware fragments and
their other local owners are all distinct from one another and from the core.
Give B_1 all hardware
                       a=1-T-2t                        (6)
on an additional branch with zero local supply. This hardware contributes no
local payment; B_1 still has full local payment epsilon. It is usable at the
outer stage with grid. Total hardware is exactly H=1, and physical local
production is M=T+2t. All permissions and actual identities are fixed across
every feasible report of this finite instance.

For example L>=8 makes a>0: t<(5/2)e^-L and
 M<1/8+5/256<1. The nonnegative hardware fragments, positive local coefficients,
and positive grids below make every listed owner active at the full profile.

3. EXACT GRID ACCOUNTING AND BASELINE FUNDING
Write Z=Z_core+2Z_tiny for the entire full local-payment total. Give only G,A
positive grids:
                   p_G=1, p_A=1-Z+epsilon.              (7)
Thus G's full account is 1+epsilon and is capped at 1. A's full account is
                   b_A=p_A+epsilon=1-Z+2epsilon.        (8)
Every other opposing account is its local payment, at most epsilon. For all
sufficiently small epsilon, p_A>0 and 0<b_A<1. Explicitly, for L>=8,
Z<=CM<5/4(1/8+5/256)<1, while Z>=T>2epsilon. These establish both inequalities.

The full uncapped opposing sum is P+Z=2+epsilon; the SOLE overflow is G's
epsilon. Therefore the full outer capped sum is EXACTLY S=2. Also
 P+M=2-epsilon(rho+2v-2)>1,                             (9)
since epsilon rho tends to zero. Consequently the outer hardware side is short,
F=H=1, its coefficient is c, and the full raw nested budget is exactly C.
The maximum full local payment is exactly epsilon, attained by B_1,G,A.

4. THREE SEPARATE FEASIBLE OWN REPORTS
The envelope optimizes each actual owner's own report separately. The following
three reports need not and are not combined into a simultaneous deviation.

(i) B_1 deletes its own local bundle on every core branch, retains hardware a,
and changes nothing on the tiny branches. Each core branch remains hardware
short: rho becomes rho-1>=2 and its coefficient becomes e q_core. Its own
local account becomes zero, all other core local accounts multiply by e, and
core hardware accounts decrease. Their capped-total change is
               Delta=epsilon[K rho-(2e-1)]>0.           (10)
For small epsilon all changed core accounts remain below 1. The grids and tiny
branches are unchanged, so S becomes 2+Delta. B_1's exact raw-payment gain is
               g_1=c[a(1-exp(-Delta))-epsilon].         (11)

(ii) G deletes ONLY its local resource on its own tiny branch, retaining p_G=1.
Its outer capped account stays 1, so its own epsilon local loss does not affect
the outer capped sum. Equation (4) and the uncappedness of the other changed
accounts give S'=2-B epsilon. For small epsilon S'>1. Since G has no hardware,
its exact raw-payment gain is
               g_G=c[exp(B epsilon)-1].                (12)

(iii) A deletes ONLY its local resource on its own tiny branch, retaining p_A.
Its full and changed accounts are both uncapped and positive. Now its own
epsilon loss is included, so S'=2-(1+B)epsilon>1. Its exact raw-payment gain is
               g_A=c[exp((1+B)epsilon)(b_A-epsilon)-b_A]. (13)

All other owners may use their full reports in their own envelope. Hence the
same fixed full profile obeys the exact lower bound
                    sum_i E_i >= C+g_1+g_G+g_A.         (14)
This is a lower bound, not an exact formula for the complete envelope budget.

5. ASYMPTOTIC COEFFICIENT AND STRICT IMPROVEMENT
The core increment is exactly
 Delta=epsilon[K(L-log L-log 2)-e].
Since Delta=O(epsilon L), Delta^2=o(epsilon), and
 T Delta=K epsilon+o(epsilon), equations (6),(11) give
 g_1=cK epsilon(L-log L)-c[2e+K log 2]epsilon+o(epsilon).
The two tiny branches remove only 2t=O(epsilon) from B_1's hardware, causing
an additional loss at most 2ct Delta=O(epsilon^2 L)=o(epsilon).

By (8), b_A tends to 1. Equations (12),(13) give respectively
 g_G=cB epsilon+O(epsilon^2),
 g_A=c[(1+B)b_A-1]epsilon+O(epsilon^2)
    =cB epsilon+o(epsilon).
Substitution in (14) proves (2) for EVERY sufficiently small real epsilon.
Using a fractional fixed core owner avoids integer phase errors. Counts grow
because k=floor L and the number of full core local owners both diverge.

For a simple exact comparison, exp(1/5)>6/5 and exp(19/5)>1+19/5>4. Hence
 B>(4/5)(12/5-1-1/4)=23/25.
The elementary series bound e<11/4 gives K<7/4, and
                 2B>46/25>7/4>K.                      (15)
Thus the coefficient improvement over the previous single-response lower is
the strictly positive constant c(2B-K). No optimization over v is asserted.

All asymptotics concern this mechanism's ACTUAL own-report envelope. They
preserve fixed actual owners and arbitrary permitted reports. The construction
does not establish global coefficient optimality or a universal mechanism lower.
