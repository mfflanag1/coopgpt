AT MOST FOUR EXACT CONVEX PROGRAMS AT LOW EFFECTIVE GRID
16 October 2026. Addendum to JOINT_RESPONSE_CONVEX_LOCAL_FRONTIER.txt.
All hypotheses and actual-owner conventions of that theorem remain in force.
In particular this is an exact local-response representation after justified
hardware restoration, not an independent coalition-floor substitution.

MAIN CONSEQUENCE
Let pi=sum_k min(p_k/H,1), and let c_i be the number of actual opponents whose
affine opposing accounts genuinely cross H on the branch-price box Q. If
                       pi<=51/40,
then c_i<=2, so the exact envelope representation needs at most FOUR convex
programs, regardless of the number of owners or productive branches.
Each program still has one variable per affected branch, plus its own-account
variable. This is a convex representation, not a polynomial bit-complexity or
finite-precision exact-truthfulness claim.

There are admissible families with both counts growing and all four cap regions
nonempty at pi=5/4. Thus the count cannot in general be replaced by one crossing
opponent. This does not show that every algorithm must solve four programs.

1. EXACT OPPONENT COORDINATE CEILINGS
For k!=i, define U_k=max_{q in Q} z_k(q). These are exact coordinate maxima of
the affine responses in the parent theorem. On a variable branch j, write
 T=H_j, h_k=h_kj, d_k=min(l_kj,T) for k!=i,
 R=sum_{k!=i}d_k/T>=1, q_high=exp(1-R)/2,
 q_low=q_high exp(-u_max/T), 0<u_max<=T.
For the signed-part convention x_+=max(x,0), x_-=min(x,0), the exact branch
coordinate maximum is
                U_kj=h_k+q_high(d_k-h_k)_+
                            +q_low(d_k-h_k)_-.         (1)
Each coefficient independently chooses the appropriate endpoint of q_j.
Because distinct branches' q_j coordinates are independently feasible, the
EXACT aggregate maximum is the sum of (1) plus the fixed-branch payments.
Different opponents' maxima need not be simultaneously attained. No argument
below assumes their simultaneous attainment.

Summing (1), discarding its nonpositive last terms, and using h_i>=0 gives
 sum_{k!=i}U_kj
   <=sum_{k!=i}h_k+q_high sum_{k!=i}d_k
   <=T+(T/2)R exp(1-R)<=3T/2.                         (2)
The last inequality follows from R exp(1-R)<=1 for R>=1.
For a fixed branch let T_j=min(H_j,L_j) be its PHYSICAL output, which need not
equal H_j. Its fixed opponent payments sum to at most C*T_j<3T_j/2, where
C=1+1/(2e). Zero-output fixed branches contribute zero. The variable branches
have physical output H_j throughout. Therefore
                         sum_{k!=i}U_k<=3M/2.          (3)
This treats nonvariable branches and their possible opposite pool orientation
explicitly; replacing their physical output by hardware would be unnecessary.

2. CAP COUNT AND THE FOUR-PROGRAM CONSEQUENCE
For a genuinely crossing opponent k,
        min_Q[p_k+z_k(q)]<H<max_Q[p_k+z_k(q)].
Nonnegative local payments imply p_k<H, and (1) implies p_k+U_k>H. If the
crossing set is nonempty, summing these STRICT inequalities gives
 c_i < sum_crossing p_k/H + sum_crossing U_k/H
     <= pi + 3M/(2H).                                 (4)
For the empty set c_i=0 directly; there is no need to assert a strict bound by
zero in a degenerate profile. In the physical hardware-bottleneck model M<=H.
Thus pi<=51/40 gives
                  c_i<51/40+3/2=111/40<3,
and the integer c_i is at most two. The parent theorem's enumeration then has
at most 2^2=4 programs. Cap equalities alone do not create a crossing opponent;
constant or one-sided equality accounts can use the fixed assignment there.

3. SHARPER, OPTIMAL AGGREGATE-CEILING CONSTANT
There is in fact a sharp refinement of (3):
       sum_{k!=i}U_k <= D*M,
                 D=1+exp(-1/e)/2 <3/2.                (5)
On a variable branch let
 A=sum_{k!=i}(d_k-h_k)_+, B=-sum_{k!=i}(d_k-h_k)_-.
Then A-B=R*T-(T-h_i) and B<=T-h_i. Also q_low>=q_high/e. Consequently
 sum_{k!=i}U_kj=T-h_i+q_high*A-q_low*B
 <=T-h_i+q_high[(R-1)T+h_i]+q_high(1-1/e)B
 <=T[1+q_high(R-1/e)]-h_i[1-q_high/e]
 <=T[1+q_high(R-1/e)].                                (6)
The scalar function exp(1-R)(R-1/e)/2 on R>=1 has derivative with the sign
of 1+1/e-R. Its maximum at R=1+1/e is exp(-1/e)/2. This proves the variable
branch bound in (5). Fixed branches obey the same bound because C<D: indeed
exp(-1/e)>exp(-1)=1/e. Summing physical branch outputs proves (5).

Thus the sharper general nonempty-set count is
                         c_i<pi+D*M/H.                 (7)
For example pi<=3-D, together with M<=H, still guarantees at most two crossings.
The rational threshold 51/40 in the main consequence needs only the simpler
proof (2)-(4); it does not depend on evaluating D numerically.

SHARPNESS OF (5), WITH GROWING COUNTS.
For every r>=1, take r branches with T=1/r. Owner i has no hardware, and holds
local T on every branch. On branch j add three distinct actual opponents:
one hardware-only owner with hardware T, one local-only owner with local T,
and one local-only owner with local T/e. Use different opponents on every
branch, and no grid reports. Then R=1+1/e, q_high=exp(-1/e)/2 and
q_low=q_high/e. The hardware opponent's maximum is T(1-q_low); the two local
opponents' maxima sum to R*T*q_high. Their total coordinate maxima equal
T[1+q_high]=D*T. Summing branches attains (5), with H=M=1, r productive branches,
and 3r+1 active actual owners. The maximizing reports differ between opponent
coordinates, as allowed in the definition of their individual maxima.
All parent-theorem hypotheses hold for i: it has no hardware, its full outer
gap is positive, and its opponents alone supply each branch's hardware.
This sharpness statement uses the full real report domain; rational capacities
can approach it continuously. It is not a subsidy lower bound.

4. TWO CROSSINGS AND FOUR NONEMPTY REGIONS REALLY OCCUR
For each r>=1, take 2r productive branches in two groups of r. Each branch has
T=1/(2r). Two actual hardware owners A_1,A_2 each hold all hardware on one group,
so their total holdings are 1/2 each. Each A_g has grid 5/8. A distinguished
owner i has local T on EVERY branch and no hardware or grid. Every branch has
one further distinct local-only owner B_gj with local T. There is no other
resource. Thus H=M=1, pi=5/4, and there are 2r+3 active actual owners.

Every variable branch has R=1, q_high=1/2 and q_low=c=1/(2e). The exact account
range of each A_g is
 [5/8+(1/2)(1-1/2), 5/8+(1/2)(1-c)]
                    =[7/8,9/8-c/2].                   (8)
Its lower endpoint is less than one, and the upper endpoint is greater than
one because e>2. All B_gj accounts stay at most T/2<1. Hence EXACTLY the two
hardware owners are genuinely crossing opponents of i.

Within either group, keep i's full local report to make A_g's account exceed
one. Alternatively, report T/2 on every branch of that group; then each price
is exp(-1/2)/2>1/4 since e<4, and A_g's account is strictly less than one.
These choices are independent across the two groups. All four subsets of
{A_1,A_2} are therefore realized as strict cap patterns by actual whole-bundle
underreports. Owner i retains positive local payment in every such report,
so each pattern also gives a feasible positive-gap program, not merely a zero
payment boundary. Every branch remains productive and all identities fixed.

This proves that two-crossing/four-nonempty-region behavior persists with both
owner and productive-branch counts growing. It does not assert four separate
maximizations are information-theoretically necessary, nor resolve the classes
excluded by the parent theorem's exact-restoration, local-supply, or gap
hypotheses.
