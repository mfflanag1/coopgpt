1. HARDWARE COUPLING AND JENSEN DIRECTION
On the actual negative-gap subset, d_i=p_i+z_i<a_i<=1, so z_i+g_i<=a_i.
For a light set with total hardware a and negative mass G, this gives
sum_negative z_i<=a-G. If G>0, W/G is a weighted average of z_i with nonnegative
weights g_i/G<=1. Thus W/G<=epsilon and W/G<=sum_negative z_i<=a-G. The same
actual subset is used throughout; no merged or virtual owner appears.

Because M<=1, b(z)=exp(-Kz[1+log(1/z)]) is a lower bound on b_M(z), hence a
safe, possibly weaker negative saving. It is decreasing and convex on [0,1].
Weighted Jensen therefore LOWER-bounds total saving by G*b(W/G). Using the
upper bound on W/G reduces that lower saving, in the correct direction.
G=0 has zero contribution and needs no ratio convention beyond this endpoint.

The light gap identity G_plus=r-Q_J+G gives the displayed combined correction.
For G<=a-epsilon the final term G[A-b(epsilon)] increases with G. The remaining
range is exactly z=a-G in [0,min(epsilon,a)]. This proves V_epsilon(a) is a
safe maximum over the whole feasible G range; it need not be attained by one
actual report. The case a=0 reduces to the zero tail term.

2. CONCAVITY AND GRID-INDEPENDENT MAXIMUM
I rederived b'=-Klog(1/z)b and b''=(K/z+K^2log^2(1/z))b. Consequently
F''=2b'-(a-z)b'' is strictly negative on the interior for a>0. F'(z) tends to
positive infinity at zero, and F'(a)=b(a)-A<0 when that endpoint is included.
The stated unique-root-or-truncated-endpoint characterization is correct.

For positive epsilon, u=A-1>0. Since V>=F(0)=u*a, the affine tail numerator
u*r+v is nonnegative whenever r>=max(0,d_J-1), using a+a_J=1. Differentiating
exp(-r)(u*r+v) gives the claimed maximum at
max(r_min,1-v/u). At epsilon=0 the response vanishes and the division by u is
unnecessary; the inherited heavy-tail notation takes epsilon>0 for this formula.

3. ONE-EIGHTH REGIME
When every owner satisfies z_i<=1/8, the tail set is all actual owners. Its
hardware a=1 and Q_J=0, so adding the raw sum yields
1+(exp(-r)/2)[A*r+V]. The exact estimates imply F'(1/8)>17/24>0. Concavity
therefore puts the maximum at that endpoint, giving V=(7/8)[A-b(1/8)]<A.
The subsequent maximum over every real r>=0 is at r=1-V/A.

The resulting funding expression increases in A and decreases in b, so the
substitution A<5/4, b>1/2 is valid. Its exponent is exactly
-1/8-(7/8)(1/2)/(5/4)=-19/40. The reciprocal quadratic exponential bound gives
7081/5081<7/5<C^2. The condition is only on the full local payments; no
restriction on the counterprofiles, actual hardware concentration, positive-gap
count, grid overflow or number of branches was introduced.
