TRUTHFULNESS FORCES THE LOW-CONCENTRATION PLATEAU
16 October2026. Independent constructive lane; candidate for parent audit.
No regularity assumption; fixed actual owners in every comparison.

ARCHITECTURE
Use the water level, capped long accounts and product P from
OPTIMAL_SEPARABLE_TILT_THEOREM.txt, but allow ANY real-valued function f on
(0,1], with h(s,t)=1/2+f(t)-f(s). Assume this rule is own-report monotone
on every finite fixed-owner two-input instance. No continuity, measurability,
monotonicity or differentiability of f is assumed. Nonnegative payments and
coalition coverage are not needed for the necessity argument below.

CLAIM
There is a constant c such that f(t)=c for every0<t<=2/3. Adding a constant
to f changes no payment, so all such universally truthful rules can be
normalized to f(t)=0 on(0,2/3]. The value f(0) is never evaluated at positive
production and may be defined as0. Thus the plateau premise in the optimal
separable-tilt theorem is a consequence of truthfulness, rather than an
additional restriction on the architecture.
For the matching lower bound alone, the weaker equality f(1/2)=f(2/3)
already suffices; it is forced using only four fixed pure owners.

1. ALL-ACTIVE PROFILES FORCE LOCAL CONSTANCY.
Fix an integer k>=2. Use two pure short owners with capacities1/2,1/2 and
k pure long owners with capacities d_1,...,d_k, all strictly between
  lambda=3/[2(k+1)] and1.
For k=2 the auxiliary cap1/2 is exactly at the water level; for k>2 it is
strictly active. In both cases the water equation is
  (k+1)lambda=3/2,
and the long total is strictly greater than1. The profile's orientation,
short concentration s=1/2, active caps and water level therefore persist
under sufficiently small own changes of any one long report.
For each pure long owner j,
  y_j=h(1/2,t)*(lambda/(1/2))*lambda^k/product_{ell!=j}d_ell.
The factor following h is positive and independent of j's own cap d_j.
Hence own-report monotonicity is exactly monotonicity of f(t) along that
coordinate change.

The feasible interval of unique-maximum concentrations is
  I_k=(1/k, 1/[1+(k-1)lambda])
     =(1/k, 2(k+1)/(5k-1)).
Indeed, for any t in I_k choose a largest cap L<1 and equal residual caps r
with r/L=(1-t)/[(k-1)t]. The inequalities defining I_k ensure that this
ratio lies in(lambda,1), so L can be chosen close enough to1 to give
lambda<r<L<1. This realizes t at an interior all-active profile.

Hold the residual caps fixed and vary the unique maximum L over a small
open interval. The resulting t=L/(L+(k-1)r) increases continuously and
strictly with L, with an open image around the original t. Every pair of
values in that image is compared by one actual owner's permitted report
increase. Thus f is nondecreasing on that image.
Next hold L and all but one residual cap fixed, and vary that residual
owner's cap over a small open interval. Now t strictly decreases with its
report. The same payment cancellation proves that f is nonincreasing on
an open image around t. On the intersection of the two images, f is both
nondecreasing and nonincreasing, hence constant.
This proves local constancy at every t in I_k. A locally constant function
on an interval is constant there; no prior regularity is required.

2. OVERLAPPING INTERVALS COVER EVERY POSITIVE t BELOW2/3.
For k=2, I_2=(1/2,2/3); for k=3, I_3=(1/3,4/7), which overlaps I_2.
For k>=4, the lower endpoint1/k is below1/3 and the upper endpoint
2(k+1)/(5k-1) is greater than2/5, so I_k overlaps I_3. As k tends to
infinity, the lower endpoints tend to0. Thus the union of the I_k is
(0,2/3), and the constants on all intervals agree. Call the common value c.
Owner counts vary across these independent necessity examples, but every
report comparison keeps its own finite owner set fixed. There is no cloning
operation within an incentive comparison.

3. THE ENDPOINT2/3 IS FORCED BY FINITE COMPARISONS.
Keep two short owners at1/2 each and two long owners at(L,1/2), with
1/2<L<=1. The water level is1/2 and P=1/(2L). The larger long owner's
payment is
  (1/2)[1/2+f(L/(L+1/2))-f(1/2)].
For L<1 its concentration lies in(1/2,2/3), where f=c. Comparing any such
L with L=1 by the SAME owner's report increase gives f(2/3)>=c.

Now fix the largest long cap at1 and increase the other long cap R from
1/2 to any R in(1/2,1). The water level remains1/2, P=1/(4R), and the
changing residual owner's payment is
  (1/4)[1/2+f(1/(1+R))-f(1/2)].
For R>1/2 the concentration lies in(1/2,2/3); at R=1/2 it is2/3.
Own monotonicity therefore gives f(2/3)<=c. Hence f(2/3)=c exactly,
without a limiting or continuity argument.

4. FOUR OWNERS ALREADY FORCE f(1/2)=f(2/3).
This observation makes the architecture funding lower bound finite-owner.
The k=2 argument gives f=c on(1/2,2/3), and Section3 gives f(2/3)=c,
without using any conclusion about f(1/2). To force that remaining value,
start the two long owners at equal reports d,d with1/2<d<1. Increase one
slightly; its payment factor outside h is unchanged and its concentration
moves from1/2 into(1/2,2/3). Its monotonicity gives c>=f(1/2).
Conversely, fix one long cap L in(1/2,1), start the other cap R in(1/2,L),
and increase R to L. Its concentration moves from(1/2,2/3) to1/2 while
its own payment factor outside h again stays fixed. This gives f(1/2)>=c.
Consequently f(1/2)=f(2/3)=c using four fixed pure owners throughout.

COROLLARY: THE EXACT ARCHITECTURE OPTIMUM HAS NO PLATEAU ASSUMPTION.
For an arbitrary universally truthful separable shape f, subtract c=f(1/2).
Payments are unchanged, and the normalized function is zero on(0,2/3].
The regularity-free ceiling proof in Section11 of
OPTIMAL_SEPARABLE_TILT_THEOREM.txt therefore applies directly. Its exact
Gamma is a lower bound for EVERY universally truthful shape in this fixed
water-product/separable-difference architecture. The constructed C1 convex
shape achieves Gamma with all-profile coalition coverage. Thus Gamma is
the exact optimum for the entire separable-difference architecture.
The fixed water-product P and h(s,t)=1/2+f(t)-f(s) remain genuine restrictions;
this is not an unrestricted mechanism lower bound or a priority claim.
