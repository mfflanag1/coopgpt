1. PRECISE CLASS AND SHARP CLAIM
At positive production orient the smaller input pool as x, with total A.
Let M=max_i x_i, d_i=min(z_i,A), D=sum_i d_i, L=max_i d_i, s=M/A and t=L/D.
The actual owners are fixed. Let lambda solve
  min(M,lambda)+sum_i min(d_i,lambda)=A+M,
using lambda=max(M,max_i d_i) when D=A. Set
  P=[min(M,lambda)/M]*product_{i:d_i>0}min(d_i,lambda)/d_i.
A concentration-price rule has the form
  p=h(s,t)P, y_i=(1-p)x_i+p d_i,
for a SINGLE real-valued function h on(0,1]^2. Impose balance consistency
  h(s,t)+h(t,s)=1 for all s,t in(0,1].                 (CP_balance)
This guarantees that the two resource orientations agree at aggregate
balance. Assume own-report monotonicity for all finite fixed-owner instances.
The lower bound below requires no regularity of h, no separability, and no
prior constant-price or low-concentration plateau assumption.

Every such rule has worst-case funding factor at least
  Gamma=1+(-17rho^2+24rho-8)(1+rho)^3/
            [216rho(1-rho)(2rho-1)],                  (CP_optimum)
where rho is the unique root in(2/3,1) of
  68rho^5-235rho^4+308rho^3-199rho^2+64rho-8=0.
The factor is approximately1.12875129848746585421.
A continuous separable rule h(s,t)=1/2+f(t)-f(s), specified in Section5,
is truthful for all mixed owners, nonnegative and coalition-covering, and
attains exactly Gamma. Hence Gamma is the exact optimum for the entire
balance-consistent concentration-price class, not only for separable tilts.

In particular this gives the exact optimum for the EARLIER class in
root/CONCENTRATION_PRICE_OBSTRUCTION.txt: h continuous on(0,1]^2 and
payments continuous across pool ties. That class implies(CP_balance),
as recalled in Section6. Its lower bound1097/972 is strictly improved to
Gamma and matched by a rule belonging to the same continuous class.
This is an architecture theorem, not an unrestricted mechanism lower bound.

2. TRUTHFULNESS FORCES h(1/2,t)=1/2 ON[1/2,2/3].
Write H(t)=h(1/2,t). Use FOUR fixed pure owners: two short capacities1/2,1/2
and two long capacities u,v with1/2<v<u<1. The smaller pool has total1,
the auxiliary cap is1/2, and the water level is1/2. Thus
  P=1/(4uv), t=u/(u+v),
  y_u=H(t)/(4v), y_v=H(t)/(4u).                       (CP_cancel)
When the larger long owner increases u, its outside factor1/(4v) is fixed
and t increases; own monotonicity therefore makes H nondecreasing locally.
When the smaller long owner increases v, its outside factor1/(4u) is fixed
and t decreases; the same condition makes H nonincreasing locally.

Every t in(1/2,2/3) is realized at an interior such profile: choose
  t/[2(1-t)]<u<1, v=u(1-t)/t.
Each own-coordinate map covers an open concentration interval around t.
On their intersection, every pair is compared in both directions, so H is
constant there. It follows that H is locally constant on the connected
interval(1/2,2/3), and therefore constant on that interval. Call the value c.
No derivative or continuity assumption has been used.

The endpoint1/2 is fixed by balance consistency: H(1/2)=1/2. This is also
linked to the interior by finite incentives, rather than assumed continuity.
Start the two long owners at equal capacities d,d with1/2<d<1. Increase
one slightly while keeping both below1. Its payment factor outside H stays
fixed and t moves from1/2 into the interior, so c>=H(1/2)=1/2.
Conversely fix one long cap L in(1/2,1), begin the other at R in(1/2,L), and
increase R to L. Its outside factor again stays fixed while t moves to1/2,
so H(1/2)>=c. Therefore c=1/2.

For the upper endpoint, keep long capacities(L,1/2), with1/2<L<=1.
The water level is1/2, P=1/(2L), and the larger owner's payment is
  (1/2)H(L/(L+1/2)).
Increasing L from any value below1 to1 gives H(2/3)>=1/2. Now keep the
largest long cap fixed at1 and increase the other cap R from1/2 to any
R in(1/2,1). Its payment is(1/4)H(1/(1+R)), giving H(2/3)<=1/2.
Thus H(t)=1/2 on the whole closed interval[1/2,2/3], without taking limits.
Every comparison keeps the same four actual owners and their input roles.

3. A FINITE REPORT COMPARISON GIVES THE GLOBAL PRICE CEILING.
Use the same short capacities1/2,1/2 and long capacities1,R, where
0<R<=1/2. The water level is1-R, the auxiliary cap is inactive, and
P=1-R. The residual long owner's payment is
  Y(R)=H(1/(1+R))R(1-R).
At R=1/2, Section2 gives Y(1/2)=1/8. Its coordinatewise underreport from
1/2 to R therefore satisfies Y(R)<=1/8. Substituting t=1/(1+R) gives
  H(t)<=t^2/[8(1-t)(2t-1)], 2/3<=t<1.                (CP_ceiling)
There is no regularity assumption, integration, asymptotic approximation,
or change of owners in this implication.

Balance consistency now transfers this bound to the opposite slice:
  h(t,1/2)=1-H(t)>=1-t^2/[8(1-t)(2t-1)].             (CP_reverse)
This is the only use of balance consistency beyond H(1/2)=1/2. It is a
material class assumption; the theorem does not silently assert it for
arbitrary discontinuous tie-breaking rules.

6. RELATION TO THE EARLIER CONTINUITY CLASS.
Suppose h is continuous on(0,1]^2 and the payments are continuous across
aggregate pool ties. For any s,t in(0,1], choose finite positive pure-owner
accounts with equal totals1 and maxima s,t. Approaching that balanced
profile from the first scarce orientation pays a pure first-input owner
(1-h(s,t))a_i. Approaching from the reverse scarce orientation pays it
h(t,s)a_i. Positive a_i and continuity force(CP_balance). Thus all assumptions
of the earlier continuous class imply those used for this lower bound.
Conversely the achieving rule is continuous across ties, so the exact
optimum in that earlier class is Gamma as well.

Limits: the fixed water level/product and a common concentration-only scalar
