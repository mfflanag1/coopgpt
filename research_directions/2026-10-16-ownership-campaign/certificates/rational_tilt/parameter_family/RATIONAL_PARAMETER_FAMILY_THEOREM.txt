EXACT TRUTHFUL PARAMETER INTERVAL FOR THE RATIONAL TILT FAMILY
16 October 2026. Independent candidate proof; parent coordinates audit.
This adapts the accepted rational-tilt proof; its water-product rule is unchanged.
Every changed shape inequality and funding maximum is proved below.
No priority claim and no unrestricted optimality claim.

MODEL AND THEOREM.
Fix any finite set of actual owners. Owner i reports nonnegative real
capacities(a_i,b_i), subject to any fixed eligibility restrictions. Reports
bind coalition production v(S)=min(sum_S a_i,sum_S b_i). Utility is payment;
the incentive condition compares every whole-owner coordinatewise underreport.
There are no private costs, identity changes, or additional payment floors.

For every parameter a>0 define f_a below and use the same water-product
rule. The rule covers every coalition and is nonnegative for every a>0.
It is universally own-report monotone over arbitrary finite fixed-owner
instances and all their profiles IF AND ONLY IF
  4/45<=a<=2/3.                                        (PF_interval)
Necessity is for this universal class; a particular restricted eligibility
pattern may exclude the four-owner obstructions.
For a in this interval, its exact sharp funding factor is
  gamma(a)=1+max_{0<s<=1}[(1-2f_a(s))F(s)],
  F(s)=1/8 for s<=1/2, F(s)=(1+s)^3/(54s) for s>=1/2.   (PF_gamma)
The function gamma(a) is strictly increasing on the truthful interval.
Its unique optimum within this one-parameter family is a0=4/45, with
  gamma0=1+(-27s_*^2+77s_*-38)(1+s_*)^3/[54s_*(41s_*-26)],
where s_* is the unique root in(2/3,1) of
  -3321s^4+9122s^3-10019s^2+5092s-988=0.                (PF_optimum)
Numerically s_* is about0.6790666428875705 and gamma0 about1.1288005100532479.
The algebraic definitions are exact; decimal certification is supplementary.
The four-owner concentration profile attains each factor. These are exact
claims about this specified family, not a globally optimal truthful mechanism,
a lower bound for all possible rules, or a historical priority claim.

1. DEFINITION.
Orient the two pools so that A=sum x_i<=sum z_i. If A=0 pay everyone0.
Otherwise set
  M=max_i x_i, d_i=min(z_i,A), D=sum_i d_i, L=max_i d_i,
  s=M/A, t=L/D,
  f(u)=0 for0<=u<=2/3,
  f(u)=f_a(u)=(1/10)v^2/[a+(1-a)v], v=3u-2, for2/3<u<=1,
  h(s,t)=1/2+f(t)-f(s).
Choose the same water level as in the reviewed water-product rule:
  min(M,lambda)+sum_i min(d_i,lambda)=A+M.
When D=A take lambda=max(M,max_i d_i); otherwise the solution below that
maximum is unique. Define
  P=[min(M,lambda)/M]*product_{i:d_i>0}min(d_i,lambda)/d_i,
  p=h(s,t)P, y_i=(1-p)x_i+p d_i.                        (RT_rule)
The auxiliary cap M determines a scalar; it is not an owner or coalition
member. A zero cap contributes product factor1 and is omitted.

For all a>0, f is continuously differentiable, nondecreasing and convex,
with f(2/3)=0 and f(1)=1/10. Thus0<=f<=1/10. In the incentive proof assume
4/45<=a<=2/3. Writing D_a(v)=a+(1-a)v,
  f'(u)=(3/10)v[2a+(1-a)v]/D_a(v)^2,
  f''(u)=9a^2/[5D_a(v)^3] foru>2/3,
  f'(1)=(3/10)(1+a)<=1/2.
Hence0<=f'<=1/2 throughout the truthful interval.
Therefore2/5<=h<=3/5 and0<P<=1, so0<p<1. At equality of the two physical pool totals, D=A,P=1;
swapping the pools exchanges s,t and h(s,t)+h(t,s)=1, so every owner's
payment agrees from both orientations. Payments are continuous across caps,
maxima, water-active boundaries, D=A, concentration thresholds, and balance.
At A->0, the convex-combination formula gives0<=y_i<=A, so zero is continuous.

For any coalition S, its short sum X=sum_S x_i dominates its production.
Its capped long sum also dominates production, since X<=A and
  sum_S min(z_i,A)>=min(sum_S z_i,A)>=min(X,sum_S z_i).
Their convex combination proves coalition coverage and nonnegativity.

2. WATER-CELL IDENTITIES AND TILT BOUNDS.
Normalize the point being differentiated to A=1; this does not hold A fixed
along an own SHORT-coordinate change. Write x=x_i,d=d_i,s=M and q=2/3.
On a D>1 cell the water level is below1. The reviewed water equation gives:
  - for an uncapped changing long coordinate, beta=(log P)' is -1/d when
    d>lambda and -1/lambda when d<lambda;
  - for a short coordinate, let e=1 if its owner is the strict maximum-short
    owner, otherwise e=0, and let k count long reports strictly above A.
    If the auxiliary cap is inactive, beta=1/lambda-k>=0.
    If it is active, beta=(1+e)/lambda-e/s-k and P<=lambda/s.
    In the latter case beta>=1-s/lambda.
These follow by differentiating the water equation and the logarithm of its
active product factors. For example, in the active auxiliary case the water
equation is(m+1)lambda+U=1+s, and its active product derivative gives the
displayed beta; m counts active actual long caps and U their inactive sum.
Long caps at the physical cap1 are active and contribute to k.

If t>2/3, at most one actual long cap is water-active. Indeed, if the largest
cap L and another cap were active, their residual actual total R=D-L would
exceed lambda. The actual water-truncated mass is at least1, so also
lambda+R>=1. Thus R>max(lambda,1-lambda)>=1/2, contradicting
R=L(1-t)/t<L/2<=1/2. If L alone is active, all others are inactive and
lambda+R>=1, hence lambda>=1-R.

Uniformly for4/45<=a<=2/3, the following shape bounds hold:
  f'(u)u(1-u)<=1/9,
  f'(u)u(1-u)<=1/16.                                  (PF_bounds)
The first follows from f'<=1/2 and u(1-u)<=2/9 when u>2/3; both sides of
the derivative expression vanish below that threshold. To prove1/16 put
v=3u-2 in(0,1), D=a+(1-a)v. The expression is
  B(a,v)=v[2a+(1-a)v](v+2)(1-v)/(30D^2).
For fixed v, its a-derivative has the sign of
  v^2-a(2-v)(1-v).
Its unconstrained maximum over positive a is therefore at

and that maximum equals(2-v)^2(v+2)/120. For v>=1/4 this last expression
is decreasing and at most147/2560<1/16. For v<=1/4, a_*(v)<=1/21<4/45;

This proves the uniform bound, including v=0,1 by continuity.
Convexity and the endpoint values also give
  f(t)<=(3/10)(t-2/3), hence1/2+f(t)<=(3/10)(1+t)
for2/3<=t<=1.                                       (PF_secant)

Under a short increase, s'=e-s. If k=0 then t'=0; when f'(t)>0 and k>0,

In particular h'>=-1/6, h'_+<=11/18, and for e=1, h'_+<=1/9.

3. OWN LONG-COORDINATE MONOTONICITY.
Above the physical cap1, changing the long report changes nothing. Below it,
the owner's payment derivative is
  p[1+(d-x)(beta+eta)], eta=h'/h.                       (RT_long)
If the owner is the strict largest long-cap owner, then
  eta=f'(t)t(1-t)/(h d)>=0.
When active, eta*d<=5/18<1. When inactive,
eta=f'(t)(1-t)/(hD)<=5/12<1/lambda. Therefore beta+eta<=0, and the constant
term in(RT_long) is nonnegative: d eta in the active case, or
1-d/lambda+d eta in the inactive case. The coefficient of x is nonnegative.

For a nonmaximum long owner, eta=-f'(t)t/(hD)<=0. If t<=2/3 it is zero and
the original water-product proof applies. Otherwise the dominant-cap lemma
makes this owner inactive. The coefficient of x in(RT_long) is positive,
so it suffices to bound its constant term at x=0.
If L is active, d<=R and R=L(1-t)/t<1/2. Its constant term is at least
  1-R/lambda-f'(t)t(1-t)/h.
It is therefore enough to show
  H(s):=h(s,t)(1-R/lambda)>=f'(t)t(1-t).                (PF_long)
For fixed t,R, the water level is1-R when s<=1-R, and(1+s-R)/2 otherwise.
On the first range H decreases in s, so its minimum is at s=1-R.
On the active-auxiliary range put w=1+s-R. The derivative of H has the sign
of N=-f'(s)w(w-2R)+2Rh. Differentiating on smooth cells gives
  N'=-f''(s)w(w-2R)-2w f'(s)<=0,
since w-2R>=2-4R>=0. N is continuous at s=2/3; hence H can have an
interior maximum but no strict interior minimum. Its minimum over all s
is attained at one of s=1-R and s=1. This minimization is an algebraic
upper-domain comparison; it does not change the actual owner's reports.

At s=1, H>=(2/5)(2-3R)/(2-R)>=2/15>1/16, so(PF_long) holds.
At s=1-R with R<=1/3, H>=(2/5)(1-2R)/(1-R)>=1/5>1/16.
For R>=1/3, f(1-R)=0 and
  H=(1/2+f(t))(1-2R)/(1-R).
Because L<=1, R<=(1-t)/t; the ratio in H decreases with R. Thus it remains
only to prove, with v=3t-2,
  f'(t)t(1-t)<=(1/2+f(t))*3v/(2v+1).                  (PF_critical)
After multiplying positive denominators, this is exactly C(a,v)>=0, where
  C(a,v)=a(45a-4)+(-90a^2+86a-2)v
          +(45a^2-72a+42)v^2+(12-8a)v^3+2(1-a)v^4.   (PF_polynomial)
All coefficients are nonnegative on4/45<=a<=2/3. The constant is
nonnegative by the lower parameter bound. The concave coefficient of v
has endpoint values74/15 and46/3, so is positive throughout. The coefficient
of v^2 has negative discriminant -2376 and positive leading coefficient.
The last two coefficients are nonnegative because a<=2/3<1. This proves
(PF_critical), hence every active nonmaximum-long case.
If L is inactive instead,
d/lambda<=R/L=(1-t)/t<1/2, while d|eta|<=5/18<1/2. This again proves
nonnegativity. Tied maxima and cap endpoints follow by continuity.

4. OWN SHORT-COORDINATE MONOTONICITY: EASY CASES.

6. MAXIMUM SHORT OWNER AND THE BALANCE INEQUALITY.
Now e=1,x=s. If t<=2/3, h<=1/2,h'<=0 and the original inequality
P(1+beta*s)<=2 proves(RT_hard)>=0. Suppose t>2/3. If k=1 then L=1;

The only remaining case is k=0. Here d'=0 and h'=-f'(s)(1-s)<=0. The
expression(RT_short) is affine in its own opposite account d in[0,s],
with the current global quantities fixed. At d=s it is1-p>=0. It suffices
to check d=0 as an algebraic endpoint bound, not a change of actual reports.
If L is inactive, only the auxiliary cap is active, t<s, h<=1/2, and
P(1+beta*s)=2, so the result follows immediately.

Suppose L is active and the auxiliary cap inactive. Then s<=lambda<=L and
P=lambda/L. The d=0 derivative multiplied by L is
  L-h(lambda+s)+lambda*s*f'(s)(1-s).
Replace f(t) by f(L), which only decreases this expression. Its resulting
slope in lambda is at most -1/2+s f'(s)(1-s)<=-7/16<0. Increase lambda to L.
It remains to prove
  (L-s)/2 >= [f(L)-f(s)](L+s)-L s(1-s)f'(s).           (RT_balance)
For s<=2/3 and L>2/3, divide the right side by L-s. With L fixed its
maximum over s<=2/3 is at s=2/3. Writing v=3L-2 and D=a+(1-a)v, this is
  f(L)(L+2/3)/(L-2/3)=v(v+4)/(10D)<=1/2,
since the inequality is equivalent to(v-1)(v+5a)<=0 on[0,1]. If L<=2/3
the right side vanishes. For s>2/3 and L>s put b=1-a>0 and
D_s=a+b(3s-2),D_L=a+b(3L-2). The exact difference identity is
  f(L)-f(s)=[3/(10b)](L-s)[1-a^2/(D_L D_s)].
The right side of(RT_balance), divided by L-s, becomes
  Q(L)=[3/(10b)](L+s)[1-a^2/(D_L D_s)]
        -K L/(L-s), K=s(1-s)f'(s)>=0.
The first term has derivative
  [3/(10b)][1+a^2(3bs+2-3a)/(D_L^2 D_s)]>0;
the second has derivative K s/(L-s)^2>=0. Here2-3a>=0 is essential.
Thus Q(L)<=Q(1). With v=3s-2 and D=a+(1-a)v, simplification gives
  Q(1)=(1+s)[f(1)-f(s)]/(1-s)-s f'(s)

The bracket2a+(1-2a)v=2a(1-v)+v is positive. This formula applies for s<1;
at s=1 there is no L>s to check. At L=s the undivided right side is
nonpositive. This proves(RT_balance), including all endpoints.

Finally suppose both L and the auxiliary cap are active. The d=0 derivative

For the owner with the cap, d=A,d'=1 and its derivative is

Each coordinate segment has finitely many choices of orientation, maximum

9. ALL DOMINANT-LONG PROFILES SATISFY THE SAME FUNDING CURVE.
Suppose t>2/3. At most one actual cap L is active. If it is active, put
R=D-L<L/2. With s,R fixed, the water level does not depend on L:
lambda=1-R for an inactive auxiliary cap, or(1+s-R)/2 for an active one.
The expression
  E=h(s,L/(L+R))*[min(s,lambda)/s]*lambda*(L+R-1)/L
increases with L: both h and1+(R-1)/L increase, since R<1. Raise L to1.
This preserves the water-active pattern and never decreases E.

If no actual cap is active, only the auxiliary cap is active. At fixed D,
transfer mass from other actual caps to a largest cap until it reaches lambda.
This is feasible since D>1>lambda. The water level and product stay fixed,
while t and h increase. Then use the preceding argument to raise that cap
to1 at fixed residual mass R. Thus every dominant-long case reduces to
L=1,0<=R<1/2,t=1/(1+R), without decreasing its scalar funding expression.
These are funding comparisons, not incentive assertions about other owners.

After this reduction the excess is exactly the payment to a pure residual
long owner of capacity R in a profile with one other long owner of capacity1
and short maximum s. To see this without altering any incentive domain,
observe that every residual cap is below the water level, so splitting or
combining their mass changes neither P nor the scalar excess. The formula
for a pure residual owner's payment is pR, which equals the excess because
D-1=R. A finite pure-short profile with total1 and maximum s always exists.

Hold that comparison profile's short reports and unit long cap fixed, and
increase the residual owner's R from its present value to1/2. The long-own
monotonicity already proved in Section3 applies, including any auxiliary
water-level crossing, and proves the scalar excess cannot decrease. The comparison is only a scalar funding bound; the synthetic profile has
its own fixed owner list throughout the R increase. This argument uses an
already established derivative inequality and is not
circular: the incentive proof used no funding assertion. Equivalently, on
an active-auxiliary cell the derivative of the excess has sign
  h(1+s-3R)-R(1+s-R)f'(t)t^2,
which is exactly the pure residual-owner inequality(PF_long).

At R=1/2, t=2/3, so f(t)=0. If s<=1/2, the auxiliary cap is inactive and
the excess is1/8. If s>=1/2, it is
  [1-2f(s)](s+1/2)^2/(16s)<=[1-2f(s)]F(s)=g_a(s).
The comparison follows from
  32(1+s)^3-27(2s+1)^2=(2s-1)^2(8s+5)>=0.
Since max g_a>=1/8, every dominant-long profile has excess at most max g_a.
This funding argument works uniformly for the entire truthful interval.

  partial f_a(s)/partial a=-v^2(1-v)/(10[a+(1-a)v]^2)<0

11. NECESSITY OF THE LOWER PARAMETER ENDPOINT.
Suppose0<a<4/45. Use four fixed pure owners: short reports(1/2,1/2,0,0)
and long reports(0,0,1,R), where R=1/2-u with u>0 small. The residual long
owner has zero short report, and the auxiliary cap is inactive. Its payment is
  Y_a(R)=[1/2+f_a(1/(1+R))]R(1-R).
It has derivative
  Y_a'(R)=[1/2+f_a(t)](1-2R)-R(1-R)f_a'(t)t^2,
  t=1/(1+R).
Since f_a(2/3)=f_a'(2/3)=0 and f_a''(2/3+)=9/(5a), Taylor expansion from
the right (or direct differentiation of the rational expression) gives
  lim_{u->0+} Y_a'(1/2-u)/u=1-4/(45a)<0.
Thus Y_a'<0 on an open interval just below1/2. Choosing two capacities
R_low<R_high within this interval gives Y_a(R_low)>Y_a(R_high): the same
actual owner profits by a coordinatewise underreport. No appeal to finite
sampling is needed. If a is rational the capacities can be chosen rational;
for arbitrary real a the theorem's real report domain permits the same
argument. The threshold is the first coefficient of(PF_polynomial), so the
sufficiency and obstruction meet exactly.

12. NECESSITY OF THE UPPER PARAMETER ENDPOINT.
Suppose a>2/3. The algebraic identity(PF_balance_margin) still holds for
any a>0. Fix any s in(2/3,1). Then2-3a<0 and2a(1-v)+v>0, so Q(1)>1/2.
Take four fixed pure owners with short reports(s,1-s,0,0) and long reports
(0,0,L,1-L), with s<L<1 sufficiently close to1. At full aggregate balance,
P=1 and the pools have common total1. A small decrease of the first short
owner's report makes that pool strictly short, with k=0, one active long
cap L and an inactive auxiliary cap. The owner's derivative from below,
multiplied by L, tends at balance to
  (L-s)/2-[f_a(L)-f_a(s)](L+s)+L s(1-s)f_a'(s).
As L->1, this tends to(1-s)[1/2-Q(1)]<0. Hence it is negative for L close
enough to1. By continuity of the derivative on a sufficiently small
one-sided neighborhood, a small underreport strictly increases the same
owner's payment. Pool orientation agrees at balance, so this is a genuine
whole-rule incentive failure. This proves necessity of a<=2/3.

Sections3--7 prove sufficiency; Sections11--12 prove necessity. The exact
interval(PF_interval) and its best factor(PF_optimum) are therefore proved.
The broader 9/8 lower bound, restrictions of other rule classes, and current
historical novelty remain separate questions.
