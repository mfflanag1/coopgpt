CHECKED ARGUMENTS
1. Short-coordinate stage. Every other short-role contribution increases
as T falls. Each long-role contribution q(T)min(l_k,T) decreases, so their
positive losses really sum to the difference in the aggregate qD; one is
not incorrectly replacing a sum of positive parts by an aggregate loss.
On a cap cell, r'= (m-r)/T and q'=q(r-m)/T. Thus
(qD)'=q[r(r-m)+m]=q[r^2-m(r-1)] between0 and q r^2. The maximum of
q r^2 on r>=1 is2/e. Integrating through finitely many cap boundaries and
using a<=x_i proves the short-stage coefficient4/e.

2. Long-coordinate stage before and after pool switch. Before the switch,
other short-role contributions decrease and other long-role contributions
increase when the owner's long report falls. After the switch, the old long
pool becomes short. The remaining old-long contributions (1-q_new)l_k
increase as its total falls, and each old-short contribution
q_new min(x_k,T_new) decreases. Payments agree at the switch. Therefore
complete deletion really dominates each external positive loss, even for
mixed recipients; a recipient's loss is bounded by its old-short-role loss.

3. No-switch case. Deletion removes exactly d=min(b,T) from the capped long
mass. Normalizing T=1 gives q(exp(d)-1) as an upper bound on all short-role
loss, including the deviator's short role. The exponential secant on[0,1]
proves the claimed coefficient. Counting the deviator's role is harmless
because it only enlarges an upper bound on other recipients' losses.

4. Post-switch case. If the remaining long total r is below1, its former
caps are all uncapped; full capped ratio is d+r>=1. After deletion the
old-short cap ratio R lies in[1,1/r]. Since R exp(1-R) decreases for R>=1,
the remaining aggregate old-short payment is at least exp(1-1/r)/2.
The rearrangement to A(r)exp(d)<=1+(e-1)d is exact. For
A(r)=2exp(r-1)-exp(r-1/r), A(r)<=1 is equivalent to
exp(x)+exp(-x/(1-x))>=2, x=1-r. Differentiating the latter sum and taking
the log ratio of its positive and negative terms gives
1/r-r+2log r. Its derivative is -(1-r)^2/r^2 and it vanishes at1, hence
it is nonnegative for0<r<=1. This proves the desired monotonicity.
The r=0 limit and d endpoints are valid; at r=0, initial feasibility forces
d=1. Multiplying by exp(d)>0 and applying the same secant finishes the bound.

5. Whole-owner and branch quantifiers. The first stage lowers the original
short coordinate, so orientation stays short. At its endpoint the owner's
long-role contribution is no larger than at the full profile because both
q(T) and its capped long report are nondecreasing in T. Thus the second-stage
coefficient can be charged to the FULL long-role contribution. Combining
positive parts across stages and then across branches is valid. The two
roles are bookkeeping terms; no strategic player or report domain is split.
Zero-output branches remain zero under all allowed underreports.

SHARPNESS (AN ADDITION TO THE SOURCE CLAIM)
Use three fixed pure owners, with short reports(1,0,0) and long reports
(0,1,1). Let owner2 withhold its long unit completely. At full reports

uniformly for this primitive. All capacities are rational and owners fixed;
only the rule's exponential payments are transcendental.
