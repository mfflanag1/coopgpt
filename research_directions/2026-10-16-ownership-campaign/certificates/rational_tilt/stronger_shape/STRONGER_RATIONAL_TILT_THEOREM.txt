A STRONGER RATIONAL TILT WITH SHARP FUNDING FACTOR ABOUT 1.129754606
16 October 2026. Independent candidate proof; parent coordinates audit.
This adapts the accepted rational-tilt proof; its water-product rule is unchanged.
Every changed shape inequality and funding maximum is proved below.
No priority claim and no unrestricted optimality claim.

MODEL AND THEOREM.
Fix any finite set of actual owners. Owner i reports nonnegative real
capacities(a_i,b_i), subject to any fixed eligibility restrictions. Reports
bind coalition production v(S)=min(sum_S a_i,sum_S b_i). Utility is payment;
the incentive condition compares every whole-owner coordinatewise underreport.
There are no private costs, identity changes, or additional payment floors.

The mechanism below is nonnegative, covers every actual coalition at every
report, and is monotone in every own coordinate of every mixed owner. Its
exact worst funding factor is
  gamma=1+(-18s_*^2+39s_*-13)(1+s_*)^3/[270s_*(3s_*-1)], (RT_gamma)
where s_* is the unique root in(2/3,1) of
  -162s^4+306s^3-255s^2+104s-13=0.
Numerically gamma is about 1.129754606127951. The definition by the quartic
and rational expression is exact; decimal certification is supplementary.
This is the sharp factor of this specified rule, not a lower bound against
arbitrary truthful mechanisms or an optimization over all possible tilts.

1. DEFINITION.
Orient the two pools so that A=sum x_i<=sum z_i. If A=0 pay everyone0.
Otherwise set
  M=max_i x_i, d_i=min(z_i,A), D=sum_i d_i, L=max_i d_i,
  s=M/A, t=L/D,
  f(u)=0 for0<=u<=2/3,
  f(u)=(3u-2)^2/[5(3u-1)] for2/3<u<=1,
  h(s,t)=1/2+f(t)-f(s).
Choose the same water level as in the reviewed water-product rule:
  min(M,lambda)+sum_i min(d_i,lambda)=A+M.
When D=A take lambda=max(M,max_i d_i); otherwise the solution below that
maximum is unique. Define
  P=[min(M,lambda)/M]*product_{i:d_i>0}min(d_i,lambda)/d_i,
  p=h(s,t)P, y_i=(1-p)x_i+p d_i.                        (RT_rule)
The auxiliary cap M determines a scalar; it is not an owner or coalition
member. A zero cap contributes product factor1 and is omitted.

On[0,1], f is continuously differentiable and nondecreasing, with
  0<=f<=1/10, 0<=f'<=1/2,
  f'(u)=3/5-3/[5(3u-1)^2] for u>2/3.
In fact f'(1)=9/20, so the derivative bound1/2 is conservative.
Therefore2/5<=h<=3/5 and0<P<=1, so0<p<1. At equality of the two physical pool totals, D=A,P=1;
swapping the pools exchanges s,t and h(s,t)+h(t,s)=1, so every owner's
payment agrees from both orientations. Payments are continuous across caps,
maxima, water-active boundaries, D=A, concentration thresholds, and balance.
At A->0, the convex-combination formula gives0<=y_i<=A, so zero is continuous.

For any coalition S, its short sum X=sum_S x_i dominates its production.
Its capped long sum also dominates production, since X<=A and
  sum_S min(z_i,A)>=min(sum_S z_i,A)>=min(X,sum_S z_i).
Their convex combination proves coalition coverage and nonnegativity.

2. WATER-CELL IDENTITIES AND TILT BOUNDS.
Normalize the point being differentiated to A=1; this does not hold A fixed
along an own SHORT-coordinate change. Write x=x_i,d=d_i,s=M and q=2/3.
On a D>1 cell the water level is below1. The reviewed water equation gives:
  - for an uncapped changing long coordinate, beta=(log P)' is -1/d when
    d>lambda and -1/lambda when d<lambda;
  - for a short coordinate, let e=1 if its owner is the strict maximum-short
    owner, otherwise e=0, and let k count long reports strictly above A.
    If the auxiliary cap is inactive, beta=1/lambda-k>=0.
    If it is active, beta=(1+e)/lambda-e/s-k and P<=lambda/s.
    In the latter case beta>=1-s/lambda.
These follow by differentiating the water equation and the logarithm of its
active product factors. For example, in the active auxiliary case the water
equation is(m+1)lambda+U=1+s, and its active product derivative gives the
displayed beta; m counts active actual long caps and U their inactive sum.
Long caps at the physical cap1 are active and contribute to k.

If t>2/3, at most one actual long cap is water-active. Indeed, if the largest
cap L and another cap were active, their residual actual total R=D-L would
exceed lambda. The actual water-truncated mass is at least1, so also
lambda+R>=1. Thus R>max(lambda,1-lambda)>=1/2, contradicting
R=L(1-t)/t<L/2<=1/2. If L alone is active, all others are inactive and
lambda+R>=1, hence lambda>=1-R.

For u>q put v=3u-2 in(0,1]. Then
  f(u)=v^2/[5(1+v)],
  f'(u)=3v(v+2)/[5(v+1)^2], f''(u)=18/[5(3u-1)^3]>0.
The following bounds will be used:
  f'(u)u(1-u)<=1/9,
  f'(u)u(1-u)<=(4/5)(u-q),
  f'(s)s(1-s)<=1/20.                                   (RT_bounds)
The first follows from f'<=1/2 and u(1-u)<=2/9. For the second,
  f'(u)u(1-u)/(u-q)=(v+2)^2(1-v)/[5(v+1)^2]<=4/5,
since(v+2)/(v+1)<=2 and1-v<=1. For the third, the desired inequality
is equivalent to
  P4(v)=4v^4+12v^3+3v^2-10v+3>=0.
Its second derivative48v^2+72v+6 is at least6 on[0,1]. At v0=2/5,
P4(v0)=219/625 and P4'(v0)=-102/125. Strong convexity therefore gives
  P4(v)>=219/625-(102/125)(v-2/5)+3(v-2/5)^2
        >=219/625-(102/125)^2/12=4608/15625>0.
All three bounds are zero on u<=q.

Under a short increase, s'=e-s. If k=0 then t'=0; when f'(t)>0 and k>0,
necessarily k=1, L=1, and t'=t(1-t). Consequently
  h'=f'(t)*1_{k=1}*t(1-t)-f'(s)(e-s).                  (RT_hprime)
In particular h'>=-1/6, h'_+<=11/18, and for e=1, h'_+<=1/9.

3. OWN LONG-COORDINATE MONOTONICITY.
Above the physical cap1, changing the long report changes nothing. Below it,
the owner's payment derivative is
  p[1+(d-x)(beta+eta)], eta=h'/h.                       (RT_long)
If the owner is the strict largest long-cap owner, then
  eta=f'(t)t(1-t)/(h d)>=0.
When active, eta*d<=5/18<1. When inactive,
eta=f'(t)(1-t)/(hD)<=5/12<1/lambda. Therefore beta+eta<=0, and the constant
term in(RT_long) is nonnegative: d eta in the active case, or
1-d/lambda+d eta in the inactive case. The coefficient of x is nonnegative.

For a nonmaximum long owner, eta=-f'(t)t/(hD)<=0. If t<=2/3 it is zero and
the original water-product proof applies. Otherwise the dominant-cap lemma
makes this owner inactive. The coefficient of x in(RT_long) is positive,
so it suffices to bound its constant term at x=0.
If L is active, d<=R and
  lambda-d>=1-2R>=L-2R=3D(t-2/3),
whereas
  lambda*d*|eta|
   <=f'(t)t(1-t)/h<=2(t-2/3)<3D(t-2/3).
Thus1-d/lambda+d eta>=0. If L is inactive instead,
d/lambda<=R/L=(1-t)/t<1/2, while d|eta|<=5/18<1/2. This again proves
nonnegativity. Tied maxima and cap endpoints follow by continuity.

4. OWN SHORT-COORDINATE MONOTONICITY: EASY CASES.
The exact derivative of the SAME actual owner's complete payment is
  y_i'=1-p+p d'+p beta(d-x)+P h'(d-x),                 (RT_short)
where d' in{0,1}. No opposite account is removed or assigned to a fictitious
owner. If d>=x,beta>=0, a lower bound is1-h-1/6>0. If d>=x,beta<0, the
auxiliary cap is active; use -beta<=s/lambda-1 and P<=lambda/s to obtain
the same lower bound. If d<x,beta<0, discard its favorable beta term.
For a maximum-short owner,1-h-1/9>0 suffices. For a nonmaximum-short owner,
x<=1/2, and1-h-(11/18)/2>=17/180>0.

It remains d<x,beta>=0. A sufficient lower bound is
  1-hP(1+beta*x)-P h'_+ x.                            (RT_hard)

5. NONMAXIMUM SHORT OWNER.
Here e=0 and x<=min(s,1-s). If s>2/3, the water identities imply
P(1+beta*x)<=1/s<=3/2 in either auxiliary-cap orientation. In the inactive
case use lambda>=s and P<=1; in the active case use P<=lambda/s.
Moreover, by(RT_bounds),
  h'_+x<=(1-s)/9+f'(s)s(1-s)<=1/27+1/20=47/540.
Thus(RT_hard)>=1-(3/5)(3/2)-47/540=7/540>0.

Suppose s<=2/3. If t<=2/3 both tilt and its derivative vanish, and the
reviewed half-prefactor derivative proof applies. Otherwise put V=t-2/3>0.
The largest actual long cap L is active: with inactive auxiliary cap this
follows from D>1, and with active auxiliary cap, inactivity of L would give
t<=L<=lambda<s<=2/3, a contradiction.

With inactive auxiliary cap, lambda=1-R>=2-1/t and x<=1/2, so the original
half-prefactor margin in(RT_hard) is at least
  (lambda-x)/(2lambda)>=3V/4.
With active auxiliary cap, lambda=(1+s-R)/2<s gives R>1-s and s>1/2.
Then t<1/(2-s), and the original margin is at least
  (2s-1)/(2s)>=3V.
Both statements follow by substituting the water identities in

The additional cost from the tilt is at most
  2f(t)+(1/2)f'(t)t(1-t)<= (3/5)V.                    (RT_cost)
Indeed, writing v=3t-2, the difference between3/5 and that ratio is

This identity follows by substituting the rational f and f'. The cost bound

6. MAXIMUM SHORT OWNER AND THE BALANCE INEQUALITY.
Now e=1,x=s. If t<=2/3, h<=1/2,h'<=0 and the original inequality
P(1+beta*s)<=2 proves(RT_hard)>=0. Suppose t>2/3. If k=1 then L=1;
the water identities give P(1+beta*s)<=1 in both auxiliary-cap orientations.
Thus1-h-h'_+>=1-3/5-1/9>0 suffices.

The only remaining case is k=0. Here d'=0 and h'=-f'(s)(1-s)<=0. The
expression(RT_short) is affine in its own opposite account d in[0,s],
with the current global quantities fixed. At d=s it is1-p>=0. It suffices
to check d=0 as an algebraic endpoint bound, not a change of actual reports.
If L is inactive, only the auxiliary cap is active, t<s, h<=1/2, and
P(1+beta*s)=2, so the result follows immediately.

Suppose L is active and the auxiliary cap inactive. Then s<=lambda<=L and
P=lambda/L. The d=0 derivative multiplied by L is
  L-h(lambda+s)+lambda*s*f'(s)(1-s).
Replace f(t) by f(L), which only decreases this expression. Its resulting
slope in lambda is at most -1/2+s f'(s)(1-s)<=-9/20<0. Increase lambda to L.
It remains to prove
  (L-s)/2 >= [f(L)-f(s)](L+s)-L s(1-s)f'(s).           (RT_balance)
For s<=2/3 and L>2/3, divide the right side by L-s. With L fixed its
maximum over s<=2/3 is at s=2/3. Writing v=3L-2, that maximum is
  f(L)(L+2/3)/(L-2/3)=v(v+4)/[5(v+1)]<=1/2.
This expression increases on[0,1], since its derivative is
(v^2+2v+4)/[5(v+1)^2]>0, and equals1/2 at1. If L<=2/3 the right side
vanishes. For s>2/3 and L>s, use the exact difference identity
  f(L)-f(s)=(3/5)(L-s)[1-1/((3L-1)(3s-1))].
The right side of(RT_balance), divided by L-s, is
  Q(L)=(3/5)(L+s)[1-1/((3L-1)(3s-1))]
        -K L/(L-s), K=s(1-s)f'(s)>=0.
The first term has derivative
  (3/5)[1+(3s+1)/((3L-1)^2(3s-1))]>0;
the second has derivative K s/(L-s)^2>=0. Hence Q(L)<=Q(1). With
v=3s-2, direct simplification gives the strict balance margin
  Q(1)=(1+s)[f(1)-f(s)]/(1-s)-s f'(s)
      =(5v^2+8v+5)/[10(1+v)^2]
      =1/2-v/[5(1+v)^2]<=1/2.                         (RT_balance_margin)
This formula applies for s<1; at s=1 there is no L>s to check.
At L=s the undivided right side is nonpositive. This proves(RT_balance),
including all endpoints.

Finally suppose both L and the auxiliary cap are active. The d=0 derivative
multiplied by L is
  L-lambda-2lambda[f(t)-f(s)]+lambda^2 f'(s)(1-s).
If L<s, then h<=1/2 and L>lambda, so it is positive. Otherwise increase
f(t) to f(L). The derivative of the resulting expression in lambda is at
most -1+2s f'(s)(1-s)<=-9/10<0. Increasing lambda to s gives exactly the
inactive-auxiliary expression at lambda=s, already proved nonnegative.
This exhausts every short-coordinate derivative on D>1 cells.

7. PERSISTENT D=A AND GLOBAL OWN-REPORT COMPARISONS.
On a persistent D=A interval with A below the other pool total, exactly one
long cap is positive and equals A. Hence t=1,P=1,h=3/5-f(s), and
h'=-f'(s)(e-s). For an owner without that cap, a maximum-short owner has
derivative at least1-h; a nonmaximum-short owner loses at most
x s f'(s)<=s(1-s)f'(s)<=1/20, so its derivative is at least7/20.
For the owner with the cap, d=A,d'=1 and its derivative is
1+h'(1-x)>=1-1/6>0. Long changes within this face are capped and irrelevant;
departures are continuous and covered by the adjacent cells.

Each coordinate segment has finitely many choices of orientation, maximum
owner, physical caps, and water-active caps. On each such choice the water
level is affine in the varying report, and the defining inequalities are
linear. The extra concentration thresholds are also linear after clearing
positive denominators. Thus the segment has finitely many cell intervals.
The proved nonnegative derivatives and continuity give global own-coordinate
monotonicity. Successive coordinates of the SAME owner give every permitted
whole-bundle comparison. No finite-grid premise occurs in this proof.

9. ALL DOMINANT-LONG PROFILES SATISFY THE SAME FUNDING CURVE.
Suppose t>2/3. At most one actual cap L is active. If it is active, put
R=D-L<L/2. With s,R fixed, the water level does not depend on L:
lambda=1-R for an inactive auxiliary cap, or(1+s-R)/2 for an active one.
The expression
  E=h(s,L/(L+R))*[min(s,lambda)/s]*lambda*(L+R-1)/L
increases with L: both h and1+(R-1)/L increase, since R<1. Raise L to1.
This preserves the water-active pattern and never decreases E.

If no actual cap is active, only the auxiliary cap is active. At fixed D,
transfer mass from other actual caps to a largest cap until it reaches lambda.
This is feasible since D>1>lambda. The water level and product stay fixed,
while t and h increase. Then use the preceding argument to raise that cap
to1 at fixed residual mass R. Thus every dominant-long case reduces to
L=1,0<=R<1/2,t=1/(1+R), without decreasing its scalar funding expression.
These are funding comparisons, not incentive assertions about other owners.

In this reduction
  f(t)=(1-2R)^2/[5(1+R)(2-R)].
If s<=1-R the auxiliary cap is inactive and E=h R(1-R). Put u=1/2-R.
Since(1+R)(2-R)=2+R-R^2>=2, dropping the favorable -f(s) gives
  E<=[1/2+(2/5)u^2](1/4-u^2)<=1/8.
If s>=1-R the auxiliary cap is active and
  E2(s,R)=h(s,1/(1+R))*R*(1+s-R)^2/(4s).
For fixed s its derivative in R has the sign of
  K=h(1+s-3R)
       -(9/5)(1-2R)R(1+s-R)/[(1+R)^2(2-R)^2].
Indeed h_R=-(9/5)(1-2R)/[(1+R)^2(2-R)^2]. Use h>=2/5,
s>=1-R, R<=1/2, 1+s-R<=2, and(1+R)^2(2-R)^2>=4 to obtain
  K>=(1-2R)[4/5-9/20]=(7/20)(1-2R)>=0.
The allowed interval1-s<=R<=1/2 is preserved while increasing R.
Thus E2 is at most its value at R=1/2:
  [1-2f(s)](s+1/2)^2/(16s)<=[1-2f(s)]F(s)=g(s).
The last comparison follows from

10. SHARP MAXIMUM, ATTAINMENT, AND COMPUTATION.
For s<=1/2, g=1/8; for1/2<=s<=2/3, g=F increases, with
g(2/3)=125/972>1/8. Thus the maximum inside(2/3,1) is globally dominant.
For s>=2/3,
  g(s)=(-18s^2+39s-13)(1+s)^3/[270s(3s-1)],
  g'(s)=(1+s)^2 J(s)/[270s^2(3s-1)^2],
  J(s)=-162s^4+306s^3-255s^2+104s-13.
Here J(2/3)=5/3>0,J(1)=-20<0,J'(2/3)=-20. Also
  J''(s)=-1944s^2+1836s-510<0
for every real s, because its leading coefficient is negative and its
discriminant is -594864<0. Thus J' is decreasing and negative on[2/3,1],
so J has exactly one root s_* there. The funding curve g has its unique
global maximum at s_*, proving(RT_gamma). Rational bisection of J together
with monotone bounds on1-2f and F gives rigorous decimal enclosures.

For any s>=1/2 take two pure short owners of reports s,1-s and two pure
long owners each of report1. Then t=1/2,lambda=(1+s)/3, P=lambda^3/s,
and the excess is exactly g(s). Taking s=s_* attains gamma with four fixed
actual owners, all positive. Rational approximations approach this value,
but the all-real theorem includes its exact algebraic attainer.

For rational reports, every operation in f, h, water filling, P, and payments
is rational. Sorting caps gives O(n log n) comparisons followed by O(n)
arithmetic operations; bit complexity must also count input/output lengths.
This theorem concerns the two-input physical game and its report-independent
positive sums. It does not automatically replace a primitive inside nested
network results that require separate role-payment bounds. The unrestricted
9/8 lower remains separate, and historical novelty has not been established.

11. RELATION TO THE PRECEDING RATIONAL RULE.
This shape is pointwise larger than the preceding f_old(s)=(3s-2)^2/(10s)
for2/3<s<1, with equality at both endpoints. Their ratio is
  f(s)/f_old(s)=2(v+2)/[3(v+1)]>1 for0<v<1.
Consequently the new g is strictly smaller on that interval. Since the
previous unique maximizer lies inside it, the new sharp factor is strictly
smaller. This compares the two specified rules only. A complete incentive
proof is still necessary because increasing f can worsen dominant-long or
mixed-owner derivatives; Sections2--7 supply that proof independently of
this funding comparison.
