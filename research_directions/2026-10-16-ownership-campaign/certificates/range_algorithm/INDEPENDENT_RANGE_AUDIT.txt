1. Independent account-space formulation
----------------------------------------
Fix n actual owners, h_k>=0, p_k>=0, H=sum h_k, P=sum p_k, and owner i.
Write a=h_i, p_-=sum_{k!=i}p_k. The only needed range assumptions are
  beta>=p_i, K>=beta+p_-.
The feasible opposing vectors satisfy b>=p, b_i<=beta, sum b<=K.
They form a nonempty compact set. For H=0 the objective is identically zero.
For n=1, own-account monotonicity gives max Phi_1=min(H,beta).
Henceforth H>0 and n>=2. There is at least one other account available to
receive slack, which is essential in the short-regime witness construction.

The independent implementation is verify_range_mechanism.py. The primitive
uses the original formula directly and treats A=B as (a_i+b_i)/2, T=0 as zero.
Physical coalition worth is rebuilt by removing every absent owner's entire
bundle before computing min(H(S),p(S)+sum_j min(h_j(S),l_j(S))).

2. Short opposing pool: complete derivation and attainment
---------------------------------------------------------
The total T lies in [P,U], U=min(K,H); this interval exists exactly when P<=H.
At fixed T, the payment is
  (1-q(T))c+q(T)min(a,T),
  q(T)=exp(1-rho(T))/2, rho(T)=sum_k min(h_k,T)/T.
Because T<=H, rho>=1 and 1-q>=1/2>0. Thus the optimal own account is
  c(T)=min(beta,T-p_-).
It is feasible: T>=P ensures c>=p_i; putting every other account initially
at p_k leaves nonnegative residual T-c-p_-, which can be assigned to any
one other owner. No upper bounds are imposed on those other accounts.

Let T0=beta+p_-. On [P,min(T0,U)], the preceding witness has all other
accounts equal to p and raises only c=T-p_-. Primitive own-account
monotonicity therefore makes the objective nondecreasing there.

On T>=T0, c=beta and q(T) is nondecreasing, since each
min(h_k,T)/T=min(h_k/T,1) is nonincreasing. If beta<=a, then
min(a,T)-beta>=0 and is nondecreasing, so the payment is nondecreasing.
If beta>=a, then T>=T0>=beta>=a and the gap a-beta<=0 is constant, so
the payment is nonincreasing. Consequently
  T*=U                    when beta<=a;
  T*=min(U,T0)            when beta>=a.
At beta=a both prescriptions agree in value; beyond T0 the value is a.
At T=0 the value is zero. This proves the entire short formula, including
zero endpoints and the beta=a crossing, with an explicit maximizing vector.

3. Long opposing pool: complete derivation and attainment
--------------------------------------------------------
The region exists exactly when K>=H. Put c=b_i and d_c=min(c,H). The
payment is
  a + exp(1-rho)(d_c-a)/2,
  rho=sum_k min(b_k,H)/H >=1.

Positive gap d_c>=a.
The smallest capped total is max(H,d_c+S_-), where
  S_-=sum_{k!=i}min(p_k,H).
Both terms are lower bounds. To attain them, fix c and initially use p_-.
If c+p_- >=H, the initial capped total is already >=H: either one account
is >=H, or no account is capped and the capped total equals the total.
If c+p_- <H, all initial accounts are <H. Add H-c-p_- to one other
account. That account does not exceed H, since the initial total was at
least its initial value. The new total and capped total are both H. It is
within budget because K>=H. Hence
  rho_min(d)=max(1,(d+S_-)/H).
For a<=d<=H, the factor exp(1-rho_min(d))(d-a) is nondecreasing. On either
smooth piece its derivative is exp(1-rho_min)[1-(d-a)rho_min'], where
rho_min' is 0 or 1/H, so the bracket is nonnegative. The pieces join
continuously. Thus the maximum positive-gap choice uses d=min(beta,H).
Taking c=beta is feasible and attains that capped value, even if beta>H.

Negative gap d_c<=a.
When beta<a, necessarily beta<H; every feasible c<=beta is uncapped.
For fixed c, define W_-=sum_{k!=i}(p_k-H)_+. The largest capped total is
  min(c+(n-1)H,K-W_-).
The first term is the accountwise cap; the second subtracts unavoidable
overflow at the other owners from the total budget. To attain this minimum,
start at b_i=c and b_{-i}=p_{-i}, then distribute remaining budget into
other accounts below H until they are full or the budget is exhausted.
The attainable maximum is at least H: if some p_k>=H, that account alone
provides H; if none does, W_-=0 and both c+(n-1)H and K are >=H.
Thus the constructed vector lies in the long region. Its ratio is
  rho_max(c)=min((c+(n-1)H)/H,(K-W_-)/H).
As c increases, rho_max is nondecreasing and a-c is nonnegative and
nonincreasing. Therefore exp(1-rho_max(c))(a-c) is nonincreasing, and
the payment is nondecreasing. Its optimum is c=beta.

If beta>=a, c=a is feasible whenever the feasible interval contains a,
and positive-gap values are at least a, whereas negative-gap values are
at most a. If p_i>a, every feasible c already has nonnegative gap.
Hence the positive-gap optimum dominates all negative-gap choices.
At d=a, the objective is exactly a and either formula agrees, regardless
of which cap-ratio expression is used. This establishes the complete long
formula with no assumption that the optimizing vectors are physical reports.

4. Boundary and whole-owner incentive checks
--------------------------------------------
At total opposing capacity H, every account is at most H, so rho=1 and
the short and long primitive formulas both give (h_i+b_i)/2. At K=H,
the long feasible set is the total-H face; it introduces no discontinuous
larger value. At P=H the short face is already included in the long region.
At beta=a, the maximizing-total switch has equal values as proved above.

The original range-inclusion incentive proof is valid. An own h/l increase
raises H_i, beta=B_i and K, fixes all other H_k and all p, and therefore
expands D_i; the outer primitive is monotone in its own H_i at each old
feasible b. An own p_i increase by delta translates every old feasible
b to b+delta e_i, raises the own ceiling and total ceiling by exactly delta,
and cannot reduce the primitive payment. Coordinate telescoping covers every
whole-owner comparison. Other owners' local payments may move either way;
the proof never assumes their monotonicity.

For completeness, the primitive's own-coordinate monotonicity can also be
checked directly, independently of any cloning argument. In the A<=B region,
with T=A, own b_i below A has derivative q[1+(a_i-b_i)/A]>=0; above A its
derivative is zero. For own a_i, if b_i>=A the derivative is
1+q'(A-a_i)>=1. If b_i<A, it is 1-q+q'(b_i-a_i). On smooth pieces,
0<=q'<=q rho/A. If b_i>=a_i the derivative is nonnegative immediately;
otherwise it is >=1-q(1+rho)>=0, since
(1+rho)exp(1-rho)/2<=1 for rho>=1. The B<=A argument is symmetric.
The primitive is continuous across cap crossings and A=B; at zero its
individual payment is at most T and tends to zero. These local derivatives
therefore give global coordinate monotonicity, including all boundaries.

The full local-account vector p+z belongs to every D_i. Hence each R_i is at
least its nested-rule payment. Branchwise coalition coverage of the primitive
and then outer coalition coverage prove physical coalition coverage. A null
owner has a=beta=p_i=0 and receives zero. If grand F=0, either H=0 or K=0,
and every payment is zero. No semantic substitution of virtual account vectors
for actual ownership or actual reports is needed in any of these arguments.
