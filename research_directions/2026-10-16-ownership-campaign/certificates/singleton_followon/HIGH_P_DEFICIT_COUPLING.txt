Conclusion
----------
For the range mechanism R, and also for every owner-specific selection of
opposing accounts satisfying the frozen range inequalities, the P>=H regime
has the stronger funding bound

 sum_i R_i/F <= 7/4 - (1/2)exp(-C)(3/2-C) < 137/80,
 C=1+1/(2e), F=H.

The displayed transcendental constant is approximately1.701631631731788;
that decimal is informational only. The clean rational bound137/80=1.7125
uses elementary inequalities. This is uniform in the number of actual owners,
branches and real report capacities. It is not a lower bound or sharpness claim.
It transfers to the smaller exact-local-total range rule and to the actual
own-report envelope, because the same account-range inequalities hold.

Assumptions inherited from the already proved local primitive
------------------------------------------------------------
Normalize H=1; zero H has zero payments. Let actual hardware accounts be a_i,
sum a_i=1, shared accounts p_i, P=sum p_i>=1, local payments z_i, Z=sum z_i,
and local production m=M/H in[0,1]. The local role decomposition satisfies

 z_i=z_i^h+z_i^l, z_i^h<=a_i, sum_i z_i^l<=1/2, Z<=C m.

For owner i, every admissible opposing vector b obeys
 b_k>=p_k, b_i<=B_i=p_i+z_i, sum_k b_k<=K=P+C m.
Different owners may use different vectors. All bounds below are individual
and uniform over their vectors before summation.

Define
 u_i=min(p_i,1), v_i=min(B_i,1), U=sum_i u_i>=1,
 D_+=sum_i(v_i-a_i)_+, D_-=sum_i(a_i-v_i)_+,
 t=C m in[0,C].

Positive gaps
-------------
For v_i>=a_i and a chosen opposing own cap d_i=min(b_i,1), an actual
positive payment excess requires d_i>=a_i. Its opposing capped ratio obeys
 rho>=U-u_i+d_i, since every other account is at least its p lower bound.
Consequently

 Phi_i-a_i <= (1/2)exp(1-U)exp(-(d_i-u_i))(d_i-a_i)
            <= (1/2)exp(1-U)(v_i-a_i).

The first expression increases in d_i on[a_i,1]: its derivative after
removing positive constants is exp(-(d_i-u_i))(1-d_i+a_i)>=0.
Also d_i>=u_i, so exp(-(v_i-u_i))<=1 at the upper endpoint.
If the actual gap is negative the same upper bound holds automatically.

Negative gaps: the missing coupling
-----------------------------------
If v_i<a_i<=1, then B_i<1 and p_i<1. In particular this owner contributes
no unavoidable shared-account overflow. Define the total baseline overflow

 W=sum_k(p_k-1)_+=P-U.

All of it is carried by owners k!=i. Since b_k>=p_k, any opposing vector
has at least W wasted mass above the per-account cap1. Hence

 rho=sum_k min(b_k,1) <= K-W = U+t.

The actual own cap d_i<=v_i<a_i. Its primitive payment therefore satisfies

 Phi_i = a_i-(1/2)exp(1-rho)(a_i-d_i)
       <= a_i-(1/2)exp(1-U-t)(a_i-v_i).

This inequality survives taking the maximum separately for every owner.
Summing positive and negative owner classes gives the retained-deficit bound

 sum_i R_i <= 1+(1/2)exp(1-U)[D_+-exp(-t)D_-].                 (A)

Uniform optimization of the coupled gaps
---------------------------------------
Since v_i<=u_i+z_i,

 D_+ <= U+sum_i(z_i-a_i)_+ <= U+sum_i z_i^l <= U+1/2.          (B)

Also sum_i v_i<=U+Z and D_+-D_-=sum_i v_i-1, so

 D_- >= max(0,D_+-U-Z+1).                                    (C)

The function d-exp(-t)max(0,d-U-Z+1) is nondecreasing in d: its slopes
are1 and1-exp(-t), both nonnegative. Therefore (B),(C) imply

 D_+-exp(-t)D_- <= U+1/2-exp(-t)(3/2-Z),

where the last positive part may be removed because Z<=t<=C<3/2.
Since Z<=t and (3/2-t)exp(-t) decreases on[0,C] (its derivative is
(t-5/2)exp(-t)<0),

 D_+-exp(-t)D_- <= U+1/2-exp(-C)(3/2-C).

Put a0=1/2-exp(-C)(3/2-C)>0; positivity follows from1<C<3/2.
The function exp(1-U)(U+a0) decreases for U>=1, because its derivative
is exp(1-U)(1-U-a0)<0. Inserting U=1 in(A) now proves

 sum_i R_i <= 1+(1/2)[3/2-exp(-C)(3/2-C)].                    (D)

No owner splitting, finite profile grid, or coincident maximizing vector has
been used. Owners with v_i=a_i contribute no excess or deficit and may be
placed in either class. Cases t=0 and coordinates equal to zero are included;
the inequalities use non-strict endpoint conditions throughout.
