UNIVERSAL RETENTION AND POSITIVE-GRID FUNDING UNDER OUTER DOMINANCE
16 October 2026. Candidate theorem; independent audit pending.

This note concerns the original exponential primitive and its fixed two-level
composition. It is not a best-mechanism lower bound. In particular, when P=0
the physical game is additive across branches and can use a direct primitive
without nesting. The value of the result is its unrestricted local response
lemma and its application to the actual own-report envelope with P>0.

DEFINITIONS AND DOMAIN.
Actual owners and their eligible coordinates are fixed. Owner i reports
nonnegative hardware h_ij, local resource l_ij, and grid capacity p_i. Every
coordinatewise whole-owner underreport is allowed; zero reports are included.
Put H_i=sum_j h_ij, H=sum_i H_i, P=sum_i p_i,
  T_j=min(sum_i h_ij,sum_i l_ij), M=sum_j T_j,
  F=min(H,P+M).
For nonnegative vectors a,b, the exponential primitive Phi has output zero
when T=min(sum a,sum b)=0. Otherwise denote the shorter vector by s and the
longer by l, and put
  rho=sum_i min(l_i,T)/T, q=exp(1-rho)/2,
  Phi_i(a,b)=(1-q)s_i+q min(l_i,T).
At a tie the two formulas agree. Put c=1/(2e), C=1+c. The primitive is
continuous, coordinatewise monotone in the recipient's own coordinates,
and coalition covering. Its total payment is T[1+q(rho-1)]<=CT.

Local payments are z_i=sum_j Phi_i(h_.j,l_.j), Z=sum_i z_i. Let
  b_i=p_i+z_i, B=P+Z, N_i=Phi_i(Hvector,bvector).
The actual envelope is
  E_i(report)=sup_{report_i'<=report_i} N_i(report_i',report_-i).
Its feasible underreport set expands with the recipient's report, so E is
own-report monotone; E>=N preserves coalition coverage. Every theorem below
uses the full continuous set of actual whole-owner underreports. Counts of
owners and branches are unrestricted. No selected finite domain is used.

Write delta_j=CT_j-Z_j, and split the full-profile slack as
  Delta_-=sum_{rho_j<=2} delta_j,
  Delta_+=sum_{rho_j>2} delta_j,
omitting zero-output branches. Thus Delta_-+Delta_+=CM-Z.

THEOREM 1: UNIVERSAL LOCAL RETENTION.
For each owner choose an arbitrary, possibly different, own underreport on
a single branch. Denote its resulting own payment and total by z_i^i,Z^i.
If the full branch has output T and slack delta=CT-Z, then
  sum_i [(Z-Z^i)/3-(z_i-z_i^i)]_+ <=(20/21)delta.        (UR)
If its full capped long ratio rho>=2, every summand is zero. Consequently,
for arbitrary whole-owner underreports on all branches,
  sum_i [(Z-Z^i)/3-(z_i-z_i^i)]_+ <=(20/21)Delta_-.      (UR_multi)

PROOF OF THEOREM 1.
Normalize full T=1 and use the potential f_i=z_i-Z/3. For each owner follow
its own underreport by first reducing its original short coordinate, then
its original long coordinate. Only the chosen owner's coordinates change.

SHORT STAGE. On a smooth cap cell let t be the current short total, r the
current capped long ratio, m the number of capped long accounts, x the
owner's short share, d its capped long share, and a in{0,1} its own cap
indicator. Primes denote increasing its short coordinate. Direct
differentiation gives
  z_i'=1-q+q(r-m)(d-x)+qa,
  Z'=1+q[r(r-1)-m(r-2)-1].
If x<=2/3 then
  3f_i'>=2-q(r^2+r+2)+qmr>=0,
since q(r)(r^2+r+2) decreases from2 on r>=1. The current own short share
decreases under its short underreport. Thus only an owner whose FULL short
share exceeds2/3 can gain in this stage; at most one actual owner qualifies.
For arbitrary x<=1 and r>=2,
  3f_i'>=2-q(r^2+2r+2)+qm(r+1)>0,
because q(r)(r^2+2r+2) decreases on r>=2 and equals10c<2 at2.
For 1<=r<=2,
  delta'=C-Z'>=c-q(r-1)>=0,
  2Z'-3z_i'-C
    <=-(1+C)+q[2r^2+r+1+m(1-2r)]
    <=-(1+C)+7/(2sqrt(e))<0.
The last scalar product is maximized at r=3/2; the strict comparison follows
from 8/3<e<3. Hence -f_i'<=delta'/3 on this interval. During an underreport
r increases, so integrating all possible negative derivatives costs at most
the initial delta/3. Above r=2 the potential cannot increase. The aggregate
SHORT-stage gain is therefore at most delta/3.

LONG STAGE. At its start normalize the remaining short total to1, write
R for the capped long ratio, s for the owner's short share, x for its capped
long share, and q_R=exp(1-R)/2. Removing u from that long share has formal
original-orientation potential
  f_art(u)=s-1/3+q_R exp(u)[x-s-(R-1)/3-(2/3)u].          (UC)
When R>=2, its derivative q_R exp(u)[x-s-1+(2-R)/3-(2/3)u]
is nonpositive. The other owners' long total is at least R-x>=1, so no
pool switch can occur. This stage then has no positive gain.

When 1<=R<=2, put epsilon=2-R. The derivative in(UC) is at most
q_R exp(u)[epsilon/3-(2/3)u]. Thus its maximum possible gain is at most
  (2/3)q_R[exp(epsilon/2)-1-epsilon/2] <= delta_stage/3,
where delta_stage=q_R[exp(-epsilon)-1+epsilon]. In fact this gain is at most13delta_stage/42. Here is an exact certificate
from the independently derived post-switch lemma. For epsilon in[0,1],
  exp(-epsilon)>=1-epsilon+epsilon^2/2-epsilon^3/6,
  exp(epsilon/2)<=1+epsilon/2+epsilon^2/8+epsilon^3/42.
The first follows from the positive fourth-order Taylor remainder; in the
second the tail beginning with epsilon^3/48 has successive ratios at most
1/8. Thus, for the displayed gain bound G,
  delta_stage-3G
   =q_R[exp(-epsilon)+1+2epsilon-2exp(epsilon/2)]
   >=q_R[epsilon^2/4-(3/14)epsilon^3]>=q_R epsilon^2/28.
Since delta_stage<=q_R epsilon^2/2, this gives
  G<=delta_stage/3-delta_stage/42=13delta_stage/42.

For completeness, (UC) also bounds any post-switch maximum, even though it
is only an algebraic continuation there. If the other actual long total
v<1, put t=remaining own long+v<1, S=sum_k min(s_k,t), a=min(s,t),
so t<=S<=1. The TRUE potential after the switch is
  2t/3-v+q[a+v-2t/3-S/3], q=exp(1-S/t)/2.
Its derivative in S is q[S+t-3(a+v)]/(3t), so its maximum over[t,1] is
at an endpoint. At S=t it is t/6-v/2+a/2, at most the crossing value
f_cross=1/6-v/2+s/2. At S=1 replacing a by s increases it. Put
q_b=exp(1-1/t)/2, q_a=exp(1-t)/2, w=s+v, L=(2t+1)/3. The resulting
upper bound is f_b=2t/3-v+q_b(w-L). If f_b>f_cross, necessarily w<L,
because f_b-f_cross decreases in w and equals(t-1)/3 at w=L. Directly,
  f_art-f_b=(1-q_a-q_b)(w-L)>=0.
Here q_a+q_b>=1: the derivative of exp(1-t)+exp(1-1/t) is nonpositive
on(0,1], since the ratio of its positive and negative terms is
exp(t-1/t-2log t)<=1. Thus the true post-switch potential never exceeds
max(f_cross,f_art). The crossing itself belongs to the formal curve, which
proves the asserted full LONG-stage bound. The t=0 endpoint is continuous.

COUNTING LONG GAIN. Let full short share be s_i and full capped long share
d_i, so sum_i(s_i+d_i)=1+rho. If the intermediate short total is t, then
t>=1-s_i, R>=rho, and x<=d_i/t. Positive long gain requires
  x-s>(1+R)/3, hence x>(1+rho)/3.
Therefore d_i+s_i>=tx+1-t>=x>(1+rho)/3. At most two actual owners meet
this strict inequality. For intermediate R<=2 its unnormalized slack is
at most the full slack: t<=1, R>=rho, and c-q(R)(R-1) decreases on[1,2].
For R>=2 there is no gain. Thus aggregate LONG gain is at most13delta/21.
Add the SHORT-stage delta/3 bound. Positive parts are subadditive across
the two stages, proving(UR) with coefficient20/21. If full
rho>=2 both stages have zero gain. Continuity handles ties and zero totals.
Sum this result over branches, using positive-part subadditivity, to obtain
(UR_multi). This also covers any zero-output branch, whose output and all
payments remain zero under an underreport.

THEOREM 2: ACTUAL-ENVELOPE FUNDING WITH POSITIVE GRID.
Assume only that the full profile has B>0 and
  sum_i min(H_i,B)/B>=2.                                (OD)
No bound on local ratios, no zero-grid restriction, and no account-side
restriction is imposed. Write
  kappa=(7/10)-(e/5),
  Bmax=(7c-1)/3, kappa_P=3 Bmax^2/(8c).
Then F=P+M, and
  sum_i E_i
    <=C(P+Z)+(20C/21)Delta_-+(1+kappa)Delta_++kappa_P P
    =C^2 F-(Cc-kappa_P)P-(C/21)Delta_--(c-kappa)Delta_+
    <=C^2 F.       (OD_bound)
All three displayed saving coefficients are strictly positive. Underreports
may leave condition(OD); the condition is imposed only on the evaluated
full profile. The mechanism E remains defined and truthful everywhere.

PROOF OF THEOREM 2.
Fix an arbitrary own underreport separately for each owner. Write Z^i,z_i^i
for its resulting local payments and eta_i=p_i-p_i'>=0. Its opposing total
and own opposing account are
  B^i=B+(Z^i-Z)-eta_i,
  b_i^i=b_i-(z_i-z_i^i)-eta_i.
Local own monotonicity implies D_i=b_i-b_i^i>=0. Restore the owner's OUTER
hardware only to H_i; this can increase its payment by primitive own
monotonicity and leaves the already computed local accounts fixed.
Condition(OD) gives H>=2B, while B^i<=P+CM<=C(P+M)<=CB<2B. Thus with
restored hardware the opposing pool is short at every point of comparison.
Also H>=2B>=2(P+M), so F=P+M.

TOTAL-INCREASING CASE. When B^i>B, the restored owner's gain is at most
  (1/2)[(B^i-B)-D_i]_+.
To see this, first increase the opposing total while holding its own
opposing account fixed, then decrease that own account at the final total.
The primitive's positive derivative in the total at fixed own account is
at most1/2, while its derivative in the own account is1-q>=1/2. The total
derivative bound follows on a cap cell from q r<=1/2: if own hardware is
uncapped, the derivative is q'(H_i-b_i)<=q r; if capped it is
q+q'(t-b_i)<=q r, since at least one hardware account is then capped.
All cap boundaries are continuous. Moreover
  (B^i-B)-D_i=(Z^i-Z)-(z_i-z_i^i)-2eta_i.
The universal total-increase lemma gives
  sum_i[(Z^i-Z)-(z_i-z_i^i)]_+<=2 Delta_+.
Its full proof is in UNIVERSAL_TOTAL_INCREASE_LEMMA.txt: low-ratio branches
have nonincreasing totals; high-ratio branches charge the potential z_i+Z
by convexity in the actual own capped share. Hence total-increasing gains
sum to at most Delta_+.

TOTAL-NONINCREASING CASE. Normalize full B=1, put a=H_i/B, z=b_i/B,
t=B^i/B<=1, u=1-t, D=D_i/B, epsilon=[u/3-D]_+.
The elementary outer quadratic bound is
  gain/B <=(3/(8c))(B_i)_+^2+C epsilon,
  B_i=2c(z-a)-(1-c)/3.                                 (OD_quad)
For clarity, in the only nontrivial case z-D>a and t>a, direct subtraction
is (q0-q(t))(z-D-a)-(1-q0)D. Under(OD), q0<=c and
0<=q0-q(t)<=2c(1-t). Hence this is at most
  B_i u-(2c/3)u^2+C epsilon,
whose maximum is(OD_quad). The other cases have no positive gain. The
global q-difference estimate uses the cap-cell Lipschitz bound2c for
t>=1/2, and q0<=c<=2c(1-t) for t<1/2. Its detailed cap-cell proof is
in SATURATION_RETENTION_THEOREM.txt. Equality and zero endpoints follow
by continuity.

Grid underreporting helps the retention inequality rather than harming it:
  B epsilon=[(Z-Z^i)/3-(z_i-z_i^i)-(2/3)eta_i]_+.
Theorem1 therefore gives sum_i B epsilon_i<=(20/21)Delta_-.

It remains to bound the quadratic terms. At most one B_i is positive,
because (b_i-H_i)_+/B sums to at most1 and B_i>0 requires this share to
exceed(1-c)/(6c)>1/2. Also B_i<=Bmax. On each branch,
  z_ij-h_ij<=(1-q_j)T_j,
whether hardware or local resource is short. Consequently
  B B_i<=Bmax P+sum_j b(rho_j)T_j,
  b(r)=2c(1-q)-(1-c)[1+q(r-1)]/3.
On[1,2], b is increasing because
  b'(r)=q[2c+(1-c)(r-2)/3]>0
(already positive at1 since7c>1), and b(2)=(1-c)(5c-1)/3<0.
For every r>=2 the exact scalar certificate in
UNBOUNDED_LOCAL_RATIO_FUNDING_THEOREM.txt gives
  b(r)<=(4/5)[c-q(r-1)].
Thus B B_i<=Bmax P+(4/5)Delta_+, and
  (3B/(8c))sum_i(B_i)_+^2
    <=kappa_P P+kappa Delta_+.
Together with the retention terms and total-increasing gains this proves
the first bound in(OD_bound), since the full raw total is at most CB.
The bound is uniform over the separately selected deviations, so taking
their separate suprema proves it for the actual envelope E.

Finally c-kappa=(2e-5)(e-1)/(10e)>0. Also 1/7<c<1/5 gives
Bmax<2/15 and kappa_P<7/150<1/7<c<Cc. Hence the grid saving is positive.
The exact replicated saturation profiles from
TWO_SIDED_LOCAL_FUNDING_THEOREM.txt have P=0, every local rho=2, and outer
ratio2, so E=N and sum E=C^2F. They make both active counts grow. This is
sharpness for the specified nested envelope on class(OD), not a lower
bound against all mechanisms or a claim of field-level novelty.

BOUNDARY OF THE RESULT.
This does not establish C^2 funding when the full outer capped ratio is
below2, especially when whole-owner deviations can switch the outer short
pool. Nor does it assert that the positive-grid saving is optimal. The
separate scalar inequalities and response lemmas are analytical proofs;
finite numerical tests are only falsification diagnostics.
