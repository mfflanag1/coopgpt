DIMENSION-FREE AGGREGATE RETENTION DEFECT
16 October 2026. Candidate structural theorem for independent audit.

Notation and the primitive are as in SATURATION_RETENTION_THEOREM.txt.
Write c=1/(2e), C=1+c. At one full positive-output branch let T be the
short total, rho its capped long ratio, z_i the primitive payments, and
Z=sum_i z_i=T[1+(rho-1)exp(1-rho)/2]. Define the nonnegative budget slack
  delta=CT-Z=T[c-(rho-1)exp(1-rho)/2].
Every owner i may choose its OWN arbitrary whole-owner underreport; these
underreports need not be consistent with one common reduced profile. Denote
its resulting own payment by z_i^i and resulting total payment by Z^i.
Then
  sum_i [ (Z-Z^i)/3 - (z_i-z_i^i) ]_+ <=350 delta.          (Q)
If rho>=9/5, the constant350 can be replaced by e/3<1. If rho>=2,
the left side is identically zero. Counts and capacities are unrestricted.

Consequently, for any number of branches with arbitrary mixed ownership,
let z_i and Z be the aggregate local accounts, and delta_total=CM-Z. Under
arbitrarily different whole-owner underreports indexed by i,
  sum_i [ (Z-Z^i)/3 - (z_i-z_i^i) ]_+ <=350 delta_total.    (Q_sum)
If all productive branches have rho>=9/5, replace350 by e/3. Unlike a
separate continuity estimate for each owner, this statement has NO owner
count factor. It is not a global funding bound for the nested envelope.

PROOF, HIGH-RATIO BRANCHES. Put lambda=1/3 and normalize the full T=1.
Let s_i and h_i be i's short and long accounts, and let
  w_i=s_i+min(h_i,1),   sum_i w_i=1+rho.
Implement an underreport by first reducing the short account, then the long
account. Study the potential F_i=z_i-lambda Z. Its total increase is exactly
the expression inside the positive part in(Q).

During the short-account stage the long vector is fixed, the short total
t lies in[1-s_i,1], and its capped ratio r(t)>=rho. The derivative argument
in Theorem A of SATURATION_RETENTION_THEOREM.txt applies without requiring
rho>=2: the total derivative is positive and the own/total derivative ratio
is at least
  f(r)=[1-q(1+r)]/[1+q(r^2-r-1)],  q=exp(1-r)/2.
The denominator is positive for r>=1. The bound is increasing in the number
of capped accounts: the relevant cross difference is r-1+q>0. Also
  f'(r)=q r[r-2+q(r+2)]/[1+q(r^2-r-1)]^2 >=0
for r>=1. Indeed the bracket equals1/2 at r=1 and has derivative
1-q(r+1)>=0. Finally f(9/5)>=1/3 is equivalent to exp(4/5)>=221/100;
the degree-four Taylor lower sum already proves the strict inequality.
Thus when rho>=9/5, reducing the short account cannot increase F_i.
Cap endpoints and t=0 follow by continuity. In the latter case there is
no subsequent productive long-account stage.

Fix the short total t>0 at the end of this first stage. Normalize it to1
temporarily, write R for its initial capped long ratio, x=min(h_i,t)/t,
and y for i's remaining short account divided by t. Reducing any uncapped
overflow above t has no effect. While the original short pool stays short,
let u be the reduction of i's capped long account. Direct differentiation
with respect to i's long account gives the potential derivative
  q(u)[1-x+y-lambda(2-R)+(1-lambda)u],                    (Q_derivative)
where q(u)=q(0)exp(u)<=1/2 in this regime. The bracket increases with u.

If R>=2, the initial bracket is nonnegative. The other capped long accounts
sum at least R-x>=1, so this orientation cannot switch. Consequently the
long stage also cannot increase F_i. In particular if full rho>=2 there is
no retention defect at all, even if the total local payment increases.

Suppose 9/5<=R<2 and write epsilon=2-R<=1/5. The initial bracket can be
negative only when x-y>1-lambda epsilon. Its negative magnitude is at most
lambda epsilon, and it increases at speed1-lambda. Integrating the possible
negative derivative, using q<=1/2, bounds the potential increase by
  t lambda^2 epsilon^2/[4(1-lambda)]=t epsilon^2/24.
Since
  c-(R-1)exp(1-R)/2=c[1-(1-epsilon)exp(epsilon)]
                         >=c epsilon^2/2,
and c>1/6, this increase is strictly less than half the budget slack at
this intermediate profile. That slack is at most the ORIGINAL delta:
its short total t is at most1, its ratio R is at least full rho, and the
slack per unit output decreases on[1,2]. Hence the increase is at most
delta/2.

There can be a later pool switch only if the other actual long accounts
have total v<1. They have v>=R-x>=R-1>=4/5. After the switch the original
long account becomes short; its fraction of the new short total is at most
1-v<=epsilon<=1/5, and the new capped long ratio r is at most1/v<=5/4.
Let m count capped accounts in this new long pool. The same differentiation
as above bounds the derivative of F_i with respect to the now-short own
account below by
  1-lambda-q[1-lambda+(epsilon-lambda)r+lambda r^2
                         -m(epsilon+lambda(r-2))].
Because epsilon+lambda(r-2)<=1/5+(1/3)(5/4-2)<0, the bracket is maximized
at m=r. The lower bound is therefore at least
  1-lambda-q(1-lambda+lambda r)>=2/3-1/2=1/6.
Here exp(1-r)(2+r)/6 decreases for r>=1. Thus further reduction after a
pool switch cannot increase the potential. All pool ties are continuous.

It remains essential to SUM errors without multiplying by the owner count.
Keep the sharper individual error bound delta/(12c), obtained before using
c>1/6 above. A positive error requires a negative long-stage derivative.
Let d_i=min(h_i,1) be the owner's FULL capped long account. At the stage
of that negative derivative, all other normalized capped accounts sum at
least their full capped accounts: min(h_k,t)/t>=min(h_k,1). Hence
  R>=x+rho-d_i.
The negative-derivative condition x-y>1-lambda(2-R) and x<=1 imply
  lambda(d_i-rho+1)>y>=0,
so d_i>rho-1. Since sum_i d_i=rho and rho>=9/5>3/2, at most TWO owners
can satisfy this inequality: three such accounts would sum to more than
3(rho-1)>rho. Thus the aggregate error is at most
  2 delta/(12c)=delta/(6c)=(e/3)delta.
If full rho>=2, there are no errors, as proved above. This count is over
actual owners and requires no role separation or owner splitting.

PROOF, LOW-RATIO BRANCHES. For1<=rho<9/5, a coarse estimate suffices because
delta is bounded below by a fixed fraction of T. The total primitive payment
Z is globally3/2-Lipschitz in the L1 norm of the resource coordinates. On a
cell where one coordinate belongs to the long pool its derivative is either
zero (if capped) or q(2-r), of absolute value at most1/2. For a coordinate
in the short pool its derivative is
  1+q[r(r-1)-m(r-2)-1].
For1<=r<=2 this lies between0 and C<3/2 (maximize at m=r). For r>=2 its
maximum occurs at m=0 and is at most1+(5/2)exp(-2)<3/2; its minimum is
positive. Continuous cap and pool boundaries preserve the Lipschitz bound.

Before evaluating any own underreport we may truncate that owner's full
long account at T: overflow above the original T affects neither the full
payment nor any later profile whose short account has only decreased.
The total size of the two relevant own changes is at most T w_i, where
w_i is defined after normalization. Primitive own monotonicity gives
z_i-z_i^i>=0. Consequently its retention defect is at most
  (1/3)(Z-Z^i)_+ <=(1/2)T w_i.
Summing gives at most(1/2)(1+rho)T<7T/5.

For rho<=9/5 the slack per unit output is at least
  c-(2/5)exp(-4/5) >1/250.
For completeness this last numerical inequality has rational certificates:
e<87/32, hence c>16/87; and exp(4/5) exceeds its degree-five Taylor sum.
Substitution of that rational sum gives16/87 minus its reciprocal times2/5
strictly greater than1/250. The bound e<87/32 follows by summing through
degree six and bounding the remaining tail geometrically by1/4410.
Thus7T/5<=350 delta. This proves(Q) for low ratios as well.

MULTIBRANCH STEP. For each owner the global potential change is the sum of
its branch potential changes. The positive part of a sum is at most the
sum of positive parts. Sum first over owners, then over branches, applying
the local bounds. Zero-output branches remain zero. This proves(Q_sum)
without adding owners, splitting owners, or correlating their underreports.

SCOPE AND REMAINING OBSTACLE. This is a dimension-free response inequality
and gives aggregate retention-defect convergence as CM-Z tends to zero.
The constants are deliberately simple; no optimality is claimed. The
nested actual-envelope funding conjecture still needs an outer slack/gain
inequality. In particular, branches with rho>2 may INCREASE their total
payment after an own underreport. The signed retention inequality remains
valid, but the saturation-face proof's premise Z^i<=Z then fails. No
neighborhood or global C^2 funding conclusion follows merely from(Q_sum).
