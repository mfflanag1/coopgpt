SHARP ACTUAL-ENVELOPE FUNDING WITH LOCAL RATIOS ON BOTH SIDES OF TWO
16 October 2026. Candidate theorem for independent audit.

Use the fixed actual owners, full report coordinates, and exponential
two-pool primitive of the adjacent theorem files. Write c=1/(2e), C=1+c.
At the evaluated full profile assume P=0, Z>0, every productive local
branch has capped long ratio rho_j in[9/5,11/5], and
  r0=sum_i min(H_i,Z)/Z>=2.
Let Delta=CM-Z. Then, allowing ALL whole-owner underreports, including
those that leave this class, the actual envelope satisfies
  sum_i E_i<=CZ+(e/3)Delta
            =C^2F-(C-e/3)Delta<=C^2F.                   (TS)
The sharper first bound separates the two local regimes. Write Delta_-
for the sum of local budget slacks on branches with rho_j<=2, and Delta_+
for the sum on branches with rho_j>2. Then
  sum_i E_i<=CZ+(e/3)Delta_-+(exp(1/5)/2)Delta_+.         (TS_split)
Counts and mixed ownership are unrestricted. F=M under these hypotheses.
This is a bound for the actual envelope of the specified rule, and its C^2
constant is sharp within this class by the exact saturation examples.
It remains a restricted-class theorem, not an unrestricted funding result
or an all-mechanism lower bound.

For clarity, sharpness also persists with BOTH active counts growing.
Take any positive integer r and r disjoint copies of the following local
block, retaining all4r actual owners and all2r productive branches. A block
has owners A,B,D,W. Branch1 has hardware A:1+2c, B:1, and local power W:1.
Branch2 has hardware A:1, D:1+2c, and local power W:1. All omitted reports
are zero. Each local ratio is2, each block has M=2 and Z=2C, and its
hardware total is4C with each owner's hardware at most2C. In the full
r-block profile the outer capped sum is therefore4rC=2Z. The saturation
theorem gives E=N and sum E=C^2F. This is an unbounded active-owner and
active-branch family of exact attainments for the rule's bound.

LEMMA: AGGREGATE TOTAL-INCREASE CHARGE ON A HIGH-RATIO BRANCH.
Consider a branch with full short total T and rho=2+epsilon, where
0<=epsilon<=1/5. For arbitrarily different own underreports indexed by i,
  sum_i [ (Z^i-Z)-(z_i-z_i^i) ]_+<=exp(1/5)delta,         (TS_gain)
where delta=CT-Z. More precisely exp(1/5) can be exp(epsilon).

PROOF. Normalize T=1, let x_i=min(h_i,1) denote the full capped long
account and y_i the full short account. The other long accounts have
capped sum rho-x_i>=1, so they alone cover the FULL short total. The
original short pool therefore remains short under every own underreport.
Implement an underreport by reducing the long account FIRST, followed by
the short account. Study the potential J_i=z_i+Z. During the second stage,
both own payment and total payment weakly decrease: own payment is
monotone, while total payment has a strictly positive short-coordinate
derivative, as established in the quantitative retention theorem. Thus
this second stage cannot increase J_i.

Overflow above1 in the long account has no effect. On reducing its capped
long account by u, direct differentiation of J_i with respect to that
long account yields
  q(u)[1-x_i+y_i-epsilon+2u],
with q(u)=exp(1-rho+u)/2. Its bracket increases with u. An increase in
J_i when the report is REDUCED is possible only if initially
  x_i-y_i>1-epsilon.
This forces x_i>1-epsilon. There are at most two such owners, since
three would contribute more than3(1-epsilon)>=12/5>rho to the full
capped long sum. The potentially negative derivative lasts for at most
epsilon/2 units, during which rho-u>=2 and therefore q(u)<=c.
Its integral is at most c epsilon^2/4. This bounds every positive gain
of J_i, including gains from finite deviations, because the derivative
can switch sign only once and the subsequent short stage cannot help.

Meanwhile
  delta=c[1-(1+epsilon)exp(-epsilon)]
       =c integral_0^epsilon v exp(-v)dv
       >=c exp(-epsilon)epsilon^2/2.
Each of the at most two positive gains is at most exp(epsilon)delta/2.
Summing and rescaling proves(TS_gain). The epsilon=0 case follows either
directly from the derivative or by continuity. No fictitious or split
owners occur in this argument.

UNIVERSAL OUTER TOTAL-INCREASE LEMMA.
Fix the full outer hardware vector, its own coordinate a, and an opposing
own account b. While the opposing pool is short and has total t>=b, the
own payment is V(t,b)=(1-q(t))b+q(t)min(a,t). Its partial derivative in b
is1-q(t)>=1/2. Its partial derivative in t is at most1/2.
For the latter, put r=sum min(H_k,t)/t and let m count capped hardware
accounts. Then q'=q(r-m)/t. If a<t, the derivative is q'(a-b)<=qr.
If a>t, this owner's cap counts toward m>=1, and the derivative is
  q+q'(t-b)<=q(1+r-m)<=qr.
Finally qr=(r/2)exp(1-r)<=1/2 for r>=1. Negative derivatives cause no
problem for this upper bound; continuity supplies cap endpoints.
Consequently for t>=t0>=b0>=b,
  V(t,b)-V(t0,b0)<=(t-t0)/2-(b0-b)/2,                   (TS_outer)
provided the opposing pool stays short throughout this interval.

ACCOUNT AND OUTPUT BOUNDS FOR THE ENLARGED LOCAL INTERVAL.
At a full local branch, q>=exp(-6/5)/2>3/20. For local power short,
its payment minus its own hardware is at most(1-q)T<17T/20. For
hardware short the same difference is at most qT<=T/2. Hence
  z_i-H_i<=(17/20)M.                                    (TS_account)
The surplus g(r)=(r-1)exp(1-r)/2 increases up to2 and decreases thereafter.
At9/5 it exceeds1/6 as in the preceding theorem; at11/5 it exceeds
(6/5)(3/20)=9/50>1/6. Thus
  Z/M>7/6,   (z_i-H_i)/Z<51/70.                         (TS_output)
Here exp(6/5)<10/3 has an elementary rational certificate: e<87/32, and
exp(1/5)<1+1/5+(1/50)/(1-1/15)=171/140, so their product is less
than14877/4480<10/3. We also use c<7/38, from e>19/7.

PROOF OF THE FUNDING THEOREM. Choose a separate whole-owner underreport
for every owner, and write G_i=Z^i-Z, D_i=z_i-z_i^i>=0. Restore only that
owner's outer hardware to its full report, which weakly increases its
payment. The restored hardware total is at least2Z>7M/3>CM, while every
underreported local total is at most CM. Thus the opposing pool remains
short for all intermediate totals used below.

For owners with G_i>=0, apply(TS_outer) with t0=Z, t=Z^i, b0=z_i and
b=z_i^i. Their payment gain over full N_i is at most
  (G_i-D_i)/2 <=[G_i-D_i]_+/2.
For branches with full rho<=2, total local payment cannot increase under
underreports, by Preliminary Fact1 of NEAR_SATURATION_FUNDING_THEOREM.txt;
own payment cannot increase either. Such a branch contributes no positive
part to G_i-D_i. For the other branches apply(TS_gain). Positive parts
of sums are at most sums of positive parts. Consequently the SUM of these
owners' gains is at most exp(1/5)Delta_+/2.

For owners with G_i<0, normalize full Z to1 and put a=H_i/Z, z=z_i/Z,
t=Z^i/Z<=1, b=z_i^i/Z. Set
  epsilon_i=[(1-t)/3-(z-b)]_+.
The quantitative retention theorem charges these defects ONLY to branches
with rho<2; branches with rho>=2 have zero retention defect. Therefore
  sum_i epsilon_i<=(e/3)Delta_-/Z.
The full outer coefficient q0<=c and q0-q(t)<=2c(1-t) on[1/2,1], by
the outer coefficient lemma already proved. The argument proving
V(t,b)<=V(1,z)+epsilon_i remains valid with the enlarged account bound:
if a>=z there is no gain; if a<z then z-a<51/70. For t<1/2,
  q0(z-a)<(7/38)(51/70)<1/6,
so b<=z-1/6+epsilon_i<=V(1,z)+epsilon_i. For t>=1/2 and b>a,t>a,
the possible gain is bounded by epsilon_i because
  (1-c)/3-2c(z-a)>
     31/114-(7/19)(51/70)=31/114-51/190=1/285>0.
The remaining cases b<=a or t<=a have no gain. These are the same
convex-combination and coefficient arguments as in(NS_owner), with the
new constants explicitly checked. Summing yields at most(e/3)Delta_-.

Add the two owner classes and the full raw total, which is at most CZ.
This gives(TS_split), uniformly for every tuple of independent deviations.
Supremizing owner by owner proves the same statement for E. Finally
exp(1/5)/2<e/3, since exp(4/5)>3/2. Thus(TS) follows from
Delta=Delta_-+Delta_+ and F=M. No compactness or maximum attainment is
needed, and no underreport is required to remain in the stated class.

EXACT DIAGNOSTIC EXERCISING THE NEW CASE.
There are four owners I,A,B,W and two branches. On branch1 put hardware
1,1,1/5,0 respectively, and local power0,0,0,1. On branch2 give I
hardware3 and give everyone local power0. All grid reports are zero.
Put q=exp(-6/5)/2, Z=1+(6/5)q. The productive local ratio is11/5.
The outer full capped ratio is1+(6/5)/Z>2 because Z<6/5.
Owner I has full local payment q and outer hardware4, which remains
capped under a sufficiently small underreport of its branch1 hardware.

At the full point the left derivative of its local payment in that report
is zero, while dZ/dh_I1=q(2-11/5)=-q/5<0. The outer coefficient is
Q(t)=exp(-(6/5)/t)/2 near t=Z. Its payment has derivative in t
  Q(t)[1+(6/5)(t-q)/t^2]>0.
Thus dN_I/dh_I1<0, and a sufficiently small underreport strictly raises
both the local total and its raw payment. This full profile satisfies the
new theorem, but was excluded by the earlier local-ratio upper bound2.
It shows explicitly why the total-increase charge is needed. This is a
failure of raw incentives only, not of the actual-envelope funding bound.
