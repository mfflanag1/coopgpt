AN UNBOUNDED CLASS WITH A SHARP C-SQUARED ACTUAL-ENVELOPE BOUND
16 October 2026. Candidate theorem for independent audit.

Use the fixed-owner two-level model and exponential primitive of the
adjacent files. Write c=1/(2e), C=1+c. Assume at the evaluated full profile:
 (i) P=0;
 (ii) every productive local branch has capped long ratio rho_j in[9/5,2];
 (iii) Z=sum_i z_i>0 and the full outer capped hardware ratio satisfies
       r0=sum_i min(H_i,Z)/Z>=2.
There is no restriction on owner count, branch count, mixed ownership,
unused hardware, or the underreport profile being tested. In particular
underreports need not preserve any of conditions(ii)--(iii).
Put Delta=CM-Z. Then the actual own-report envelope satisfies
  sum_i E_i <=C Z+(e/3)Delta
             =C^2 F-(C-e/3)Delta <=C^2 F.                (NS)
Here F=M since H>=2Z>=2M. The constant C^2 is sharp on this class as a
bound for this particular envelope rule: the saturation examples attain it.
This is not an all-mechanism lower bound or a global C^2 theorem.

The class contains arbitrarily many owners and branches with arbitrary
mixed ownership. Its local ratio restriction has positive width, and its
outer restriction is a full one-sided region, not an equality constraint.
At least some full-profile restrictions remain; this does not resolve the
unrestricted envelope funding problem.

PRELIMINARY FACT 1: LOCAL TOTALS CANNOT INCREASE.
If a full branch has short total T and capped long ratio at most2, its total
primitive payment is at least that at ANY coordinatewise reduced profile.
For a direct proof, retain the original short/long names. If the new short
pool is the original long resource, the new actual total of the original
short resource is at most T. The primitive total is at most its actual long
total, hence at most T, which is at most the original payment.
Otherwise let t<=T be the new short total and S(t)=sum_k min(h_k,t) for the
FULL original long vector. The reduced capped long ratio is at most S(t)/t,
so its total payment is at most
  G(t)=t[1+g(min(S(t)/t,2))],  g(r)=(r-1)exp(1-r)/2.
This uses that g increases up to2 and then decreases. Where S(t)/t>=2,
G(t)=Ct is increasing. On other cap cells it is the full-long-vector total,
whose derivative is positive by the derivative formula in the quantitative
retention proof. G is continuous across all boundaries. Thus G(t)<=G(T),
and G(T) is the full branch total because its full ratio is at most2.
Consequently every own underreport has aggregate local total Z^i<=Z.

PRELIMINARY FACT 2: ACCOUNT AND TOTAL BOUNDS.
At every full local branch the coefficient q lies in[c,1/2]. If local power
is short, its owner's payment minus its hardware is at most(1-q)T<=
(1-c)T. If hardware is short, that difference is q[min(l_i,T)-h_i]
and is at most qT<=T/2<=(1-c)T. Zero-output branches contribute zero
payment and nonnegative hardware. Hence, for every owner,
  z_i-H_i<=(1-c)M.                                      (NS_account)
Also g is increasing on[9/5,2], and
  Z/M>=1+(2/5)exp(-4/5)>7/6.                            (NS_total)
For the last inequality, convexity gives exp(4/5)<1/5+(4/5)e<12/5,
using e<11/4. We will also use c<3/16, from e>8/3.

PRELIMINARY FACT 3: OUTER COEFFICIENT CONTROL.
Normalize the full Z to1 and fix the full hardware vector. Write
  q(t)=exp(1-sum_k min(H_k,t)/t)/2, q0=q(1), 0<t<=1.
The assumption r0>=2 gives q(t)<=q0<=c. On[1/2,1], q is2c-Lipschitz.
Indeed, on a cell with m capped accounts and uncapped hardware total A,
  q'(t)=exp(1-m-A/t)A/(2t^2).
If m=0, A=r0>=2, so monotonicity of A exp(-A/t) for A>=2>=t gives
q'(t)<=exp(1-2/t)/t^2<=1/e=2c. If m=1, the full capped contribution
of that one account is at most1, hence A>=r0-1>=1; the bound becomes
q'(t)<=exp(-1/t)/(2t^2)<=2/e^2<2c. If m>=2, the inequality
A exp(-A/t)<=t/e gives q'(t)<=exp(-m)/(2t)<=exp(-m)<2c.
Continuity supplies cap boundaries. Therefore
  0<=q0-q(t)<=2c(1-t) for1/2<=t<=1.                     (NS_q)

PROOF OF(NS). Choose an arbitrary, separate whole-owner underreport for
each owner i. Normalize by the FULL Z. Let a=H_i/Z, z=z_i/Z, t=Z^i/Z,
and b=z_i^i/Z for this owner's particular reduced profile. Then
  0<=t<=1, 0<=b<=min(z,t).
Define its nonnegative normalized retention defect
  epsilon_i=[(1-t)/3-(z-b)]_+.
The strengthened quantitative retention theorem gives
  sum_i epsilon_i<=(e/3)Delta/Z.                         (NS_defect)
Underreporting own hardware at the outer level can only decrease the
primitive payment. Restore that hardware to its full report, retaining the
reduced local accounts. The opposing pool of total t is short, because the
full hardware total is at least2. The resulting own payment is
  V(t,b)=(1-q(t))b+q(t)min(a,t).
Its full raw payment is V0=(1-q0)z+q0 min(a,1). We claim
  V(t,b)<=V0+epsilon_i.                                 (NS_owner)
The zero-total case is immediate.

If a>=z, at t<z one has V<=t<=z<=V0. At t>=z, replace b by z and use
monotonicity of q(t) and min(a,t)-z to obtain V<=V0. Suppose a<z instead.
Then a<1, V0=z-q0(z-a)>a, and(NS_account)--(NS_total) give
  z-a<=(1-c)M/Z<6(1-c)/7.

If t<1/2, the defect definition gives b<=z-1/6+epsilon_i. Meanwhile
  q0(z-a)<=6c(1-c)/7<6c/7<9/56<1/6.
Thus b<=V0+epsilon_i, while min(a,t)<=a<V0. Their convex combination
V(t,b) also satisfies(NS_owner).

If1/2<=t<=1 and either t<=a or b<=a, then V<=a<V0. Otherwise t>a and
b>a, so using(NS_q) and the retention-defect definition gives
  V0-V(t,b)
    =(1-q0)(z-b)-(q0-q(t))(b-a)
    >=(1-t)[(1-c)/3-2c(z-a)]-(1-c)epsilon_i
    >=(1-t)(1-c)[1/3-12c/7]-(1-c)epsilon_i
    >=-(1-c)epsilon_i.
For the first inequality use z-b>=0 and then z-b>=(1-t)/3-epsilon_i;
this avoids reversing a coefficient inequality when that last expression
is negative. The square bracket is positive since c<3/16 yields
1/3-12c/7>1/3-9/28=1/84. This proves(NS_owner) in all cases.

Sum(NS_owner) over the independently chosen deviations and rescale by Z.
The full outer primitive total is Z[1+g(r0)]<=CZ. Together with(NS_defect)
this gives the first inequality in(NS). Taking the supremum separately for
each owner is legitimate because the owner set is finite and the argument
holds for every tuple of independently chosen deviations; no attainment or
common deviation is assumed. Since Z=CM-Delta and F=M, the displayed
identity follows. Finally C-e/3>0, for example e/3<11/12<1<C.

SHARPNESS AND SCOPE. The existing four-owner, two-branch calibration with
P=0, both local ratios2, and outer ratio2 has sum E=C^2F by the saturation
theorem. This proves sharpness of this envelope-rule bound on the present
class. It does not assert that this game requires that funding from every
truthful coalition-covering mechanism. The entire argument retains the
same actual owners and evaluates every coordinatewise own underreport.

PROFILE-CONDITION EXTENSION, INCLUDING OUTER RATIOS BELOW2.
Keep P=0 and the local interval[9/5,2], but replace condition(iii) by the
following explicit sufficient conditions. Let r0=sum min(H_i,Z)/Z>=1,
q0=exp(1-r0)/2, A0=(1-c)M/Z, and
  L0=max(1/e,2/(e r0)).
If
  q0 A0<=1/6,   L0 A0<=(1-q0)/3,                        (NS_extended)
then the same funding bound(NS) holds. These are full-profile scalar
conditions; individual underreports still need not satisfy them.

To prove the extension, the outer coefficient has Lipschitz constant L0
on[1/2,1]. On a cap cell with m=0, A=r0 and its derivative has global
maximum2/(e r0), attained at t=r0/2 if that point is in the interval.
With m>=1, A exp(-A/t)<=t/e gives q'(t)<=exp(-m)/(2t)<=1/e.
The preceding proof then uses z-a<=A0, q0 A0<=1/6 in its small-total
case, and (1-q0)/3-L0 A0>=0 in its large-total case. In that case keep
the exact coefficient1-q0 rather than replacing it by1-c. All other
arguments, including the aggregate defect bound and F=M, are unchanged.

In particular(NS_extended) holds uniformly for r0>=39/20, so the funding
theorem includes a neighborhood below the outer saturation ratio2.
Here are rational bounds certifying this nonessential concrete threshold.
For39/20<=r0<=2, q0<=exp(-19/20)/2<97/500: the degree-four Taylor lower
sum for exp(19/20) exceeds250/97. Also e>19/7 and e<11/4 give
  L0<=40/(39e)<280/741,   A0<6(1-c)/7<54/77.
Consequently
  q0 A0<(97/500)(54/77)<1/6,
  L0 A0<(280/741)(54/77)<403/1500<=(1-q0)/3.
All comparisons are rational. The elementary lower bound e>19/7 follows
already from its degree-five Taylor sum. For r0>=2, the main theorem's
constants apply. No optimization or significance claim attaches to39/20;
the profile-dependent criterion(NS_extended) is the more informative form.

THE ENVELOPE IS SUBSTANTIVE EVEN INSIDE THIS CLASS.
Here is a full-profile raw-incentive failure satisfying the theorem's
hypotheses. There are four actual owners W,A,B,D and three branches, the
third having zero local power. Put q=exp(-9/10)/2 and
  Z=2+(9/10)q+c.
On branch1 give A hardware1, W local power1, D local power9/10. On
branch2 give A and B hardware1 each, W local power1. On branch3 give
D hardware2Z-3, and no local power to anyone. All omitted accounts and
all grid accounts are zero. The local ratios are19/10 and2, and all
outer hardware accounts 2,1,2Z-3 are strictly below Z, with sum2Z.
Owner W has no hardware and local payment z_W=q+1-c.

Decrease only W's branch1 local report from1. At the full point the
left derivative of its local payment is q(1-1)=0, whereas the derivative
of total local payment is q(2-19/10)=q/10>0. The outer hardware caps
remain inactive near this point, so the derivative of W's raw payment is
  dN_W/dl_W1=-(2c/Z)z_W(q/10)<0.
Thus a sufficiently small strict underreport raises W's raw payment:
E_W>N_W at this profile. The funding theorem still applies to its full
actual envelope. This diagnostic is an exact derivative argument, not an
incentive obstruction for every possible mechanism.
