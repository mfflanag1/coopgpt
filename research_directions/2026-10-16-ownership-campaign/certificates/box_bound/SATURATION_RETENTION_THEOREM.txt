LOSS RETENTION AT LOCAL SATURATION AND EXACT ENVELOPE ON THE C-SQUARED FACE
16 October 2026. Candidate structural theorem for independent audit.

Write c=1/(2e), C=1+c, and lambda=(1-3c)/(1+c). In particular0<c<1/5,
0<lambda<1. The elementary bound e>1+1+1/2+1/6=8/3 suffices for c<1/5.
The primitive and nested rule are defined in the adjacent box theorem.

THEOREM A: SHARP OWN-LOSS RETENTION
Consider one positive-output primitive branch. Let its full short-pool total
be T>0, and suppose its full capped long-pool ratio is exactly2. For any fixed
owner i and any coordinatewise underreport of both its accounts, write z_i'
for its resulting payment and Z' for the resulting total payment. At the full
profile write z_i and Z=CT. Then
  z_i-z_i' >= lambda (Z-Z') >=0.                            (A)
The coefficient lambda is the largest universal coefficient in(A), even when
i owns only the short resource. Counts and capacities are unrestricted.

PROOF. Normalize T=1 and call the two resources short and long according to
the FULL profile. Let s in[0,1] be i's full short account and h its long
account. Its capped long account is at most1. Because the full capped long
sum is2, all OTHER long accounts have capped sum at least1, hence actual
sum at least1. Consequently they alone cover the original short total.
The short resource therefore remains the short pool throughout every own
underreport. Its new total is t in[1-s,1]. We may implement an arbitrary
underreport in two steps: first reduce the short account while keeping h;
then reduce the long account at that new short total.

For the first step, let S(t)=sum_k min(h_k,t), r(t)=S(t)/t. Concavity of
min(h_k,t) and S(1)=2 imply S(t)>=2t, so r(t)>=2 for0<t<=1. Away from cap
breakpoints let m be the number of long accounts capped at t and let
A be the sum of the uncapped accounts. Thus r=m+A/t and
  q(t)=exp(1-r)/2, q'(t)=q(t)(r-m)/t.
Owner i's short account is s-1+t in[0,t], and its capped long account is
x(t)=min(h,t). Its payment and the total payment are
  z_i(t)=(1-q)(s-1+t)+q x(t),
  Z(t)=t+q(S(t)-t).
Since x'(t) is0 or1 and0<=s-1+t<=t, differentiation gives
  z_i'(t)=1-q+q x'(t)+q'(t)[x(t)-(s-1+t)]
            >=1-q(1+r-m),
  Z'(t)=1+q[r(r-1)-m(r-2)-1] >0.
The lower bound for z_i' is positive because r>=2,
q(1+r)<=3c<1. At fixed r the derivative ratio lower bound
  [1-q(1+r-m)]/[1+q(r(r-1)-m(r-2)-1)]
is nondecreasing in m: its numerator increases, while its positive denominator
decreases, as m grows. It is therefore at least
  f(r)=[1-q(1+r)]/[1+q(r^2-r-1)].
For r>=2 this function is nondecreasing. Indeed q'=-q with respect to r,
and direct differentiation gives
  f'(r)=q r[r-2+q(r+2)]/[1+q(r^2-r-1)]^2 >=0.
Thus z_i'(t)>=f(2) Z'(t)=lambda Z'(t). Integrate over the finitely many cap
pieces from the reduced t to1. Continuity supplies all cap/tie endpoints;
t=0, when admissible, follows by the continuous zero-output limit.

For the second step, keep that short total t fixed and reduce i's long report.
The other short accounts sum1-s<=t. The other capped long accounts at t sum
at least t: each min(h_k,t)>=t min(h_k,1), and their full capped sum is at
least1. Their total payment therefore has the form
  (1-s)+q[sum_(k!=i)min(h_k,t)-(1-s)],
with a nonnegative bracket. Reducing i's long report weakly increases q,
so the opponents' total payment weakly increases. Meanwhile i's own payment
weakly decreases by primitive own-account monotonicity. If its payment loss
in this step is D>=0 and the opponents' gain is A>=0, the total payment loss
is D-A. Therefore the increase of the left side minus lambda times the total
loss is D-lambda(D-A)=(1-lambda)D+lambda A>=0. This preserves the inequality
from the first step, regardless of the sign of this step's total change.
Finally Z'<=CT'=Ct<=C=Z by the primitive total bound, proving the second
inequality in(A). Rescale T. QED.

SHARPNESS. Owner i holds the entire short capacity1 and no long account.
Three other owners each hold long capacity2/3. At the full profile the capped
long ratio is2 and all long accounts are strictly below the short total.
Reduce i's short report infinitesimally. Here m=0, x(t)=0 and i's short
account equals t, so both derivative inequalities above are equalities at
t=1, and its own-loss/total-loss ratio tends to f(2)=lambda. No larger
uniform constant is valid. This sharpness is for the response inequality,
not an all-mechanism funding lower bound.

MULTIBRANCH COROLLARY. Suppose every productive branch has full capped
long-pool ratio2. Branches of zero output remain zero under own underreports.
Summing(A) over arbitrary branches shows that every fixed owner i satisfies
  z_i-z_i' >= lambda (Z-Z_i'),                              (A_sum)
where Z=sum_k z_k=CM and Z_i' is the total local payment after that particular
owner's underreport. The same actual owners are retained; different owners'
underreports need not be the same. The owner may hold both resources on many
branches; the orientation of the short pool may differ from branch to branch.

THEOREM B: THE ACTUAL ENVELOPE EQUALS N ON THE FULL SATURATION FACE
Assume P=0. Suppose every positive-output local branch has capped long-pool
ratio2, and write T0=Z=CM>0. Suppose also
  sum_i min(H_i,T0)=2T0.                                  (B_face)
Then the actual own-report envelope satisfies
  E_i=N_i for every owner i,
  sum_i E_i=C^2 F.
This holds for arbitrary mixed ownership, arbitrary finite owner and branch
counts, and all coordinatewise whole-owner underreports from such a profile.
It proves safety of the complete raw-bound saturation face. It does not
establish a neighborhood or a global C^2 envelope bound.

EXACT CHARACTERIZATION OF THIS FACE. At every profile with F>0,
  sum_i N_i=C^2 F
holds if and only if all the hypotheses of Theorem B hold. Indeed,
  sum_i N_i<=C min(H,P+Z),   Z<=CM.
If F=H, the first bound is at most CF<C^2F. Otherwise F=P+M<H, and
  sum_i N_i<=C(P+Z)<=C(P+CM)=C^2F-C(C-1)P.
Equality forces P=0 and Z=CM, as well as equality in the outer primitive
bound and min(H,Z)=Z. For a positive-output primitive the total/T is
1+(rho-1)exp(1-rho)/2, whose unique maximum C occurs at rho=2.
Thus Z=CM forces rho=2 on every productive branch. Equality in the outer
primitive forces its long capped ratio2; its short pool is Z, since
min(H,Z)=Z. This is exactly(B_face). Conversely these conditions give
sum_i N_i=C^2M=C^2F. Arbitrary hardware or local accounts on zero-output
branches are allowed: they do not contribute to Z but their hardware
still contributes to the outer H_i. The proof does not remove those owners
or those resources. If F=0, all underreport profiles have zero production,
so all actual-envelope payments are zero separately.

PRELIMINARY ACCOUNT BOUND. On every full productive local branch the coefficient
is c. If local power is short, its payment to i is at most c h_i+(1-c)T.
If hardware is short, h_i<=T and
  (1-c)h_i+c min(l_i,T) <= c h_i+(1-c)T,
because (1-2c)h_i+c min(l_i,T)<=(1-c)T. A zero-output branch contributes
zero payment and nonnegative hardware. Summing gives
  z_i <= c H_i+(1-c)M.                                    (B_account)

OUTER LIPSCHITZ LEMMA. Normalize T0=1 and retain the full hardware vector H.
More generally, allow its capped sum at1 to be ANY r0>=2; Theorem B itself
uses r0=2. Define
  q(t)=exp(1-sum_i min(H_i,t)/t)/2.
For0<t<=1, concavity gives sum min(H_i,t)>=r0*t>=2t, hence
q(t)<=q(1)<=c.
On t in[1/2,1], q is2c-Lipschitz. To see this on each cap cell, let m count
capped hardware coordinates and A sum the uncapped coordinates. Then
  q'(t)=exp(1-m-A/t) A/(2t^2).
If m=0, A is the full hardware sum and is at least r0>=2. The function
A exp(-A/t) decreases for A>=2>=t, so
  q'<=exp(1-2/t)/t^2<=1/e=2c,
since the last expression increases on0<t<=1. If m=1, the one currently
capped account contributes at most1 to the capped sum at full total1;
all other accounts are below t<=1. Hence A>=r0-1>=1. The map
A exp(-A/t) decreases for A>=1>=t. Thus
  q'(t)<=exp(-1/t)/(2t^2)<=2/e^2<1/e
on[1/2,1]; its maximum on this interval occurs at t=1/2. If m>=2, use
A exp(-A/t)<=t/e to obtain
  q'(t)<=exp(-m)/(2t)<=exp(-m)<=1/e^2<1/e.
Continuity across cap breakpoints completes the Lipschitz bound. Thus
  0<=q(1)-q(t)<=2c(1-t)   for1/2<=t<=1.
For0<t<1/2 the same bound follows from q(1)<=c<=2c(1-t), so it holds
on the entire interval(0,1]. In the r0=2 saturation case q(1)=c and hence
  0<=c-q(t)<=2c(1-t)   for1/2<=t<=1.                       (B_q)

ENVELOPE PROOF. Normalize all accounts by T0, so M=1/C. Fix owner i and write
a=H_i/T0 and z=z_i/T0. Under an arbitrary own underreport, let t=Z_i'/T0 and
b=z_i'/T0. We have0<=t<=1,0<=b<=min(z,t), and(A_sum) gives
  b<=z-lambda(1-t).                                      (B_retention)
Restore only i's outer hardware to its full report. Primitive own monotonicity
can only increase its payment. The full hardware sum is at least2, so the
opposing pool of total t<=1 is short. Its resulting payment is therefore
  V(t,b)=(1-q(t))b+q(t)min(a,t).
The full payment is V(1,z)=(1-c)z+c min(a,1).

If a>=z, the common-total argument applies: at t<z, V<=t<=z<=V(1,z);
at t>=z, first bound b by z, then use monotonicity of q(t) and min(a,t)-z
on[z,1]. Thus V(t,b)<=V(1,z).

Suppose a<z. Since z<=1, a<1, and the full payment is
  V0=(1-c)z+ca=z-c(z-a)>a.
By(B_account), z<=ca+(1-c)/C, so
  z-a<=(1-c)/C.
If t<1/2, retention gives b<=z-lambda/2. The inequality
  lambda/2 > c(1-c)/C >= c(z-a)
follows from1-5c+2c^2>0, because c<1/5. Hence b<V0. Also min(a,t)<=a<V0,
so the convex-combination formula gives V(t,b)<V0.

For1/2<=t<=1, if t<=a or b<=a, then V<=a<V0. Otherwise t>a and b>a.
Using(B_q), retention, and b-a<=z-a gives
  V0-V(t,b)
    =(1-c)(z-b)-(c-q(t))(b-a)
    >=(1-t)[(1-c)lambda-2c(z-a)]
    >=(1-t)(1-c)(1-5c)/C >=0.
Thus every actual underreport payment is at most its full N_i, after even
allowing the favorable hardware restoration. Since the full report belongs
to the envelope domain, E_i=N_i.

Finally(B_face) makes the outer primitive's total equal C*T0=C^2M. Also
H>=2T0=2CM>M and P=0, so F=M. This proves the total equality. QED.

STATUS
The complete proofs above are independent of any optimization pass count.
Finite checks may corroborate them but cannot replace them. The global
C^2-envelope conjecture remains unresolved. This result excludes an entire
natural extremal face, not all nearby profiles and not all possible witnesses.
