PROVED GENERAL SAFE REGIME
Let B_i=p_i+z_i and K=P+CM. If B_i<=H_i for every owner at the full profile,
then even the original range rule R satisfies
  sum_i E_i <= sum_i R_i <= C^2 F.
Counts, capacities, and mixed ownership are unrestricted. This is a
profilewise structural regime, not an all-profile ownership-domain theorem.

Proof. If K=0 or H=0, every payment is zero. Suppose first 0<K<=H. For any
owner's relaxed opposing vector c, its total T<=K. Its own c_i<=B_i<=H_i.
The short-pool optimization gives
  Phi_i(H,c)<= (1-q_K)B_i+q_K min(H_i,K),
  q_K=exp(1-sum_i min(H_i,K)/K)/2.
For T<B_i, the payment is at mostT<=B_i; for T>=B_i, restore the own account
toB_i and use that q(T) and min(H_i,T)-B_i are nonnegative and nondecreasing.
Since sum_iB_i=P+Z<=K, summing yields
  sum_iR_i <=K[1+q_K(rho_K-1)]<=CK,
where rho_K=sum min(H_i,K)/K. Here F=P+M, so CK<=C^2F.

Now let K>=H>0. In the short opposing regime each owner's payment is at
most(B_i+H_i)/2<=H_i. In the long regime the own capped account is at most
B_i<=H_i, and the capped total ratio is at mostK/H. Hence
  Phi_i<=H_i+exp(1-K/H)(B_i-H_i)/2<=H_i.
Thus sumR_i<=H. If F=H this is efficient; otherwise F=P+M and
H<=K=P+CM<=C(P+M)=CF. This also proves the claimed C^2 bound. QED.

CONSEQUENCE
Any violation of the C^2 target requires at least one owner with B_i>H_i.
Those are exactly the positive opposing-account excesses for which reducing
other local responses can help an outer payment. This signed-gap split is a
concrete restriction on a valid actual-envelope counterexample.

REJECTED SHORTCUT: P=0 DOES NOT MAKE N TRUTHFUL
A particularly simple one-branch example has hardware owners(.5,.5), a pure
local-power owner with capacity1, and all shared p=0. Full local payments are
(.25,.25,.5), so the outer power payment is.25. If that owner reports.9,
  q=exp(-1/9)/2,
  z_power=.9(1-q), Z=.9+.1q,
and its outer payment is
  z_power[1-exp(1-1/Z)/2] >.25.
Thus even a hardware-free local owner may benefit from an underreport with
P=0. This is only a shortcut falsification; the full funding is far below C^2.
check_zero_grid_monotonicity.py records another finite diagnostic failure,
with mixed ownership. Neither is an all-mechanism impossibility.

SATURATION GEOMETRY (EXPLORATORY, NOT A GLOBAL PROOF)
The primitive total is T[1+(rho-1)exp(1-rho)/2], maximized at rho=2.
Therefore raw nested funding can attain C^2F when local capped ratios and
the outer capped ratio are all2. A diagonal-envelope counterexample near
that face must compare incentive gain with second-order budget slack.
One transparent perturbed family has local hardware(1,1,epsilon), local
power1, and unused hardware chosen so the deviating unit-hardware owner's
outer account is capped while the other outer hardware sums toZ0. Put
  c=1/(2e), q0=c exp(-epsilon), Z0=1+q0(1+epsilon).
When that owner reduces its first hardware account from1 to1-delta,
  zi_delta=q0 exp(delta)(1-delta),
  Z_delta=1+q0 exp(delta)(1+epsilon-delta),
  Q_delta=exp(-Z0/Z_delta)/2,
  Ni_delta=zi_delta+Q_delta(Z_delta-zi_delta).
The infinitesimal optimal gain is only about8.4% of the local raw C^2-budget
slack. This expansion is a route diagnostic, not a theorem excluding all
nearby profiles or all diagonal deviations. It suggests that any general
proof must retain local and outer capped-ratio slack simultaneously.
