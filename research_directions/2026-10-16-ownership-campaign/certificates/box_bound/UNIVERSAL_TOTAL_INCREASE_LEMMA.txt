UNIVERSAL TOTAL-INCREASE CHARGE AND A BROADER FUNDING CERTIFICATE
16 October 2026. Candidate theorem for independent audit.

Keep the same exponential primitive, c=1/(2e), C=1+c, fixed actual owners,
and arbitrary coordinatewise whole-owner underreports indexed separately
by owner. At ANY branch with full capped long ratio rho>=2,
  sum_i[(Z^i-Z)-(z_i-z_i^i)]_+<=2(CT-Z).                 (U)
There is no upper bound on rho, owner count, or capacities.

PROOF. Normalize the full short total T=1. Other long accounts alone have
actual total at least rho-min(h_i,1)>=1, so the original short pool stays
short under every own underreport. Reduce own long account first, then own
short account. The short stage cannot increase J_i=z_i+Z, since both own
payment and total payment increase with a short report. Overflow above1
is irrelevant to the first stage.

Let x=min(h_i,1) in[0,1], y=s_i>=0, and q=exp(1-rho)/2 at the full
profile. Reducing the capped long account by u in[0,x] changes J_i by
  q{exp(u)[x-y+rho-1-2u]-(x-y+rho-1)}.
This expression decreases with y, because the coefficient of y is
q[1-exp(u)]<=0. Ignore y to bound it above, and write the maximum as
  g_rho(x)=q max_(0<=u<=x){exp(u)[x+rho-1-2u]-(x+rho-1)}.
The maximizing u is clip((x+rho-3)/2,0,x).

The function g_rho is convex on[0,1], with g_rho(0)=0. Here is a complete
piecewise verification. On a zero-optimizer piece it is identically zero.
On an interior-optimizer piece its derivative in x is q(exp(u)-1) and its
second derivative is q exp(u)/2>0. On an upper-endpoint optimizer piece
u=x, the first derivative is q[exp(x)(rho-2-x)-1] and the second is
q exp(x)(rho-3-x)>=0, since this piece requires x<=rho-3. Values and
first derivatives agree at both kinds of transition. Convexity therefore
gives g_rho(x)<=x g_rho(1). Since the full capped accounts sum to rho,
  sum_i[J_i^i-J_i]_+<=rho g_rho(1).
This inequality also bounds each complete underreport, including its later
short stage. Direct substitution gives
  g_rho(1)=q[2exp((rho-2)/2)-rho],  2<=rho<=4;
  g_rho(1)=q[e(rho-2)-rho],        rho>=4.

It remains to prove rho g_rho(1)<=2delta, where delta=c-q(rho-1).
For2<=rho<=4 put u=(rho-2)/2. After division by2q, the difference
2delta-rho g_rho(1) becomes
  (exp(u)-1-u)^2+u^2>=0.
For rho>=4 put v=rho-2>=2. After division by q the same difference is
  F(v)=2exp(v)-(e-1)(v^2+2v)+2.
At v=2, F(2)=2[(e-2)^2+1]>0 and F'(2)=2(e^2-3e+3)>0.
Also F''(v)=2exp(v)-2(e-1)>0 for v>=2. Thus F remains positive.
Rescale T to prove(U). This argument requires no sharpness assertion for
the coefficient2 and no fictitious owners.

LOW-RATIO BRANCHES CANNOT INCREASE THEIR TOTAL.
Here is the full justification used when aggregating(U). Suppose the full
branch has short total T0 and capped long ratio1<=rho<=2. Reduce the chosen
owner's original short account first. On every cap cell the derivative of
the total with respect to increasing that account is
  Z'=1+q[r(r-1)-m(r-2)-1]>0.
For1<=r<=2 its bracket is at least -1, while q<=1/2; for r>=2 its minimum
over0<=m<=r is r-1>=1. Thus this first stage cannot increase the total.
Let T<=T0 and R>=rho be the resulting short total and capped ratio.

If R<=2, reduce the owner's long account. Before any pool switch, its
capped ratio decreases within[1,2], on which1+q(r)(r-1) is increasing;
the total payment therefore cannot increase. After a pool switch, the
original short pool has become a long pool of fixed actual total T. The
primitive total is at most its capped long sum, hence at most T, and so
is still at most its total before this second stage.

If R>2 instead, the full capped long sum bounds the intermediate one:
  2T<RT=sum_k min(full long_k,T)<=rho*T0.
During the entire second stage the output is at most T, hence its payment
total is at most CT<=C*rho*T0/2. The function
  f(rho)=1+q(rho)(rho-1)
is concave on[1,2], since f''(rho)=q(rho)(rho-3)<0. The difference
f(rho)-C*rho/2 is therefore concave, nonnegative at both endpoints
(values1-C/2 and0), and nonnegative everywhere on that interval. Thus
CT<=T0*f(rho), the ORIGINAL full payment total. The zero-output endpoint
is immediate. This proves total nonincrease for every whole-owner
underreport, including all pool switches and intermediate ratios above2.
The owner's own payment also cannot increase by primitive own monotonicity,
so these branches contribute no positive part to(U_outer).

OUTER CONSEQUENCE.
Under P=0 and full outer capped ratio r0>=2, the restored full hardware
total is at least2Z>=2M>CM. Every actual local total is at most CM.
The universal outer slope bound in TWO_SIDED_LOCAL_FUNDING_THEOREM.txt
therefore applies to every total-INCREASING own deviation. Its gain over
full N_i is at most half the positive part in(U), summed across branches.
Branches whose full rho<=2 cannot increase their total and contribute
no positive part. Therefore the aggregate gain from all owners whose
particular deviation increases the aggregate local total is at most
  Delta_+=sum_(j:rho_j>2)(CT_j-Z_j).                     (U_outer)
This is a dimension-free result for arbitrary local ratios.

BROAD FUNDING COROLLARY.
Suppose P=0, every productive local ratio is at least9/5 (with NO upper
bound), the full outer capped ratio r0>=2, and the actual full account
excess satisfies the directly evaluable condition
  A=max_i (z_i-H_i)_+/Z <=(1-c)/(6c).                    (U_account)
Then the actual envelope satisfies
  sum_i E_i<=CZ+(e/3)Delta_-+Delta_+
            <=CZ+Delta=C^2F-cDelta<=C^2F,               (U_funding)
where Delta=CM-Z and Delta_- sums branches with rho<=2.
Indeed, all total-increasing owners are covered by(U_outer). For decreasing
totals, the exact retention proof uses only the account upper bound A.
Its large-total coefficient is nonnegative because
(1-c)/3-2cA>=0; its small-total condition follows from
cA<=(1-c)/6<1/6. Thus each such owner's gain is at most its retention
defect. The quantitative theorem charges their aggregate defects at
(e/3)Delta_-, because branches with rho>=2 have no such defect.
Sum, use e/3<1 and F=M, and take independent suprema over owner reports.

MORE GENERAL SLACK CERTIFICATE.
Under P=0, r0>=2, and(U_account), local ratios below9/5 can also be allowed.
Let Delta_bad sum their slacks, Delta_mid sum ratios in[9/5,2], and
Delta_high sum ratios above2. The same proof, using the global quantitative
retention theorem on low-ratio branches, gives
  sum_i E_i<=CZ+350Delta_bad+(e/3)Delta_mid+Delta_high.
In particular C^2 funding is certified whenever
  (350-C)Delta_bad <=(C-e/3)Delta_mid+(C-1)Delta_high.
These sufficient profile conditions are explicit and computable from the
full report. They do not establish an unrestricted C^2 theorem; arbitrary
positive grid reports and profiles violating the account/slack conditions
remain outside this conclusion.
