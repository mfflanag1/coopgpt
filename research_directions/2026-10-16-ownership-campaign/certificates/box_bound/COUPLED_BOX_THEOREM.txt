SHARP RESPONSE COUPLING AND A TRUTHFUL REFINEMENT OF RECIPIENT BOXES
16 October 2026. Candidate theorem for independent audit.

SUMMARY
A sharp local response inequality gives a linear constraint coupling an
owner's own auxiliary account to its opponents' accounts. Intersecting the
recipient-specific box with this constraint preserves fixed-owner, all-real-
profile incentives and coalition coverage for arbitrary mixed ownership.
The resulting rule is pointwise no larger than Rbox and retains its7/4 bound.
A stronger zero-hardware boundary constraint is compatible with all-profile
truthfulness; on the cyclic obstruction family its exact funding ratio is
less than1.401, whereas Rbox exceeds1.5001. No general3/2 bound is asserted.

Notation and the definitions of Phi,z,H_i,p_i,beta_i,K,Dbox_i are as in
CYCLIC_THREE_HALVES_OBSTRUCTION.txt and the audited mixed/ box theorem.
An owner's hardware and local-power bundles may have arbitrarily many
coordinates. Actual owners are fixed throughout all incentive comparisons.

1. THE SHARP MIXED-OWNER RESPONSE COEFFICIENT
For each integer k>=1, let u_k be the unique number in(0,1) satisfying
  u_k exp(u_k)=exp(1-k)/2,
and set
  eta_k = k u_k/(1-u_k)^2.
Equivalently u_k=W_0(exp(1-k)/2), but this notation is unnecessary: monotone
bisection of u exp(u) gives the coefficient to any requested precision.
Set eta_0=1.

THEOREM. Fix an owner i. Suppose every branch has at most k OTHER owners with
positive hardware accounts. Then its actual local payments satisfy
  sum_(t!=i) z_t >= eta_k (z_i-H_i).
The assertion holds at every nonnegative real profile, with arbitrary owner
and branch counts. For k>=1, eta_k is the largest universal coefficient for
this inequality, even for a single branch. It satisfies
  0<eta_k<=k/[2 exp(k-1)-1]<=1.
For k=0 the right-hand excess is always nonpositive; eta_0=1 is a convention,
not a finite optimality claim.

PROOF ON ONE BRANCH. Write h_i,l_i for i's accounts and T for branch output.
A zero-output branch is trivial. If hardware is the short pool, q<=1/2 and
  z_i-h_i = q(min(l_i,T)-h_i) <= q(T-h_i),
whereas opponents' hardware roles alone total (1-q)(T-h_i). Hence opponents'
payments are at least z_i-h_i whenever this excess is positive, and the
eta_k bound follows from eta_k<=1. Negative excess needs no argument.

Suppose local power is short. If h_i>=T then z_i<=T<=h_i, so again the claim
is trivial. Otherwise set
  d=1-h_i/T in(0,1],
  y=sum_(t!=i)min(h_t,T)/T.
Since total hardware is at leastT and each capped opponent contributes at
most1, we have d<=y<=k. The capped hardware ratio is1-d+y, so
  q=exp(d-y)/2,
  z_i-h_i <= (1-q)dT,
  sum_(t!=i)z_t >= q y T.
Therefore it suffices to minimize
  f(d,y)=y/[d(2 exp(y-d)-1)]
over0<d<=1 and d<=y<=k.

For fixed d, the derivative with respect to y has the sign of
  2 exp(y-d)(1-y)-1.
Its derivative is -2y exp(y-d)<0 for y>0. Thus f(d,.) has at most one
interior turning point, a maximum, and its minimum on[d,k] is at an endpoint.
At y=d its value is1. At y=k it is k/D_k(d), where
  D_k(d)=d[2 exp(k-d)-1].
Now D_k'(d)=2 exp(k-d)(1-d)-1 and
D_k''(d)=-2 exp(k-d)(2-d)<0 on(0,1]. Its derivative is positive at0 and
negative at1, so it has a unique maximizer d*=1-u_k. The stationarity
condition is exactly u_k exp(u_k)=exp(1-k)/2. At that point
  D_k(d*)=(1-u_k)^2/u_k.
Thus min_d f(d,k)=eta_k. Since d=1 is an admissible comparison,
eta_k<=k/[2 exp(k-1)-1]<=1; the final inequality follows from
2 exp(k-1)-1>=k for k>=1. This proves the one-branch inequality.
Summing branch inequalities gives the theorem, even when individual branch
excesses are negative. The common coefficient uses the same bound k on every
branch; no unproved monotonicity in k is needed.

SHARPNESS. For k>=1 take one branch, local-power owner i with local capacityT
and hardware capacity u_k T, and k other owners each with hardware capacityT
and no local power. Then d=1-u_k, y=k. Owner i's positive excess is exactly
(1-q)(1-u_k)T and its opponents receive qkT, attaining ratio eta_k. Capacities
are real, as allowed by the model. Rational approximation gives arbitrarily
nearby ratios if rational input profiles are required, by continuity and
strict positivity of the excess. This sharpness concerns this response
inequality, not an all-mechanism funding lower bound.

2. THE REFINED MECHANISM ON THE FULL MIXED-OWNERSHIP DOMAIN
At a profile define, separately for each owner i,
  k_i=max_j #{t!=i:h_tj>0}.
This number depends only on OPPONENT hardware reports and therefore stays
fixed under every underreport or increase of i's entire own bundle. It need
not remain fixed when a different owner changes its report; truthfulness
places no monotonicity restriction on that owner's effect on i's payment.

Let Deta_i be Dbox_i intersected with the single linear constraint
  sum_(t!=i)(c_t-p_t) >= eta_(k_i)[(c_i-p_i)-H_i].             (C_eta)
Define Reta_i=max_(c in Deta_i) Phi_i(Hvector,c).
The domain is compact. It is nonempty because the full vector p+z satisfies
(C_eta), the exact recipient intervals, the own ceiling, and the total ceiling.
Thus the maximum exists, including all tie/zero cases by continuity of Phi.

COVERAGE AND ENVELOPE DOMINATION. Full-profile feasibility gives Reta_i>=N_i,
so every coalition is covered because N is coalition covering. More strongly,
consider any actual underreport of i, with local responses z' and hardware
H_i'<=H_i. Restore its own shared-power account in the auxiliary vector:
  c_i=p_i+z_i', c_t=p_t+z_t' for t!=i.
The audited box argument puts this vector in Dbox_i. The new inequality is
also satisfied, because the response theorem at the underreport gives
  sum_(t!=i)z_t' >= eta_(k_i)(z_i'-H_i')
                   >= eta_(k_i)(z_i'-H_i).
Restoring i's own shared power and outer hardware can only increase its
primitive payment. Consequently
  N_i <= E_i <= Reta_i <= Rbox_i,
where E is the actual own-report envelope of N. All these comparisons retain
the same actual owners; no cloning or owner-dependent population is used.

ALL-PROFILE WHOLE-OWNER INCENTIVES. Increase any local report of i. Its own
ceiling and total ceiling increase; opponent lower endpoints decrease and
upper endpoints increase, because its set of possible underreports expands.
Constraint(C_eta) is unchanged, so its feasible domain expands. If the increase
is in hardware, H_i also increases, which WEAKENS(C_eta), and the primitive is
nondecreasing in its own hardware coordinate. The coefficient eta_(k_i) stays
fixed because k_i excludes i. Therefore the payment cannot decrease.
If i increases its shared power by delta>=0, translate c_i to c_i+delta.
Its own bounds and total ceiling increase by delta; (c_i-p_i) is unchanged,
and all other terms of(C_eta) are unchanged. Primitive own-coordinate
monotonicity again proves payment monotonicity. Composing these coordinate
changes proves whole-owner truthfulness at every real profile. In particular,
(C_eta) has the correct direction: increasing hardware RELAXES its right side.
The inherited total funding bound is sum_i Reta_i<=7F/4.

3. STRONGER ZERO-HARDWARE BOUNDARY, STILL ON THE FULL DOMAIN
For k>=1 set kappa_k=k/[2 exp(k-1)-1], and set kappa_0=1. The separate
hardware-free response lemma gives sum_(t!=i)z_t>=kappa_(k_i) z_i whenever
H_i=0. Define Dc_i as follows:
  if H_i>0, use Deta_i;
  if H_i=0, intersect Dbox_i with
       sum_(t!=i)(c_t-p_t) >= kappa_(k_i)(c_i-p_i).          (C_0)
Let Rc_i=max_(c in Dc_i) Phi_i(Hvector,c).
For H_i=0,k_i=0 there is no hardware anywhere, all local payments vanish,
and the mechanism is zero; the stated convention is harmless.

The hardware-free response lemma is proved in the cyclic-obstruction file.
It applies throughout i's underreport box whenever H_i=0, so E_i<=Rc_i.
Since kappa_k>=eta_k, Dc_i is a subset of Deta_i at this boundary. While H_i
remains zero, the domain-expansion and own-p translation arguments are
unchanged. When an own-report increase crosses from H_i=0 to H_i>0, the
stronger constraint(C_0) is dropped, and
  Dc_i(old) subset Deta_i(old) subset Deta_i(new)=Dc_i(new).
The outer primitive's own hardware also increases. Hence the boundary switch
preserves truthfulness. A jump in the payment is allowed; continuity of the
final mechanism across that switch is not claimed. This construction requires
no restriction on which owners may own hardware at other profiles.
Thus on the entire mixed domain,
  N_i<=E_i<=Rc_i<=Reta_i<=Rbox_i,
with all coalition and incentive guarantees and the same global7/4 bound.

4. A QUANTIFIED CONSEQUENCE WITH UNBOUNDED ACTIVE COUNTS
Use the cyclic family in CYCLIC_THREE_HALVES_OBSTRUCTION.txt: for each n>=3
there are n hardware owners, n active branches, and one common local-power
owner w. Let r=19/10,s=83/40,q=exp(1-r)/2,a=rq,b=1-q,z=a+b.
Write kappa=kappa_2=2/(2e-1) and q0=exp(1-s/z)/2.

Every hardware payment already equals N_i=Rbox_i=a+(s-a)q0. Envelope/baseline
domination therefore forces Rc_i=Reta_i=Rbox_i for these owners.
For w, its opponent lower endpoints are0, its own ceiling is nb, and its
coupling(C_0) requires total opposing payment at least(1+kappa)c_w.
All feasible totals are below H=ns because K=nz<H. The zero-hardware outer
payment is c_w[1-q_outer(T)], with q_outer nondecreasing. It is consequently
at most c_w[1-q_outer((1+kappa)c_w)]. This expression is nondecreasing for
0<=c_w<=nb by the derivative calculation in the cyclic-obstruction file.

The upper bound at c_w=nb is attained in the refined auxiliary domain: take
  c_w=nb, c_i=kappa b for every hardware owner i.
The full-profile response inequality gives kappa b<=a, so each opponent
coordinate lies between0 and its upper endpoint (which is at least a).
The total n(1+kappa)b is at most n(a+b)=K. All hardware accounts s are below
nb, hence also below this total. Therefore exactly
  Rc_w=nb[1-exp(1-s/((1+kappa)b))/2].
For every n>=3 the exact funding ratio of Rc on this family is
  Gc=z+(s-a) exp(1-s/z)/2-b exp(1-s/((1+kappa)b))/2 <1401/1000.
The strict inequality is certified with exact Fraction/Taylor arithmetic by
check_cyclic_obstruction.py. Numerically Gc is approximately1.400330258092.
Thus the refinement excludes the entire Rbox counterfamily, which instead
has ratio>15001/10000. This is a uniform consequence for an unbounded active
family, not a global1.401 or3/2 theorem.

5. FINITE EXPLICIT OPTIMIZATION (GENERAL OWN HARDWARE)
This section gives an exact mathematical reduction, not a claim of polynomial
time or incentive-safe floating-point implementation. Each owner's coupled
problem has the form
  p<=x<=beta, ell_t<=c_t<=u_t(t!=i), x+sum c_t<=K,
  sum c_t >= Pminus+gamma(x-p-a),
where a=H_i, H=sum H_t, p=p_i, Pminus=sum_(t!=i)p_t,
and gamma=eta_(k_i), or gamma=kappa_(k_i) at a=0. Let
  L=sum ell_t, U=sum u_t.
The own ceiling x=beta is feasible, because the full profile is feasible.
For H=0 the value is0. For one actual owner the constraint is nonbinding and
the value is F. Suppose henceforth H>0 and at least two actual owners.

SHORT OPPOSING POOL. The feasible total interval is
  T in[p+L, min(H,K,beta+U)],
if the upper endpoint is at least the lower endpoint. For fixed T, the
primitive is increasing in x, so choose
  x*(T)=min{beta, T-L, [T-Pminus+gamma(p+a)]/(1+gamma)}.
Its lower feasible x bound is max(p,T-U). Both lower-bound candidates are
at most beta, because p<=beta and T<=beta+U. Both are at most T-L, because
T>=p+L and U>=L. Finally p satisfies the coupling upper bound since
T>=p+L>=p+Pminus>=p+Pminus-gamma*a. The candidate T-U satisfies it because
  U>=Pminus+gamma(beta-p-a)>=Pminus+gamma(T-U-p-a).
The first inequality follows from feasibility at the own ceiling beta and
the opponent upper total U. Thus the maximum of the lower bounds is at most
each of the three upper bounds, proving feasibility of x*(T). Opponent
interval sums fill every value from L to U, so a corresponding vector exists
for every T in the displayed interval. The upper bound T<=K supplies the
remaining total-budget constraint.
The value to maximize is
  f(T)=x*(T)+q(T)[min(a,T)-x*(T)],
  q(T)=exp(1-sum_t min(H_t,T)/T)/2.
At T=0 define f(0)=0.

Partition this one-dimensional interval at the hardware caps H_t and at
intersections of the three affine expressions defining x*. On each open cell,
write x*=vT+w, min(a,T)-x*=dT+e, and
  q(T)=exp(1-m-C/T)/2,
where m counts capped hardware coordinates and C is the sum of the others.
Then
  f'(T)=v+q(T)[d+dC/T+eC/T^2],
  f''(T)=q(T) C/T^3 [dC+e(C/T-2)].
If C=0, f is affine. Otherwise f'' has at most one zero unless it vanishes
identically. Split at that possible zero. On each resulting interval f' is
monotone, so an interior maximizer is either a root of f' or a whole constant
interval; there are at most two nontrivial stationary candidates per cell.
Endpoints and these bracketed one-dimensional roots determine the exact
short-pool maximum. Alternatively a uniform grid with a derivative bound
computes this continuous scalar maximum to arbitrary precision. Near T=0 all
positive hardware coordinates are capped, so C=0; there is no singularity.

LONG OPPOSING POOL. This regime exists iff K>=H and beta+U>=H. The feasible
own-account interval is
  x in[max(p,H-U), beta].
For a fixed x, the minimum feasible opponent total is
  S0(x)=max{L,H-x,Pminus+gamma(x-p-a)}.
The ceiling feasibility at beta ensures S0(x)<=U and x+S0(x)<=K throughout
this interval. Split at x=a and x=H.

If x<a, the own gap x-a is negative, so maximize the capped opponent total.
Let V=sum_(t!=i)min(u_t,H) and W=sum_(t!=i)(ell_t-H)_+.
The maximal capped ratio is
  rho(x)=min{(x+V)/H,(K-W)/H}.
It is attained by filling useful capacities up to H. If this exhausts the
budget, its opponent total is K-x>=S0(x), so every total lower constraint is
already met. Otherwise all useful caps have been filled. If the resulting
total is below S0(x), every remaining upper capacity lies above a filled cap
and can be added as overflow without changing the capped objective. There
is enough such capacity because S0(x)<=U; if there were none, the current
total would already equal U. Adding only the mass required to reach S0(x)
also respects the budget because x+S0(x)<=K.
The value a+exp(1-rho(x))(x-a)/2 is increasing in x on x<a, because rho is
nondecreasing there and the gap is negative. Hence only the largest feasible
x in this negative-gap segment is needed.

If x>=a, minimize the capped opponent total. It suffices to use total S0(x),
since decreasing an opponent coordinate never increases its capped value.
The concave function sum min(c_t,H) on the interval slice sum c_t=S0(x)
attains its minimum at a vertex. At a vertex all but at most one opponent
coordinate are at their lower or upper endpoints. Enumerate the remaining
coordinate j and endpoint choices for all other opponents. If their total
is D, then c_j=S0(x)-D. Split x into intervals on which one affine branch
of S0 is active, own cap min(x,H) is fixed to one affine branch, and the
pivot cap min(c_j,H) is fixed to one affine branch. All feasibility conditions
are linear in x, so each candidate has an explicitly computed interval.
On it the capped ratio is (lambda x+mu)/H and the own gap is
nu x+xi-a, where nu is0 or1. Here lambda>=0: the only negative slope in S0
is -1 from H-x, and it can be active only while x<=H, when the own cap has
slope1. Therefore the candidate value is
  a+exp(1-(lambda x+mu)/H)(nu x+xi-a)/2.
If nu=0 it is nonincreasing; if nu=1 its only possible interior maximizer
is x=a+H/lambda when lambda>0 (the own cap is x, so xi=0). If lambda=0
it is increasing or constant. Evaluate interval endpoints and the stated
stationary point whenever it lies in the interval.

Taking the maximum of all short and long candidates computes Rc or Reta.
Long-pool endpoint enumeration can be exponential in the actual owner count;
no polynomial preprocessing claim is made. The short component uses a finite
number of explicit one-dimensional monotone-root searches. Exponentials and
the uniquely specified coefficient u_k are computable to arbitrary precision.
Finite-precision payment rounding that preserves exact incentives is a
separate problem, not established by this reduction.

LIMITS
The sharp response coefficient decays as k grows. This theorem does not give
a global3/2 funding bound, solve arbitrary network technologies, or show
optimality of the resulting payment rule. It supplies a sharp general response
inequality, a truthful full-domain refinement, an exact evaluation reduction,
and a quantified improvement on a family with unbounded active counts.
