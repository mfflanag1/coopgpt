REMOVING THE ACCOUNT-SIDE CONDITION: UNBOUNDED LOCAL RATIOS
16 October 2026. Candidate theorem for independent audit.

Keep fixed actual owners, arbitrary mixed ownership, and the same exponential
two-level rule N and its actual own-report envelope E. Put c=1/(2e), C=1+c.
At the evaluated full profile assume only:
  P=0; every productive local capped long ratio rho_j>=9/5;
  Z>0 and sum_i min(H_i,Z)/Z>=2.
There is NO upper bound on local ratios or owner/branch counts, and NO
account-imbalance condition. Every actual whole-owner underreport is allowed,
including underreports that violate these full-profile conditions.

Write Delta_-=sum_(rho_j<=2)(CT_j-Z_j), Delta_+=sum_(rho_j>2)(CT_j-Z_j),
and Delta=Delta_-+Delta_+=CM-Z. Define
  kappa=7/10-e/5.
Then
  sum_i E_i<=CZ+(Ce/3)Delta_-+(1+kappa)Delta_+
            =C^2F-C(1-e/3)Delta_-
                    -(c-kappa)Delta_+ <=C^2F.            (UL)
Both displayed slack-saving coefficients are strictly positive. Here F=M,
and C^2 is sharp for this rule on this class, including with both active
counts growing, by the exact replicated saturation construction.

This removes the account-side condition in UNIVERSAL_TOTAL_INCREASE_LEMMA.txt.
It does not remove the lower local-ratio, outer-ratio, or zero-grid conditions.

QUADRATIC NEGATIVE-TOTAL GAIN LEMMA.
Normalize full Z=1. Fix owner i, restore its outer hardware to the full
report, and write a=H_i/Z, z=z_i/Z. For a particular own underreport
with total t<=1 and own local account b<=z, put
  u=1-t, D=z-b>=0, epsilon=[u/3-D]_+.
Its restored payment gain over full N_i is at most
  (3/(8c))(B_i)_+^2+C epsilon,
  B_i=2c(z-a)-(1-c)/3.                                  (UL_quad)
If a>=z there is no gain, by the common-total monotonicity argument. If
a<z but either b<=a or t<=a, the payment is at most a, below its full value.
In every remaining case t>a and b>a. With q0=q(1), direct subtraction gives
  gain=(q0-q(t))(b-a)-(1-q0)D.
For the full outer capped ratio>=2, q0<=c and
  0<=q0-q(t)<=2c(1-t)=2cu for ALL0<=t<=1.
For t>=1/2 this is the previously proved Lipschitz bound; for t<1/2 it
follows simply from q0-q(t)<=c<=2c(1-t). Therefore
  gain<=2cu(z-a-D)-(1-c)D
       =2cu(z-a)-(1-c+2cu)D
       <=B_i u-(2c/3)u^2+C epsilon.
The last step uses D>=u/3-epsilon and1-c+2cu<=1+c=C. Maximizing the
quadratic over all real u>=0 gives(UL_quad). Zero-total and cap boundary
cases follow directly or by continuity.

AT MOST ONE LARGE ACCOUNT.
Let A_i=(z_i-H_i)_+/Z. Since A_i<=z_i/Z and sum_i z_i/Z=1, sum_i A_i<=1.
For B_i>0 one must have
  A_i>(1-c)/(6c)>1/2,
where the last inequality follows from c<1/4. Thus at most ONE actual owner
has B_i>0. Also, for that owner,
  B_i<=Bmax=(7c-1)/3,
because A_i<=1. The needed upper bound on its square will now be charged
to aggregate HIGH-ratio local slack rather than imposed as a restriction.

LOCAL CHARGE FOR THE ACCOUNT EXCESS.
On every branch with coefficient q=exp(1-rho)/2, an owner's payment minus
its hardware is at most(1-q)T. If local power is short this is immediate;
if hardware is short its excess is at most qT<=(1-q)T. Zero-output
branches contribute zero local payment and nonnegative hardware.
Consequently, for every owner with B_i>0,
  Z B_i=2c(z_i-H_i)-(1-c)Z/3
       <=sum_j b(rho_j)T_j,
  b(r)=2c(1-q)-(1-c)[1+q(r-1)]/3.                       (UL_b)
For9/5<=r<=2, b(r)<=0. Indeed
  b'(r)=q[2c+(1-c)(r-2)/3]>0
on this interval, while b(2)=(1-c)(5c-1)/3<0.
For ALL r>=2,
  b(r)<= (4/5)[c-q(r-1)].                               (UL_scalar)
A complete elementary certificate for this scalar inequality follows below.
Using(UL_b)--(UL_scalar) gives
  B_i<=(4/5)Delta_+/Z.
Since at most one B_i is positive,
  sum_i(B_i)_+^2 <= Bmax (4/5)Delta_+/Z.
Combining this with(UL_quad) shows that the aggregate quadratic extra term,
after rescaling, is at most
  (3/(8c)) Bmax (4/5)Delta_+
       =[(7c-1)/(10c)]Delta_+=kappa Delta_+.              (UL_charge)

SCALAR CERTIFICATE FOR(UL_scalar).
Put v=r-1>=1, q=exp(-v)/2,
  alpha=(5-23c)/15, k=(7+5c)/15.
The right side minus the left side in(UL_scalar) equals
  alpha+(1/2)exp(-v)(2c-kv).
Its global minimum occurs at v*=1+2c/k and equals
  alpha-(k/2)exp(-v*).
The elementary bounds2/11<c<7/38 imply
  alpha>29/570, k<301/570, v*>5591/3311.
The degree-four Taylor lower polynomial of exp(5591/3311) exceeds301/58.

  (k/2)exp(-v*)<(301/1140)(58/301)=29/570<alpha.
All comparisons are rational except the Taylor inequality, whose remainder
is positive. The bounds on c follow from19/7<e<11/4, already established
by elementary series bounds. Thus(UL_scalar) holds for every r>=2,
without any numerical optimization or bounded search.

COMPLETING THE FUNDING PROOF.
Choose one arbitrary whole-owner underreport for each owner. Restore its
outer hardware. The full hardware total is at least2Z>=2M>CM, so the
opposing pool stays short even when its underreported local total increases.
Partition owners according as their particular underreport has Z^i>Z or
Z^i<=Z. By the universal total-increase lemma and outer half-Lipschitz
bound, the aggregate gain of the first group is at most Delta_+.

For the second group apply(UL_quad). The quantitative retention theorem
gives sum_i epsilon_i<=(e/3)Delta_-/Z: branches with full rho>=2 have zero
retention defect, and those in[9/5,2] have the e/3 aggregate bound. Thus
the epsilon terms cost at most(Ce/3)Delta_-, and(UL_charge) bounds the
quadratic terms by kappa Delta_+. The full raw total is at most CZ.
Adding these quantities gives the first inequality in(UL), uniformly for
all separately chosen owner deviations. Taking their separate suprema
therefore proves the same statement for E.

Finally H>=2Z>=2M and P=0 imply F=M, and CZ=C^2M-C(Delta_-+Delta_+).
The low-ratio saving is positive because e<3. The high-ratio saving is
also positive, since
  c-kappa=1/(2e)-7/10+e/5
          =[(2e-5)(e-1)]/(10e)>0
using e>5/2. This proves(UL) with its full claimed scope. No owner-count
factor, selected finite report domain, or hidden membership restriction
on underreports appears in the argument.
