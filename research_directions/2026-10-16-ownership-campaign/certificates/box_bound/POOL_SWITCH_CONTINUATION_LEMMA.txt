EXACT POOL-SWITCH CONTROL FOR THE ONE-THIRD RETENTION POTENTIAL
16 October 2026. Candidate lemma for independent audit.

Consider a primitive branch at the start of a LONG-account reduction stage.
Normalize its currently fixed short total to1. Its full capped long ratio
at this stage is R in[1,2]. Fix the actual owner being reduced. Let s in[0,1]
be its short account, x in[0,1] its capped long account, and v the actual
long total of the other owners. The potential is F=z_i-Z/3. The short
accounts are fixed throughout this stage. Uncapped own overflow above1 is
irrelevant until the capped long account begins to fall.

If v>=1, the original short resource stays short and no pool switch occurs.
The usual original-orientation formula applies. Suppose v<1 instead, so
a switch can occur. All other long accounts are then below1 and their
capped sum is v; hence R=x+v at the start of the effective reduction.
The formal original-orientation potential at remaining own long account
h=x-u, including h+v<1, is
  F_art=s-1/3+q_R exp(u)[x-s-(R-1)/3-(2/3)u],
  q_R=exp(1-R)/2,  0<=u<=x.                             (PS_art)
For h+v<1 this is an algebraic continuation only, not an asserted actual
payment or an admissible use of the wrong pool orientation.

CLAIM. At every point after the pool switch the TRUE potential satisfies
  F_actual<=max(F_cross,F_art),                          (PS)
where F_cross is its true value at the tie h+v=1. Consequently the largest
potential gain during the ENTIRE long-account stage is bounded by the
largest gain of the single formal expression(PS_art).

PROOF. Put t=h+v<1. At the true post-switch profile the original long
resource is short, with own short account t-v. Let
  S=sum_k min(s_k,t),  a=min(s,t).
Since the original short accounts sum to1, t<=S<=1. With
q=exp(1-S/t)/2 the true potential is
  F_actual=2t/3-v+q[a+v-2t/3-S/3].                       (PS_true)
For fixed t,a,v its derivative with respect to S is
  q[S+t-3(a+v)]/(3t).
The sign can change only from negative to positive, so its maximum over
the enlarged interval S in[t,1] occurs at an endpoint. These endpoints
need not describe feasible cap sums; they are used only as upper bounds.

At S=t the expression is t/6-v/2+a/2. As a=min(s,t), this is increasing
in t and is at most
  F_cross=1/6-v/2+s/2.
At S=1 replace a by s, which can only increase the expression. Write
  q_b=exp(1-1/t)/2,  w=s+v,  L=(2t+1)/3.
This endpoint bound is
  F_b=2t/3-v+q_b(w-L).
At w=L one has F_b-F_cross=(t-1)/3<0. Also F_b-F_cross is decreasing
in w because its coefficient is q_b-1/2<0. Therefore
  F_b>F_cross implies w<L.                              (PS_case)

Write q_a=exp(1-t)/2, the formal coefficient in(PS_art). Direct subtraction
at the SAME remaining own report gives
  F_art-F_b=(1-q_a-q_b)(w-L).
For0<t<=1, q_a+q_b>=1. To prove this, the derivative of
exp(1-t)+exp(1-1/t) is nonpositive: the ratio of its positive derivative
term to the negative term is exp(t-1/t-2log t)<=1. Indeed
psi(t)=t-1/t-2log t has derivative(t-1)^2/t^2>=0 and psi(1)=0.
The exponential sum is therefore at least its value2 at t=1.
Combining this fact with(PS_case) proves F_art>=F_b whenever the latter
could exceed F_cross. Together with the S=t endpoint bound, this proves(PS).
The t=0 case follows by continuity, or directly from zero production.

The tie value F_cross itself occurs on the formal curve at u=R-1, which
belongs to[0,x] because R>=1 and v<1. Before the tie, the formal and true
potentials agree. Thus the claimed bound on the entire stage follows.

EXACT LONG-STAGE GAIN BOUND.
Put epsilon=2-R in[0,1]. In(PS_art) the derivative with respect to u is
  q_R exp(u)[a0-(2/3)u],
  a0=x-s-1+epsilon/3 <=epsilon/3.
If a0<=0, there is no gain. Otherwise its maximum gain, even after
enlarging the allowed u interval, is
  (2/3)q_R[exp((3/2)a0)-1-(3/2)a0]
      <=(2/3)q_R[exp(epsilon/2)-1-epsilon/2].
The local budget slack at the start of this stage is
  delta=c-q_R(R-1)=q_R[exp(-epsilon)-1+epsilon].
For all0<=epsilon<=1,
  2exp(epsilon/2)-exp(-epsilon)<=1+2epsilon.
Indeed let H(epsilon)=1+2epsilon-2exp(epsilon/2)+exp(-epsilon).
It has H(0)=H'(0)=0, and its second derivative
exp(-epsilon)-(1/2)exp(epsilon/2) strictly decreases and changes sign
at most once. Therefore H' first increases and then decreases, so H
has no interior minimum below its endpoint values. The other endpoint
is H(1)=3-2sqrt(e)+1/e>1/33>0, using e<11/4 and sqrt(e)<5/3.
This proves the scalar inequality and hence the full stage bound
  (F_final-F_initial)_+<=delta/3.                       (PS_gain)
The proof explicitly includes every pool-switch continuation and requires
no restriction R>=9/5.

COUNTING CONSEQUENCE FOR THE PARENT RETENTION ARGUMENT.
Suppose this long stage follows a reduction of the SAME owner's short
account from a full branch with normalized short total1 and ratio rho<=2.
Let t be the short total at the start of the long stage, d_i the owner's
original capped long account, and s_i its original short account. A positive
gain requires x-s>(1+R)/3, hence x>(1+R)/3>=(1+rho)/3.
But d_i>=tx and s_i>=1-t, so
  d_i+s_i>=tx+1-t>=x>(1+rho)/3.
Since sum_i(d_i+s_i)=1+rho, at most TWO actual owners can have a positive
long-stage gain. If the intermediate R<=2, its slack is at most the full
branch slack, because t<=1 and slack per unit output decreases on[1,2].
If R>=2, long-stage retention defect is zero by the earlier theorem.
Thus the aggregate long-stage contribution is at most2delta_full/3.
Combining this with a separately proved aggregate short-stage contribution
of at most delta_full/3 yields the proposed global one-third-retention
defect bound delta_full. That short-stage estimate is not re-proved here;
this file supplies the missing switch argument and the complete long stage.
