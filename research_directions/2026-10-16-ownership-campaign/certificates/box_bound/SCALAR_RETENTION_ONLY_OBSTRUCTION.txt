WHY A SINGLE RETENTION COEFFICIENT CANNOT CLOSE THE GENERAL OUTER GAP
16 October 2026. Exact obstruction to a proposed proof relaxation.

This is not a physical counterexample to the C^2 envelope conjecture. It
shows that discarding local ownership/account geometry and retaining only
one scalar loss-retention inequality is insufficient, even if that scalar
coefficient is strengthened as far as the local primitive permits.

Write c=1/(2e), C=1+c. Normalize the full outer opposing total to B=1.
There are four fixed actual owners. Their outer hardware accounts are
  Hvector=(0,2/3,2/3,2/3), H=2.
Their opposing accounts are
  bvector=(beta,(1-beta)/3,(1-beta)/3,(1-beta)/3),
where0<beta<1. Full capped hardware ratio is2, so q=c and the full raw
total is exactly C. For owner0 consider aggregate counterfactuals with
  total t=1-u, own opposing account b_0'=beta-lambda*u,
where0<lambda<1 and u>0 is sufficiently small. These accounts are feasible:
the other accounts can sum to1-beta-(1-lambda)u>=0, and b_0'<=t whenever
  u<=(1-beta)/(1-lambda).
For sufficiently small u the hardware accounts remain uncapped, since
t>2/3. Consequently owner0's payment along the counterfactual is exactly
  W(t)=[1-q(t)][beta-lambda(1-t)],
  q(t)=exp(1-2/t)/2.
Its derivative at t=1 is
  W'(1)=lambda(1-c)-2c beta.                            (SR_derivative)
Thus if
  beta>lambda(1-c)/(2c),                                (SR_beta)
a small total reduction strictly INCREASES owner0's payment, even though
its own account loss is exactly lambda times the total loss. Other owners
can select their full reports in the abstract envelope. The summed envelope
therefore exceeds the full primitive funding bound C B=C.

For lambda=1/3 one may take beta=9/10. Then
  W'(1)=(5-32c)/15<0,
and feasibility holds for0<u<3/20. The inequality c>5/32 follows, for
example, from e<3. The clipping in the feasible transform
  b_0(t)=min(t,max(0,beta-lambda(1-t)))
is inactive for these small u, so adding that feasibility condition does
not repair the relaxation.

MORE GENERALLY, an abstract scalar-retention proof of preservation of CB
would need at least
  lambda>=lambda_needed=2c/(1-c),                        (SR_needed)
because otherwise some beta strictly between the threshold in(SR_beta)
and1 yields the exact counterexample above.

But the actual local primitive cannot satisfy zero-slack retention with
such a large coefficient. Take one branch with one pure short-resource
owner of capacity1 and three other owners each with long capacity2/3.
The full local ratio is2, total payment is C, and local slack is zero.
When the short owner changes its account near1, no long account is capped.
At the full point its own-payment derivative is1-3c and total-payment
derivative is C. Therefore any universally valid inequality
  own payment loss >=lambda*(total payment loss)
on these zero-slack reports must have
  lambda<=lambda_available=(1-3c)/(1+c).                 (SR_available)
This is the sharp saturation-retention coefficient proved independently
in SATURATION_RETENTION_THEOREM.txt.

The two requirements are incompatible:
  lambda_available<lambda_needed
is equivalent to1-6c+c^2<0. Since2/11<c<1/5 and this polynomial decreases
there, its value is less than its value at2/11, namely -7/121<0.

CONCLUSION FOR THE RESEARCH ROUTE.
No single universally valid linear retention coefficient can, by itself,
justify a generic outer CB guarantee over all feasible opposing-account
vectors. A successful proof must retain additional information, such as
the local role/account bounds, the relationship between account excess and
local slack, or ownership-specific coupling. The dominant-hardware theorem
escapes this obstruction because its hardware cap geometry prevents the
uncapped configuration above. The constructed aggregate counterfactuals
need not be realizable by any particular underlying physical reports, and
no such realizability or physical funding lower bound is asserted here.
