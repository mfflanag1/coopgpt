Heterogeneous hardware with additive power rights
15 October 2026 -- scoping supplement, not a submitted manuscript

1. Model and funding benchmark

Fix finitely many real owners i and hardware types j=1,...,m. Owner i reports
(g_i1,...,g_im,q_i)>=0: hardware capacities and power entitlements. One owner may
hold both kinds of input. All coefficients p_j,e_j are fixed and strictly
positive. They denote public service value and incremental power per activity.
For aggregate g,q define
  F(g,q)=max sum_j p_j x_j
          subject to 0<=x_j<=g_j and sum_j e_j x_j<=q.
For coalition S, v_b(S)=F(sum_{i in S} g_i, sum_{i in S} q_i).
Owner sets and technology are fixed while reports range over all nonnegative
real bundles. A feasible deviation decreases any coordinates of that owner's
bundle. Utilities are payments only. No assertion covers private opportunity
costs, overreports, coordinated deviations, false identities or owner changes.

Let T=v_b(N). Fixed-profile required funding is
  B(b)=min {sum_i z_i : z>=0, sum_{i in S}z_i>=v_b(S) for every S}.
Nonnegative packing LP duality gives B(b)=T. Thus any payment above T in this
model is not caused by the absence of an efficient core at a fixed profile.
For a fixed institution and report domain D, define
  Gamma(D)=inf_y sup_{b in D:B(b)>0} sum_i y_i(b)/B(b),
where one rule y must be nonnegative, coalition-covering and own-report monotone
on all D; require y=0 at B=0. Static subsidy is B-T; additional funding is
sum y-B. These quantities must not be conflated.

2. Two-input rule for actual owners with mixed bundles

An owner i holds (a_i,b_i)>=0. Put A=sum a_i, B=sum b_i, T=min(A,B).
If T=0 pay everyone zero. Otherwise call a minimum-total input short and the
other long; at ties either choice is allowed. Write s_i,l_i for that owner's
short and long holdings, d_i=min(l_i,T), rho=sum_i d_i/T, and
  p=(1/2) exp(1-rho),
  z_i=(1-p)s_i+p d_i.
The formula is defined directly on actual owners. No owner is permitted to
split or merge strategically. It extends the exclusive-pool exponential rule
already proved in the existing supplement (hist:exponential).

Lemma. The rule covers every coalition, is nondecreasing in either coordinate
of each fixed owner, and totals at most C T, where C=1+1/(2e).

Proof. rho>=1 because total long holdings are at least T. For a coalition S,
sum_{i in S}s_i>=v(S), and
sum_{i in S}min(l_i,T)>=min(sum_{i in S}l_i,T)>=v(S).
Both weights are nonnegative and sum to one, proving coverage. Total payment is
  T[1+(rho-1) exp(1-rho)/2] <= (1+1/(2e))T,
using max_{u>=0} u exp(-u)=1.

For incentives fix all other owners and the owner's opposite coordinate.
Consider increasing its short coordinate x while it remains short. Then
T=x+a for a fixed nonnegative remainder a. Away from clipping points,
rho is nonincreasing and -rho/T<=rho'<=0, including the deviating owner's
own long holding in the clipped sum. Hence 0<=p'<=p rho/T, and x<=T.
The derivative of its short payment is
  ((1-p)x)'=1-p-xp' >= 1-p(1+rho) >=0.
The last inequality follows since (1+rho) exp(1-rho)/2 decreases from one on
[1,infinity). Its other payment p min(l_i,T) also is nondecreasing, being a
product of nonnegative nondecreasing factors.

If the increased coordinate is long, T is fixed. Write d=min(x,T) and
gamma=sum_{h!=i}min(l_h,T). Then p=(1/2)exp(1-(d+gamma)/T).
For x<T, (p d)'=p(1-d/T)>=0, and beyond T it is constant. The owner's short
payment (1-p)s_i is also nondecreasing because p is nonincreasing in x.
At equality of pool totals, all holdings are <=T, rho=1 and the owner's
payment is (a_i+b_i)/2 on either side. Clipping boundaries are continuous.
At T=0 payments are zero, continuous as T decreases to zero by the total
payment bound C T and nonnegativity, and cannot
decrease from zero after an increase. Piecewise monotonicity and continuity
therefore prove coordinatewise monotonicity globally. Increasing all of one
owner's coordinates sequentially proves whole-bundle monotonicity. QED.

This is a direct, rule-specific ownership argument. A generic truthful rule on
two separately controlled accounts need not remain truthful after those
accounts are controlled by one owner. No general merger or group-proofness
claim follows from this lemma.

3. Heterogeneous-star decomposition and mechanism

Sort types once so a_j=p_j/e_j is nonincreasing. Put a_{m+1}=0 and
Delta_k=a_k-a_{k+1}>=0. Define h_j=e_j g_j and H_k=sum_{j<=k}h_j.
Then the fractional-knapsack identity is
  F(g,q)=sum_{k=1}^m Delta_k min(q,H_k).                    (1)
Indeed write u_j=e_j x_j. The objective sum a_j u_j equals
sum_k Delta_k sum_{j<=k}u_j. Each prefix is bounded by min(q,H_k), and filling
types in the fixed sorted order attains every such prefix bound simultaneously.
This proves (1), including coefficient ties and zero capacities.

For each prefix k give actual owner i the derived two-input bundle
  (H_i,k,q_i)=(sum_{j<=k}e_j g_ij,q_i).
Apply the preceding lemma to obtain z_i^k and define
  y_i(b)=sum_k Delta_k z_i^k(b).                           (2)

Theorem. On all nonnegative real profiles, (2) is nonnegative, covers every
coalition, is whole-owner underreport truthful, and satisfies
  sum_i y_i <= (1+1/(2e)) F(sum_i g_i,sum_i q_i).
The bound is independent of the numbers of owners and hardware types. Mixed
ownership of hardware and power is permitted. Explicit evaluation takes
O(m log m + nm) arithmetic operations and O(m) exponential evaluations after
prefix aggregation; this is a real-arithmetic statement, not an exact finite-bit
evaluation guarantee for transcendental numbers.

Proof. Identity (1) holds for every coalition, so summing component coverage
with fixed nonnegative Delta_k proves coverage. Each owner's own coordinate
increase raises its derived H_i,k and/or q_i, leaving other owners' derived
reports fixed. The lemma and positive weights prove incentives. Summing the
component budget bounds and applying (1) proves the budget. QED.

Each activity column touches a hardware row and the common power row, so
this is a column-support-two packing class. The result improves the established
grand-marginal 2T ceiling for this star class to about 1.183940T. It does not
improve the bound for every column-support-two matrix, arbitrary hardware
compatibility graph, multiple coupled power bottlenecks or discrete schedules.
The generic form-count theorem can separately be better on particular
technologies. We assert no pointwise payment dominance over other mechanisms.

The total bound is attained by THIS RULE in a single-type profile with three
fixed owners having bundles (1,0),(1,0),(0,1), with p_1=e_1=1. This does not
establish the optimal mechanism factor.

4. Existing lower bound and exact live gap

One type is a subclass, so the existing 9/8 lower bound applies. To make the
owner-preserving certificate explicit, use two hardware-only owners 0,1 and
two power-only owners 2,3 at profiles
  c=(1,1 | 1,1), a=(1,1 | 1,2), b=(2,1 | 1,1).
At a, coverage of {0,2},{1,2},{0,1,3} gives 2Y(a)-y_3(a)>=4.
At b, coverage of {1,2},{1,3},{0,2,3} gives 2Y(b)-y_0(b)>=4.
At c, coverage of {0,3} plus monotonicity gives
1<=y_0(c)+y_3(c)<=y_0(b)+y_3(a)<=2Y(a)+2Y(b)-8.
All three profiles have T=2. A uniform Y<=C T therefore requires 1<=8C-8,
or C>=9/8. This is the earlier lower witness, reproduced rather than claimed
as new. In particular the optimal uniform factor for the star class lies in
  [9/8, 1+1/(2e)].

More precisely, positive-sum decomposition shows that the optimal uniform
factor over arbitrary mixed-owner star technologies EQUALS that over arbitrary
mixed-owner two-input technologies: a rule for the latter composes prefixwise,
and the former contains the latter as m=1. Thus increasing heterogeneity in
this star model cannot itself open a harder minimax problem. Closing this
interval is exactly a two-input problem, not a new independent frontier.
If the infimum is not attained, compose rules within any positive epsilon of
the two-input infimum, then let epsilon decrease to zero.

5. Institutional obstructions and reduction

Veto power. If one fixed owner is required for every positive-output coalition,
paying it T and everybody else zero is efficient, covering and own-monotone.
This is the conventional veto-player construction. For m compute owners,
v({0} union S)=min(q,sum_{i in S}g_i), imposing y_0<=alpha T for fixed alpha
in [0,1] instead gives optimal uniform total factor m-(m-1)alpha. The matching
rule is y_0=alpha T, y_i=(1-alpha)min(g_i,q). Pair coverage at g_i=q=1 proves
the lower bound. This extra funding is forced already at one profile by the
distributional cap, not by incentives.

Full common access. For v(S)=min(sum_{i in S}g_i,P), fixed public P,
B(b)=sum_i min(g_i,P), attained on every profile by singleton payments
y_i=min(g_i,P), which are own-monotone. Static funding can be nT while Gamma=1.
More generally, suppose each activity consumes exactly one private resource
row (strictly positive coefficient), plus any number of nonnegative common
resource rows with fixed capacities. Every coalition plan can be split among
owners in proportion to their contribution of that activity's private row.
Each owner's allocated subplan respects its private capacities and consumes
no more of any public row than the original plan. Thus coalition value is at
most the sum of singleton values; paying singleton values is covering,
statically optimal and own-monotone. Exclude activities consuming no private
row and assume finite optima. Full public access for each deviating coalition
is an explicit optimistic outside-option convention, not simultaneous physical
feasibility for all coalitions or a claim about actual rationed markets.

Broad additive power retains sparse hardness. Given any sparse nonnegative LP
F(c)=max{p.x:Ax<=c,x>=0}, with p>0, nonzero columns of A and finite nonnegative
capacities, append a row p.x<=q. The new production function
G(c,q)=min(F(c),q), by scaling an optimal plan. Choose fixed w>=0 satisfying
A^T w>=p and embed each original report b_i as (b_i,w.b_i). This preserves
owner identities, coordinatewise underreport order and every coalition value,
because F(c)<=w.c. Every column gains just one positive entry. A truthful
covering rule for G therefore restricts to one for F with the same factor.
Choosing w dual-optimal at a specified positive target profile makes the
power row binding there; lowering q below T lowers output one-for-one. The
chosen w is then held FIXED on the full embedded domain. This establishes a
mathematical reduction, not an assertion that energy per activity equals price
in real hardware. A generic additive-power label alone does not simplify the
general sparse problem.

6. Exact obstruction to a fixed greedy order beyond the star

There are two hardware rows H1,H2, two power rows P1,P2 and three activities:
  a=(H1,P1), value 3; b=(H1,P2), value 2; c=(H2,P1), value 2.
Every activity consumes one unit of each incident resource. The resource graph
is a path, hence acyclic. Consider a greedy procedure that fixes one order of
these activities independently of reports and fills each activity as much as
remaining resources permit.

At capacities (H1,H2,P1,P2)=(1,0,1,1), optimal value is 3, so such an optimal
greedy order must put a before b. At (1,1,1,0), it must put a before c. But at
(1,1,1,1), a first yields value 3 whereas b=c=1 yields 4. Therefore no fixed
activity order is optimal at all profiles, even on this three-edge path.
This obstructs copying the star's fixed merit-order proof to arbitrary
compatibility trees. It does NOT show that a different decomposition or payment
rule cannot achieve the same factor, or that the general 2T bound is tight.

7. Contribution boundary

Known ingredients: LP core existence, fractional knapsack/merit-order identity,
positive-sum closure, veto allocation, singleton subadditivity arguments,
the existing exclusive-pool exponential mechanism and its existing 9/8 witness.
Candidate project extension: the explicitly proved mixed-owner monotonicity
lemma, yielding a constructive uniform star-technology bound and the exact
reduction of its minimax factor to the mixed two-input class. The internal
proof and finite checks do not establish historical novelty. Literature
comparisons and economic implementation qualifications accompany this note.
