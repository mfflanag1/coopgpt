A277/243 CONCENTRATION-TILTED WATER-FILL RULE
16 October2026. Candidate conventional proof, frozen for independent audit.
No historical-priority claim. Do not replace the frozen31/27 theorem until
this separate candidate has passed independent review.

MODEL AND THEOREM
Fix any finite actual owner set. Owner i reports arbitrary nonnegative real
amounts(a_i,b_i), with fixed eligibility restrictions if desired. Coalition
value is min(sum_S a_i,sum_S b_i); reports bind, utility is payment, and own
bundle withholding is the permitted strategic deviation. There are no private
costs, identity changes or marginal-payment floors.

The rule below is nonnegative, covers every actual coalition, and is monotone
in every coordinate of every actual owner's own report. Its total funding is
at most277/243 times grand production, and this factor is attained by the rule.
The theorem permits arbitrary mixed owners and unbounded finite owner counts.
It does not assert that277/243 is optimal among arbitrary mechanisms.

1. DEFINITION
Orient the pools so that A=sum x_i<=B=sum z_i. If A=0 pay all owners0.
Otherwise set
 M=max_i x_i, d_i=min(z_i,A), D=sum_i d_i, L=max_i d_i,
 s=M/A, t=L/D, psi(r)=(r-2/3)_+^2,
 h(s,t)=1/2+(1/4)[psi(t)-psi(s)].
Both s,t belong to(0,1], and17/36<=h<=19/36.
Use exactly the water level from the frozen31/27 rule:
 min(M,lambda)+sum_i min(d_i,lambda)=A+M.
If D=A use lambda=max(M,max_i d_i); otherwise the level is unique below that
maximum. Let
 P=[min(M,lambda)/M]*product_{i:d_i>0}[min(d_i,lambda)/d_i],
 p=h(s,t)P,
 y_i=(1-p)x_i+p d_i.
Zero caps are omitted from the product. The auxiliary capM is an accounting
quantity, never a strategic owner or coalition member.

At A=B, D=A and P=1. Swapping pool orientation exchanges s and t, while
h(s,t)+h(t,s)=1, so each actual owner's payment is unchanged. Thus ties have
one well-defined value, though the balanced allocation is generally tilted
away from halves. Continuity holds at all positive-A boundaries. At A->0,
0<=y_i<=x_i+d_i<=2A, so the zero case is continuous.

Coverage follows because0<=p<=1 and, for every coalition, both its short sum
and its sum of capped long reports dominate its value. This is the same exact
capping inequality as in the frozen primitive; no allocation relaxation occurs.

2. NORMALIZED WATER-CELL FACTS
For derivative and budget calculations, normalize the evaluated point to A=1.
This is a homogeneous normalization of the point, not a claim that A stays
fixed when its own short coordinate changes. Thus M=s, d_i<=1 and D>=1.
On a D>1 cell, lambda<1. Let
 U=(s-2/3)_+, V=(t-2/3)_+.
Then h=1/2+(V^2-U^2)/4.

Dominant-long-cap lemma. If t>2/3, at most one actual long cap is strictly above
lambda. Indeed, suppose the largest capL and another actual cap are active,
and let R=D-L. Then R>lambda. Also the actual water-truncated mass is at least1
(the dummy contributes at mosts to target1+s), so lambda+R>=1. Consequently
R>max(lambda,1-lambda)>=1/2. Since L<=1, this implies t=L/(L+R)<2/3, a
contradiction. If L alone is active, all other caps are inactive and
 lambda+R>=1, R=L(1-t)/t.
In particular lambda>=1-R. If L is inactive, all actual caps are inactive.

Write beta=(log P)' for a single coordinate change. For an uncapped long
coordinate d_i, beta=-1/d_i when d_i>lambda and beta=-1/lambda when d_i<lambda.
For a short coordinate, let e=1 if its owner is the strict maximum-short owner,
e=0 otherwise; then M'=e. Let c count long reports strictly aboveA. Those
c caps move at rate1, and all are water-active. If the dummy is inactive,
 beta=1/lambda-c>=0.
If the dummy is active,
 beta=(1+e)/lambda-e/s-c, P<=lambda/s.
In the latter case beta>=1-s/lambda, including when beta<0. These identities
follow by differentiating the water equation and active product factors;
they are also proved independently in WATERFILL_PRODUCT_THEOREM.txt.

3. OWN LONG-COORDINATE MONOTONICITY
A,M and x_i stay fixed. Above the capA the rule is independent of the owner's
long report. Below it, write d=d_i,x=x_i and eta=h'/h. Its payment derivative is
 p[1+(d-x)(beta+eta)].                                (L)
The only new term compared with the frozen proof is eta.

If the changing owner is the strict largest long-cap owner, d=L and
 eta=V*t*(1-t)/(2h*d)>=0.
If it is not largest,
 eta=-V*t/(2h*D)<=0.
The formulas apply on cells; tied maxima are joined by continuity.

For eta>=0, it suffices that eta<=1/d in an active cell and eta<=1/lambda in
an inactive cell: then both the constant term and the coefficient of x in(L)
are nonnegative. In an active cell,
 eta*d<=1/(27h)<=4/51<1,
using V<=1/3 and t(1-t)<=2/9 whenever V>0. In an inactive cell d=L<lambda,
 eta=V(1-t)/(2hD)<=1/(18h)<=2/17<1/lambda.

For eta<=0 and V=0 the old proof applies directly. If V>0, the dominant-cap
lemma says a nonmaximum cap cannot be active. Thus beta=-1/lambda and the
coefficient of x in(L) is positive; it suffices to check x=0.
If L is active, then d<=R and
 lambda-d>=1-2R>=L-2R=3D*V.
Meanwhile
 lambda*d*|eta|=V*t*lambda*d/(2hD)<=18V/17<3DV.
Hence1-d/lambda+d eta>=0. If L is inactive, then d/lambda<=R/L=(1-t)/t<1/2,
while d|eta|<=1/(18h)<=2/17<1/2. This proves(L)>=0 in every case.

4. OWN SHORT-COORDINATE MONOTONICITY: SETUP
Let x=x_i,d=d_i at the normalized point, and d' in{0,1}. The full derivative is
 y_i'=1-p+p d'+p beta(d-x)+P h'(d-x).                 (S)
The own opposite account is retained throughout. Under a short increase,
s'=e-s. If c=0, t'=0. If c>0, L=A and t'=t-c t^2. Whenever V>0, c can only
be1, because c>=2 would give t<=1/2. Hence
 h'=[V*1_{c=1}*t(1-t)-U(e-s)]/2.                    (H)
This yields the useful global bounds
 h'>=-1/72, and h'_+<=11/54.
For e=1, the sharper h'_+<=1/27 holds. These follow from
U(1-s)<=1/36, U*s<=1/3 and V*t(1-t)<=2/27.

4a. EASY MIXED-OWNER CASES
If d>=x and beta>=0, (S)>=1-h-1/72>0. If d>=x and beta<0, the dummy is active;
using beta>=1-s/lambda, d-x<=1 and P<=lambda/s again gives
 y_i'>=1-h-1/72>=11/24.
If d<x and beta<0, the beta term is favorable and
 y_i'>=1-h-h'_+>=29/108>0.
It remains to handle d<x and beta>=0. Dropping the nonnegative p d' and using
x-d<=x gives the sufficient lower bound
 y_i'>=1-hP(1+beta*x)-P h'_+ x.                      (R)

4b. NONMAXIMUM SHORT OWNER: e=0
Always x<=min(s,1-s)<=1/2. If s>2/3, in either dummy case
 P(1+beta*x)<=1/s<=3/2.
For the inactive dummy use lambda>=s and P<=1; for the active dummy use
P<=lambda/s and lambda<s. Also x<=1/3. Thus(R) is at least
 1-(19/36)(3/2)-(11/54)(1/3)>0.

Now s<=2/3, so U=0. If V=0 the rule and derivative reduce to the frozen
half-prefactor calculation. Suppose V>0. Then h=1/2+V^2/4 and
h'_+<=V/9. The actual maximumL must be active if the dummy is inactive.
It must also be active if the dummy is active: otherwise t=L/D<lambda<s<=2/3.

With an inactive dummy, lambda=1-R>=2-1/t. Hence
 lambda-x>=lambda-1/2>=3V/(2t).
The original half-prefactor margin is at least
 1-(1/2)(1+x/lambda)>=(lambda-x)/(2lambda)>=3V/4.
The prefactor increase costs at most(V^2/4)*2<=V/6, and the h' term costs
at most(V/9)*(1/2)=V/18. Their sum is smaller than3V/4, proving(R)>=0.

With an active dummy, lambda=(1+s-R)/2<s, so R>1-s. Since R<= (1-t)/t<1/2,
s>1/2 and t<1/(2-s). The original half-prefactor margin is at least
 1-(lambda+x)/(2s)>=(2s-1)/(2s)>=3V,
where the last inequality follows from
V<(2s-1)/[3(2-s)] and s<=2/3. The same extra costs V/6 and V/18 are smaller
than this margin. This completes every nonmaximum-short-owner case.

4c. MAXIMUM SHORT OWNER: e=1, x=s
If V=0, h<=1/2 and h'<=0. In either dummy case the frozen water identities
give P(1+beta*s)<=2, so(S)>=1-2h>=0.
Suppose V>0. There is at most one active actual cap.

If c=1 then L=1 is active. With an inactive dummy P=lambda and
P(1+beta*s)=lambda+s-lambda*s<=1. With an active dummy P=lambda^2/s and
P(1+beta*s)=2lambda-lambda^2<=1. In both cases P*s<=1 and h'_+<=1/27, so
(R)>=1-19/36-1/27=47/108>0.

If c=0 then h'=-U(1-s)/2<=0. If L is inactive, only the dummy can be active,
P=lambda/s and P(1+beta*s)=2. Since t=L/D<lambda<s, h<=1/2 and the derivative
is nonnegative.

If L is active and the dummy inactive, P=lambda/L and
P(1+beta*s)=(lambda+s)/L. Here L>=lambda>=s, t<=L and psi is2/3-Lipschitz on
[0,1], so h<=1/2+(L-s)/6. Consequently
 h(lambda+s)<= (lambda+s)/2+(L-s)(lambda+s)/6
              <=(L+s)/2+(L-s)/3<=L.
Thus(R)>=0. If L and the dummy are both active, P=lambda^2/(sL) and
P(1+beta*s)=2lambda/L. If s>=t then h<=1/2 and2h lambda<=L. If s<t<=L,
the same Lipschitz estimate gives
 2h lambda<=lambda+(lambda/3)(L-s)
            <=lambda+(L-s)/3<L,
because lambda<s and lambda<=1. This finishes the short-coordinate proof
on every D>1 cell.

4d. THE D=A FACE AND GLOBAL COMPARISONS
A persistent D=A interval with A<B has exactly one positive long cap, equal
A; hence t=1,P=1. Along a short increase h'=-U(e-s)/2. For an owner that does
not hold this long cap, d=d'=0. If e1 then h'<=0 and its derivative is>=1-h.
If e0 then x<=1-s and x h'<=1/24, so its derivative is>=1-h-1/24>0. For the
owner holding the sole long cap, d=A and d'=1; its derivative is
1+h'(1-x)>=1-1/72>0. Long-coordinate changes within this face leave the capped
amount fixed; activating another long account leaves the face continuously.

Every coordinate segment has finitely many maximum, cap and water-active
cells; the inequalities defining each choice are linear. The quadratic tilt
is continuously differentiable across s=2/3 and t=2/3, and the payments are
continuous across all remaining boundaries and the aggregate tie. Nonnegative
one-sided derivatives therefore give global own-coordinate monotonicity.
Successive coordinate changes yield every actual owner's whole-bundle
comparison, without splitting mixed owners.

5. TWO FUNDING LEMMAS FOR THE UNTILTED WATER PRODUCT
Write E0=(1/2)P(D-1) at normalized A=1.
The following sharpenings of the frozen funding proof will be used.

(i) Fixed short concentration:
 E0<=F(s), where F(s)=1/8 for0<s<=1/2,
 and F(s)=(1+s)^3/(54s) for1/2<=s<=1.
This curve is nondecreasing. To verify, apply the frozen endpoint reduction to
active actual caps. For an inactive dummy all cases give<=1/8. With an active
dummy and k remaining unit caps, k0 gives<=1/8. k1 gives<=1/8 for s<=1/2 and
(1+s)^3/(54s) above. k2 gives the same expression for s>=1/2 and<=1/8 below.
For k>=3 the increasing-in-s endpoint is bounded at s1 by
(k-1)2^k/(k+1)^(k+1)<=1/8; the other endpoint also gives<=1/8. These exhaust
the cases of the previously proved endpoint reduction. Differentiation shows
F is increasing on[1/2,1].

(ii) Dominant long concentration:
 if t>2/3 then E0<=9/64;
 if t>=3/4 then E0<=1/8.
By the dominant-cap lemma there is at most one active actual cap. If none is
active, only the dummy is active and E0<=s/8<=1/8. If the single active actual
cap is L, write R=D-L. Then R<L/2<=1/2; for t>=3/4, R<=L/3<=1/3.
With an inactive dummy lambda=1-R and
 E0=(lambda/2)(1-lambda/L)<=R(1-R)/2<=1/8.
With an active dummy lambda=(1+s-R)/2<s, so s>1-R, and
 E0=lambda^2(L+R-1)/(2sL)
    <= R(1+s-R)^2/(8s)<=R(2-R)^2/8.
The first inequality increases L to1. The second uses s>1-R and the fact that
(1+s-R)^2/s increases there, then increases s to1. The last expression is
increasing for0<=R<=1/2, giving9/64 atR1/2. When R<=1/3 it is at most25/216,
which is below1/8. This proves(ii).

6. GLOBAL277/243 FUNDING BOUND
The actual excess is E=2h E0. We show E<=34/243.

If t<=2/3, then V=0 and E<=[1-(1/2)(s-2/3)_+^2]F(s).
For s<=2/3 this is at mostF(2/3)=125/972<34/243. For s in[2/3,1], let
 g(s)=[1-(s-2/3)^2/2]*(1+s)^3/(54s).
The sign of its derivative is the sign of
 J(s)=-36s^3+27s^2+28s-14.
J is concave on this interval, with endpoint values J(2/3)=6,J(1)=5, so it is
positive throughout. Therefore g increases to
 g(1)=(17/18)*(4/27)=34/243.

If t>=3/4, use(ii) and2h<=19/18 to get E<=19/144<34/243.
For the remaining2/3<t<3/4, V^2<1/144. If s<=4/5, then(i) gives
 E<= [1+1/288] F(4/5)=(289/288)*(27/200)=867/6400<34/243.
If s>=4/5, U^2>=4/225 and(ii) gives
 E<= [1+(1/2)(1/144-4/225)]*(9/64)
   =(2387/2400)*(9/64)=7161/51200<34/243.
These four cases cover all s,t. Thus total funding<=1+34/243=277/243.
All comparisons are exact rational inequalities, including the last strict
comparison (34*51200-7161*243=677>0).

7. ATTAINMENT, EVALUATION AND LIMITS
At one short-only owner of amount1 and two long-only owners each of amount1,
s=1,t=1/2,lambda=2/3,P=8/27,h=17/36. Thus p=34/243 and funding is277/243.
The balanced prefactor here varies with concentration, so the frozen31/27
common-coefficient lower with balanced HALF payments does not apply.

The formula remains a common capped-coefficient rule, but its balanced weights
are generally not halves. All operations are rational on rational input;
water filling has the same sorting/arithmetic complexity as before. This
candidate does not establish any further Lipschitz, role-payment, false-name,
private-cost or marginal-floor property. It cannot automatically replace the
primitive of a composed network construction that requires such properties.
The existing9/8 unrestricted lower is unchanged; a surviving theorem narrows
the continuous two-pool interval to[9/8,277/243].
