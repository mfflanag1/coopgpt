SHARP SIGNED OTHER-RECIPIENT LOSS UNDER LOCAL-ONLY UNDERREPORTS
17 October 2026. Candidate theorem; independent analytical review requested.
Actual fixed owners, arbitrary mixed hardware/local holdings, arbitrary finite
owner and branch counts, all nonnegative real reports. Original symmetric
exponential two-input primitive. This supersedes the preceding 3/2 and 4/e
bounds; it does not supersede positive-part outgoing-loss results.

Let v_* be the unique solution of v exp(v)=1/2 in(0,1), and put
 Lambda=(1-v_*)[2exp(v_*)-1]=(1-v_*)^2/v_*, approximately1.19479.

THEOREM. Fix one actual owner i and decrease any of its local-resource
coordinates, holding all hardware, grid and other owners' reports fixed.
If z,z' are full and changed aggregate local-payment vectors, then

                 [sum_{k!=i}(z_k-z'_k)]_+ <= Lambda z_i.       (1)

The constant Lambda is exact. If the local pool was originally short on every
changed branch, the sharper bound is the sum of i's full LOCAL-role payments,
and its sharp constant is one. If the local pool was originally long, Lambda
is sharp even with only two actual owners. No owner identities change.

IMPORTANT SCOPE. The losses in (1) are SIGNED: other recipients' gains count
negatively. This is not a Lambda bound on the SUM of POSITIVE losses. Outer
caps may remove cancellation. A separate cap-accounting argument is required
before using (1) in a general funding bound. No novelty assertion is made.

0. PRIMITIVE AND SCALE
On a branch with shorter total T>0, capped longer accounts d_k=min(long_k,T),
shorter accounts s_k, rho=sum d_k/T, and q=exp(1-rho)/2, owner k receives
 (1-q)s_k+q d_k.
Thus rho>=1 and0<q<=1/2. At T=0 all payments are zero. At a tie the expression
is (short_k+long_k)/2, independent of orientation. Payments are continuous and
homogeneous. We normalize each nonzero original shorter total to one.

1. ORIGINAL-SHORT LOCAL INPUT: SHARP CONSTANT ONE
This section transcribes and independently checks the separate proof
mixed/outer_scalar/review_13_8/SHARP_SHORT_SIGNED_OTHER_LOSS.txt.
Write own local amount d=1-v, other local total v, full capped hardware sum
r>=1, and q=exp(1-r)/2. Full own LOCAL-role payment is U=(1-q)d.
After underreporting, local total t lies in[v,1] and hardware remains long.
For t>0 let R=sum_k min(h_k,t) and q_t=exp(1-R/t)/2. Actual fixed caps give
 tr<=R<=r and q_t<=q. Own hardware-role payment weakly decreases because both
its cap and its coefficient decrease. Adding that nonnegative own hardware
loss to the actual signed OTHER loss yields the accounting upper

                   L_aug=q(r-v)-q_t(R-v).                    (2)

If d=0 there is no change. If v=0, L_aug<=qr<=1-q=U, including new total0,
because q(r+1)<=1 for r>=1. Assume0<v<1.
For fixed t, the new term (R-v)exp(-R/t) increases then decreases in R, so
its minimum on[tr,r] occurs at an endpoint. At R=tr,
 L_aug=qr(1-t)<=qr d<=(1-q)d.
At R=r the upper increases as t decreases, hence is maximal at t=v. Put
y=r(1-v)/v. Then
 L_aug/d <=qr[1+(r-1)/y](1-exp(-y))<=qr^2<=1-q.        (3)
Here1-exp(-y)<=1 and(1-exp(-y))/y<=1 give the middle bound; the last follows
from r^2+1<=2exp(r-1), using the first three nonnegative exponential terms.
Thus signed actual OTHER loss<=U. Two separate balanced owners, one holding
hardware1 and the other local1, followed by local deletion, attain equality.

2. ORIGINAL-LONG LOCAL INPUT BEFORE A POOL SWITCH
Normalize full hardware total to one. Let s be the deviator's hardware,
d=min(its local report,1), v=sum_{k!=i}min(local_k,1), and
 q=exp(1-d-v)/2. Its full payment is z=(1-q)s+qd. If d=0 there is no effective
local change. If a changed local report stays above its full cap, nothing
changes. Otherwise write u for the decrease in its cap.
Before a pool switch the other caps are fixed, and their signed payment loss is

                    L=q(exp(u)-1)(1-s-v).                   (4)

If1-s-v<=0 this is nonpositive. Otherwise v<1, so all other local reports
were individually uncapped and their raw total is also v. The remaining
hardware-short condition gives u<=d+v-1<=dv. Since0<=u<=1,
 exp(u)-1<=(e-1)u=Ku. Consequently
 L/z<=K v(1-v)<=K/4<1.                                  (5)
The same computation covers equality and the switching boundary. If the full
local pool was tied it can instead be assigned to Section1.

3. ORIGINAL-LONG INPUT AFTER A POOL SWITCH: ACTUAL COALITION FLOOR
A switch requires the other raw local total v<1; it agrees with the full
capped total above. The changed local short total t lies in[v,1]. Let
 A=1-s be the OTHER owners' hardware total,
 W=sum_{k!=i}min(h_k,t), and q_new the actual new coefficient.
For t>0, q_new<=1/2. The exact signed other-payment loss is

          L=(1-q)(A-v)+q_new(v-W).                         (6)

If A>=v, then W>=min(A,t)>=v, so
 L<=(1-q)(A-v)<=(1-q)(1-v).
At fixed original d,v, this upper decreases with s, while its full own-payment
denominator (1-q)s+qd increases with s. Thus any positive ratio is bounded
by its s=0 value

 L/z <= (1-v)[2exp(d+v-1)-1]/d.                           (7)

If A<=v, then A<=v<=t, every OTHER hardware account is individually at most t,
and W=A exactly. Equation(6) becomes
 L=(1-q-q_new)(A-v)<=0,
because q+q_new<=1. This deals explicitly with arbitrary own mixed hardware.
It is also the actual-opponents' coalition-floor argument: those owners
retain production min(A,v) throughout the change, and their payments cover it.
No hardware fragmentation or counterfactual owner reassignment is used.

For0<v<1, original feasibility gives1-v<=d<=1. The derivative of the right
side of(7), apart from its nonnegative prefactor/d^2, has numerator
 2exp(v-1)exp(d)(d-1)+1.
Its derivative in d is2exp(v-1)exp(d)d>=0. The function therefore decreases
then increases, or is monotone, and its maximum is at an endpoint. The two
endpoint values are
              1,                    (1-v)[2exp(v)-1].        (8)
At v=0 feasibility forces d=1, and the bound is one (including t=0 by direct
zero-payment convention). A strict switch is impossible for v>=1. Hence all
zero and switching boundaries are covered.

4. EXACT SCALAR MAXIMUM AND ATTAINMENT
Let f(v)=(1-v)[2exp(v)-1] on[0,1]. Its derivative is1-2v exp(v). Since v exp(v)
strictly increases from0 to e, it has a unique maximum at v_*. This maximum
is greater than one, for f'(0)=1. Thus it dominates both endpoints in(8) and
also(5), establishing Lambda on every original-long branch.

For exact equality take TWO actual owners. Owner i holds local1 and no
hardware. The other owner holds hardware1 AND local v_*. Then q=exp(-v_*)/2,
full z_i=q, and the other payment is(1-q)+q v_*. When i reports local0, the
other owner is the sole productive owner, with output v_* and payment v_*.
Its signed loss is(1-q)(1-v_*), whose ratio to z_i is exactly Lambda.
Both owners and all their eligible resources are fixed across these reports.

5. ARBITRARY BRANCH COUNTS AND WHOLE LOCAL BUNDLES
Restore each branch's scale. On original-short branches the signed loss is
at most its own short role, hence at most its full branch payment; on
original-long branches it is at most Lambda times that full payment. Because
Lambda>1, summing over every branch changed by this same actual owner gives
sum_{k!=i}(z_k-z'_k)<=Lambda z_i. Unchanged and zero-output branches cause no
problem. Taking the positive part proves(1). The two-owner construction
already proves the constant cannot be decreased; parallel repetitions permit
arbitrary productive branch counts without changing the ratio.

This is an exact local-only signed-response theorem. The physical hardware
coordinates are held fixed. Simultaneous hardware withholding has not been
included, and no statement is made about the exact residual funding constant.
