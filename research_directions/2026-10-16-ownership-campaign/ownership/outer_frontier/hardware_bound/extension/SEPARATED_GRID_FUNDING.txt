HARDWARE-BOTTLENECK FUNDING WHEN GRID OWNERS ARE SEPARATE
16 October 2026. Candidate theorem; independent audit pending.
Original exponential primitive and actual whole-report envelope. This is a
structural ownership theorem with unrestricted owner and branch counts, not a
claim about arbitrary grid/local mixed ownership or optimal funding.

MODEL AND HYPOTHESES
Use fixed actual owners and nonnegative real reports h_ij,l_ij,p_i. Put
 H_i=sum_j h_ij, H=sum_i H_i, P=sum_i p_i,
 T_j=min(sum_i h_ij,sum_i l_ij), M=sum_j T_j,
 F=min(H,P+M), c=1/(2e), C=1+c.
The primitive Phi has, for shorter vector s and longer vector l,
 T=min(sum s,sum l), rho=sum_i min(l_i,T)/T,
 q=exp(1-rho)/2, Phi_i=(1-q)s_i+q min(l_i,T).
Zero-output payments are zero. Let z_i=sum_j Phi_i(h_.j,l_.j), Z=sum_i z_i,
 N_i=Phi_i(Hvector,pvector+zvector), and let E_i be the supremum of N_i over
all actual coordinatewise own-bundle underreports. Counts are unrestricted;
each recipient may select a different actual underreport. Owners are never
split or merged. The envelope is defined on the entire domain; the following
conditions restrict only the profile at which its budget is evaluated.

Assume H>0, P>=H, and every owner with p_i>0 has
                     h_ij=l_ij=0 for every j.          (SG)
Thus positive grid supply is owned separately from current hardware/local
reports. Owners outside this group may mix arbitrarily many hardware and local
coordinates. Hypothesis(SG) may equivalently be imposed by fixed eligibility,
but no change in eligibility is used in the proof.

THEOREM
Under these hypotheses,
 sum_i E_i <=Gamma H=Gamma F,
 Gamma=3/4+1/e+sqrt(e)/6 <(1+1/(2e))^2=C^2.              (1)
Numerically Gamma is approximately1.3926663. This is a proved uniform upper
bound for the specified nested envelope on the stated ownership class; neither
optimality of Gamma nor sharpness against all mechanisms is asserted.

DEPENDENCIES
We use previously proved properties of the exponential primitive: own-report
monotonicity, continuity, coalition coverage, and total payment at most CT.
We also use its all-profile local-response inequalities, for separately chosen
actual owner underreports (superscript i):
 sum_i[(Z-Z^i)/3-(z_i-z_i^i)]_+ <=kappa Delta_-,          (2)
 sum_i[(Z^i-Z)-(z_i-z_i^i)]_+ <=2 Delta_+,               (3)
where kappa=20/21 (kappa=1 is sufficient here),
 Delta_- and Delta_+ are the sums of delta_j=CT_j-Z_j on full local branches
with rho_j<=2 and rho_j>2, respectively. Zero-output branches contribute zero.
The consolidated sources are
 certificates/box_bound/UNIVERSAL_RETENTION_AND_OUTER_DOMINANCE_THEOREM.txt
for(2) and certificates/box_bound/UNIVERSAL_TOTAL_INCREASE_LEMMA.txt for(3).
These inequalities concern actual fixed owners and the full continuous domain.

PROOF
Normalize H=1. Every individual local payment is at most its branch output,
so every aggregate local account z_i^i is at most M^i<=M<=1.
For an arbitrary own underreport by a nongrid owner, restore its OUTER hardware
coordinate to full H_i while retaining the locally recomputed payments. This
analytical comparison increases its outer payment by primitive own monotonicity.
Its grid coordinate is zero and cannot change. Grid owners have no hardware or
local report, so their local payments stay zero under every owner's deviation.

Set pi=sum_i min(p_i,1)>=1. Every restored outer comparison is hardware-short,
since the unchanged grid total alone is at least1. Grid caps are fixed, while
no local recipient is capped. Thus its opposing capped total is EXACTLY
                  S^i=pi+Z^i,
not merely an estimate in terms of an uncapped total. Put
 q0=exp(1-pi-Z)/2 <=1/2, a_i=H_i, g_i=z_i-a_i.
For a nongrid owner, write D_i=z_i-z_i^i>=0. The restored outer payment is
                  a_i+q_i(z_i-D_i-a_i),
where q_i=exp(1-pi-Z^i)/2. Its full raw payment is a_i+q0*g_i.
For a grid owner, its only possibly positive coordinate is p_i. With others
fixed, the primitive is own-monotone, so E_i=N_i exactly. In particular grid
owners introduce no own-envelope gain despite their arbitrary reports/count.

LOCAL ROLE BOUND
For each branch decompose Phi_i=u_ij+v_ij into hardware and local-resource
components. If hardware is short, u=(1-q)h_i and v=q min(l_i,T); hence u<=h_i
and sum_i v=q rho T<=T/2=H_j/2. If local resource is short, u=q min(h_i,T)
and v=(1-q)l_i. Again u<=h_i, and
 sum_i v=(1-q)T<=rho T/2<=H_j/2.
The middle inequality follows because f(r)=r/2-1+q(r) vanishes at1 and has
derivative1/2-q(r)>=0 for r>=1. In both cases q(r)r<=1/2.
Summing branches gives
                sum_i(g_i)_+ <=sum_ij v_ij<=1/2.       (4)
Consequently at most one owner has g_i>1/3, and every such g_i is at most1/2.

TOTAL-DECREASING COMPARISONS
Suppose Z^i<=Z, put u=Z-Z^i>=0 and epsilon_i=[u/3-D_i]_+. Exact subtraction
of the two outer payments gives
 gain_i=q0[(exp(u)-1)g_i-exp(u)D_i]
       <=q0 f_{g_i}(u)+q0 exp(u)epsilon_i,
 f_g(u)=exp(u)(g-u/3)-g.                                (5)
Since pi+Z^i>=1, q0 exp(u)<=1/2. For g<=1/3, f_g is nonincreasing from0,
because f_g'(u)=exp(u)[g-(1+u)/3]<=0. If1/3<g<=1/2 its maximum over u>=0
occurs at u=3g-1 and equals exp(3g-1)/3-g, which increases in g. Hence
             max_{u>=0} f_g(u)<=K=sqrt(e)/3-1/2.
By(4) at most one owner requires this positive K allowance. Taking positive
parts in(5), summing, and applying(2), gives
 sum_{Z^i<=Z}(gain_i)_+ <=q0 K+(kappa/2)Delta_-.         (6)
K>0 follows from exp(1/2)>1+1/2.

TOTAL-INCREASING COMPARISONS
Suppose Z^i>Z, write u=Z^i-Z>0 and D=D_i. Interpolate only the two aggregate
numbers t=Z+s and own local account z_i-(D/u)s, for0<=s<=u. This is an
algebraic interpolation, not an assumed actual report path. It keeps the own
local account nonnegative, because the endpoint z_i-D is nonnegative. The
outer expression on this path is
 W(s)=a_i+q0 exp(-s)[z_i-(D/u)s-a_i].
Its derivative is
 W'(s)=q0 exp(-s)[a_i-z_i+(D/u)s-D/u].
Because a_i<=1 and the interpolated own local account is nonnegative, the
bracket is at most1-D/u. If D>=u the derivative is nonpositive. If D<=u,
it is at most q0(1-D/u), uniformly in s. Therefore
                  (gain_i)_+<=q0(u-D)_+.
Equation(3), valid for the independently selected actual deviations before
this algebraic interpolation, now yields
 sum_{Z^i>Z}(gain_i)_+<=2q0 Delta_+<=Delta_+.            (7)
The distinction between actual local profiles in(3) and the scalar comparison
path used only for the outer expression is essential.

SUM AND EXACT CONSTANT
Full raw outer funding is at most C. Combining(6)--(7), using q0<=1/2 and
kappa<=1, gives
 sum_i E_i <=C+K/2+Delta_-+Delta_+.
Each local primitive has total Z_j>=T_j, since its capped long ratio is>=1.
Thus Delta_-+Delta_+=CM-Z<=cM<=c. Consequently
 sum_i E_i<=C+c+K/2=3/4+1/e+sqrt(e)/6=Gamma.
The estimates were uniform over independent actual owner underreports, so
passing to their separate suprema is justified. Scaling restores H.

For the strict comparison with C^2, note
 C^2-Gamma=1/4+1/(4e^2)-sqrt(e)/6>0.
Indeed e<11/4<25/9 implies sqrt(e)<5/3, and e<3 implies1/(4e^2)>1/36.
Hence the right side is greater than1/4+1/36-5/18=0. For completeness,
e<11/4 follows by summing through1/4! and bounding the remaining positive tail
by(1/5!)/(1-1/6)=1/100, giving e<65/24+1/100<11/4.
All equalities/pool ties are continuous; zero local outputs stay zero under
underreporting. The case H=0 has all physical and payment values zero, but is
excluded from the normalized statement only to avoid division by zero.

LIMITATION
If an owner mixes grid with local resources or hardware, it can alter locally
computed accounts while remaining grid-capped. Then S^i=pi+Z^i fails, and its
own-envelope gain need not vanish. The half-gap recipient-response theorem
provides a cap-sensitive account inequality for this missing case, but has not
yet established a C^2 funding theorem there. No conclusion for P<H is asserted
by this note.
