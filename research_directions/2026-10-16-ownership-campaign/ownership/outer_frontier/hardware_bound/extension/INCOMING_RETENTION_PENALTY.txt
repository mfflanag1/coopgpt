INCOMING RETENTION PENALTY AND GENERAL NEGATIVE-GAP CONCENTRATION
16 October 2026. Candidate; independent review pending.
Dependencies: accepted GLOBAL_DIFFUSE_SECOND_ORDER.txt and
SHARP_LOCAL_ONLY_ENTROPY.txt, and the original exponential primitive's own
monotonicity. Fixed actual owners throughout; unit-coefficient depth-two model.
Put K=e-1, c=1/(2e), C=1+c, epsilon=max z_i/H, and L=log(1/epsilon).

RESULTS
(A) For one original-long coordinate, incoming to other recipients obeys
 I<=v[(K-1)V+D],                                         (1)
where v is the OTHER capped-long mass divided by full short total, V is the
owner's full long-role payment, and D is its ACTUAL own payment loss under
that coordinate underreport (its own short coordinate stays fixed). Both pool
orientations, switches, mixed owners and real reports are included.

(B) Combining (1) with sharp entropy and actual negative-gap accounting gives
for the unrestricted diffuse own-envelope supremum W:

 limsup_{epsilon->0} [W-C-cKepsilon(L-log L)]/epsilon
 <=c(K-2).                                              (2)

The coefficient is an upper, not a claimed exact value. This is a general
inequality/optimization, not a new bounded lower witness or a strategic merging
of identities. The actual global own-envelope rule is unchanged.

1. PROOF OF THE LONG-COORDINATE RETENTION PENALTY
Normalize the full short total to 1. Let d<=1 be the owner's capped long
report, rho=d+v the full capped ratio, q=exp(1-rho)/2, and V=qd.
Let I denote OTHER recipients' total positive increase, D the owner's actual
own payment loss. Own monotonicity gives D>=0. Zero d is immediate.

Before a pool switch, reduce its cap by u in[0,d]. The incoming bound is
I<=q(e^u-1)v. Its remaining long-role payment is V'=q(d-u)e^u. Consequently
 I/v+V'<=q[(1+d-u)e^u-1]<=q(e^d-1)<=K V                 (3)
if v>0. The first expression is nondecreasing in u, with derivative
q(d-u)e^u>=0. The owner's short-role payment also decreases, so D>=V-V'.
Equation (3) yields I<=v[(K-1)V+D]. If v=0, I=0 directly.

After a pool switch, v<1 is the actual other original-long total, and the new
short total t lies in[v,1]. Let q_new be the new coefficient. Its capped ratio
is at most 1/t and at least 1, so
 exp(1-1/t)/2<=q_new<=1/2.
As in the sharp local-only entropy proof, opponents' original-short roles all
weakly decrease and
 I<=v(1-q-q_new).
The owner's new original-long (now short) role is Z'=(1-q_new)(t-v), whereas
its original-short role also weakly decreases. Thus D>=V-Z'. It suffices to
show I+vZ'<=K v V, or

 (1-q_new)(1+t-v)<=q(1+K d).                             (4)

For the right side, d ranges over[1-v,1]. The function
f(d)=exp(-d)(1+K d) increases then decreases, so its minimum on this interval
is at an endpoint. At d=1 it equals1; at d=1-v it is at least1 because
exp(1-v)<=1+K(1-v), the exponential's chord inequality on[0,1]. Hence
q(1+Kd)>=exp(1-v)/2.

For the left side set a=1-v, so a>=1-t. The function (t+a)exp(-a) is
nonincreasing for a>=1-t. Therefore, using q_new's lower bound,

 2(1-q_new)(t+a)exp(-a)
 <=[2-exp(1-1/t)](t+a)exp(-a)
 <=[2-exp(1-1/t)]exp(t-1)<=1.

The last inequality is exp(1-t)+exp(1-1/t)>=2, proved in the accepted sharp
local-only entropy note. Thus the left side of (4) is at most exp(a)/2,
which proves (4). When v=0 all other original-long accounts are zero and I=0;
zero final total is handled directly. Restoring an arbitrary short total T
multiplies I,V,D by T and leaves v dimensionless. This proves (1).

2. COMBINE BRANCHES WITHOUT DISCARDING THE OWNER'S LOSS
Work in the hardware-restoration regime S>=3/2+e epsilon; all actual envelopes
may retain hardware/grid and change local resources only. Let X be full
production on originally hardware-short branches, as in the global proof.
On such a branch j write V_j for this owner's full long-LOCAL role, D_j>=0
for its actual own local-payment loss, and
 R_j=(V_j-D_j)_+, W_j=K V_j-R_j.
These are auxiliary real quantities, not reports or new owners. They satisfy
0<=R_j<=V_j and (K-1)V_j<=W_j<=K V_j.

The retention bound (1) and the sharp entropy bound together give
 I_j<=W_j log(T_j/(2V_j)).                               (5)
Indeed, if D_j<V_j then (1) bounds I_j by
v_j[(K-1)V_j+D_j]=v_j W_j, and
v_j<=log(T_j/(2V_j)) as in the sharp entropy proof. If D_j>=V_j then
W_j=K V_j and the sharp entropy bound directly gives (5).

Put V=sum_j V_j, R=sum_j R_j, W=K V-R. Log-sum applied with weights W_j
(and omitting zero terms) yields
 sum_j W_j log(T_j/(2V_j))
 <=W log[(sum_j T_j W_j/V_j)/(2W)]
 <=W log(KX/(2W)).                                      (6)
Original-short LOCAL branches contribute incoming at most z_i-V. The owner's
actual total local-payment loss D includes the nonnegative losses on every
branch, so D>=sum_j(V_j-R_j)=V-R. Therefore

 I<=z_i-V+W log(KX/(2W)),
 D>=V-R, W=KV-R, 0<=R<=V<=z_i<=epsilon.                  (7)

The entropy term is zero at W=0. Actual parameters also obey V<=X/2. In the
asymptotic range used below, X>=sqrt(epsilon)>2epsilon, so the relaxed range
V<=epsilon automatically satisfies this condition and all logarithms in (7)
are nonnegative.

3. THE ACTUAL OWN-LOSS CHARGE IS (g+1)D
Fix a full negative outer gap g=a_i-d_i>0. It implies the owner's opposing
account is uncapped, and its local-only underreport reduces that account by
exactly D. Opponents' capped-total increase is at most I, even with arbitrary
opponent overflow, so S'-S<=I-D. Consequently its actual envelope gain is
bounded at this report by
 q0[g-exp(D-I)(g+D)], q0=exp(1-S)/2.
Since exp(D)(g+D)>=g+(g+1)D and 1-exp(-I)<=I,

 gain_i<=q0[g I-beta(g+1)D],                             (8)
 beta=exp[-Kepsilon(1+L)].

The sharper local-only bound in SHARP_LOCAL_ONLY_ENTROPY.txt gives
I<=max{Kepsilon L,e epsilon}<=Kepsilon(1+L) for all sufficiently small epsilon.
This is the dependency justifying exp(-I)>=beta; the earlier GLOBAL bound
with an additional epsilon term is not needed. No approximation is used in
(8). Truthful reporting remains an allowed zero-gain choice. This extra gD charge accounts for the owner's own
uncapped local loss decreasing the outer total as well as its own account.

4. A CONVEX COMMON SCALAR BOUND CONCENTRATES THE CHARGE
For X>=sqrt(epsilon), define J_X(g) as the supremum of

 g[epsilon-V+W log(KX/(2W))]-beta(g+1)(V-R),              (9)

over 0<=R<=V<=epsilon, W=KV-R. It is a relaxation of (7)--(8), obtained by
replacing z_i by epsilon. Each objective is affine in g with nonpositive
intercept. J_X is convex, nonnegative, and J_X(0)=0 (V=R=0 is allowed).
It follows directly from convexity that, for nonnegative g_i,

 sum_i J_X(g_i)<=J_X(sum_i g_i).

For example, compare each g_i to their sum G by the convex chord from0 toG
and sum. Convexity, nonnegativity and J_X(0)=0 also imply J_X is nondecreasing
on g>=0. The hardware-role accounting gives sum_i g_i=G_-<=1-X/2.
Thus ALL actual negative owners together gain at most

                   q0 J_X(1-X/2).                     (10)

This is concentration of a common scalar upper bound, not merging actual
owners, their reports, their local outputs or their incentives.

For sqrt(epsilon)<=X<=1/4 let g=1-X/2>=7/8. At fixed V, the derivative of
(9) with respect to R is
 -g[log(KX/(2W))-1]+beta(g+1).
Uniformly over W<=Kepsilon, this is negative for all sufficiently small
epsilon, since log(KX/(2W))>=L/2-log2 and g>=7/8. Hence the maximum has R=0.
With R=0, the derivative with respect to V is
 K g[log(X/(2V))-1]-g-beta(g+1),
again uniformly positive for V<=epsilon and all sufficiently small epsilon.
The maximum has V=epsilon. Therefore, uniformly in this X range,

 J_X(1-X/2)=K(1-X/2)epsilon log(X/(2epsilon))
               -beta(2-X/2)epsilon.                    (11)

5. OUTSIDE THIS X RANGE A FIXED FRACTION OF THE LEADING TERM IS LOST
For X<=sqrt(epsilon), the older uniform incoming estimate gives at most
(K/2)epsilon L+O(epsilon) for each negative recipient (and O(epsilon) if
X<epsilon). The summed negative-gap mass is at most1. For X>=1/4 it is at
most7/8, while the incoming bound is at most Kepsilon L+O(epsilon).
The positive-gap contribution is O(epsilon), uniformly after maximizing the
outer ratio: q0(A-1)(r+1), A=exp(Kepsilon), is O(epsilon) for every r>=0.
Combining each bound with the raw expression 1+q0 r and optimizing r as in
the accepted global proof shows that these two regions have respectively
funding at most
 C+(cK/2)epsilon L+O(epsilon),
 C+(7cK/8)epsilon L+O(epsilon).
They are eventually below the accepted lower C+cKepsilon(L-log L)+O(epsilon).
The small-S profiles excluded from hardware restoration also remain a fixed
amount below C. They do not affect the limsup in (2).

6. FINAL UNIFORM SCALAR OPTIMIZATION
Set Y=X/2. The maximum of (11) divided by epsilon, in the remaining X region,
is the maximum of
 K(1-Y)(L+log Y)-beta(2-Y).
Its derivative is K(1/Y-1-L-log Y)+beta, and its second derivative is negative.
For small epsilon the unique interior maximizer, written x=1/Y, satisfies
 x+log x=L+1-beta/K.
Thus x=L-log L+1-1/K+o(1), since beta tends to1. At this optimum the value is

 Q_epsilon=K[x-2+1/x]-beta
           =K(L-log L-1)-2+o(1).                        (12)

The maximizing X~2/L lies in [sqrt(epsilon),1/4]. Add the accepted positive
charge q0(A-1)(r+1) and raw total1+q0 r. The same exact outer-ratio maximum is

 sum_i E_i<=1+c A exp[(A-1+epsilon Q_epsilon)/A].         (13)

Expansion uses epsilon^2 L^2=o(epsilon), and gives
 sum_i E_i<=C+cKepsilon(L-log L)+c(K-2)epsilon+o(epsilon).
All optimization errors are explicit scalar functions of epsilon, independent
of owner or branch count. Together with the excluded-region estimates this
proves (2) uniformly over the unrestricted W class.

The matching leading/loglog theorem remains unchanged. The new one-capped
lower in DIFFUSE_ONE_CAPPED_GRID_LOWER.txt, if independently accepted, gives
liminf at least -c[e+1+Klog2]; there remains a nonzero order-epsilon gap.
