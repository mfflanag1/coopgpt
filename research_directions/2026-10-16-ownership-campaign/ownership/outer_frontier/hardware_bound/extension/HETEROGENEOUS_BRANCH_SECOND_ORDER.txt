HETEROGENEOUS-BRANCH SECOND-ORDER LIMITATION
16 October 2026. Candidate; independent review pending.
This is a broader CLASS-SPECIFIC result. It does not establish a general
second-order upper bound for W(epsilon). All actual identities remain fixed.

THEOREM
Consider the class in Section 1. Let W_het(epsilon) be its supremum of actual
own-report-envelope funding, normalized by H=1, with every full local payment
z_i<=epsilon. As epsilon decreases to zero,

 W_het(epsilon)=C+cK epsilon[L-log L]+O(epsilon),
 L=log(1/epsilon), c=1/(2e), C=1+c, K=e-1.

The upper is in fact C+cK epsilon[L-log L]+o(epsilon). Thus arbitrary
branch-specific ratios, arbitrary local-resource bundles/payment heterogeneity,
and multiple owners holding hardware elsewhere cannot remove the loglog loss
within this architecture. The matching order follows from the previously
proved common-proportion subclass. The order-epsilon constant for this larger
class is not determined.

1. PRECISE CLASS
There are finitely many productive branches j. Hardware supply T_j>0 and local
supply rho_j T_j, with rho_j>=3. Write M=sum_j T_j<=1. Every local coordinate
is at most T_j; thus its report can be written d_ij T_j, with 0<=d_ij<=1 and
sum_i d_ij=rho_j. Neither d_ij nor rho_j is required to be common across j.

Each productive hardware owner is a pure hardware-only actual owner on just
one branch, holds at most T_j/2, and has full local payment at most epsilon.
All productive hardware is partitioned among such fixed owners. Local-resource
owners may have arbitrary coordinates d_ij across productive branches, and may
also have hardware a_i on a branch with zero local supply. They have no productive
branch hardware or grid. Require sum_i a_i=1-M. Owners with zero local reports
may hold some of this outside hardware. The case M=1 allows every a_i=0.

Two additional pure grid owners each have P/2, where P=2-Z and Z is the full
local payment total. Since M<=Z<=CM and C<2, P>0. All full opposing accounts
are below 1 for epsilon<1, and P/2<1. The full outer opposing total is exactly
2. Hence physical F=H=1, full outer coefficient c, and full raw total C.
Every actual owner can underreport arbitrary parts of its full real bundle.
No restriction on those deviations, number of owners, or number of branches is
imposed. The local-payment diffuseness hypothesis is max_i z_i<=epsilon.

2. PURE HARDWARE AND GRID OWNERS HAVE NO ENVELOPE GAIN
Pure grid owners affect no local payment; primitive own monotonicity gives E=N.
For a pure productive hardware owner on branch j the proof in Section 2 of
DIFFUSE_WITNESS_SECOND_ORDER_OBSTRUCTION.txt applies without common branch
ratios or common local cap vectors. For completeness, decreasing hardware by
delta raises that branch's capped ratio R>=rho_j>=3. On a smooth cap cell,

 dZ_j/dh=1+q[R^2-R-1+m(2-R)],
 1<=dZ_j/dh<=1+5/(2e^2)<3/2.

All changed outer opposing accounts remain uncapped: each local payment is at
most M-delta<=1-delta=H', and each grid account is P/2<=1-M/2<=H' because
delta<=T_j/2<=M/2. The changed opposing total is at least 2-3delta/2, so its
outer ratio R_o>=2. Write Q for the outer coefficient and q for the changed
local coefficient. The deviator's payment is h(1-Qq). Derivatives with respect
to increasing h satisfy
 0<=Q'<=1/(2H'), 0<=q'<=1/[2(T_j-delta)], Q,q<=1/2.
Also h/H'<=1 and h/(T_j-delta)<=1/2. Therefore
 d[h(1-Qq)]/dh>=1-1/4-1/4-1/8=3/8.
Continuity treats every cap boundary. Its actual own envelope equals N.

3. ONLY LOCAL OWNERS WITH NEGATIVE OUTER GAPS CAN GAIN
Fix one local-resource owner i, holding outside hardware a_i. That hardware
can be restored after any local underreport as an ACTUAL feasible report: it
has no effect on local payments, and outer primitive own monotonicity applies.
Thus it suffices to consider local underreports with full a_i retained.

On branch j this owner reduces its cap share by u_j in [0,d_ij]. The local
ratio stays at least rho_j-1>=2. The total branch payment is
 T_j[1+q(r)(r-1)], q(r)=exp(1-r)/2.
Its derivative with respect to r is T_j q(r)(2-r)<=0. Consequently every such
underreport raises or preserves the local total, so Delta=Z'-Z>=0. Its own
local payment decreases or stays fixed: z_i'<=z_i by primitive monotonicity.
All opposing accounts remain uncapped, because every local payment is at most
M<=1 and the fixed grid accounts are below 1. The outer ratio is 2+Delta.
The actual gain is therefore

 N_i'-N_i=c[a_i(1-exp(-Delta))+exp(-Delta)z_i'-z_i].       (1)

If z_i>=a_i this gain is nonpositive. Indeed if z_i'>=a_i, both the coefficient
of the nonnegative gap and the gap itself decrease. If z_i'<a_i, the changed
payment is below a_i, whereas the original payment was at least a_i.
Thus only owners with negative full gap a_i>z_i can gain.
For every owner, dropping its nonpositive own-local-payment term in (1) gives

 N_i'-N_i<=c a_i Delta.                                  (2)

Since z_i'<=z_i, the total increase Delta is at most the positive incoming
change to the other local recipients. The accepted sharp entropy theorem gives

 Delta<=K z_i[1+log(M/z_i)],                              (3)

with continuous zero value at z_i=0. This applies to the arbitrary mixed
multi-branch actual local bundle; here its outside hardware is restored.

4. SUMMATION AND THE LOCAL-PRODUCTION TRADEOFF
For fixed M, the function z[1+log(M/z)] is increasing on [0,M]. If M>=epsilon,
(2)--(3) imply each envelope gain is at most
 cK a_i epsilon[1+log(M/epsilon)].
Sum over all local owners and use sum a_i=1-M. The pure hardware and grid
owners have no gain by Section 2. Therefore

 sum_i E_i-C<=cK epsilon(1-M)[1+log(M/epsilon)]             (4)

for M>=epsilon. If M<epsilon, use z[1+log(M/z)]<=M instead: the sum of gains
is at most cK M(1-M)<=cK epsilon, which is asymptotically smaller than (4)'s
maximum. These bounds make no strategic merger of owners: the sum of their
fixed outside-hardware endowments is an ordinary accounting identity.

For M in [epsilon,1], let f(M)=(1-M)(1+L+log M). It is strictly concave:
 f''(M)=-1/M-1/M^2<0.
For small epsilon its unique interior maximizer, written x=1/M, satisfies
 x+log x=L+2. Hence x=L-log L+2+o(1). At that optimum,
 f(M)=(1-1/x)(x-1)=x-2+1/x=L-log L+o(1).
Substituting in (4) proves the claimed upper.

The common-proportion class in DIFFUSE_WITNESS_SECOND_ORDER_OBSTRUCTION.txt
is a subclass here. Its exact premium is
 cK epsilon[L-log L]-c[2e+K log 2]epsilon+o(epsilon).
This supplies the lower to within O(epsilon), proving the theorem.

5. SCOPE AND THE REMAINING GENERAL PROBLEM
This expands the class-specific loglog obstruction beyond identical local
proportions and common branch ratios. It does not allow productive hardware
owned jointly with local resources, grid held by mixed owners, low local ratios,
or an arbitrary full outer profile. Those exclusions are substantive. In
particular, this is not a second-order upper bound for the unrestricted W.
The current unrestricted theorem matches only the leading coefficient cK of
epsilon log(1/epsilon).
