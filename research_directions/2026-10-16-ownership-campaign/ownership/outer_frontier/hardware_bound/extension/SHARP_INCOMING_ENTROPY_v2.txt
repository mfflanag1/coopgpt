1. IMPROVED LONG-STAGE SWITCH ESTIMATE
Use the notation of Section3 of the frozen v1: at start of the original-long
stage normalize short total to1, own capped long amount d<=1, coefficientq,
and long role V=qd. If no switch occurs, incoming positive gain is <=K V rho,
where K=e-1 and rho is the initial capped ratio.

If a switch occurs, the other actual original-long total v<1 and the reduction
until the tie is u=d+v-1=rho-1, with0<=u<=d<=1 and q=exp(-u)/2. The pre-switch
gain is <=q(exp(u)-1)v. The short-stage estimate after the tie gives at most
(1-v)/2 for the rest of the reduction. Hence the total is at most
 q[(exp(u)-1)v+exp(u)(1-v)]=q(exp(u)-v).
Since v=1+u-d,
 exp(u)-v=d+[exp(u)-1-u].
For u>0, usingd>=u andexp(u)-1-u>=0,
 [exp(u)-v]/d<=1+[exp(u)-1-u]/u=(exp(u)-1)/u<=K.
Atu=0 the ratio is1 ifd>0, and a zero d has no effect. Thus the entire long
stage has incoming gain <=K V in the switching case, and <=K V rho in all
cases because rho>=1. Restoring T and using V/T=qd<=q gives
 G_long<=K V[1+log(T/(2V))].                              (1)
The upper bound applies to any partial reduction, not only complete deletion.

3. THE CONSTANT K IS SHARP
On one branch take one pure hardware owner with amount1 and n pure local-resource
owners each with amount1, n>=3. All actual identities and roles remain fixed
throughout the deviation. Then T=M=1,rho=n,q=exp(1-n)/2. One resource owner i
hasz_i=q; let it reduce its own amount from1 to0. The new coefficient iseq.
The other n-1 resource owners each gainKq, and the hardware owner losesKq.
Therefore the total positive OTHER gain is exactly(n-1)Kq, and
 G_i/{z_i[1+log(M/z_i)]}=K(n-1)/(n+log2) ->K.
No smaller universal multiplicative constant in(I-sharp) is possible. This is
also an exact obstruction to any universal linear incoming boundG_i<=L z_i:
G_i/z_i=K(n-1) diverges. Counts grow, but each member uses one fixed owner set
and an ordinary feasible own-coordinate underreport.

6. SHARP DIFFUSE LIMIT, WITH GROWING BRANCH AND OWNER COUNTS
