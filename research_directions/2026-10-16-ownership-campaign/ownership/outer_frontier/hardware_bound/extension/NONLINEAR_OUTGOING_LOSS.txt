NONLINEAR OUTGOING LOSS CONTROL BY THE ACTUAL OWNER'S PAYMENT LOSS
16 October2026. Candidate theorem; independent audit required.
Original exponential primitive; fixed actual owners, all real nonnegative
reports, simultaneous whole-bundle underreports, unrestricted counts.
No optimality or historical-priority claim.

THEOREM
Let z_i be an owner's full aggregate local payment, z_i^i its payment after
an arbitrary actual own-bundle underreport, and D_i=z_i-z_i^i>=0. Then
 L_i:=sum_{k!=i}(z_k-z_k^i)_+ <=B sqrt(z_i D_i),          (NL)
 where A^2=32/e^2, K=e-1, B^2=K^2+A^2.
In particular B<11/4. A sharper accounting version is
                 L_i^2 <=(A^2 U_i+B^2 V_i)D_i,         (NL_role)
where U_i,V_i sum its full short-role and long-role payments branchwise,
so U_i+V_i=z_i. The roles are accounting components, never separate owners.
Together with SHARP_OUTGOING_LOSS.txt, one may take the minimum of the
right side of(NL) and (e-1)z_i.

The essential new feature is L_i=0 whenever D_i=0. A bound linear in D_i
cannot hold with any universal finite coefficient, as the exact family below
shows. The numerical value of B is not claimed optimal.

SETUP ON ONE BRANCH
For original shorter vector s and longer vector l, put T=sum s,
 d_k=min(l_k,T), rho=sum d_k/T, q=exp(1-rho)/2,
 z_k=(1-q)s_k+q d_k. If T=0 all values are zero. Normalize initial T=1.
For the deviating owner i put U=(1-q)s_i, V=q d_i, so z_i=U+V.
Follow its actual underreport by first reducing its ORIGINAL short coordinate,
then reducing its ORIGINAL long coordinate. Primitive own monotonicity gives
nonnegative own-payment losses in every stage, summing exactly to D_i.
The positive loss of any other recipient is at most the sum of its positive
losses over the stages. All derivatives below are taken in the increasing
coordinate direction; positive cross derivatives bound losses when it falls.

1. A SHORT-STAGE QUADRATIC LOSS BOUND
Let full own short amount be x, other-short total v=1-x, and current short
total t in[v,1]. The current own short amount is t-v. On a smooth cap cell,
let m count capped long accounts, r=sum_k min(l_k,t)/t, q=exp(1-r)/2.
Then q'=q(r-m)/t>=0. The positive cross derivatives satisfy
 sum_{k!=i}(z_k')_+
 <=q' sum_{k!=i}min(l_k,t)+qm
 <=q[r^2-m(r-1)]<=q r^2<=2/e.                          (1)
This is the same derivative bound used in the sharp outgoing theorem.

The owner's derivative, with d=min(l_i,t) and own cap indicator a, is
 z_i'=1-q+q'(d-(t-v))+qa
      >=1-q-q(r-m)(1-v/t).                             (2)
We claim the uniform lower bound
                       z_i'>=(1-t+v)/2.                (3)
If m>=1, (2) is at least1-qr>=1/2, and v<=t proves(3).
If m=0, the full original-long total is at least1 and unchanged, so r>=1/t.
Consequently q<=exp(1-1/t)/2<=t/2, while qr<=1/2. Substitution in(2) gives
 z_i'>=1-t/2-(1-v/t)/2=(1-t+v/t)/2>=(1-t+v)/2.
The bound exp(1-1/t)<=t for0<t<=1 follows from
log(t)-1+1/t>=0, whose value is0 at1 and whose derivative is(t-1)/t^2<=0.

If the short amount falls by epsilon in[0,x], (1) and(3) imply
 positive outgoing loss H_s <=(2/e)epsilon,
 own payment loss D_s >=v epsilon/2+epsilon^2/4.         (4)
Since the initial short-role payment U>=x/2, we have
 A^2 U D_s >=(4/e^2)x epsilon(2v+epsilon)
             >=(4/e^2)epsilon^2 >=H_s^2.              (5)
The middle inequality uses x(2v+epsilon)-epsilon=v(2x-epsilon)>=0.
Thus a short-stage reduction costs at most A sqrt(U D_s), A=sqrt(32)/e.
Its long-role component q min(l_i,t) is nonincreasing during this stage,
so the long-role payment V_* at the start of the next stage satisfies V_*<=V.

2. LONG-STAGE CONTROL BEFORE A POOL SWITCH
Normalize the short total at this stage's start to1 (scaling will be restored).
Let d in[0,1] be the own capped long amount, s the own short amount, and q0
the initial coefficient. Changes while its uncapped long amount exceeds1
have no effect and can be omitted. Reducing the capped long amount by
u in[0,d] without a pool switch changes q to q0 exp(u). The positive loss to
other recipients is at most
                     H_l<=q0(exp(u)-1),                (6)
because their total original-short amount is at most1.
The ACTUAL own-payment loss is
 D_l=q0[exp(u)u-(exp(u)-1)(d-s)]
     >=q0[exp(u)u-(exp(u)-1)d].                         (7)
Its initial long-role component is V_*=q0 d. We show
                         H_l^2<=K^2 V_* D_l,
                         K=e-1.                        (8)
For u>0 the denominator
 d[exp(u)u-(exp(u)-1)d]
is concave in d on[u,1], hence minimized at d=u or d=1. At d=u its value
is u^2; convexity of exp gives(exp(u)-1)/u<=e-1 for0<u<=1.
At d=1 it is psi(u)=1-(1-u)exp(u)>0. The ratio
 R(u)=(exp(u)-1)^2/psi(u)
is increasing on(0,1]. Its derivative has the sign of
 2+u-(2-u)exp(u),
which vanishes at0 and has derivative psi(u)>=0. Therefore R(u)<=R(1)=(e-1)^2.
These two endpoint inequalities prove(8); u=0 is immediate. The use of the
full actual D_l in(7) covers arbitrary mixed owners, including nonzero s.

3. LONG-STAGE CONTROL THROUGH A POOL SWITCH
If the long reduction crosses the tie, let v<1 be the other ACTUAL long total.
At the crossing the owner's new short amount is a=1-v, with a<=d<=1. Put
u_c=d-a=d+v-1>=0. Its initial q0=exp(-u_c)/2 and V_*=q0 d. Its new SHORT-role
payment at the crossing is U_cross=a/2. We have
                      U_cross<=V_*,                    (9)
since d-a=u_c>=d(1-exp(-u_c)), using d<=1 and1-exp(-u_c)<=u_c.
Split actual own loss into D_pre and D_post before and after the crossing.
Equation(8) bounds pre-crossing outgoing loss by K sqrt(V_* D_pre).
After the crossing, the originally long coordinate is now short and remains
short; apply the short-stage result(5) from that crossing profile to bound the
post-crossing loss by A sqrt(U_cross D_post)<=A sqrt(V_* D_post).
Cauchy--Schwarz gives
 H_l<=sqrt(K^2+A^2) sqrt(V_*(D_pre+D_post))
      =B sqrt(V_* D_l).                                (10)
Without a switch(8) also implies(10), since K<=B. Any partial reduction is
covered: no coordinate is required to be reduced all the way to zero.

4. BOTH COORDINATES, THEN ALL BRANCHES
On the original branch, combining(5),(10), and V_*<=V yields
 L_branch<=A sqrt(U D_s)+B sqrt(V D_l)
          <=sqrt[(A^2U+B^2V)(D_s+D_l)]
          <=B sqrt[(U+V)D_branch].                     (11)
This uses two stages of the SAME actual owner. No virtual identity, role
change in eligibility, or artificial coalitional incentive is introduced.
All changes of scale preserve the homogeneous inequalities in(5),(8)--(11).

For a simultaneous own-bundle underreport on arbitrary branches, the positive
loss of each recipient's aggregate payment is at most the sum of its positive
branch losses. Sum(11) over branches and apply Cauchy--Schwarz again:
 L_i<=sum_j sqrt[(A^2U_ij+B^2V_ij)D_ij]
     <=sqrt[(A^2U_i+B^2V_i)sum_jD_ij].
Each D_ij>=0 by local own monotonicity and sum_jD_ij=D_i. This proves(NL_role)
and(NL). Cap/pool formulas are continuous, the coordinate paths have finitely
many boundaries, and the zero-total limit is continuous. Zero-output branches
remain zero under underreporting.

The convenient rational constant B<11/4 follows from
 e<11/4 and e>8/3:
 (e-1)^2+32/e^2 <49/16+9/2=121/16.
The series bounds e>8/3 and e<11/4 were recorded in SHARP_OUTGOING_LOSS.txt
and SEPARATED_GRID_FUNDING.txt, respectively.

WHY A NONLINEAR COUPLING IS NECESSARY
Use one hardware owner of capacity1 and two local-resource owners of capacity1
each. For one local-resource owner decrease its report from1 to1-epsilon,
with0<epsilon<1. Put c=1/(2e). The full payment of that owner is z_i=c, its
actual own payment loss is
 D_i=c[1-(1-epsilon)exp(epsilon)],
and the OTHER positive payment loss is the hardware owner's loss
 L_i=c[exp(epsilon)-1].
As epsilon decreases to0, D_i/(c epsilon^2) tends to1/2 whereas
L_i/(c epsilon) tends to1. Thus L_i/D_i is unbounded, and every universal
bound of the form L_i<=B0 sqrt(z_i D_i) must have B0>=sqrt(2).
This demonstrates necessity of the square-root scale, not optimality of B.
At complete deletion in the same family, D_i=z_i=c and L_i=(e-1)c. Thus the
stronger universal constant lower bound is B0>=e-1; the exact optimal constant
remains between e-1 and the displayed B. The small-deletion limit explains
why the exponent1/2, rather than a linear payment-loss charge, is needed.

CAP CONSEQUENCE
At fixed full hardware threshold H and restored grid, let
 w_i=(p_i+z_i-H)_+ and Dcap_i=min(p_i+z_i,H)-min(p_i+z_i^i,H).
Then D_i<=Dcap_i+w_i. Therefore
 S-S^i <=Dcap_i+min{(e-1)z_i, B sqrt[z_i(Dcap_i+w_i)]}.   (12)
If the own cap stays unchanged, outgoing cap loss is at most
min{(e-1)z_i,B sqrt(z_i w_i)}. In particular, a recipient exactly at its
cap with w_i=0 cannot keep that cap unchanged while causing any positive
loss to other capped accounts. This eliminates the specific loss-free
account relaxation used in CAPPED_SUMMATION_OBSTRUCTION.txt, although it
does not by itself prove a full C^2 funding bound in mixed-grid institutions.
