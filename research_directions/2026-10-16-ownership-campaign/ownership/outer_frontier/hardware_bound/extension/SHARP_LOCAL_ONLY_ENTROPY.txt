1. EXACT LONG-COORDINATE INCOMING BOUND, INCLUDING POOL SWITCHES
In one exponential two-pool primitive, hold all reports except one actual
owner's ORIGINAL-LONG coordinate fixed. Its own original-short coordinate may
be nonzero and is retained; all other owners may be mixed. Normalize the full
short total to T=1. Write d=min(own long report,1), rho=sum capped long reports,
q=exp(1-rho)/2, V=qd. Let I be the total positive payment increase to OTHER
actual owners after any underreport of this original-long coordinate.
Then

 I<=q(e^d-1)(rho-d)<=K V log(1/(2V)),                     (1)

with zero value if d=0. Restoring T gives K V log(T/(2V)).

Proof before a switch. Any reduction above the cap 1 has no effect. If the
capped coordinate falls by u<=d while its pool stays long, the coefficient
changes to q e^u. The other long capped accounts remain fixed. Their increases
are at most q(e^u-1) times those capped long accounts; their original-short
roles decrease. Positive-part subadditivity therefore gives
 I<=q(e^u-1)(rho-d)<=q(e^d-1)(rho-d).

Proof after a switch. The other ORIGINAL-LONG raw accounts now have total
v=rho-d<1. They were all uncapped at the full profile. The new short total t
lies in [v,1]; its original-short opposing accounts have raw total 1 and capped
sum at most 1. Hence the new coefficient is at least
 q_new>=exp(1-1/t)/2>=exp(1-1/v)/2
when v>0. Also sum_k min(s_k,t)>=t because sum_k s_k=1>=t, so the
new capped ratio is at least 1 and q_new<=1/2. Together with q<=1/2 this
ensures 1-q-q_new>=0; no positive-part sign is missing in (2).
Every opponent's original-short role is now a long role, of size
q_new min(s_k,t)<=s_k/2, no greater than its full role (1-q)s_k. Only its
original-long role can contribute an incoming increase. Thus
 I<=v[1-q-q_new]
   <=v[1-q-exp(1-1/v)/2].                                (2)
For 0<v<=1,

 exp(1-v)+exp(1-1/v)>=2.                                 (3)

Indeed the logarithm of the ratio of the positive and negative terms in the
derivative of their sum is v-1/v-2log v. Its derivative is (1-v)^2/v^2>=0,
and it equals zero at v=1; hence it is nonpositive for v<=1. The sum in (3)
is nonincreasing up to its value 2 at 1. Since rho=d+v,
q e^d=exp(1-v)/2. Inequality (3) therefore transforms (2) into
 I<=q v(e^d-1), proving the first bound in (1) across the switch as well.
At v=0 there are no other original-long reports and every opponent's original-
short role decreases, so I=0 directly. This also covers zero endpoint output.

For the second bound in (1), if d>0 then
 rho-d=log(1/(2V))+log d+1-d<=log(1/(2V)),
using log d<=d-1. Also (e^d-1)/d<=e-1=K for 0<d<=1.
Both right-hand factors are nonnegative. Multiplying proves (1).

The functional bound is sharp for every V/T<=1/(2e). Take d=1 and
rho=1+log(T/(2V))>=2, with this pure long owner separate from all short owners
and all other pure long owners. Choose fixed other long reports, each <=T,
whose sum is (rho-1)T. Fully delete the chosen owner's long report. Its pool
remains long (possibly tied), and the other long owners gain exactly
K V(rho-1)=K V log(T/(2V)). The short owners only lose. All owners remain fixed;
real rho is realized using a final fractional long report if needed.

2. JOINT SHORT-LOCAL AND LONG-LOCAL INCOMING BUDGET
Now use the unit-coefficient depth-two model of GLOBAL_DIFFUSE_SECOND_ORDER.
After restoring hardware and grid as in that theorem, only local-resource
underreports remain. Let X be the full production on branches whose hardware
is originally short. For actual owner i let v_i be its total original-long
LOCAL role payment on those branches, and z_i its full total local payment.
Original-short LOCAL branches contribute incoming at most z_i-v_i; applying
(1) and log-sum to the original-long LOCAL branches gives the sharper joint
bound

 I_i<=z_i-v_i+K v_i log(X/(2v_i)).                         (4)

There is no +K v_i term. Zero roles use the continuous convention. The
contribution z_i-v_i also bounds unused hardware-role payments, so it is a
safe upper even for arbitrary mixed actual owners.

Let z_i<=epsilon and define A0=2 exp(1+1/K). If X>=A0 epsilon, the right side
of (4), at fixed z_i, is nondecreasing in v_i on 0<=v_i<=z_i because its
v derivative is -1+K[log(X/(2v_i))-1]>=0. Its maximum is at v_i=z_i.
The resulting function K z_i log(X/(2z_i)) is increasing in z_i<=epsilon
under the same condition. Therefore

 I_i<=K epsilon log(X/(2epsilon))  if X>=A0 epsilon.       (5)

If X<A0 epsilon, maximize (4) directly. Put v*=X/(2 exp(1+1/K)). The
concave function -v+K v log(X/(2v)) has global maximum K v*, so
 I_i<=epsilon+K X/[2 exp(1+1/K)]<(1+K)epsilon.             (6)
Thus the low-X region has only O(epsilon) incoming, with a fixed explicit
constant. It cannot compete with the logarithmic maximum as epsilon tends
to zero. These estimates hold for each arbitrary actual bundle underreport.

3. CONSEQUENCE FOR THE GENERAL SECOND-ORDER UPPER
Retain the exact restoration lemma, positive-gap comparison and hardware-role
accounting G_-<=1-X/2 from GLOBAL_DIFFUSE_SECOND_ORDER. Replace its Section 4
entropy bound by (5)--(6). Its optimized scalar entropy factor can now be
chosen as the maximum of e and

 K(1-X/2)log(X/(2epsilon)), A0 epsilon<=X<=1.

Put Y=X/2. The nonconstant factor is K(1-Y)(L+log Y), where L=log(1/epsilon).
For small epsilon its unique interior maximum has x=1/Y satisfying
x+log x=L+1; hence x=L-log L+1+o(1). The maximal value is

 F_epsilon=K[x-2+1/x]=K(L-log L-1)+o(1).                 (7)

The lower cutoff A0 epsilon does not affect this optimizer, which has
X~2/L; the constant e contribution is eventually smaller.
The exact same outer-ratio optimization gives

 sum_i E_i<=1+c A exp[(A-1+epsilon F_epsilon)/A],
 A=exp(Kepsilon),                                        (8)

for all full profiles in the hardware-restoration region. Its expansion is

 sum_i E_i<=C+cKepsilon[L-log L+1]+o(epsilon).             (9)

The excluded small-S profiles stay a fixed amount below C by the existing
profilewise diffuse bound. Equation (9) is therefore a UNIFORM upper for the
unrestricted W(epsilon) of that theorem, permitting arbitrary mixed owners,
arbitrary grid overflow, all real reports and unbounded finite counts.

Combined with the accepted class-specific actual-envelope lower,

 -c[2e+Klog2] <= liminf [(W-C-cKepsilon(L-log L))/epsilon]
 <=limsup [(W-C-cKepsilon(L-log L))/epsilon] <=cK.

The limits need not exist; this statement does not claim an exact remaining
coefficient. The principal new statement is the sharp long-coordinate response
bound (1), with a genuine all-switch proof and exact unbounded sharp examples.
