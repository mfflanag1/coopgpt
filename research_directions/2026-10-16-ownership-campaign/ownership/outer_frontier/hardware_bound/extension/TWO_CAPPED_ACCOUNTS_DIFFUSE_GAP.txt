TWO FULL CAPPED ACCOUNTS HALVE THE DIFFUSE LEADING PREMIUM
16 October 2026. Candidate structural corollary; review pending.
Same unit-coefficient model, actual own-report envelope and fixed actual owners
as GLOBAL_DIFFUSE_SECOND_ORDER.txt. Normalize H=1, impose max_i z_i<=epsilon,
and put c=1/(2e), C=1+c, K=e-1, L=log(1/epsilon).

Let W_2(epsilon) be the supremum restricted to full profiles with at least TWO
outer opposing accounts p_i+z_i>=1. Equality at the cap counts. Then

 W_2(epsilon)=C+(cK/2)epsilon L+O(epsilon).                (1)

This is a profile-structure theorem for this specified actual envelope, not a
lower bound against all truthful rules or an exact order-epsilon coefficient.
In particular such profiles cannot be near extremizers for the unrestricted
leading premium cKepsilon L. Arbitrary mixed ownership and full overflow remain
allowed in the upper; the lower uses an explicit unbounded fixed-owner family.

1. A CAPACITY COST FOR TWO FULL CAPPED ACCOUNTS
Let S=sum_i min(p_i+z_i,1), r=S-1. Two full capped accounts imply S>=2, r>=1.
Choose two of them. All other accounts contribute at least their full local
payments, because z_i<=epsilon<1. Therefore
 S>=2+Z-z_i-z_j>=2+Z-2epsilon>=2+X-2epsilon,               (2)
where X is full production on originally hardware-short branches. This is
actual full-profile accounting; it does not replace any opponent capped floor
by an uncapped extension.

2. UNIFORM UPPER
For all small epsilon, S>=2 implies the exact hardware-restoration hypothesis
S>=3/2+e epsilon. Hence only local-resource underreports need be optimized.
If r>=2, the generic diffuse profilewise bound (1) in the global proof is
uniformly a fixed amount below C for all small epsilon: its limit maximum over
r>=2 is 1+exp(-2)<C. Such profiles do not affect (1).

Suppose 1<=r<=2 and write y=r-1 in[0,1]. The raw funding loss from C is
 c[1-(1+y)exp(-y)]
 =c integral_0^y t exp(-t)dt>=c y^2/(2e).                (3)
When X>=4epsilon, equation (2) gives y>=X/2, so (3) is at least cX^2/(8e).
The uniform local-only incoming bound gives total negative-gap gain at most
 cKepsilon log(X/epsilon)+O(epsilon); the constant is independent of owners,
branches or X in this region. It follows from
I_i<=epsilon+Kepsilon[1+log(X/epsilon)] and G_-<=1.
The positive-gap gain is O(epsilon) uniformly because
q0(A-1)G_+<=3c(A-1), A=exp(Kepsilon), in 1<=r<=2.
Thus funding is bounded by

 C-cX^2/(8e)+cKepsilon log(X/epsilon)+O(epsilon).           (4)

Maximizing the displayed X-dependent expression over all X>0 gives
X^2=4eKepsilon and maximum
 (cK/2)epsilon L+(cK/2)[log(4eK)-1]epsilon.
If X<4epsilon, the same local-only bound gives only O(epsilon) incoming and
hence O(epsilon) premium; this region is harmless. These are explicit uniform
scalar bounds, establishing the upper in (1).

3. MATCHING UNBOUNDED ACTUAL-REPORT LOWER
For every small epsilon choose T=sqrt(epsilon),
 rho=1+log(T/(2epsilon))=1+L/2-log2, q=epsilon/T.
Restrict, for example, to L>=6. Then rho>=3, so deleting the distinguished
unit local share keeps rho-1>=1 and gives the positive Delta used below.
Use any growing number k of productive branches, each with hardware T/k.
Fixed local-resource owners have cap shares d_i in[0,1], sum_i d_i=rho,
with d_1=1; each owns d_i T/k on every branch. Real rho is realized with
full shares and one fractional remainder. Their full local payments are
z_i=epsilon d_i. Fragment each branch's hardware among pure actual hardware
owners so every full local payment is at most epsilon; finite fragmentation
always suffices. Give local owner1 the remaining hardware 1-T on a branch
with zero local resource. Give TWO further pure grid owners grid1 each.
All reports and owner identities remain fixed except during ordinary own
underreports. H=1, M=T, P=2, so the physical bottleneck is hardware.

The core local total is Z=T+epsilon(rho-1), and the full capped sum is
S=2+Z: the two grid accounts are capped exactly at1 and every other account
is a local payment at most epsilon. Hence full raw funding is
 1+c exp(-Z)(1+Z)=C+O(epsilon),
since Z=sqrt(epsilon)+O(epsilon L) and Z^2=epsilon+o(epsilon).

Owner1 deletes all of its core local reports, keeping its hardware. The changed
core coefficient is e q, and the total capped change is
 Delta=epsilon[K rho-(2e-1)]=(K/2)epsilon L+O(epsilon).
Its exact actual payment gain is
 c exp(-Z)[(1-T)(1-exp(-Delta))-epsilon]
 =(cK/2)epsilon L+O(epsilon).
Truthful reports are available to every other owner, so this gives the lower
in (1). Choosing k=floor(L) ensures growing productive-branch counts; hardware
fragmentation and local shares also give unbounded active-owner counts.
No maximization over virtual owner identities is involved.

The result supplies a concrete structural restriction for any sharp diffuse
extremizer: at the full profile, it must have at most one full capped opposing
account. It does not prohibit caps appearing after a deviation, nor assert a
fixed finite-dimensional representation of the remaining report problem.
