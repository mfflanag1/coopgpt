EXACT OBSTRUCTION TO SUMMING THE CAPPED-RECIPIENT OUTGOING BOUNDS
16 October2026. Scope: a proof relaxation, not an actual-envelope counterexample.
This family has arbitrarily many fixed owners and arbitrarily many branches.

For arbitrary positive integers m,n, use m branches. Each branch has n distinct
hardware-only owners, each with hardware1/(mn) on its branch. One fixed actual
owner G holds local resource1/m on EACH branch and grid3/5. A second owner L
holds local resource1/(5m) on EACH branch. All remaining coordinates are zero.
Thus H=1, M=1, P=3/5, and F=1 is a physical hardware bottleneck. There are
mn+2 active actual owners. This is one fixed-owner continuous technology for
each m,n; no splitting or merging occurs in any incentive statement.

Put q=(1/2)exp(-1/5). Every branch's local ratio is6/5. Aggregate local
payments are z_G=q, z_L=q/5, and hardware payments sum1-q. Since q>2/5,
owner G's opposing account3/5+q is fully capped at1. All other accounts are
uncapped. Write
 R=1-4q/5, S=1+R, q0=(1/2)exp(-R).
The full raw outer payments of all owners other than G sum exactly
                     1-q0(1-R).                        (1)

The valid FULLY_CAPPED_RECIPIENT_BOUND gives G the upper bound
 q0 exp(min((e-1)q,R))=1/2,                              (2)
because (e-1)q>R. Its refined SHORT/LONG charge gives exactly the same result:
G has U_G=0 and V_G=q, so there is no improvement from that refinement.
Hence summing this one individual upper bound with only the FULL RAW payments
of every other recipient gives
             B_relax=3/2-(1-R)exp(-R)/2 >C^2.           (3)
Every other envelope is at least its full raw payment. Consequently, merely
combining the individually valid capped-recipient bound with bounds for the
remaining recipients cannot prove C^2 if it retains the value(2): even granting
zero envelope gain to ALL remaining owners leaves the assigned budget too big.

ELEMENTARY EXACT CERTIFICATE
First exp(1/5)<1/(1-1/5)=5/4 by comparing its power series with the geometric
series; the inequality is strict. Thus q>2/5 and G really is capped.
Also e>27/10, since its series through1/4! is65/24>27/10. Therefore
 e-1/5>5/2>2exp(1/5), equivalently q(e-1/5)>1, which is exactly(e-1)q>R.

Next exp(1/5)>6/5, so1-R=(2/5)exp(-1/5)<1/3 and R>2/3. The function
(1-r)exp(-r) decreases for r<2. Since exp(2/3)>1+2/3+2/9=17/9>15/8,
 (1-R)exp(-R)/2 < exp(-2/3)/6 <4/45.
Thus B_relax>127/90. Meanwhile e>27/10 gives
 C^2=1+1/e+1/(4e^2)<1+10/27+25/729=1024/729.
Finally127/90-1024/729=47/7290>0. All comparisons are exact; no optimizer
or numerical extrapolation is needed.

INTERPRETATION AND STOPPING CONDITION
This does NOT assert E_G=1/2, does NOT claim the abstract cap-mass reduction is
an actual underreport, and does NOT refute C^2 funding. The point of the
outgoing bound is precisely that it may overestimate the cap mass removable
while keeping the owner's capped account fixed. Here attaining the relaxed
bound would require information about the owner's local-payment loss that
was discarded by charging outgoing loss only to its FULL local payment.
The theorem's scope is the natural summation of these separate individual
upper bounds. It does not obstruct a stronger joint inequality combining own
loss, ordinary retention, local geometry, and the available outer funding slack.
Because m,n are arbitrary, this limitation persists with unbounded owner and
branch counts and is not an artifact of a fixed-size sampled report domain.
