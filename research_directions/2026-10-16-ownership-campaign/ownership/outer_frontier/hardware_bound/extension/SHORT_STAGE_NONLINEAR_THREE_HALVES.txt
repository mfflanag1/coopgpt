LEMMA
Normalize a primitive's full shorter total to1. Reduce only one actual owner's
coordinate in that shorter pool, keeping all other reports fixed. Let U be its
full SHORT-role payment, D its full actual own-payment loss, and H_out the sum
of OTHER recipients' positive losses. Then
                         H_out^2<=(9/4) U D.            (SNL)
This permits mixed owners, arbitrary real reports and all cap changes.

PROOF
Let full own short amount be x, final short total t, epsilon=1-t<=x, full q=u,
final q=v<=u. Put r=1-log(2u), R=1-log(2v), so R>=r>=1. The full and final
capped long masses are r and tR; hence tR<=r. Write H for the positive loss.
The total long-role payment at short total T is q(T)rho(T)T. Its derivative
on a cap cell is q[rho^2-m(rho-1)]>=0 and bounds the sum of the OTHER positive
cross derivatives. Consequently
                         H<=ur-vtR.                    (1)
The same derivative is at most2/e, so also H<=(2/e)epsilon.

The recipient's long-role component decreases during the short reduction.
Its actual payment loss is therefore at least the loss of its short-role
component:
 D>=D_s=epsilon(1-v)-x(u-v), U=(1-u)x.                  (2)
For fixed u,v,t the product U D_s is concave in x on[epsilon,1]. It is
therefore at least the smaller of its two endpoint values. We bound H^2
against each endpoint separately. This is a scalar inequality; no reports
or actual owner identities are being changed in the mechanism.

At x=epsilon, U=D_s=(1-u)epsilon>=epsilon/2. Thus
 H^2/(U D_s)<=(4/e)^2<9/4, since e>8/3.
At x=1,
 H^2/(U D_s) <=(ur-vtR)^2/[(1-u)((1-u)-t(1-v))].       (3)
The denominator is positive for0<=t<r/R, except for trivial zero-change limits.
Indeed f(s)=(1-q(s))/s decreases on s>=1, because
 f'(s)=[q(s)(s+1)-1]/s^2<=0. Thus
 (1-v)/R<=(1-u)/r, which gives denominator nonnegativity through t=r/R.
The quadratic-over-positive-linear expression in(3) is convex in t. Its
maximum on[0,r/R] is therefore at an endpoint (including continuous limits).
At t=0 its value is [ru/(1-u)]^2<=1.

At t=r/R write delta=R-r>0, A=1-exp(-delta), and z=ru/(1-u). Direct
substitution reduces the endpoint value to
                         z^2 A^2(r+delta)/(delta-zA).  (4)
Here z<=1/r<=1. To see this, (r^2+1)q(r) decreases from1 on r>=1: its
derivative is -q(r)(r-1)^2. Thus r^2u<=1-u, as required. The denominator
in(4) is strictly positive because delta>A and z<=1. Hence(4) is at most
 z A^2(1+z delta)/(delta-zA)
 <=A^2(1+delta)/(delta-A)=G(delta).                     (5)
The second inequality follows because z(1+z delta)/(delta-zA) increases on
[0,1]: its derivative has the positive numerator
 delta[1+2z delta-z^2 A].
If delta=0, then u=v and (3) equals z^2(1-t)<=1 directly, so this limiting
case need not divide by delta-A.

EXACT SCALAR CERTIFICATE
For every delta>0,
 4(1+delta)(1-exp(-delta))^2<9(delta-1+exp(-delta)),       (6)
which gives G(delta)<9/4. Let F be the right side minus the left side of(6).
Then
 exp(2delta)F=(5delta-13)exp(2delta)+(17+8delta)exp(delta)
               -4(1+delta).
The constant and linear Taylor coefficients vanish. For n>=2 its coefficient
is [2^(n-1)(5n-26)+8n+17]/n!, giving successively1/2,-1/2,1/24,41/120 for
n=2,3,4,5. Every coefficient for n>=6 is positive. Therefore
 exp(2delta)F >=delta^2[60-60delta+5delta^2+41delta^3]/120>0.
For0<delta<=1 the bracket is60(1-delta)+5delta^2+41delta^3>0. For delta>=1,
its derivative123delta^2+10delta-60 is positive and its value at1 is46.
This establishes(6) on the full unbounded real interval, without a grid.

Both x endpoints now satisfy H^2<=(9/4)U D_s, and concavity transfers the
bound to every original x. Since D>=D_s, this proves(SNL). Continuity handles
zero totals, cap boundaries, and zero changes. Scaling restores arbitrary
full short totals.
