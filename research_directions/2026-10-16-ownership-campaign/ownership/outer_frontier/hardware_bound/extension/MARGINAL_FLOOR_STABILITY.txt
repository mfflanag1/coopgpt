MARGINAL-FLOOR STABILITY FOR THE EXPONENTIAL LOCAL RULE
16 October 2026. Candidate analytical theorem with an exact scalar certificate;
independent review pending. Actual fixed owners, all-real reports, arbitrary
branches. No historical-priority claim or full funding claim.

For the original exponential local primitive put c=1/(2e), C=1+c. For an owner i,
let M=sum_j min(H_j,L_j), let M_{-i}=sum_j min(H_j-h_ij,L_j-l_ij) be the actual
opponents' coalition value, let mu_i=M-M_{-i}, and let z_i be its local payment.
Let delta=CM-Z>=0, where Z is the sum of all local payments. Then
 z_i >=mu_i/2,                                                  (1)
 z_i >=(1-c)mu_i-(4/5)sqrt(M delta).                             (2)
In particular, at zero local budget slack delta=0, the stronger floor is
z_i>=(1-c)mu_i. Both constants in the zero-slack and half-floor statements are
attained by elementary pure-owner profiles. The4/5 stability coefficient is a
convenient certified upper bound, not an optimality claim.

1. ONE BRANCH: EXACT MARGINAL IDENTITY
Ignore zero-output branches and normalize its shorter total T to1. Let the
owner's short amount be s in[0,1], its capped long amount d in[0,1], and
rho=sum_k min(long_k,1)=1+u, u>=0. Then q=.5exp(-u), and
 z_i=(1-q)s+qd,     delta_branch=c-qu.
The owner's loss of production upon removal is
 mu=max{s,(d-u)_+}.                                      (3)
To check(3), if opponents' actual long total is below1, all those long amounts
are uncapped and their total is rho-d=1+u-d; the remaining short total is1-s.
If opponents' actual long total is at least1, its capped total is also at least1,
so d-u<=0 and only the short side can bind. This includes a capped own long
report and does not replace any actual owner by virtual accounts.

2. HALF-MARGINAL FLOOR
If mu=s, z_i>=(1-q)s>=s/2. Otherwise mu=d-u>0 implies0<=u<d<=1. Then
 qd=.5exp(-u)d>=.5(d-u),
since d(1-exp(-u))<=du<=u. This proves(1) on one branch.

3. STABILITY WHEN THE SHORT COORDINATE SETS THE MARGINAL
Suppose mu=s. If u>=1 then q<=c and z_i>=(1-c)mu directly. For0<=u<=1,
 (1-c)mu-z_i <=(q-c)s <=q-c.
Put t=exp(1-u) in[1,e]. Then
 (q-c)^2/(c-qu)=c (t-1)^2/[1-t+t log t].
The ratio (t-1)^2/[1-t+t log t] increases on[1,e], with continuous value2 at1
and value(e-1)^2 at e. Its derivative has the sign of
 (t+1)log t-2t+2,
whose derivative is log t-1+1/t>=0 for t>=1. Therefore
 (q-c)^2 <=c(e-1)^2 (c-qu) <(16/25)(c-qu).                (4)
The last strict inequality is certified by rational upper bounds on e in the
attached checker. This proves(2) in the short-marginal case.

4. STABILITY WHEN THE LONG COORDINATE SETS THE MARGINAL
Suppose mu=d-u>s, hence0<=u<d<=1. Dropping the nonnegative short payment gives
 (1-c)mu-z_i <=(1-c)(d-u)-qd
             <=F(u):=(1-c)(1-u)-.5exp(-u),              (5)
because the coefficient1-c-q is positive. The function F decreases for u>=0:
F'=-(1-c)+q<0. It is already negative at u=3/4, as verified exactly below.
It remains to prove, for0<=u<=3/4,
                   max(F(u),0)^2 <=(16/25)[c-.5u exp(-u)].          (6)

5. EXACT ALL-REAL CERTIFICATE FOR(6)
certify_marginal_stability.py uses only Fraction arithmetic. The constants obey
 1957/720 < e <11743/4320,
so c is bounded below by clo=2160/11743. Degree13/12 Taylor polynomials of
exp(-x) give lower/upper bounds for every x>=0, by the Lagrange remainder.
The checker first proves c(e-1)^2<16/25 using the monotonicity of
(e-1)^2/(2e) for e>1, and proves F(3/4)<0 using clo and a degree13 lower bound.

Split[0,3/4] into512 closed equal intervals[lo,hi]. Since F decreases and
c-.5u exp(-u) decreases on[0,1], throughout such an interval
 F(u)<=Fhi=(1-clo)(1-lo)-exp_lower(-lo)/2,
 c-.5u exp(-u)>=delta_lo=clo-hi*exp_upper(-hi)/2.
If Fhi<=0, (6) is automatic. Otherwise the script checks delta_lo>0 and
Fhi^2<=(16/25)delta_lo using exact rational comparisons. All512 intervals pass;
474 have positive Fhi. The largest certified squared ratio is about.584413,
strictly below16/25=.64. No floating acceptance, sampling inference, unresolved
interval, or assert-dependent check is used. The exact fraction is recorded in
the accompanying JSON. Normal and -O runs agree.
This completes(6), then(5), and hence(2) on one branch.

6. HOMOGENEITY AND ARBITRARY BRANCHES
Restoring scale T gives z_ij>=(1-c)mu_ij-(4/5)sqrt(T_j delta_j). Sum over branches
and apply Cauchy--Schwarz to obtain(2), since sum_j T_j=M, sum_j delta_j=delta,
and actual coalition marginals add across independent local branches. The same
summation proves(1). Zero-output branches have zero marginal and payment.
All actual owner coordinates are fixed and may be arbitrarily mixed across
branches; no assumptions on owner or branch count enter.

7. FIXED OPPONENTS' COALITION CONSEQUENCE
After any actual own underreport by i, the other owners' local resources are
unchanged. Coalition coverage therefore gives sum_{k!=i}z'_k>=M_{-i}. For H=1,
their total capped opposing account is consequently at least
 min{1,P_{-i}+M_{-i}} >=min{1,P_{-i}+R_i},
where
 R_i=max{0,M-2z_i,M-[z_i+(4/5)sqrt(M delta)]/(1-c)}.       (7)
Here the cap inequality sum min(x_k,1)>=min(sum x_k,1) is elementary. It remains
valid through arbitrary opposing cap changes. This lower bound is a fixed-actual-
coalition constraint, complementary to the outgoing-loss inequalities.
For a full-capped positive owner, after restoring its own outer hardware/grid,
its payment is at most a_i+.5exp(-min{1,P_{-i}+R_i})(1-a_i).
This is an individual bound; simultaneous funding still requires summation.
