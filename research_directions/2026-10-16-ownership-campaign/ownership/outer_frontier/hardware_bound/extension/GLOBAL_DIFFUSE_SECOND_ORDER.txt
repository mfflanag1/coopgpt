THEOREM
Use the original symmetric exponential two-pool primitive at every branch and
at the outer pool, with fixed actual owners and arbitrary nonnegative real
reports. Let E be the actual own-report monotone envelope of the resulting raw
nested rule. Let W(epsilon) be the supremum of sum_i E_i/H over all finite
depth-two instances and physical hardware-bottleneck profiles with H>0 and
max_i z_i/H<=epsilon. Owner and branch counts are unbounded; mixed local,
hardware and grid ownership and arbitrary full grid overflow are allowed.
Then, with c=1/(2e), C=1+c, K=e-1 and L=log(1/epsilon),

 W(epsilon)=C+cK epsilon[L-log L]+O(epsilon).              (SO-global)

Thus the epsilon loglog(1/epsilon) loss is intrinsic to the worst ACTUAL
own-envelope funding at second order, not merely to the parallel witness class.
This is a theorem about this specified envelope, not a lower bound against all
truthful mechanisms. It does not determine the order-epsilon coefficient or
settle the unrestricted C^2 funding frontier.

1. NOTATION AND ACCEPTED DEPENDENCIES
The model is the unit-coefficient depth-two hardware/local/grid model: fixed
actual owner i reports nonnegative hardware h_ij and local resource l_ij on
branches j, and grid p_i. Put H_j=sum_i h_ij, L_j=sum_i l_ij, T_j=min(H_j,L_j),
M=sum_j T_j, H=sum_j H_j and P=sum_i p_i. Physical output is
F=min(H,P+M). Local payments are z_i=sum_j phi_i(h_j,l_j), using the original
symmetric exponential two-pool rule, and raw nested payments are
N_i=phi_i((sum_j h_kj)_k,(p_k+z_k)_k). This theorem does not silently extend
the calculation to arbitrary input coefficients or public output values.

Normalize H=1. Write a_i=H_i, b_i=p_i+z_i, d_i=min(b_i,1), S=sum_i d_i,
r=S-1, q0=exp(-r)/2, and N_i=a_i+q0(d_i-a_i). Physical hardware bottleneck
means P+M>=1, so S>=1. Local total satisfies M<=Z<=CM, and M<=1; consequently
0<=z_i<=1 and d_i>=z_i. Diffuseness means z_i<=epsilon for every actual owner.

Accepted primitive properties: nonnegative coalition coverage; all-real
own-coordinate monotonicity; continuity including zero boundaries; and the
following estimates for an actual owner's arbitrary mixed underreport of the
local-stage physical inputs, meaning BOTH HARDWARE AND LOCAL-RESOURCE
coordinates, simultaneously across arbitrary branches:
 (i) own local payment z_i'<=z_i;
 (ii) total positive loss of OTHER local recipients <=K z_i;
      this is SHARP_OUTGOING_LOSS.txt (accepted root adjudication13), whose
      proof explicitly includes both physical coordinates, either original
      orientation, and simultaneous whole-bundle changes across branches;
 (iii) the sharp long-coordinate incoming bound, from SHARP_INCOMING_ENTROPY_v2:
      K V[1+log(T/(2V))], where V=q min(own long report,T),
      with continuous value zero when V=0. It includes all pool switches.
The general existing outer comparison gives, for positive full gap g=d_i-a_i,
 E_i<=a_i+q0 exp(K z_i)g. For a negative gap g=a_i-d_i it gives
 E_i<=a_i-q0 exp[-Kz_i(1+log(M/z_i))]g.
The latter pair also imply the accepted generic profilewise bound

 sum_i E_i<=1+(exp(-r)/2)[A r+(A-B)],                     (1)
 A=exp(Kepsilon), B=exp[-Kepsilon(1+L)],

using sum_i(a_i-d_i)_+<=1. The underlying positive-gap comparison is valid
through arbitrary outer pool switches and overflow; it is not inferred here
from an uncapped opponent floor.

2. TWO LOCAL DERIVATIVE BOUNDS
These bounds hold at every smooth cell of any two-pool primitive, for increasing
ONE physical coordinate of owner i. All other reports are held fixed. Let T>0
be the current short total, s_k the short accounts, d_k the capped long
accounts, rho=sum d_k/T>=1, and q=exp(1-rho)/2. A coordinate can be hardware
or local; its label does not enter the proof.

If the increasing coordinate is currently short, let m be the number of long
accounts strictly above T. Then q'=q(rho-m)/T>=0 and
 phi_k'=(1-q)1_{k=i}+q 1_{long_k>T}+q'(d_k-s_k).
For k!=i, only the last term can be negative. Hence
 sum_{k!=i}(-phi_k')_+<=q' sum_k s_k<=q rho<=1/2.          (2)
For ALL recipients, positive parts satisfy
 sum_k(phi_k')_+<=1-q+qm+q' sum_k d_k
 =1-q+q[rho^2-m(rho-1)]<=1+q rho^2<=1+2/e.               (3)
The last maximum follows by maximizing rho^2 exp(1-rho)/2 at rho=2.

If the increasing coordinate is currently long and below its cap T, then
q'=-q/T and phi_k'=q 1_{k=i}-(q/T)(d_k-s_k). Therefore
 sum_{k!=i}(-phi_k')_+<=q rho<=1/2,
 sum_k(phi_k')_+<=q+(q/T)sum_k s_k=2q<=1.                 (4)
If the coordinate is above its cap, its local derivative is zero. Ties and
cap boundaries are treated by continuity; along a one-coordinate interval
there are finitely many such pieces. At a zero-short-total endpoint the rule
is continuous, so the integrated inequalities hold by a positive-endpoint
limit. In particular incoming to OTHER local recipients under a hardware
DECREASE is at most one half the physical amount withheld. Own monotonicity
also implies phi_i'>=0 on every smooth cell.

3. EXACT HARDWARE RESTORATION IN A FULL-PROFILE REGION
Claim: if

                     S>=3/2+e epsilon,                 (5)

then every actual owner can attain its envelope supremum while truthfully
reporting ALL its hardware and grid, optimizing only its local-resource bundle.
More precisely, restoring its grid and then its hardware to any feasible own
underreport weakly increases its actual raw nested payment.

Grid restoration changes no local payment and increases only that owner's
outer opposing coordinate. Primitive own monotonicity makes it harmless.
After grid restoration, consider ANY remaining feasible underreport of owner i,
with total reported hardware H'<=1 and changed local payments z_k'. If H'>0,

 sum_k min(p_k+z_k',H')/H'
 >=sum_k min(p_k+z_k',1)
 >=S-[(z_i-z_i')+sum_{k!=i}(z_k-z_k')_+]
 >=S-e z_i>=3/2.                                        (6)

Here the middle inequality uses the 1-Lipschitz cap min(.,1), and the last uses
accepted own monotonicity and the sharp outgoing Kz_i theorem. The inequality
min(b,H')/H'>=min(b,1) is valid for every b>=0 and 0<H'<=1.
Thus outer hardware is short at every such report, and its capped ratio rho_o
is at least 3/2. This remains true throughout each coordinate of hardware
restoration, since every intermediate report is still an underreport of the
same fixed actual owner at the original full profile. The bound is relative
to the full z_i, not an unproved monotonicity of opponents' local payments.

Now increase one hardware coordinate of owner i by a small amount. Write H,a,d
for the CURRENT outer total hardware, own hardware, and own capped opposing
account; rho for the current outer capped ratio, Q=exp(1-rho)/2, and m for the
number of outer opposing accounts at or above H. Local derivatives z_k'
refer to this physical hardware-coordinate increase; current quantities in
this paragraph are not the full-profile quantities of Section 1.
Let W=sum_{k: p_k+z_k<H} z_k'. On a smooth cell,

 Q'=Q(rho-m-W)/H,
 N_i'=1-Q+Q d'+Q'(d-a),                                  (7)

where d' is either z_i'>=0 if strictly uncapped or 1 if capped, using the
capped convention at equality. If an opposing account equals H on a set of
positive measure, its derivative equals H'=1 almost everywhere on that set.
Thus persistent cap ties are included in m, not omitted from both m and W.
By (2)--(4), W>=-1/2 and W<=1+2/e.
If d-a=-g<=0, with 0<=g<=H, then
 N_i'>=1-Q-Q(g/H)(rho-m+1/2)
       >=1-Q(rho+3/2)>0.                                 (8)
Indeed (rho+3/2)exp(1-rho)/2 decreases for rho>=3/2, and
its value there is 3/(2 sqrt(e))<1 because sqrt(e)>3/2.
If d-a=g>=0, then rho-m>=0 and 0<=g<=H imply
 N_i'>=1-Q-Q(g/H)(1+2/e)
       >=1-Q(2+2/e)>0.                                   (9)
For rho>=3/2, Q<=1/(2 sqrt(e))<1/3 and 2+2/e<11/4<3.
Thus (9) is strictly positive. A tie d=a is included by either bound.

Each finite coordinate path is continuous and piecewise differentiable, so
these inequalities integrate. If the starting H'=0, the raw payment is zero;
nonnegativity and a positive-H' limiting argument suffice. Restoring every
hardware coordinate therefore weakly increases actual payment. This proves
the claim without treating a virtual restoration as an actual report: every
restored coordinate belongs to this same fixed actual owner.

4. LOCAL-ONLY INCOMING ENTROPY AND A HARDWARE ACCOUNTING LOSS
Assume (5) and use Section 3 to restrict to actual local-resource underreports.
Classify productive branches using their FULL-profile orientation. At ties,
choose hardware short. At a tie q=1/2, so its hardware-role mass is T_j/2
and the accounting below is unaffected by this convention. Let J_h denote those
branches and put

 X=sum_{j in J_h} T_j, 0<=X<=M<=1.

On every branch outside J_h the owner's local coordinate is originally short.
Its decrease remains on that short side. The incoming bound (2) is at most
half its withheld amount, hence at most its FULL local-short role payment.
On a branch in J_h the local coordinate is originally long. Let V_ij be its
full long-role payment there. The accepted long incoming bound (iii), with
hardware held fixed, includes any subsequent switch to the local-short side.
Let v_i=sum_{j in J_h}V_ij. By log-sum, the total incoming under this owner's
local-resource underreport is bounded by

 I_i<=z_i-v_i+K v_i[1+log(X/(2v_i))].                    (10)

If X=0 or v_i=0 its long term is zero. The other full role payments are
nonnegative, so subtracting v_i from z_i indeed bounds the local-short roles.
In particular, by weakening log(X/(2v_i)) to log(X/v_i), if X>=epsilon then

 I_i<=epsilon+Kepsilon[1+log(X/epsilon)]=:J_epsilon(X),    (11)

because v[1+log(X/v)] is increasing for 0<=v<=X and v_i<=z_i<=epsilon.
If 0<=X<epsilon, the same function is at most X, giving
 I_i<=epsilon+KX<=e epsilon.                              (12)

The incoming entropy mass X incurs a definite hardware allocation charge.
Let U_i be the full sum of this owner's HARDWARE-role local payments, over
all branches. Always U_i<=a_i and z_i>=U_i. On j in J_h, the aggregate
hardware-role payment is (1-q_j)T_j>=T_j/2. Consequently

 G_-:=sum_i(a_i-d_i)_+
 <=sum_i(a_i-z_i)_+<=sum_i(a_i-U_i)
 =1-sum_i U_i<=1-X/2.                                   (13)

No owners or roles are reassigned in this inequality. The ordinary full-profile
accounting identity sum_i a_i=1 is used with nonnegative actual role payments.
It holds with arbitrary mixed grid ownership and arbitrary full grid overflow.

5. SUMMED OUTER FUNDING BOUND
For a negative full outer gap g=a_i-d_i>0, local-only underreports have a_i
unchanged and d_i'<=d_i. Their changed capped total satisfies S'<=S+I_i;
Section 3 ensures hardware stays short. Hence their actual payment is at most
 a_i-q0 exp(-I_i)g,
and their envelope gain over N_i is at most q0 g[1-exp(-I_i)]<=q0 g I_i.
For positive gaps, retain the accepted general comparison from Section 1:
 E_i-N_i<=q0(A-1)(d_i-a_i), A=exp(Kepsilon).
Also G_+-G_-=r and G_-<=1, so G_+<=r+1.

If X>=epsilon, (11)--(13) therefore imply

 sum_i E_i<=1+(exp(-r)/2)
  {A r+(A-1)+epsilon(1-X/2)[1+K+K log(X/epsilon)]}.        (14)

If X<epsilon, replace the final epsilon term by e epsilon, using (12).
All these are whole-envelope bounds: every owner's own deviation may differ,
and each individual estimate uses the same fixed full-profile quantities.

6. UNIFORM OPTIMIZATION OVER PRODUCTION AND THE OUTER RATIO
Define F_epsilon as the maximum of e and
 (1-X/2)[1+K+K log(X/epsilon)] over epsilon<=X<=1.
For small epsilon,

 F_epsilon=K(L-log L)+1+K log 2+o(1).                    (15)

To check this, divide the displayed function by K, put Y=X/2 and
beta=1+1/K. It becomes (1-Y)(L+log Y+log2+beta).
Its second derivative is -1/Y-1/Y^2<0. The unique interior maximizer for
large L, written x=1/Y, satisfies
 x+log x=L+log2+beta+1.
Thus x=L-log L+log2+beta+1+o(1), and the value at that maximizer is
 x-2+1/x=L-log L+log2+1/K+o(1). Multiplication by K gives (15).
The boundary X<epsilon contributes only e and is eventually smaller.

Set t_epsilon=A-1+epsilon F_epsilon. Equation (14), allowing every r>=0,
gives
 sum_i E_i<=1+(1/2) max_{r>=0} exp(-r)(A r+t_epsilon)
 =1+c A exp(t_epsilon/A)                                 (16)
for sufficiently small epsilon, when t_epsilon/A<1. The maximizer is
r=1-t_epsilon/A. Since A=1+Kepsilon+O(epsilon^2), t_epsilon=O(epsilon L),
and epsilon^2 L^2=o(epsilon), (15)--(16) yield

 sum_i E_i<=C+cKepsilon(L-log L)
              +c[2K+1+K log 2]epsilon+o(epsilon).         (17)

This is a uniform upper over all profiles satisfying (5), all fixed actual
owner sets, all finite branch counts, and all real report choices. Uniformity
comes from the explicit scalar bound (16), not from exchanging profilewise
asymptotic error terms with a supremum.

For the excluded profiles S<3/2+e epsilon, we have 0<=r<1/2+e epsilon.
Apply the generic profilewise bound (1). Uniformly over that interval it
converges, as epsilon tends to zero, to
 1+max_{0<=r<=1/2} r exp(-r)/2
 =1+exp(-1/2)/4<C.
The strict inequality follows from sqrt(e)<2. Thus, for all sufficiently small
epsilon, every excluded profile has funding strictly below C and cannot alter
the asymptotic upper (17).

7. MATCHING LOWER AND SCOPE
The accepted class-specific optimum in
DIFFUSE_WITNESS_SECOND_ORDER_OBSTRUCTION.txt is an allowed subclass of the
present definition of W. It has actual envelope funding
 C+cKepsilon(L-log L)-c[2e+K log2]epsilon+o(epsilon).
Its all-small-epsilon real-cap construction uses fixed actual owners in each
finite instance, with unbounded owner and productive-branch counts available.
Combining that lower with (17) proves (SO-global).

The coefficient of epsilon log(1/epsilon) and the coefficient of the negative
epsilon loglog(1/epsilon) term are therefore both exactly c(e-1) for W. The
coefficient of the remaining order-epsilon term is not identified. These are
properties of the actual envelope of this particular nested primitive; they
are not statements about optimal funding among all truthful rules, empirical
energy pooling, or current literature novelty.

REGULARITY CLARIFICATION (no change to the inequalities): in Section 3 one
need not assume finitely many outer cap crossings. On every compact interval
with positive total hardware, the raw payment along a single hardware-coordinate
restoration is absolutely continuous: local coordinate formulas are continuous
with finitely many local cap/orientation cells and bounded one-sided derivatives,
and the outer formula uses finite sums, minima and a smooth exponential divided
by a hardware total bounded away from zero. Equations (7)--(9) therefore apply
almost everywhere and integrate. The zero-total endpoint follows by continuity.

INDEPENDENT-AUDIT TIE CLARIFICATION: Section 3 uses the capped convention
for an opposing account equal to H. This handles persistent equalities while
leaving every inequality unchanged. Local formulas can use the same convention
at cap ties; isolated nondifferentiable boundaries are handled by continuity.
