A GENERAL ORDER-EPSILON LOWER IMPROVEMENT WITH ONE CAPPED GRID OWNER
16 October 2026. Candidate; independent review pending.
This is a new actual-report family inside the same unrestricted W(epsilon),
not a claim that its remaining coefficient is optimal. Existing proofs remain
unchanged. Put c=1/(2e), C=1+c, K=e-1, L=log(1/epsilon).

THEOREM
For all sufficiently small epsilon>0, an explicit finite instance with fixed
actual owners, max local payment epsilon, H=1 and full outer capped sum S=2
has actual own-envelope funding at least

 C+cKepsilon(L-log L)-c[e+1+Klog2]epsilon+o(epsilon).       (1)

Both productive-branch and active-owner counts can tend to infinity. All
reports and deviations are real and feasible; owners never split or merge.

1. CORE ENTROPY BRANCHES AND A TINY RESPONSE BRANCH
Take T=1/L, rho=1+log(T/(2epsilon)), and q=epsilon/T. Then rho>=3 for all
small epsilon. Choose any positive integer k, for example k=floor(L), and k
core productive branches each with hardware T/k. Their local reports are
fixed proportions d_i T/k with d_i in[0,1], sum d_i=rho, and d_1=1. Choose
as many full-cap owners as necessary and one fractional remainder if needed.
These local owners B_i own no grid. Each has full local payment epsilon d_i.

Fragment each core branch's hardware among pure hardware-only actual owners
so each full local payment (1-q)h is at most epsilon; choose finite equal
fragments sufficiently small, and each fragment at most half its branch total.
The core local payment total is
 Z_core=T+epsilon(rho-1).

Add one further productive branch of hardware t=2e epsilon. Partition this
hardware equally among SIX new pure hardware-only owners. Add two new actual
local/grid owners G and A, each holding local report t on this branch, and no
hardware. Its capped ratio is 2, its coefficient is c, and
 z_G=z_A=c t=epsilon.
Each new pure hardware owner's local payment is (1-c)t/6<epsilon, since
(1-c)2e/6=(2e-1)/6<1. The branch local payment total is t+epsilon.

All remaining hardware a=1-T-t is held by core owner B_1 on a branch with zero
local supply. It is positive for all small epsilon. This extra hardware has
zero full local payment, so B_1 still has z_1=epsilon. No other owner has
hardware on this unproductive-local branch. It remains usable with grid at the
outer production stage.

2. FIXED GRID REPORTS AND EXACT FULL CAPPED SUM
Give G grid p_G=1. Give A grid
 p_A=1-Z_core-t>0.
All other actual owners have grid zero. Therefore the full opposing account of
G is 1+epsilon and is capped at 1. Owner A has the UNcapped opposing account

 b_A=1-Z_core-t+epsilon in(0,1).

The full local payments of all other owners sum to Z_core+t-epsilon, and are
individually at most epsilon<1. Consequently
 S=1+b_A+(Z_core+t-epsilon)=2
EXACTLY. Hardware H=1 and total local production M=T+t. Grid total is
P=2-Z_core-t, so P+M=2-epsilon(rho-1)>1 for all small epsilon. Thus F=H=1.
The full raw nested budget is C. Every full local payment is at most epsilon,
and equality holds for B_1,G,A. All owners are fixed and active in this instance.

3. TWO DIFFERENT ACTUAL OWN REPORTS GIVE THE LOWER
First let B_1 delete its local resource on EVERY core branch and retain its
hardware. The tiny response branch and both grid owners' payments are unchanged.
Each core branch remains hardware-short, with coefficient e q. The opposing
capped-total change is exactly
 Delta=epsilon[K rho-(2e-1)]>0.
All changed core local accounts remain below 1, G remains capped, and A's
unchanged account remains uncapped. Owner B_1's own local account falls from
epsilon to zero. Its exact gain is

 gain_1=c[(1-T-t)(1-exp(-Delta))-epsilon].                (2)

Second, in a DIFFERENT own-envelope report, let A delete only its local resource
t on the tiny branch, retaining its grid p_A. The branch becomes balanced, so
G's local payment increases by Kepsilon, but its outer account remains capped
at 1. The six hardware recipients jointly lose Kepsilon in local payments;
A loses its own epsilon. All these latter accounts are uncapped. Thus the
outer capped total is now exactly 2-e epsilon, and A's opposing account is
b_A-epsilon=p_A. Its exact gain is

 gain_A=c[exp(e epsilon)(b_A-epsilon)-b_A].                (3)

Its hardware is zero, so no hardware restoration comparison is involved.
Every other owner may use its truthful report in its envelope. Since the
own-report envelope takes an independent supremum for each fixed actual owner,
these two distinct feasible reports give
 sum_i E_i>=C+gain_1+gain_A.
This does not combine the two reports into one simultaneous deviation.

4. ASYMPTOTICS FOR ALL SMALL EPSILON
With T=1/L,
 rho=L-log L+1-log2,
 Delta=epsilon[K(L-log L-log2)-e].
Using 1-exp(-Delta)=Delta+O(Delta^2), epsilon^2 L^2=o(epsilon), and
T Delta=Kepsilon+o(epsilon), equation (2) becomes
 gain_1=cKepsilon(L-log L)-c[2e+Klog2]epsilon+o(epsilon).
The extra t=2e epsilon hardware reduces this by only t Delta=O(epsilon^2 L),
which is already absorbed by o(epsilon).

Also b_A tends to 1, and b_A-1=O(1/L)+O(epsilon L). Equation (3) gives
 gain_A=c(e b_A-1)epsilon+O(epsilon^2)
        =cKepsilon+o(epsilon).
Adding proves (1), since 2e-K=e+1. Choosing k=floor(L) ensures productive
branch counts tend to infinity; core hardware fragmentation and the cap vector
also give growing active-owner counts. Each instance separately has one fixed
owner set and ordinary feasible whole-bundle reports.

5. WHY TWO FULLY CAPPED GRID ACCOUNTS DO NOT GIVE TWO SUCH GAINS HERE
If both G and A are instead made fully capped at 1 while leaving this entropy
core, the uncapped core productive-hardware payment mass is approximately
T=1/L. Hence S-2 is at least of order 1/L, not O(epsilon L). The full raw
budget 1+(S-1)exp(1-S)/2 loses order 1/L^2 below C, much larger than the desired
epsilon log(1/epsilon) premium. This invalidates the tempting attempt to add
TWO independent cKepsilon gains by making both grid owners capped. The present
construction uses exactly one cap and keeps S=2 by an explicit fixed choice
of the other grid report. It makes no assertion that every two-cap construction
is impossible.
