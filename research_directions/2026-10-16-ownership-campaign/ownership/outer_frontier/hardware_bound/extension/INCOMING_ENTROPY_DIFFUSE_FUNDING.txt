INCOMING RESPONSE AND DIFFUSE LOCAL PAYMENTS: A GENERAL FUNDING REGIME
16 October 2026. Candidate proof, awaiting independent review.
Original symmetric exponential primitive. Fixed actual owners, arbitrary finite
owner and branch counts, arbitrary nonnegative real reports and whole-bundle
underreports. No novelty claim. All grid overflow is allowed.

1. MODEL AND TWO STATEMENTS
On one local branch let T be the shorter pool total, s_i the shorter coordinate,
d_i=min(l_i,T) the capped longer coordinate, rho=sum_i d_i/T, and
 q=exp(1-rho)/2, z_i=(1-q)s_i+q d_i.
The zero-output rule is zero. Ties and caps are continuous. Let c=1/(2e),
C=1+c, K=e-1. The established primitive is coalition covering and own-report
monotone, with each z_i<=T and T<=sum_i z_i<=CT.

With any number of local branches, let T_j be branch output, M=sum_j T_j,
z_i=sum_j z_ij, and Z=sum_i z_i. Write z_k^i for aggregate local payments after
any one actual owner i underreports any of its coordinates. Then

 G_i := sum_{k!=i}(z_k^i-z_k)_+
      <= (e+1) z_i [1+log(M/z_i)].                         (I)

The right side is defined to be zero at z_i=0. This is an incoming POSITIVE
response bound, not a bound on changes of total payment alone.

For the depth-two production model, actual owner i has branch hardware h_ij,
local resources l_ij, and grid p_i. Put H_i=sum_j h_ij, H=sum_i H_i, P=sum_i p_i,
F=min(H,P+M), and b_i=p_i+z_i. The raw nested payment is the same exponential
primitive applied to outer pools (H_i)_i and (b_i)_i. Let E_i be its actual
own-report monotone envelope over coordinatewise underreports of owner's
whole hardware/local/grid bundle. At every physical hardware bottleneck,
H>0 and H<=P+M, define epsilon=max_i z_i/H. Then 0<=epsilon<=1, and

 sum_i E_i/H <= 1 + (A/2) exp(-B/A),                       (D)
 A=exp(K epsilon),
 B=exp(-(e+1)epsilon[1+log(1/epsilon)]).

At epsilon=0 use A=B=1. In particular,

 max_i z_i/H <= 1/20  ==> sum_i E_i/H <= 6931/4995 < 7/5 < C^2.  (F)

This is a profilewise bound on the SAME actual envelope; it is not a new
payment formula, an exact envelope evaluation algorithm, or a claim of C^2
funding on all profiles. The number of positive outer gaps can grow without
limit. In particular the theorem does not rely on the one-positive-gap case.

2. INCOMING RESPONSE WHEN AN ORIGINAL SHORT COORDINATE IS REDUCED
First reduce only the deviator's original SHORT coordinate, leaving its long
coordinate fixed. This pool remains short. On a smooth cap cell with
m=#{k:l_k>T}, differentiation with respect to the increasing short report gives
 q'=q(rho-m)/T,
 z_k'=q'(d_k-s_k)+q*1_{l_k>T}, k!=i.
For a capped other recipient, d_k=T>=s_k, so this derivative is nonnegative:
that recipient cannot gain when the short coordinate is withheld. An uncapped
recipient can gain only if s_k>l_k. Therefore the sum of all positive gains
per unit short withholding is at most
 q' sum_{k!=i}(s_k-l_k)_+
 <=q' sum_{k!=i}s_k<=q(rho-m)<=q rho<=1/2.
The last inequality holds for rho>=1 because rho exp(1-rho) decreases there.
Thus the incoming positive gain over this entire stage is <=s_i/2<=U_i,
where U_i=(1-q_full)s_i is the owner's FULL original-short-role payment.
Also its long-role payment V_i=q min(l_i,T) cannot increase during this stage:
both q(T) and min(l_i,T) are nondecreasing as T increases.

3. ORIGINAL LONG COORDINATE: BOTH SIDES OF THE POOL SWITCH
At the beginning of the long stage, normalize its current short total to 1.
Let d<=1 be the deviator's capped long coordinate, q its current coefficient,
rho the capped ratio, and V=qd its current long-role payment. Reducing an
uncapped long report above 1 has no effect and may be omitted.

Before a switch, reducing the capped own long coordinate by u<=d changes q
to q exp(u), and leaves all other capped long amounts fixed. The sum of
positive OTHER gains is therefore at most
 q(exp(u)-1) sum_{k!=i}d_k
 =q(exp(u)-1)(rho-d)<=K V rho.                            (2)
Here exp(u)-1<=Ku<=Kd for 0<=u<=d<=1.

If a switch occurs, the other ACTUAL long reports sum to v<1, so none is
capped at the current short total. At the switch u=rho-1=d+v-1 lies in [0,d].
The pre-switch positive gain is <=q(exp(u)-1)v<=K V.
After the switch the original long coordinate becomes the short coordinate.
Section 2 applies to any remaining reduction: its incoming gain is at most
half the deviator's short amount at the tie, namely (1-v)/2=(d-u)/2.
At the start of this long stage q=exp(-u)/2, and
 d-u<=d exp(-u), since 1-d exp(-u)>=0 on 0<=u<=d<=1.
Thus this post-switch gain is <=V. Combining both parts gives <=e V. Together
with (2), the whole long-stage incoming gain is always <=e V rho.

Restoring scale T, V/T=qd<=q implies
 rho=1-log(2q)<=1+log(T/(2V)),
so the incoming long-stage gain is at most
 e V [1+log(T/(2V))].                                    (3)
If V=0, this stage has no effect. If T=0, every payment remains zero.

4. COMPOSITION OF ACTUAL COORDINATES AND BRANCHES
At the beginning of the long stage, V<=V_full and T<=T_full. The function
 f(v,t)=v[1+log(t/v)]
is nondecreasing in both arguments on 0<v<=t, and continuous with f(0,t)=0.
Drop the factor 2 inside the denominator in (3). First increase T to T_full
while keeping V fixed; then increase V to z_i_full<=T_full. This order matters
because z_i_full can exceed the intermediate T. Use
V<=V_full<=z_i_full<=T_full. The long-stage incoming gain is <=e f(z_i_full,T_full).
The short-stage gain is <=U_i<=z_i_full<=f(z_i_full,T_full). Positive-part
subadditivity along these two successive reports of the SAME actual owner
therefore gives <=(e+1)f(z_i_full,T_full) on a branch.

Sum over branches and use the log-sum inequality (or concavity of log):
 sum_j z_ij log(T_j/z_ij)<=z_i log(M/z_i).
Zero-payment terms are interpreted by their continuous limit. A full branch
with T_j=0 stays zero under withholding. If z_i=0, on every productive branch
both of its coordinates are zero (the two coefficients are strictly positive),
so it has no influence there. This proves (I), including all zero cases.
There are finitely many cap/pool boundaries on each coordinate path; continuity
extends the integrated derivative arguments to all real endpoints and ties.
No owner cloning, role reassignment, or virtual strategic identities occur.

5. TWO OUTER RESPONSE BOUNDS THAT ALLOW ARBITRARY OVERFLOW
Normalize H=1. Then M<=1, z_i<=M, and physical hardware bottleneck implies
sum_i b_i>=P+M>=1. Let a_i=H_i, d_i=min(b_i,1), S=sum_i d_i>=1, and
q0=exp(1-S)/2. Full raw payments are a_i+q0(d_i-a_i).

Fix any actual underreport of owner i. Compute its local response first.
Restore its outer hardware and grid to the full values, and increase its own
opposing account to its full value b_i. These are valid PAYMENT COMPARISONS
by the outer primitive's own monotonicity, not assertions about an actual
feasible underreport. The other hardware accounts and their total H=1 are
now fixed; other opposing accounts are p_k+z_k^i. The restored own cap is d_i.

Let O_i=sum_{k!=i}(z_k-z_k^i)_+. The established sharp outgoing bound gives
 O_i<=K z_i<=K epsilon.                                  (4)
The min(.,1) map is 1-Lipschitz. Therefore, for the restored capped opposing
total S_i, independently of all full or changed overflow,
 S-O_i<=S_i<=S+G_i.                                      (5)
By (I), M<=1, and monotonicity of v[1+log(1/v)] on [0,1],
 G_i<=(e+1)epsilon[1+log(1/epsilon)]=J.                   (6)

Define the scalar comparison coefficient
 k(t)=1-exp(1-1/t)/2 for 0<t<=1,
 k(t)=exp(1-t)/2 for t>=1, and k(0)=1.
It is continuous and decreasing. Also
 k(t)<=exp(1-t)/2 for every t>=0.                         (7)
For 0<t<=1, (7) is equivalent to exp(1-t)+exp(1-1/t)>=2.
The left side decreases to 2 as t increases to 1: the ratio of the positive
to negative terms in its derivative is exp(t-1/t-2log t)<=1, because
t-1/t-2log t increases to zero. The t=0 limit also satisfies (7).

For a POSITIVE full gap g_i=d_i-a_i>0, the restored payment is <=a_i+k(S_i)g_i.
Indeed this is equality when the outer hardware pool is short. If the opposing
pool is short, its raw total is S_i<1 (there are then no opposing caps);
a_i<d_i<=S_i so the owner's hardware cap is inactive. The capped hardware
sum is <=1, so its coefficient on its opposing account is <=k(S_i).
Combining (4),(5),(7) yields, for every actual underreport and hence its envelope,
 E_i<=a_i+q0 A g_i, A=exp(K epsilon).                     (8)
This remains valid when (S-O_i)<1 or even negative; no lower bound on a capped
total is improperly substituted for an uncapped total.

For a NEGATIVE full gap g_i=a_i-d_i>=0, if the restored opposing raw total is
>=1, its coefficient q_i is >=q0 exp(-G_i)>=q0 B, B=exp(-J), and its payment
is <=a_i-q0 B g_i. If that raw total is <1, its payment is
(1-q_i)d_i+q_i min(a_i,S_i)<=(a_i+d_i)/2, since q_i<=1/2; this is also
<=a_i-q0 B g_i because q0 B<=1/2. Thus
 E_i<=a_i-q0 B g_i.                                      (9)
The zero-gap boundary a_i=d_i=1, which can hide own full overflow, needs only
the primitive ceiling E_i<=1=a_i. All other negative or zero-gap owners have
b_i=d_i, so the preceding formula applies directly.

6. SUMMATION WITH NO BOUND ON POSITIVE-GAP OWNER COUNT
Let G_+ and G_- sum the positive and negative full gaps. Then
G_+-G_-=S-1=r>=0 and G_-<=sum_i a_i=1. By (8),(9),
 sum_i E_i<=1+(exp(-r)/2)[A r+(A-B)G_-]
            <=1+(exp(-r)/2)[A r+A-B].                   (10)
A>=1>=B, and the last added term has its global maximum on r>=0 at r=B/A.
This maximum is (A/2)exp(-B/A), proving (D). When epsilon=0, no local report
has an effect and the limit A=B=1 is valid directly as well.

7. EXACT CONVENIENT THRESHOLD
Assume epsilon<=1/20. Elementary rational bounds e<11/4 and log20<3 imply
 J<(15/4)(1/20)(1+3)=3/4, K epsilon<7/80.
Consequently
 A<=exp(7/80)<=1/(1-7/80)=80/73<11/10,
 B>=exp(-3/4)>=1-3/4+(3/4)^2/2-(3/4)^3/6=59/128.
The exponential estimates follow from its positive geometric majorant and
the alternating Taylor bound. The function A^2/(A+B) has positive A derivative
A(A+2B)/(A+B)^2 and negative B derivative -A^2/(A+B)^2. Since exp(-x)<=1/(1+x),
 1+(A/2)exp(-B/A)<=1+A^2/[2(A+B)]
                 <=1+(11/10)^2/[2(11/10+59/128)]
                  =6931/4995<7/5.
Finally e<68/25 gives C>161/136, whose square exceeds 7/5.
The accompanying exact Fraction checker verifies the rational exponential
bounds and all stated rational comparisons. This completes (F).

DEPENDENCIES AND STATUS
Only established primitive own monotonicity/coverage/payment ceilings and the
sharp linear outgoing theorem SHARP_OUTGOING_LOSS.txt are imported. The latter
is a separately accepted dependency (root adjudication10); the accepted sharp
nonlinear outgoing theorem also implies it because the owner loss D_i<=z_i. The incoming
lemma, outer-switch comparison, summation, and threshold are proved here.
The theorem does not establish unrestricted C^2 funding. It identifies a new
regime controlled by concentration of local PAYMENTS, not simply owner counts
or physical report fragmentation. A high-value owner can violate its hypothesis
even when arbitrarily many other owners are present.
