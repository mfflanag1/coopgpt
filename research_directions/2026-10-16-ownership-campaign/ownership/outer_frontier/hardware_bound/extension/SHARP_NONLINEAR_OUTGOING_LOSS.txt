SHARP NONLINEAR OUTGOING LOSS FOR FIXED ACTUAL OWNERS
16 October2026. Candidate theorem; independent analytical audit required.
Original exponential primitive, all nonnegative real reports, arbitrary mixed
owners, arbitrary owner/branch counts. No historical novelty claim.

THEOREM
For any actual owner's coordinatewise whole-bundle underreport, write z_i for
its full local payment, z_i^i for its payment at the underreport, and D_i=z_i-z_i^i.
Then
           sum_{k!=i}(z_k-z_k^i)_+ <=(e-1)sqrt(z_i D_i). (SN)
The constant e-1 is sharp. A stronger accounting form is
 [sum_{k!=i}(z_k-z_k^i)_+]^2
              <=[(9/4)U_i+(e-1)^2 V_i]D_i,            (SN_role)
where U_i,V_i are full short-role and long-role local payments, aggregated
branchwise, and U_i+V_i=z_i. Actual owners are never split, merged, or changed.

DEPENDENCIES
The established exponential primitive is continuous and own-coordinate
monotone. Its formula at shorter total T is
 q=exp(1-rho)/2, rho=sum min(long_k,T)/T,
 z_k=(1-q)short_k+q min(long_k,T).
We use the separately proved short-stage lemma
 SHORT_STAGE_NONLINEAR_THREE_HALVES.txt:
 reducing the original-short coordinate alone gives outgoing positive loss
 H_s^2<=(9/4)U D_s,
 where U is its initial short-role payment and D_s its actual own-payment loss.
That note supplies the full cap-cell reduction and exact all-real Taylor
certificate. The argument below proves a matching e-1 long-stage bound through
an arbitrary pool switch. Combining stages then proves(SN).

1. LONG REDUCTION WITHOUT A POOL SWITCH
Normalize this stage's full shorter total to1. Let d<=1 be the own capped long
amount, s its own short amount, and q0 the full coefficient. Ignore changes
while the uncapped long report is above1, since they affect no payment.
After reducing the capped long amount by u<=d without switching the short pool,
q becomes q0 exp(u). Others' total positive payment loss H_l is at most
q0(exp(u)-1). The actual own-payment loss obeys
 D_l=q0[exp(u)u-(exp(u)-1)(d-s)]
     >=q0[exp(u)u-(exp(u)-1)d].                         (1)
Its initial long-role payment is V=q0d. For fixed u the product
 d[exp(u)u-(exp(u)-1)d]
is concave in d on[u,1]. At d=u, its value is u^2 and
(exp(u)-1)/u<=e-1. At d=1 its value is psi(u)=1-(1-u)exp(u), and
(exp(u)-1)^2/psi(u) increases to(e-1)^2 on(0,1]. Indeed its derivative has
the sign of2+u-(2-u)exp(u), a function starting at0 with derivative psi(u)>=0.
Thus both denominator endpoints imply
                         H_l^2<=(e-1)^2 V D_l.         (2)
Zero reductions follow by continuity. This covers mixed owners since their
nonzero s only increases the actual loss in(1).

2. EXACT POSITIVE-LOSS COMPARISON AFTER A POOL SWITCH
Suppose the final actual long total t is below1. Let v<1 be the total of OTHER
original-long reports, so the owner's remaining long amount is t-v>=0.
The full own capped long amount d lies in[1-v,1], and q0=exp(1-d-v)/2.
The original-short vector has fixed total1. Its cap sum at t is D_all, and
the new coefficient is q_b=exp(1-D_all/t)/2. Put
 q_min(t)=exp(1-1/t)/2, L=1-q_min(t),                    (3)
with q_min(0)=0 by continuity. Since D_all<=1, q_b>=q_min.

For a different recipient k, denote its original short/long coordinates by
s_k,l_k. We have l_k<=v<=t, so its full and new payments are
 (1-q0)s_k+q0l_k,
 (1-q_b)l_k+q_b min(s_k,t).
If s_k<=l_k, then s_k<=t and its full-minus-new difference is
(1-q0-q_b)(s_k-l_k)<=0. Thus the set Q of recipients with positive loss is
contained in {k!=i:s_k>l_k}. Put A=sum_{k in Q}s_k and D_Q=sum_{k in Q}min(s_k,t).
Dropping a nonpositive term in their differences yields
                   H_l<=(1-q0)A-q_b D_Q.               (4)
We claim q_b D_Q>=q_min A. If A<=t, all selected coordinates are uncapped,
D_Q=A, and q_b>=q_min suffices. If A>t, then t<=D_Q<=A, while
D_all<=D_Q+(1-A). Therefore
 q_b D_Q>=(D_Q/2)exp[1-(D_Q+1-A)/t]
          >=(A/2)exp(1-1/t)=q_min A.
The second inequality holds because x exp(-x/t) decreases on x>=t. Thus(4)
gives the uniform endpoint comparison
                    H_l<=(L-q0)A<=L-q0.               (5)
Here0<=A<=1 and L-q0>=0. This accounts for actual recipient caps and mixed
reports; no imaginary recipient population is assumed.

The deviator's full own payment is (1-q0)s+q0d. Its new payment is at most
(1-q_b)(t-v)+q_b s. Since q0,q_b<=1/2, its own loss satisfies
 D_l>=q0d-(1-q_b)(t-v)+s(1-q0-q_b)
     >=q0d-L(t-v).                                    (6)
This is nonnegative, as also follows from the scalar analysis below.
Equations(5)--(6) reduce the long-stage result to an explicit two-variable
inequality without discarding the recipient's actual own-payment loss.

3. EXACT SCALAR ENDPOINT REDUCTION
Put u=d+v-1>=0, so q0=exp(-u)/2. Put epsilon=1+u-t, so t-v=d-epsilon.
The constraints imply0<=u<=t<=1 and epsilon<=d<=1. Write
 R=L/q0=(2-exp(1-1/t))exp(u)>=1.
Then (6) is q0[L epsilon/q0-(R-1)d], and the product V D_lower equals
 q0 d[L epsilon-(L-q0)d].                              (7)
For fixed u,t, expression(7) is concave in d. Its minimum on[epsilon,1]
is therefore at one of those endpoints. We show(5)^2 is at most(e-1)^2 times
each endpoint value.

Define w=t-u in[0,t] and
 C_t=(2-exp(1-1/t))exp(t), so R=C_t exp(-w).
The reciprocal-exponential inequality
 exp(1-t)+exp(1-1/t)>=2, 0<t<=1,
gives C_t<=e. For completeness, the derivative of the left side is nonpositive:
the ratio of its positive and negative derivative terms is
exp(t-1/t-2log t)<=1, since t-1/t-2log t increases to0 on(0,1].

ENDPOINT d=epsilon. Then D_lower=q0 epsilon and V=q0 epsilon. As epsilon=1-w,
 R<=e exp(-w)=exp(epsilon),
 so (L-q0)/(q0 epsilon)=(R-1)/epsilon<=e-1
by convexity of exp on[0,1]. This proves the desired endpoint estimate.

ENDPOINT d=1. Here D_lower=q0[1-Rw], and the desired squared ratio is
                         G(w)=(R-1)^2/(1-Rw).          (8)
Fix t and let w vary on[0,t]. First Rw<=1: the function w exp(-w) increases
for0<=w<=1, so
 Rw<=C_t t exp(-t)=t(2-exp(1-1/t))<=1.
The last inequality is exp(-x)>=1-x with x=1/t-1. The denominator is strict
apart from the continuous zero-change corner t=w=1.
Differentiation of(8) shows its derivative has the sign of
                 -[3-R-(R+1)w],                       (9)
since R>=1. Let J(w)=3-R-(R+1)w. Then J'(w)=Rw-1<=0, so J(w)>=J(t).
At the endpoint
 J(t)=1-3t+(1+t)exp(1-1/t)>=0.
To check the last inequality, set x=1/t-1>=0 and multiply by1/t. It becomes
                   (x+2)exp(-x)>=2-x.
The difference is0 at x=0 and has derivative1-(x+1)exp(-x)>=0.
Consequently(9) is nonpositive throughout the interval: G decreases in w.
Its maximum at w=0 is (C_t-1)^2<=(e-1)^2. The t=w=1 corner follows by
continuity of this uniform upper bound. The t=0 endpoint is its continuous
limit; it also gives ratio1 directly.

Both d endpoints now satisfy
 (L-q0)^2<=(e-1)^2 V D_lower.
Concavity in d and D_l>=D_lower give
                         H_l^2<=(e-1)^2 V D_l          (10)
through every pool switch. This proves the long-stage estimate on the full
continuous domain, including arbitrary mixed recipients and own reports.

4. FIXED-OWNER STAGE COMPOSITION AND BRANCH AGGREGATION
Return to a complete actual own underreport. Reduce its original short input
first, then its original long input. Let full roles be U,V and actual own losses
in the two stages be D_s,D_l. During the short stage the long-role payment
q min(long_i,T) is nonincreasing, so the second stage begins with V_*<=V.
Positive losses are subadditive across stages. The short lemma and(2),(10) give
 H_total<=(3/2)sqrt(U D_s)+(e-1)sqrt(V D_l)
        <=sqrt[((9/4)U+(e-1)^2 V)(D_s+D_l)]
        <=(e-1)sqrt[(U+V)(D_s+D_l)],                   (11)
since3/2<e-1. Each stage changes a coordinate of the SAME actual owner.
Every own loss is nonnegative and D_s+D_l equals its full actual payment loss.
Homogeneity restores the initial shorter total omitted by normalization.

For simultaneous changes on arbitrarily many branches, take recipientwise
positive parts, sum branch bounds, and apply Cauchy--Schwarz once more. This
proves(SN_role) and(SN). Zero-output branches stay zero under underreporting;
cap/pool boundaries are continuous and coordinate paths have finitely many
such boundaries. No bound on counts or restriction to a finite report grid
is used.

5. SHARPNESS AND CAP CONSEQUENCE
One hardware owner reports1 and two pure local-resource owners report1 each.
At the full profile q=c=1/(2e) and payments are(1-c,c,c). One resource owner
withholds its whole resource. Its own loss is D_i=z_i=c; the hardware owner's
positive loss is1/2-c=(e-1)c, and the other resource owner gains. Therefore(SN)
is equality: e-1 is the exact universal constant for this primitive.

At fixed full hardware H and restored grid define
 w_i=(p_i+z_i-H)_+,
 Dcap_i=min(p_i+z_i,H)-min(p_i+z_i^i,H).
Since D_i<=Dcap_i+w_i, cap decreases obey
 S-S^i <=Dcap_i+(e-1)sqrt[z_i(Dcap_i+w_i)].             (12)
One may additionally take the minimum with(e-1)z_i and with Z-z_i for the
OTHER-account loss. Formula(12) is a fixed-threshold account bound; outer
pool-switch effects and summation still require a separate funding proof.
The result does not itself assert general C^2 funding of the mixed-grid
nested envelope or a lower bound against other mechanisms.
