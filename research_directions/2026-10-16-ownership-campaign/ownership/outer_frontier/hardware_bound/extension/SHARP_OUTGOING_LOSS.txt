SHARP OUTGOING POSITIVE-LOSS BOUND FOR AN ACTUAL OWNER
16 October 2026. Candidate theorem; independent audit pending.
Original exponential primitive, all nonnegative real mixed reports, fixed actual
owners. Counts of owners/branches are unrestricted. No novelty claim.

THEOREM
Let z_k be the full local payments, aggregated over any number of branches.
Let z_k^i be the corresponding payments after ANY actual coordinatewise
underreport by one fixed owner i, including simultaneous changes on all branches.
Then
             sum_{k!=i}(z_k-z_k^i)_+ <=(e-1) z_i.       (OL)
The constant e-1 is sharp already on one branch with three pure owners.
This is a bound in the owner's FULL own local payment, not its payment loss.

PRIMITIVE
For shorter reports s and longer reports l, put T=sum s,
 d_k=min(l_k,T), rho=sum d_k/T, q=exp(1-rho)/2,
 z_k=(1-q)s_k+q d_k.
At T=0 all payments vanish. Ties agree, and all payment formulas are continuous.
We prove(OL) on one branch, then sum by positive-part subadditivity.

SHORT-COORDINATE REDUCTION
First decrease only i's ORIGINAL shorter coordinate to its desired report.
It remains in the shorter pool. On a smooth cap cell, put m=#{k:l_k>T}.
Differentiating as that short coordinate INCREASES gives
 q'=q(rho-m)/T>=0,
 z_k'=q'(d_k-s_k)+q 1_{l_k>T}, k!=i.
The total positive derivative of others' payments is at most
 q' sum_{k!=i}d_k+q m
 <=q rho(rho-m)+q m
 =q[rho^2-m(rho-1)]<=q rho^2<=2/e.                     (1)
The last maximum occurs at rho=2 for rho>=1. Integrating the positive
cross derivatives bounds the sum of others' positive losses from any shorter
coordinate reduction by (2/e) s_i, where s_i is its FULL amount. Its full
short-role payment is U_i=(1-q0)s_i>=s_i/2, so this stage costs at most
                         (4/e) U_i.                   (2)
The owner's long-role component V_i=q min(l_i,T) is nonincreasing during
this stage: T decreases, both q(T) and min(l_i,T) are nondecreasing in T.
Thus its value V_i^* at the start of the long stage satisfies V_i^*<=V_i.
Zero totals follow by continuity and leave no subsequent long-stage payments.

LONG-COORDINATE REDUCTION
Now keep the remaining short total fixed and normalize it to1. Let d in[0,1]
be the owner's capped original-long amount; write q0 for the coefficient at
this stage's start. Its long-role payment is q0*d. Any reduction while its
uncapped report remains above1 has no effect, and can be omitted.

If no pool switch occurs, the short total stays1 and only the owner's capped
long coordinate changes. Thus q increases from q0 to at most q0 exp(d).
Each other recipient's payment changes by (q-q0)(d_k-s_k). The total positive
loss is at most q0(exp(d)-1) sum_{k!=i}s_k, hence at most
 q0(exp(d)-1)<=(e-1)q0*d.                               (3)
This also bounds any truncated long-stage reduction before a possible switch.

If a switch can occur, the other ACTUAL long reports total v<1. In particular
none of them is capped at the initial short total. We have
              1-v<=d<=1, q0=exp(1-d-v)/2.
The pre-switch loss is at most1/2-q0, since q reaches1/2 at the tie and the
sum of other short reports is at most1.

After the switch let t be the new short total. It decreases from1 toward v,
while the now-long pool (the original short pool) has fixed total1. On each
smooth cell its capped ratio r obeys1<=r<=1/t. The same derivative calculation
as(1), now for this new short coordinate, bounds the total positive derivative
of OTHER payments by q(r)r^2. Consequently the additional positive loss is
at most the integral of max_{1<=r<=1/t}q(r)r^2 over t from v to1. Since q(r)r^2
increases through r=2 and decreases thereafter, this integral is exactly
 J(v)=
   (1/2)[1-exp(1-1/v)],                         1/2<=v<=1,
   1/2+1/(2e)-(2/e)v,                          0<=v<=1/2. (4)
At v=0 the second formula is the finite limiting integral. Thus the total
positive loss through the long stage is at most
                         1/2-q0+J(v).                  (5)
We prove that(5)<=(e-1)q0*d, which completes the stage bound even through a
pool switch. Dividing by q0, the desired inequality becomes
 F_v(d)=2[1/2+J(v)]exp(d+v-1)-1-(e-1)d<=0.             (6)
For fixed v this function is convex in d, so it suffices to check its two
endpoints d=1-v and d=1.

At d=1-v, q0=1/2, and the assertion is 2J(v)<=(e-1)(1-v).
The defining integrand of J is at most2/e, so 2J(v)<=(4/e)(1-v).
Moreover4/e<e-1: e>8/3 implies e^2-e-4>0, since the polynomial increases
for e>=8/3 and equals4/9 at8/3.

At d=1, the assertion reduces to
                    1/2+J(v)<=exp(1-v)/2.              (7)
For v>=1/2, substitute(4). Inequality(7) is equivalent to
                    exp(1-v)+exp(1-1/v)>=2.            (8)
For0<v<=1, the derivative of the left side is nonpositive: the ratio of its
positive to negative derivative terms is
 exp(v-1/v-2log v)<=1,
because v-1/v-2log v is increasing to0 (its derivative is(v-1)^2/v^2>=0).
Thus the left side decreases to2 at v=1, proving(8).

For0<=v<=1/2, substitute the second formula in(4). After multiplication by2e,
(7) becomes
                 f(v)=exp(2-v)-2e-1+4v>=0.
It is decreasing on this interval because exp(3/2)>4. Its minimum at1/2 is
 exp(3/2)-2e+1=(sqrt(e)-1)(e-sqrt(e)-1)>0.
To verify the strict signs with elementary rational bounds: e>8/3 implies
exp(3/2)>4 and sqrt(e)>13/8; the polynomial t^2-t-1 is positive at13/8 and
increasing there. This proves(7), then(6), and the complete long-stage bound
                         (e-1)V_i^*.                   (9)
The value v=1 belongs to the non-switch/tie limit; all formulas agree.

COMBINING THE TWO ACTUAL COORDINATES
For every other recipient, its positive loss over the two-stage path is at
most the sum of its positive losses over the individual stages. Equations(2)
and(9), V_i^*<=V_i, and4/e<e-1 therefore give
 sum_{k!=i}(z_k-z_k^i)_+
 <=(4/e)U_i+(e-1)V_i^*
 <=(e-1)(U_i+V_i)=(e-1)z_i.
These are two successive reports of the same fixed actual owner; the role
decomposition is numerical accounting, not a split into strategic identities.
Any partial long reduction has loss bounded by the same integrated positive
derivative argument; we do not assume the owner's final coordinate is zero.
There are finitely many cap/pool boundaries along either coordinate path,
and all payments are continuous, so the integrations apply to arbitrary real
endpoints including ties and zeros.

MULTIPLE BRANCHES
For one owner's simultaneous whole-bundle underreport, apply the one-branch
result separately on every branch. The positive loss of each aggregate recipient
payment is at most the sum of its branchwise positive losses. Summing first over
recipients and then branches yields(OL) with the full aggregate z_i. No bound
on the number of branches or owners is used.

SHARPNESS AND LIMIT
On one branch let three fixed owners report (hardware,local resource) as
 (1,0), (0,1), (0,1). The short output is1, rho=2, and q=c=1/(2e).
Payments are (1-c,c,c). The second owner withholds its resource to0; payments
become (1/2,0,1/2). The total OTHER positive loss is1/2-c=(e-1)c, exactly
(e-1) times the deviator's full own payment. The other resource owner gains,
so this is a positive-loss statement, not merely a difference of total payments.

The theorem supplies a sharp outgoing influence charge for arbitrary actual
owners. It does not itself establish C^2 funding of the nested envelope when
grid and local/hardware ownership are mixed; that outer compensation step
remains separate.
