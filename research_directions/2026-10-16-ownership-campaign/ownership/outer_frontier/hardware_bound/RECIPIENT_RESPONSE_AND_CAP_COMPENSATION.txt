RECIPIENT RESPONSE AND GRID-FUNDED CAP COMPENSATION
16 October 2026. Candidate general structural lemma. Independent audit pending.
Uses the original exponential two-input primitive with fixed actual owners.
No historical-priority claim; this is not yet a C^2 funding theorem.

THEOREM 1: ONE RECIPIENT, MANY INDEPENDENT OWNER DEVIATIONS
On one local branch let H be total hardware, and let z_k be the full payment
to a fixed recipient k. For EACH actual owner i choose any own-bundle underreport,
independently of the choices for the other owners, and let z_k^i be k's payment
at that resulting profile. Then
 sum_i (z_k^i-z_k)_+ <= H-z_k.                            (R)
The i=k summand is zero by own-report monotonicity. All capacities are arbitrary
nonnegative reals and the number of owners is unrestricted.

For multiple branches, let z_k be the aggregate local payment and H aggregate
hardware. Positive-part subadditivity gives the same inequality(R), even when
each owner changes its whole multi-branch bundle simultaneously.

PROOF: RECIPIENT CROSS-MONOTONICITY
Hold recipient k's own two reports fixed. It benefits from withholding by
others only in the input of which k itself holds MORE. It cannot benefit from
others withholding the input of which k holds LESS. To verify, write its lesser
own amount as a and greater one as b. When the a-pool is short with total T,
its payment is a+q[min(b,T)-a]. Increasing others' a-pool reports increases T,
the cap min(b,T), and q; increasing others' b-pool reports decreases q. When
the b-pool is short, its payment is b-q(b-a), because its opposite account a
is uncapped. Increasing others' b-pool reports increases q; increasing others'
a-pool reports decreases q. All boundary formulas agree. Thus an arbitrary
mixed-owner deviation can only be more favorable to k after restoring the
adverse coordinate and fully deleting the beneficial coordinate. No actual
owner is split: this only compares two permitted reports by the same owner.
If a=b, k's payment is constant and(R) is immediate.

CASE A: RECIPIENT'S HARDWARE IS ITS GREATER OWN INPUT
Let its hardware be b and own local resource be a<=b. The beneficial coordinates
are other owners' hardware reports. The magnitude of the derivative of k's
payment with respect to any such coordinate is at most1/2. If that input pool
is long, the derivative magnitude is q[min(b,T)-a]/T<=q<=1/2 when uncapped,
and zero when capped. If that input pool is short, its magnitude is
 (b-a)q'(T), where q'(T)=q(r-m)/T<=qr/T<=1/(2T),
and T>=b. Hence it is again at most1/2. Pool and cap boundaries are continuous.
Sum the individual deletion bounds over owners other than k:
 sum_i(z_k^i-z_k)_+ <=(H-b)/2 <=H-z_k,
because z_k<=b. This includes arbitrary mixed ownership by the preceding
cross-monotonicity reduction.

CASE B: RECIPIENT'S HARDWARE IS ITS LESSER OWN INPUT
If H=0 all local payments are zero. Otherwise normalize H=1, put own hardware
a, own local resource b>a, and retain b for its UNTRUNCATED own amount.

B1. If b>=1, the local-resource pool stays long after any opponent deletion.
Its own capped amount is1. Let d_i=min(l_i,1) for i!=k, r=sum_i d_i, and
q=(1/2)exp(-r). Then z_k=a+q(1-a), and deleting i's beneficial coordinate
increases k's payment by q(1-a)(exp(d_i)-1). Since0<=d_i<=1,
 exp(d_i)-1 <=(e-1)d_i.
The aggregate increase is at most(1-a)q r(e-1). Since r exp(-r)<=1/e,
 q r(e-1)<1/2<=1-q.
Thus it is at most(1-a)(1-q)=1-z_k.

B2. Let0<b<1, put u=b-a, and let B=sum_i l_i be the full local-resource total.
If B<=1, every beneficial deletion keeps that pool short with total T>=b.
The recipient payment is b-q(T)u, with derivative magnitude
 u q'(T)<=u/(2T)<=u/(2b).
The sum of the deleted coordinates is B-b<=1-b. Hence aggregate gain is at
most u(1-b)/(2b)<=(1-b)/2<=1-z_k, using z_k<=b.

B3. Still0<b<1, but B>1. Split each beneficial deletion, if necessary, at the
pool tie B=1. Let
 D=b+sum_{i!=k}min(l_i,1), r=D-1>0, q=(1/2)exp(-r).
At the full profile z_k=a+qu. Before the tie, recipient k's own cap is the
fixed b. Let Delta_i be the reduction of the capped long sum during i's
pre-tie segment. Then
 0<=Delta_i<=min(1,r),  sum_i Delta_i<=D-b=1+r-b.
The first bound holds because the capped sum stays at least1 before the tie,
and only i's capped coordinate changes. The second sums its original cap.
The corresponding aggregate payment increase is
 q u sum_i(exp(Delta_i)-1).
If0<r<=1, convex secants give
 q sum_i(exp(Delta_i)-1)
 <=[(1-exp(-r))/(2r)](1+r-b)<=(2-b)/2.
If r>=1, the same argument with cap1 gives
 q sum_i(exp(Delta_i)-1)
 <=(1/2)exp(-r)(e-1)(1+r-b)<=(2-b)/2.
For the last step, (1+r-b)exp(-r) decreases for r>=1 because b<=1;
evaluating at r=1 even gives the extra factor1-e^(-1)<1.
Thus total pre-tie gain is at most u(2-b)/2.

For the post-tie segments, each ending total is at least b, so the derivative
bound from B2 is u/(2b). Their lengths are
 ell_i=(l_i-(B-1))_+.
If any length is positive, summing over its nonempty index set gives
 sum_i ell_i<= (B-b)-(B-1)=1-b.
Thus total post-tie gain is at most u(1-b)/(2b). Combining both stages,
 sum_i(z_k^i-z_k)_+ <=u[(2-b)/2+(1-b)/(2b)].              (2)
Meanwhile1-z_k=1-b+(1-q)u>=1-b+u/2. The latter exceeds the right side of(2)
by at least
 (1-b)[1-u(b+1)/(2b)] >=(1-b)^2/2>=0,
because u<=b. This proves(R) in the final case. Scale back by H.

THEOREM 2: AGGREGATE OUTER OVERFLOW GROWTH
Return to arbitrary branches and full aggregate hardware H. Restore each
selected owner's outer hardware and grid coordinates to full after computing
its local counterfactual accounts. Define
 b_k=p_k+z_k, w_k=(b_k-H)_+, W=sum_k w_k,
 b_k^i=p_k+z_k^i, w_k^i=(b_k^i-H)_+, W^i=sum_k w_k^i.
Then
 sum_i sum_k (w_k^i-w_k)_+ <=P-W.                        (3)
Consequently sum_i(W^i-W)_+<=P-W. In fact the i=k terms in the double sum
vanish, because the local primitive is monotone in its own recipient's bundle.

PROOF. Fix k and put U=H-z_k>=0 and v_i=(z_k^i-z_k)_+. By(R), sum_i v_i<=U.
If p_k<=U then the full overflow is zero and
 sum_i(w_k^i-w_k)_+ <=sum_i[v_i-(U-p_k)]_+
                       <=[sum_i v_i-(U-p_k)]_+<=p_k.
If p_k>=U, each overflow increase is at most v_i, so their sum is at most U.
In both cases the bound is min(p_k,U)=p_k-w_k. Sum over k to prove(3).
This is the desired grid charge without an owner-count factor.

THEOREM 3: CAPPED RETENTION WITH AN EXPLICIT GRID CHARGE
Let S=sum_k min(b_k,H)=B-W and S^i=B^i-W^i. Define the full and counterfactual
own capped accounts d_i=min(b_i,H), d_i^i=min(b_i^i,H). Let Delta_- denote
the sum of local funding slacks on branches whose full local ratio is below2.
Use the universal signed-total retention constant kappa; kappa=1 is already
proved in the consolidated theorem, and the strengthened proof permits20/21.
Then
 sum_i[(S-S^i)/3-(b_i-b_i^i)]_+
 <=kappa Delta_-+(P-W)/3,                               (4)
and
 sum_i[(S-S^i)/3-(d_i-d_i^i)]_+
 <=kappa Delta_-+(P-W)/3+(2/3)L
 <=kappa Delta_-+(P+W)/3,                               (5)
where L=sum_i min(w_i,z_i)<=W. The sharper first bound in(5) remains useful
when grid amounts are much larger than hardware.

PROOF. With grid restored, B-B^i=Z-Z^i and b_i-b_i^i=z_i-z_i^i>=0.
Equation(4) follows from S-S^i=(B-B^i)+(W^i-W), the ordinary retention bound,
positive-part subadditivity and(3). For(5), let L_i=w_i-w_i^i>=0. Since only
owner i's own local account can decrease its own opposing account,
 L_i<=min(w_i,z_i).
Also d_i-d_i^i=(b_i-b_i^i)-L_i. Combining the own overflow term with W^i-W,
 (S-S^i)/3-(d_i-d_i^i)
 =[(B-B^i)/3-(b_i-b_i^i)]
    +(1/3)sum_{k!=i}(w_k^i-w_k)+(2/3)L_i.
Take positive parts, sum, and use(3) plus the bound on sum L_i. This proves(5).

SCOPE
All inequalities compare separately selected actual whole-owner underreports,
with fixed owners and arbitrary real amounts. They apply even when some local
pools switch or local totals increase. They repair the failed zero-slack cap
analogue by adding an explicit positive-grid term. They do NOT alone prove
C^2 funding of the envelope: the outer nonlinear response must still convert
these cap charges into a funding estimate using the available hardware-scale
budget. No new mechanism or data-center performance claim is made here.
