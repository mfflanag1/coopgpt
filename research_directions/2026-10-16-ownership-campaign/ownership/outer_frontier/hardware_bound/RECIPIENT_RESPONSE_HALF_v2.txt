HALF-GAP RECIPIENT RESPONSE AND GRID CAP COMPENSATION
16 October 2026. Separate v2 candidate; v1 remains frozen. Independent review
pending. Fixed actual owners, all nonnegative real mixed reports, exponential
primitive only. No historical-priority claim or full envelope funding theorem.

THEOREM A
For one fixed recipient k, choose an arbitrary own-bundle underreport separately
for each actual owner i, and denote k's resulting local payment by z_k^i.
Let H be full aggregate hardware and z_k its full local payment. Then
 sum_i(z_k^i-z_k)_+ <=(H-z_k)/2.                          (A)
This holds for arbitrarily many branches after aggregating their local payments
and hardware, even if each owner changes all its branches simultaneously.

1. CROSS-MONOTONICITY AND REDUCTION
Fix the recipient's own two coordinates, let a be the smaller and b the larger.
When the a-pool is short with total T, its payment is
 a+q[min(b,T)-a].
Increasing opponents' a-pool reports increases both T and q, whereas increasing
opponents' b-pool reports decreases q. When the b-pool is short its payment is
 b-q(b-a),
because its own opposite account a is uncapped. Increasing opponents' b-pool
reports increases q, and increasing opponents' a-pool reports decreases q.
All pool and cap boundaries agree. Thus the recipient can benefit only when
another owner withholds the input of which the recipient owns MORE. For each
other owner's arbitrary mixed deviation, restore its adverse coordinate and
then fully delete its beneficial coordinate; this only increases the recipient's
payment. These are allowed reports of the same actual owner, not split owners.
If a=b all its payments are constant. Its own underreport cannot increase its
payment, so the diagonal i=k contributes0.

On a short-total cell,
 q'(T)=q(r-m)/T, r>=1, 0<=m<=r, and qr<=1/2.             (1)
These identities will control derivatives through all cap boundaries.

2. RECIPIENT'S HARDWARE IS ITS LARGER COORDINATE
Let its hardware be b and its local resource a<=b. The beneficial coordinates
are other owners' hardware. If hardware is long, the magnitude of the derivative
with respect to an uncapped opponent hardware coordinate is
 q[min(b,T)-a]/T<=q<=1/2;
it is zero when that opponent is capped. If hardware is short, the magnitude
is (b-a)q'(T)<= (b-a)/(2T)<=1/2 since T>=b. Therefore deletion of opponent i's
hardware can raise the payment by at most h_i/2. Summing gives
 sum_i(z_k^i-z_k)_+ <=(H-b)/2 <=(H-z_k)/2,
because the recipient's payment is at most its larger own coordinate b.
Zero-output boundaries follow by continuity.

3. RECIPIENT'S HARDWARE IS ITS SMALLER COORDINATE
If H=0, everything is zero. Otherwise normalize H=1. Write own hardware a,
own local resource b>a. All other hardware stays fixed after the reduction
in Section1, so only opponents' local-resource coordinates are deleted.

3a. OWN LOCAL RESOURCE b>=1
The local-resource pool always remains long and the recipient's own cap is1.
Put d_i=min(l_i,1) for i!=k, r=sum_i d_i, q=(1/2)exp(-r). Its full payment is
z_k=a+q(1-a). Deleting i increases it by q(1-a)(exp(d_i)-1). Since d_i<=1,
 sum_i(z_k^i-z_k)_+ <=(1-a)q r(e-1).                    (2)
We show q r(e-1)<(1-q)/2, which proves(A). Set A0=e-1, so1<A0<2. The maximum
for r>=0 of (1+2A0*r)exp(-r) occurs at r=1-1/(2A0), and equals
 2A0 exp(-1+1/(2A0)).
As a function of A0>1/2 this is increasing, so it is below4exp(-3/4)<2.
The last strict inequality follows from exp(3/4)>1+3/4+9/32=65/32>2.
Consequently q[1+2r(e-1)]<1, exactly the desired estimate. The case a=1 has
zero response directly.

3b. OWN LOCAL RESOURCE0<b<1 AND FULL RESOURCE TOTAL B<=1
Put u=b-a. Along any beneficial deletion, the resource total T stays in[b,B]
and the recipient payment is b-q(T)u. By(1), its derivative magnitude is
u q'(T)<=u/(2T)<=u/(2b). The sum of all individually deleted coordinates is
B-b<=1-b. Thus aggregate gain is at most
 u(1-b)/(2b) <=(1-b)/2 <=(1-z_k)/2,
since u<=b and z_k<=b.

3c. OWN LOCAL RESOURCE0<b<1 AND FULL RESOURCE TOTAL B>1
We may replace every OTHER local-resource report by its cap at1 for this
calculation. This preserves the full payment and every payment after deleting
one opponent: if a remaining report exceeds1, the pool stays long and only
caps enter; if no remaining report exceeds1, the remaining total already
equals the sum of those caps. After this replacement write
 d_i=min(l_i,1), D=b+sum_i d_i=1+r>1,
 q=(1/2)exp(-r), u=b-a, z_k=a+qu.
Thus0<d_i<=1 and sum_i d_i=1+r-b. The gain after deleting d_i is u*f(d_i).
For d_i<=r the pool does not switch, and
 f(d_i)=q(exp(d_i)-1).                                  (3)
For d_i>r it first reaches the tie and then decreases the resource total from1
to1+r-d_i>=b. The pre-tie gain divided by u is1/2-q. The post-tie derivative
bound in3b gives the upper bound
 f(d_i)<=1/2-q+(d_i-r)/(2b).                             (4)
Define g(d) by the right sides of(3) and(4), joined at d=r when r<1.
This is continuous, nonnegative, convex on[0,1], with g(0)=0: the left
slope at r is1/2 and the right slope is1/(2b)>=1/2.

It suffices to prove
 sum_i g(d_i)<= (1/b-q)/2.                              (5)
Indeed (1-z_k)/(2u)=(1-b)/(2u)+(1-q)/2 >=(1/b-q)/2 because u<=b.

If r>=1, no switch is possible and convexity on[0,1] gives
 sum_i f(d_i)<=q(e-1)(1+r-b).
This decreases in r>=1, whereas (1/b-q)/2 increases. At r=1 the required
inequality is
 (1/2-c)(2-b)<=(1/b-c)/2, c=1/(2e).
After multiplying by2b, the right side minus the left side is
 Q(b)=(1-2c)b^2-(2-3c)b+1.
For0<=b<=1, Q'(b)<=-c<0 and Q(1)=c>0, so(5) holds.

Now0<r<1, so sum_i d_i=1+r-b lies between0 and2. A convex function g on[0,1]
with g(0)=0 has its sum at fixed total maximized by as many unit coordinates
as possible and at most one fractional coordinate. This follows by replacing
a pair of interior coordinates with either(0,x+y) or(1,x+y-1), keeping its
sum and increasing the convex objective; finitely many replacements suffice.
If r<=b, the total is at most1. Its concentrated coordinate1+r-b is above r,
so(4) gives
 sum_i g(d_i)<=1/2-q+(1-b)/(2b)=1/(2b)-q,
which is at most the right side of(5).
If r>=b, the total is at least1. Its maximizing coordinates are1 and r-b.
The latter is at most r. Therefore
 sum_i g(d_i)<=1/2-2q+(1-r)/(2b)+(1/2)exp(-b).
Subtracting this from the right side of(5) gives
 (1/2)[r/b-1-exp(-b)+(3/2)exp(-r)].                     (6)
For fixed b and r>=b, the bracket is increasing in r, since
1/b-(3/2)exp(-r)>=1/r-(3/2)exp(-r)>0;
the last inequality follows from r exp(-r)<=1/e<2/3. At r=b the bracket is
(1/2)exp(-b)>0. Hence(6) is positive, proving(5) in the last case.
This completes(A) for one branch. Multiply back by H.

4. MULTIBRANCH AGGREGATION
For any fixed recipient, the positive increase of its aggregate payment is at
most the sum of its positive branch increases. Sum first over deviating owners,
then over branches, applying(A) on each branch. The resulting bound is
(1/2)sum_j(H_j-z_kj)=(H-z_k)/2. This keeps every actual owner fixed and permits
unrelated deviations for each owner. Zero-output branches cause no difficulty.

THEOREM B: GRID OVERFLOW COMPENSATION
Let p_k be full grid reports and restore the deviator's grid before comparing
outer payments. Use the full hardware total H as the fixed cap threshold. Put
 b_k=p_k+z_k, w_k=(b_k-H)_+, W=sum_k w_k,
 b_k^i=p_k+z_k^i, w_k^i=(b_k^i-H)_+,
 U_k=H-z_k>=0,
 G=sum_k min{U_k/2, (p_k-U_k/2)_+}.
Then
 sum_i sum_k(w_k^i-w_k)_+ <=G,
 G<=P/2, and also G<=P-W.                               (7)
All diagonal increases are zero by local own monotonicity.

PROOF. For fixed k, set v_i=(z_k^i-z_k)_+. TheoremA gives sum_i v_i<=U_k/2.
If p_k<=U_k the full overflow vanishes, and
 sum_i(w_k^i-w_k)_+<=sum_i[v_i-(U_k-p_k)]_+
                    <=[sum_i v_i-(U_k-p_k)]_+
                    <=(p_k-U_k/2)_+.
If p_k>=U_k, each increase is at most v_i, so their sum is at most U_k/2.
These combine to the summand defining G. It is at most p_k/2: in the middle
range U_k/2<=p_k<=U_k use p_k-U_k/2<=p_k/2; the other ranges are immediate.
It is also at most min(p_k,U_k)=p_k-w_k. Summing proves(7).

THEOREM C: CAP-SENSITIVE RETENTION
Let S=sum_k min(b_k,H) and S^i=sum_k min(b_k^i,H). Let own capped accounts be
d_i=min(b_i,H), d_i^i=min(b_i^i,H), and let Delta_- be the sum of full local
funding slacks on branches whose full ratio is below2. The universal ordinary
retention theorem supplies constant kappa=1, or20/21 using its strengthened
long-stage certificate. Define L=sum_i min(w_i,z_i). Then
 sum_i[(S-S^i)/3-(b_i-b_i^i)]_+ <=kappa Delta_-+G/3,       (8)
 sum_i[(S-S^i)/3-(d_i-d_i^i)]_+
      <=kappa Delta_-+G/3+(2/3)L.                       (9)
In particular(8) is at most kappa Delta_-+P/6. These inequalities are uniform
over separately chosen actual whole-owner underreports.

PROOF. Write B=P+Z, B^i=P+Z^i and W^i=sum_k w_k^i. Then S=B-W,S^i=B^i-W^i.
Equation(8) follows from ordinary retention and(7) by positive-part subadditivity.
For(9), let L_i=w_i-w_i^i>=0. Since grid is restored and z_i^i<=z_i,
L_i<=min(w_i,z_i), and d_i-d_i^i=(b_i-b_i^i)-L_i. Therefore
 (S-S^i)/3-(d_i-d_i^i)
 =[(B-B^i)/3-(b_i-b_i^i)]
   +(1/3)sum_{k!=i}(w_k^i-w_k)+(2/3)L_i.
Take positive parts, sum over i, and apply(7) and sum_i L_i<=L.

SCOPE
The response and cap-compensation estimates are independent of owner count,
branch count, grid size, and local ratio. They preserve fixed owners, actual
bundle deviations, and continuous report domains. They provide an explicit
grid-funded replacement for the false slack-only cap-retention inequality.
They do not yet convert the available (C^2-C)H margin into a complete funding
bound for the hardware-bottleneck envelope; that outer response step remains.
