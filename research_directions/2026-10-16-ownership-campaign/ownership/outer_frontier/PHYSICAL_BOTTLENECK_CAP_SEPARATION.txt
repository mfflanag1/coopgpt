WHERE OPPOSING-ACCOUNT CAPS CAN MATTER
16 October 2026. Elementary structural lemma; same fixed-owner depth-two model.

For each branch, every owner's exponential primitive payment is at most that
branch's output T_j: it is a convex combination of an individual short report
and an individual capped long report, both at most T_j. Therefore under ANY
whole-owner underreport,
 z_k^i<=M^i<=M<=H
for every actual owner k, where H is full hardware total. Restoring only the
outer hardware to its full report leaves these computed local payments fixed.
Grid reports can also be restored to full before comparison, since doing so
increases only the recipient's own outer opposing coordinate and the outer
primitive is own-coordinate monotone.

For every resulting opposing account,
 p_k+z_k^i<=P+M.
Consequently, if the full physical profile satisfies P+M<=H, no opposing account
can exceed the restored hardware totalH under any actual underreport. Even if
inflated aggregate local payments make B^i=P+Z^i crossH, the outer long-pool
capped sum is exactly B^i, so the long-orientation payment depends on this total
and the own account only. Opponent redistribution does not create an additional
cap statistic in this entire physical power-bottleneck regime.

Outer opposing-account clipping can therefore matter only when P+M>H, where
physical outputF=H. Also, if P=0 there is never opposing-account overflow,
because z_k^i<=M<=H. Positive grid is necessary for the particular cap phenomenon.
This lemma alone is not a funding theorem; switching the outer short pool can
still matter when P+M<=H, but then it is a scalar total-switching problem.
