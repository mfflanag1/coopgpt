VERDICT
No substantive mathematical gap found. I independently rederived the local

1. WELL-DEFINED RULE, COVERAGE AND CONTINUITY
For A>0, M>0 and D>=A. The water target A+M does not exceed total cap massD+M.
If D>A, the sum of capped-at-level masses is strictly increasing up to the
largest cap, so its target below the total has a unique level. If D=A the
stated maximum-cap level makes every product factor1. Thus p=1/2 there and
all zero caps can safely be omitted from the product. In particular, at A=B,
all z_i<=A, D=A and y_i=(a_i+b_i)/2; orientation is unambiguous.

At a coalition S, its short total X is<=A, and sum_S min(z_i,A)>=min(Z,A)
>=min(X,Z). This and X>=min(X,Z) prove exact coalition coverage. Nonnegative
coefficients prove nonnegative payments. The dummy cap is never added to the
coalition or payment vector; it changes only the scalar p.

Water-level continuity and the product formula handle positive-A boundaries,
including a cap passing the level or two owners exchanging the maximum. As
A tends to0, x_i<=A,d_i<=A,p<=1/2 imply y_i<=3A/2, so the declared zero-payment
case is continuous. This is enough to pass from cell derivatives to endpoint
comparisons once the finite-cell argument is established below.

2. LONG-ACCOUNT DIFFERENTIATION
A,M and all other long accounts are fixed. For uncapped d_i above lambda,
its water contribution is already lambda, so lambda stays fixed and
 p'/p=-1/d_i.
For d_i below lambda, let r count all water-active caps including the dummy.
The water equation gives lambda'=-1/r and p'/p=-1/lambda. Hence the actual
owner's derivative p+(d_i-x_i)p' is respectively p*x_i/d_i>=0 or
p*(1-d_i/lambda+x_i/lambda)>=0. The unchanged own short account is correctly
included. Once z_i>=A, the payment is independent of that long report.
The D=A region either leaves the capped long account unchanged or is left
through a continuous boundary; it introduces no negative segment.

3. SHORT-ACCOUNT DIFFERENTIATION
Normalize the evaluated point to A=1, without differentiating this normalization
as though A stayed fixed along the change. Let s=M, x=x_i, d=d_i and let
M'=epsilon in{0,1} on a fixed maximum cell. Let c count long reports above A;
exactly those caps move at rate1. On a D>A cell, lambda<1, so all c moving caps
are water-active. If k is the total number of active actual long caps, c<=k.
Every inactive actual cap is constant along this short-account change.

If the dummy is inactive, its contribution M cancels the right-side M in the
water equation. Thus k lambda+u=A, lambda'=1/k and
 beta=p'/p=1/lambda-c.
Since k lambda<=1, beta>=0. Also beta<=1/lambda and x<=s<=lambda. The lower
bound y_i'>=1-p-p beta x>=1-p(1+x/lambda)>=0 follows.

If the dummy is active, differentiating(k+1)lambda+u=A+M gives
 beta=(1+epsilon)/lambda-epsilon/s-c,
and p<=lambda/(2s). These formulae include movement of both the dummy denominator
and every saturated actual long cap. For beta>=0,epsilon0, use x<=s and
p(1+x/lambda)<=(lambda+x)/(2s)<=1. For beta>=0,epsilon1, x=s and
1+beta*x=2s/lambda-cs<=2s/lambda, giving the same nonnegative derivative.

The potentially dangerous beta<0 case is also correct. Since lambda<s,
 beta>=1/lambda-c>=1-s/lambda,
where the second step uses c<=k and(k+1)lambda<=1+s. If d<=x, the coefficient
term has favorable sign. If d>x, then d-x<=1 and d'>=0, so
 y_i'>=1-p[1+(s/lambda-1)]=1-p*s/lambda>=1/2.
This is a bound on the complete mixed owner's payment, not on one resource's
payment in isolation. No sign assumption that p must increase is being made.

The D=A face can persist on a nontrivial interval, so it should not merely be
treated as an isolated crossing. The author's clarification correctly handles
it separately: p=1/2 and y=(x+d)/2 there. Along a short increase, both x_i and
d_i=min(z_i,A) are nondecreasing. This closes that face explicitly.

For a single coordinate variation there are finitely many maximum-owner,
physical cap and water-active sets. On each such choice lambda is affine and
the defining comparisons are linear, producing finitely many intervals.
Continuity, the separate D=A face and the tie-orientation formula therefore
join the nonnegative local derivatives into a global monotonicity statement.
Sequential changes prove full actual-owner bundle monotonicity.

4. FUNDING ENDPOINT REDUCTION
At A=1, total excess is p(D-1). Holding s,lambda and the inactive actual caps
fixed, a water-active actual cap d can vary in[lambda,1] without changing its
water contribution. The excess is a positive fixed multiplier times
(d+R)/d, so an endpoint weakly increases it. Moving a cap to lambda simply
absorbs it into the inactive sum; continuity preserves the value of the
formula. The water equation guarantees actual truncated mass>=1 throughout,
so D>=1 is not accidentally lost. Repeating leaves k active caps equal1.

Inactive dummy: k lambda+u=1 and excess=(k/2)lambda^k(1-lambda), with
lambda<=1/k for k>=1. k1 gives at most1/8; k>=2 is increasing up to1/k and
has maximum(k-1)/(2k^k)<=1/8. k0 gives zero.

Active dummy: the excess equals
 lambda^(k+1)[k+s-(k+1)lambda]/(2s),
with lambda<=min(s,(1+s)/(k+1)). k0 gives at most s/8. k1 has its unconstrained
maximum at(1+s)/3: for s<=1/2 the cap yields at most1/8, and for s>=1/2 its
maximum is(1+s)^3/(54s)<=4/27. For k>=2 the unconstrained optimizer
(k+s)/(k+2) lies beyond(1+s)/(k+1), since k^2-2>=s. Thus the expression
increases to the constrained endpoint. If s<=1/k the endpoint s gives at
most1/8. Otherwise the endpoint(1+s)/(k+1) gives
 (k-1)(1+s)^(k+1)/(2s(k+1)^(k+1)),
increasing in s>=1/k. At s1 this is(k-1)2^k/(k+1)^(k+1): equal4/27 for k2,
and no more than1/8 for k>=3. All divisions have positive denominators in
these cases. This independently establishes the claimed global bound.

5. ATTAINMENT, COMPUTATION AND SCOPE
The actual three-owner profile(1,0),(0,1),(0,1) gives lambda2/3,p4/27 and
funding31/27. This proves sharpness for this rule, not for all truthful rules.
Sorting positive caps and solving their interval's linear equation gives exact
rational lambda,p and payments on rational input; the arithmetic-operation
claim O(n log n) is appropriate with the author's bit-complexity caveat.

No private costs, consumption utilities, marginal-payment floors or strategic
owner splitting are added. The direct mixed proof avoids invoking an
unestablished1-Lipschitz property. The old depth-two7/4 theorem used extra
component bounds, so replacing its primitive requires a separate proof.
The stated fixed positive-sum composition corollary does follow termwise,
provided its exact coalitionwise decomposition and nonnegative report maps
really hold. No new decomposition theorem is implicit.

7. INDEPENDENT CHECK OF ROOT'S MATCHING ANSATZ LOWER
Consider any pure-owner rule with one common capped coefficient, paying a short
owner(1-p)x and a long owner p*min(z,A), and imposing half of each report at
every balanced profile. The coefficient may otherwise depend arbitrarily on
the entire profile. Fix one hardware owner and two distinct power owners.
Use the four positive profiles
 v=(4/3;2/3,2/3), u=(1;2/3,2/3),
 w=(1;1,2/3), f=(1;1,1).
At v, balanced-half normalization pays the hardware owner2/3. Its own increase
u-to-v therefore forces1-p_u<=2/3, or p_u>=1/3. The first power owner's own
increase u-to-w forces p_w>=2p_u/3. The second power owner's own increase
w-to-f forces p_f>=2p_w/3. Hence p_f>=4/27. At f, grand production is1 and
total funding is(1-p_f)+2p_f=1+p_f>=31/27. All caps and transition directions
are exactly as stated, and every actual owner is active throughout.

This is a correct matching lower for the stated common-coefficient,
balanced-half ansatz. It does not require differentiability or a formula for p.
The proposed water-fill rule belongs to the ansatz, so its31/27 bound is sharp
within that entire class. The scope restriction is essential: the sole
hardware owner is a veto owner, and paying that owner the grand value while
paying both power owners0 gives an efficient truthful coalition-covering rule
on this fixed three-owner institution. Thus these profiles do not yield an
unrestricted31/27 lower. They demonstrate the compatibility role of crossing
between short and long report contexts, rather than an intrinsic cost of that
institution.
