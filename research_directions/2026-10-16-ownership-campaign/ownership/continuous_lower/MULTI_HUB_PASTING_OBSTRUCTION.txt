EXACT INCOMPATIBILITY OF THE CONTINUOUS PRODUCT COMPLETIONS
16 October2026. Candidate proof, not yet independently reviewed.
This is a rule-architecture obstruction, not an unrestricted funding lower.
Companion: ONE_POOL_ORTHANT_THEOREM.txt.

1. QUESTION
For each balanced positive baseline(a,b), the companion gives a truthful
coalition-covering completion on the union of its two upward orthants. At its
hub let pool A receive alpha(a,b)*a and pool B receive(1-alpha(a,b))*b, where
alpha(a,b) may be chosen separately for every hub. Can these completions agree
on overlaps and therefore define one rule on their union?

Answer: no, already for four positive actual owners and four balanced hubs.
The obstruction does not depend on choosing the budget-optimizing alpha.

2. NECESSARY CONSISTENCY EQUATIONS
Fix common total T>0 and positive baseline vectors with
 P(a)=product_i a_i, Q(b)=product_j b_j.
For two A baselines a,a' and the same B baseline b, their A-orthants overlap:
choose x_i>=max(a_i,a'_i). With c_i=min(x_i,T), the common-price coefficients
of the two local completions are respectively
 q=alpha(a,b)P(a)/product_i c_i,
 q'=alpha(a',b)P(a')/product_i c_i.
All b_j are positive. Equality of even one fixed B owner's payment forces
q=q', hence
  alpha(a,b)P(a)=K(b),                              (A)
where K depends only on b over the selected family of A baselines.
The symmetric B-orthant overlap gives
  (1-alpha(a,b))Q(b)=L(a).                          (B)
Multiplying (A) by Q(b), multiplying (B) by P(a), and adding gives
  K(b)Q(b)+L(a)P(a)=P(a)Q(b).                       (C)

Choose two A baselines a0,a1 and two B baselines b0,b1. The left side of(C)
is the sum of a function of b alone and a function of a alone, so its mixed
2-by-2 difference vanishes. Therefore consistency requires
  [P(a1)-P(a0)]*[Q(b1)-Q(b0)]=0.                    (D)
This is impossible when both pools have two selected baseline products that
differ. It rules out all choices of alpha values, even without the restriction
alpha in[0,1]. In particular, allowing a report-dependent split at each hub
does not repair the disagreement.

3. EXPLICIT FOUR-OWNER WITNESS
Use two pure hardware owners and two pure power owners; every baseline total
is T=1. In each pool take baseline0=(1/2,1/2) and baseline1=(1/4,3/4).
All four Cartesian combinations are balanced hubs, all with strictly positive
reports. Their within-pool products are1/4 and3/16. Thus(D) would require
  (3/16-1/4)^2=1/256=0,
a contradiction. Overlap profiles can use the common upper vector(1/2,3/4)
on the changing pool; no unbounded limit, zero report, or identity change is
needed for this obstruction. The local completion theorem itself permits
unbounded reports, but this incompatibility is already finite and exact.

4. WHY THE PRODUCT FORM IS NOT AN ARBITRARY CHOICE WITHIN THIS SUBCLASS
Consider a single positive balanced hub(a,b), and a common-price rule on its
A-orthant that pays A owner i q(x)*min(x_i,T) and B owner j(1-q(x))*b_j.
Suppose, as in the companion completion, each changing A owner's own payment
is constant as a function of that owner's own report with all others fixed.
On the positive box x_i in[a_i,T], this means q(x)*x_i is independent of x_i.
Therefore g(x)=q(x)*product_i x_i is independent of each coordinate. Moving
coordinates one at a time shows g is constant on the entire box, with value
alpha(a,b)P(a). For x_i>=T, constancy of q(x)*T requires q to be constant in
that coordinate too. Hence the capped product formula is forced throughout
that orthant. Zero-baseline owners are not needed for this necessity claim.
The symmetric conclusion holds on each B-orthant.

Consequently the obstruction applies to all such scalar-price completions
with flat own-report payments on the changing side, not merely to one choice
of the formula's parameters. It does not apply to general own-monotone rules
whose changing-side payment may increase, owner-specific coefficients, or
non-price allocation vectors. One of those ingredients must change to paste
across all four hubs.

5. RESEARCH CONSEQUENCE AND LIMIT
The companion theorem shows that a single balanced hub, even with arbitrary
continuous multi-owner chains in either changing pool, cannot yield a lower
above9/8. The present obstruction shows exactly why independently solving all
such domains cannot be treated as a full continuous upper bound: the local
rules cannot even agree on common profiles. It does NOT prove that another
rule lacks a9/8 upper, and it does NOT provide a lower above9/8. A stronger
unrestricted lower must use genuinely interacting report contexts, while a
successful global construction must resolve their compatibility rather than
reselect the current hub without an incentive proof.
