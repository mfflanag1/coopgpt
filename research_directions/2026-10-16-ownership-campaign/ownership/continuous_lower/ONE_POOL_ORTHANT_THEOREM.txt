SHARP9/8 STOPPING THEOREM FOR TWO CONTINUOUS UPWARD ORTHANTS
16 October2026. Candidate conventional proof, not yet independently reviewed.
No historical-priority claim. Pure actual owners only.

1. EXACT DOMAIN AND CONSEQUENCE
Fix disjoint finite actual owner sets A and B, each with a nonnegative baseline
resource vector a=(a_i) and b=(b_j), satisfying sum a_i=sum b_j=T>0. Every owner
has its fixed pool role. Coalition value at any profile is the minimum of its
two resource totals. Define the report domain
 Omega_A={ (x,b): x_i>=a_i for every i in A },
 Omega_B={ (a,z): z_j>=b_j for every j in B },
 Omega=Omega_A union Omega_B.
All coordinates may grow without bound. Arbitrarily many owners can change
simultaneously within a pool. Both owner counts and the baseline heterogeneity
are unrestricted. Owners with zero baselines remain the same fixed owners.

Theorem. Omega admits nonnegative, coalition-covering, own-report-monotone
payments of total at most(9/8)T at every profile. In fact the explicit rule below
has exact worst funding factor
  1+kappa_A*kappa_B/(kappa_A+kappa_B),
where kappa_A=max_i (a_i/T)(1-a_i/T), similarly kappa_B; if both kappas vanish,
the displayed excess is defined as0. If exactly one vanishes it is also0.
The uniform factor9/8 over these domains is sharp with four actual owners.
Every profile has grand production T.

Consequence for lower-bound searches. No weighted coalition/report-transition
certificate using only profiles in Omega, with transition endpoints also in
Omega, can establish a factor above9/8. This closes arbitrary continuous
interacting transition chains confined to one changing pool at a time, from
one fixed balanced baseline. It does NOT close chains containing profiles in
which both pools differ from that baseline, and it does NOT establish a9/8
mechanism on the full continuous rectangular report domain.

2. RULE
For alpha in[0,1], pay alpha*a_i to pool-A owners and(1-alpha)*b_j to pool-B
owners at the common baseline. On Omega_A, cap c_i=min(x_i,T) and define
 r_i=a_i/c_i if c_i>0; r_i=1 if a_i=c_i=0,
 q=alpha*product_i r_i.
Pay pool-A owner i the amount q*c_i and pool-B owner j the amount(1-q)*b_j.
On Omega_B use the symmetric formula with initial long-side coefficient
1-alpha: cap d_j=min(z_j,T), define s_j analogously, q=(1-alpha)*product_j s_j,
pay pool-B owner j q*d_j and pool-A owner i(1-q)*a_i.

At the common baseline every positive ratio is1 and zero ratios have the
specified value1, so both formulas agree with the baseline payment. If a
zero-baseline owner becomes active, its ratio is0 and q=0, paying the fixed
opposite pool all T. The activating owner receives0 throughout that change.
There are no divisions by zero under the stated convention.

3. ALL COALITIONS AND ALL OWN-REPORT COMPARISONS
On Omega_A, the opposite pool B is the short pool, with total T. For any
coalition S its short amount B_S is at most T, and
 sum_{i in S cap A}c_i >= min(sum_{i in S A}x_i,T)
                        >= min(sum_{i in S A}x_i,B_S).
Its short payment(1-q)B_S and long payment q*sum_S c_i therefore cover its
value. q lies in[0,1], so payments are nonnegative. Omega_B is symmetric.

For a positive-baseline pool-A owner i on Omega_A,
 y_i=alpha*a_i*product_{k!=i}r_k.
This is independent of its own report x_i, including after its cap is reached.
If a_i=0, its own payment is0 both when inactive and when active. Hence every
own-report comparison within Omega_A is payment-neutral for changing owners.
Pool-B reports are fixed there. The analogous property holds on Omega_B.

For completeness, a pair of profiles in different orthants differing only in
one owner's report must have the baseline as one endpoint: if that owner is
in A, all B reports must equal b at both profiles, while the Omega_B profile
already has all A reports equal a. The remaining A owners must then be at a
at both profiles. The A owner's comparison is the same baseline-to-ray
comparison already covered by the Omega_A calculation. The B case is
symmetric. Thus every own-comparable pair in the entire union Omega is covered,
not just designated transition edges. Reports are continuous and unbounded.

4. EXACT BUDGET LEMMA
Let w_i>=0, sum_i w_i=1, and choose c_i in[w_i,1]. When w_i=c_i=0 use ratio1;
when w_i=0<c_i use ratio0. Define
 E(c)=(sum_i c_i-1)*product_i(w_i/c_i).
Then
  max_{c_i in[w_i,1]} E(c)=max_i w_i(1-w_i).
The maximum is attained by raising one maximizing coordinate to1 and leaving
all others at baseline. This includes zero weights by the above convention.

Proof. Activating a zero weight gives E=0. Otherwise ignore zero coordinates.
With the remaining coordinates fixed, E is a nonnegative constant times
 (c_i+K)/c_i=1+K/c_i,
where K=sum_{j!=i}c_j-1 is fixed. Thus E is monotone or constant as a function
of c_i, and a maximum can be attained with c_i at an endpoint. Iterating over
coordinates reduces the global maximum on the compact positive box to a corner.
At a corner let J be the coordinates raised to1, k=|J| and s=sum_{i in J}w_i.
Its value is
 E_J=(k-s)*product_{i in J}w_i,
with E_empty=0. For k>=2 and j in J,
 E_{J\{j}}-E_J
 = product_{i in J\{j}}w_i * (k-1-s)*(1-w_j) >=0,
because s<=1 and k>=2. Removing indices therefore never lowers the corner
value until a singleton remains. The singleton value is w_i(1-w_i), proving
the claim and its exact attainment. No asymptotic estimate is used.

Apply this with w_i=a_i/T and normalized c_i=min(x_i,T)/T. On Omega_A total
funding equals T+q(sum_i c_i*T-T), so its exact worst excess factor is
alpha*kappa_A. On Omega_B it is(1-alpha)*kappa_B. Therefore the exact worst
factor for this rule is
  1+max(alpha*kappa_A,(1-alpha)*kappa_B).
For positive kappa_A+kappa_B choose alpha=kappa_B/(kappa_A+kappa_B). If both
vanish choose alpha=1/2. This proves the displayed theorem. Since each kappa
is at most1/4, their harmonic combination is at most1/8.

For equal pool populations m>=2 and equal baseline amounts T/m, both kappas
are(m-1)/m^2 and the factor becomes1+(m-1)/(2m^2). The domain includes every
positive-binary profile from the existing binary stopping theorem whose high
reports occur in only one pool. That earlier theorem's matching lower already
uses just the hub and one raised owner in either pool, so this factor is exact
on this continuous two-orthant domain as well. This is an arbitrary-real,
arbitrary-simultaneous-change extension of that particular stopping family.

5. SHARP UNIFORM LOWER WITHOUT SYMMETRY ASSUMPTIONS
Use fixed owners A1,A2,B1,B2 and hub c=(1,1|1,1). Its domain includes
 p=(1,1|1,2) and r=(2,1|1,1). All three grand values are2.
At p the coalitions {A1,B1},{A2,B1},{A1,A2,B2} give
  2Y(p)-y_B2(p)>=4.
At r the coalitions {A2,B1},{A2,B2},{A1,B1,B2} give
  2Y(r)-y_A1(r)>=4.
At c, coalition {A1,B2} and the two own-report increases give
  1<=y_A1(c)+y_B2(c)<=y_A1(r)+y_B2(p).
Thus 2Y(p)+2Y(r)>=9, forcing a uniform funding factor at least9/8.
This is the existing four-owner lower certificate, reproduced to show sharpness
of the enlarged-domain stopping theorem; it is not claimed as a new lower.

6. LIMITS
The formula uses a public fixed balanced baseline and is only asserted on the
union Omega. Selecting a new baseline from each current report creates a new
mechanism and does not inherit the proof. Profiles with simultaneous changes
in both pools are absent. Full rectangular continuous-domain truthfulness
cannot be deduced by separately covering that domain with such orthants.
Mixed actual owners are not handled by this theorem: permitting one owner to
move both resource coordinates changes the comparison graph. No cloning,
public-price optimization or report-dependent partition is implicit.
