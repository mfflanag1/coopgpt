POST-SWITCH RETENTION FOR THE EXPONENTIAL TWO-POOL PRIMITIVE
16 October 2026. Independent bounded proof attack. No novelty claim.
Status: full candidate proof; parent coordinates independent review.

SCOPE AND RESULT
Fix the same actual owners and mixed reports throughout. Normalize the short
total at the START OF THE LONG-ACCOUNT REDUCTION STAGE to1. Its capped long
ratio is R in[1,2]. Let the changing owner's capped long report be x<=1,
its own short report y>=0, and v=R-x the other long sum. If a pool switch
is possible then v<1, so all other actual long reports equal their capped
reports; own overflow can first be removed without effect. Keep the entire
original short vector fixed during this stage. Let
 q_R=(1/2)exp(1-R), c=1/(2e), delta_R=c-q_R(R-1),
 F_i=z_i-Z/3.
For EVERY reduction of this owner's long account, including reductions past
the pool switch and the zero-output endpoint,
 F_i(reduced)-F_i(full)
 <= (2/3)q_R[exp((2-R)/2)-1-(2-R)/2]
 <= delta_R/3.                                             (1)
In fact the last bound may be replaced by13 delta_R/42.
This is an individual LONG-STAGE bound, not by itself an aggregate retention
theorem or a conclusion about the preceding short-account reduction stage.
It permits mixed owners directly; no owner splitting or pure-role assumption.

1. POTENTIAL AFTER THE SWITCH
After the switch, write t=v+a in(0,1] for the new short total, where a>=0 is
the changed owner's remaining original-long report. The original short vector
now forms the long pool. Set
 S=sum_k min(s_k,t), d=min(y,t), r=S/t, q=(1/2)exp(1-r).
The original short reports s_k sum to1, so t<=S<=1. The actual owner's short
account is a=t-v and its capped long account is d. Thus its potential is
 F(t,S,d)=2t/3-v+q[d+v-(2t+S)/3].                         (2)
For fixed t,d,v its derivative in S has the sign of
 1+S/t-3(d+v)/t.
This affine expression increases with S. Therefore the function decreases
and then increases, or is monotone, and its maximum on[t,1] occurs at an
endpoint. This relaxation does not require the two endpoint cap sums to be
realizable by the same fixed short vector; it is an upper bound on its actual
cap sum.

At S=t,
 F(t,t,d)=t/6-v/2+d/2 <=1/6-v/2+y/2=:F_cross.             (3)
This is the actual potential at the pool tie t=1.
At S=1, replacing d by y can only increase (2). Define
 q_b=(1/2)exp(1-1/t),
 B(t)=2t/3-v+q_b[y+v-(2t+1)/3].                           (4)
We will compare this upper endpoint with an algebraic continuation of the
original pool orientation.

2. THE ARTIFICIAL ORIGINAL-ORIENTATION POTENTIAL
Let
 q_a=(1/2)exp(1-t),
 F_art(t)=y-1/3+q_a[(2t+1)/3-v-y].                       (5)
Above the switch this is the actual potential with the original short pool
held at total1. Below the switch it is only an algebraic auxiliary function;
no feasibility or incentive interpretation is assigned to that continuation.
Put W=y+v-(2t+1)/3. Direct subtraction gives
 B(t)-F_cross=(t-1)/3+(q_b-1/2)W,                       (6)
 F_art(t)-B(t)=(1-q_a-q_b)W.                            (7)
For0<t<=1, q_a+q_b>=1. Indeed the derivative of
 exp(1-t)+exp(1-1/t)
is nonpositive: the logarithm of the ratio of its positive derivative term
to the magnitude of its negative term is
 phi(t)=t-1/t-2log t.
Here phi(1)=0 and phi'(t)=(t-1)^2/t^2>=0, so phi(t)<=0 for t<=1.
The exponential sum is therefore at least its value2 at t=1.

If B(t)<=F_cross, (3) already bounds both endpoints. Otherwise (6) forces
W<0, and (7), together with q_a+q_b>=1, implies F_art(t)>=B(t).
Consequently every actual post-switch profile satisfies
 F_i(reduced)<=max{F_cross,F_art(t)}.                    (8)
At t=0 necessarily v=0 and actual output and payments vanish. Its potential
is0, whereas F_cross=1/6+y/2>=0; hence (8) extends to that endpoint directly.
Also F_art(1)=F_cross, so (8) uses two values of the same continuation.

3. A GLOBAL BOUND ON THE CONTINUATION
At the initial ratio R, (5) equals the actual full-stage potential. Reduce
the original long report by u>=0, so the formal long total is R-u. Let
 epsilon=2-R in[0,1],
 alpha=1-x+y-epsilon/3 >=-epsilon/3,
using x<=1 and y>=0. Differentiation in the original own long amount gives
 q_R exp(u)[alpha+(2/3)u].
Thus the potential increase at any u>=0 is at most
 (q_R/3) integral_0^u exp(w)[epsilon-2w] dw.
This integral is maximized over u>=0 at u=epsilon/2, giving
 G_max=(2/3)q_R[exp(epsilon/2)-1-epsilon/2].              (9)
The maximum over all u>=0 is a relaxation of the actual feasible interval;
its use is valid even where the auxiliary coefficient q_a is not a mechanism
coefficient. Both t and1 in (8) correspond to u>=0 because R>=1. Hence
(8)--(9) prove the first inequality in(1), including all post-switch reports.
The same differentiation already handles all pre-switch reductions.

4. EXACT EXPONENTIAL SLACK CERTIFICATE
Since q_R=c exp(epsilon),
 delta_R-3G_max
 =q_R[exp(-epsilon)+1+2epsilon-2exp(epsilon/2)].          (10)
For0<=epsilon<=1, Taylor's theorem with the positive fourth-order remainder
gives
 exp(-epsilon)>=1-epsilon+epsilon^2/2-epsilon^3/6.
For exp(epsilon/2), the terms beginning at degree3 have first term
epsilon^3/48, and each subsequent term is at most1/8 times its predecessor.
Therefore
 exp(epsilon/2)<=1+epsilon/2+epsilon^2/8+epsilon^3/42.
Substitution into(10) yields
 exp(-epsilon)+1+2epsilon-2exp(epsilon/2)
 >=epsilon^2/4-(3/14)epsilon^3>=epsilon^2/28.
This proves G_max<=delta_R/3, strictly when R<2. More precisely,
 G_max<=delta_R/3-q_R epsilon^2/84.
Finally exp(-epsilon)<=1-epsilon+epsilon^2/2, so
 delta_R=q_R[exp(-epsilon)-1+epsilon]<=q_R epsilon^2/2.
Thus G_max<=delta_R/3-delta_R/42=13 delta_R/42, including epsilon=0.
All comparisons are exact, dimension-free, and require no numerical sweep.

5. RESCALING AND LIMITS
For a long-stage short total T>0, multiply every potential and slack in this
note by T. If this stage arose after an owner's short-coordinate withholding,
its R may differ from the original full-profile capped ratio. The parent
aggregate argument must separately justify its comparison between this stage's
slack and the original slack, and count the potentially gainful actual owners.
This note supplies only the formerly missing orientation-switch control.
