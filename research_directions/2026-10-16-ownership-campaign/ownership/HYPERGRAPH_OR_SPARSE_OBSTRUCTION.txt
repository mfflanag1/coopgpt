FIXED SPARSIFICATION AND THE PRECISE HIGHER-ARITY HIERARCHY BOUNDARY
16 October2026. Structural applications/obstructions; no historical novelty claim.

1. FIXED OWNER-PAIR DELETION CANNOT PRESERVE THE FULL REPORT DOMAIN
The already written and independently checked
mixed/forest_lower/PAIR_SPARSIFICATION_OBSTRUCTION.txt proves the general
rank-exactly-two statement: after discarding zero-profit activities, every
productive compatibility edge must remain in a report-independent pair-deletion
lift if even GRAND production value is to be preserved at every report.
It assumes independent coordinatewise reports, two distinct genuine inputs
with positive coefficients for each activity, and no added synthetic activities.
One-input substitutes and linked report domains are outside that argument.

For one positive-profit two-input activity, the conclusion is sharper at the
individual-variant level. If m hardware-only owners can supply its first row
and n power-only owners its second, each of the mn lifted variants is necessary.
If variant(i,k) is omitted, activate only owner i's first-input capacity and
owner k's second-input capacity, in the activity's input ratio. The original
activity produces positively, while the retained lift produces zero. Thus
all of K_{m,n} is forced, even though the physical activity graph is one edge.

If q owners are each eligible for BOTH rows, every ordered variant(i,k) is
similarly forced: activate only i's first-row account and k's second-row
account. Reversing which accounts are activated forces the reverse variant.
Activating both rows of one owner forces its diagonal variant as well. The
simple cross-owner compatibility graph must therefore retain K_q. All these
profiles use the SAME fixed owner set and eligibility; zero reports do not
delete identities or change the technology.

These observations concern deleting activity variants or all variants on a
pair. They do not prohibit an economically equivalent formulation with added
constraints/activities, nor deleting duplicates or dominated variants when
another route for the same pair survives. Replacing the original production
game by one that lacks these profiles changes the research question.

The same pair-only witness also precludes ANY finite multiplicative value-
approximation factor on the closed nonnegative report domain after a productive
pair is removed. On the strictly positive orthant, the obstruction persists
if reports may approach zero independently: keep the two selected accounts
fixed positive and make every other eligible account epsilon. Every retained
variant must then consume at least one small account. With finitely many fixed
positive input coefficients and fixed profits, its total value is at most a
fixed constant times epsilon, while the original activity keeps a fixed
positive value. This extension does not cover domains with fixed positive
lower bounds or economically redundant one-input substitutions.

2. EXACT MULTI-OWNER LIFT FOR HIGHER-ARITY ACTIVITIES
Consider finite LP production whose activity e consumes positive amounts
A_re on each of its distinct genuine input rows r in R_e, with nonnegative
fixed profit. Each actual owner has fixed row eligibility and reports independent
nonnegative row capacities, aggregated additively in the original formulation.
There is no need to bound |R_e| for the existence result below.

Create one accounting row(i,r) with capacity b_ir for each eligible owner-row
pair, retaining owner i as its actual owner. For each activity, introduce a
variant for EVERY eligible tuple (i_r:r in R_e). It consumes A_re from row
(i_r,r) and earns the original per-unit profit. Repeated owners in a tuple are
allowed. Its ACTUAL owner support is the set {i_r:r in R_e}, not a new player.

This lift preserves every coalition value at every report. For the nontrivial
direction, distribute each original row demand across contributing owners
without exceeding their row budgets. After division by A_re, let alpha_ir be
the owner marginal on input r; every input marginal sums to the same x_e.
For x_e>0 the explicit nonnegative tensor
  z_(i_r:r in R_e)= product_{r in R_e} alpha_(i_r,r) / x_e^(|R_e|-1)
has each required marginal. For x_e=0 use zero variants. Therefore its resource
use and objective equal the original plan. Summing variants maps any lifted
plan back to the original feasible plan. All variants are fixed by eligibility;
they are not selected according to current positive reports or a chosen optimum.
The number of variants can be exponential in activity arity; no polynomial-
size claim for unbounded arity is made here.

3. SUFFICIENT CONDITION: CONNECTED SUPPORTS IN ONE FIXED OWNER FOREST
Suppose there exists a fixed forest T on the ACTUAL owners such that the actual-
owner support of EVERY lifted variant induces a connected subtree of T.
Singleton supports are permitted; unused/null owners remain in the forest.
Then the production game admits efficient, nonnegative, coalition-covering,
whole-owner-report-monotone payments on the full report domain.

Proof. For any actual coalition S, every variant it can operate has support
contained in S. That support is connected in T, hence lies in one component
of T[S]. Every accounting-row constraint belongs to one actual owner, so the
activity system separates by those components. Production value is exactly
additive over them. It is also disjoint-coalition superadditive, report-local,
and nondecreasing in included owners' reports. Root T once and for all and pay
  y_i=v(T_i)-sum_{j child of i}v(T_j).
The classical hierarchical allocation proof now applies: telescoping gives
efficiency; disjoint superadditivity gives nonnegativity and connected-coalition
coverage; induced-component additivity handles every other coalition. When i
increases any part of its own bundle, the positive term increases, and EACH
negative term is fixed because T_j excludes i. These report-local negative
coalitions are the incentive argument. No owner-specific forest or root is
chosen after reports arrive.

This is a higher-arity application of an established hierarchy, not a new
allocation rule. It extends the rank-two compatibility-forest condition:
a two-owner support is connected exactly when its pair is an edge of T.
For larger supports it is unnecessary and overly restrictive to demand that
every pair inside the support form an edge; connectivity suffices.

One unbounded concrete class is interval-supported activity production. Place
arbitrarily many actual owners on a fixed path, allow arbitrary owner bundles,
and require each fixed lifted activity support to be an interval of that path.
Activities may have arbitrarily large arity, overlap, compete for owner resources,
and have arbitrary fixed positive input coefficients and nonnegative values.
The above hierarchy is efficient and truthful. With the path rooted at its
first endpoint, it is simply the difference of adjacent suffix-coalition values.
This conclusion requires the interval condition for ALL eligible variants,
not only for the supports appearing in one selected optimal production plan.

4. BERGE-ACYCLIC SUPPORTS ARE SUFFICIENT
Form the incidence bipartite graph whose left vertices are actual owners and
whose right vertices are the DISTINCT nonempty activity owner-support sets.
Join an owner to a support node when it belongs to that support. Suppose this
incidence graph is a forest (the stated Berge-acyclic condition). Root each
nontrivial incidence component at an actual owner. Each support node has one
parent owner; contract the incidence edge to that parent. The remaining
incidence edges join the parent owner to every other member of that support.
Contraction preserves acyclicity, and the resulting graph is a forest on the
same actual owners. Each support induces a connected star inside it. Isolated
owners are retained, and singleton support nodes contribute no extra edge.
Section3 therefore gives efficient truthful funding. Duplicate activity supports
can be identified before this construction; they do not change the production
game's structural condition.

The connected-support condition is broader than Berge acyclicity. For instance
supports{1,2},{2,3},{1,2,3} are all intervals of one owner path, although their
incidence graph has a cycle. No historical priority claim is made for either
hypergraph representation fact or its hierarchy application.

5. ORDINARY HYPEREDGE JOIN TREES ARE NOT SUFFICIENT
A different condition puts a tree on the SUPPORT SETS and requires that all
sets containing a given owner form a connected subtree. This running-
intersection/join-tree condition does not imply Section3 and does not by itself
guarantee efficient truthful funding. The following exact counterexample uses
four fixed active owners and positive, economically nonredundant activities.

Owners0,1 hold exclusive hardware resources h_0,h_1; owners2,3 hold exclusive
power resources p_2,p_3. Include all four pair activities(i,k), i in{0,1},
k in{2,3}, consuming one unit from each endpoint and earning1. Add one activity
consuming one unit of ALL FOUR resources and earning17/8.

The support hypergraph has a join tree: use the all-owner support{0,1,2,3} as
the center and its four pair supports as leaves. Every owner's incident support
nodes form a connected star. But no forest on actual owners can make all four
pair supports connected, since those pairs form K_{2,2}. Thus the stronger
support-tree hypothesis correctly rejects this example.

The extra activity is economically nonredundant: at capacities(1,1|1,1), the
pair-only technology produces value2, whereas the all-owner activity produces
17/8. Each pair activity is also nonredundant on the report profile activating
only its endpoints. This is not a zero-profit or globally dominated support
added solely to manufacture a join tree.

For general grand capacities, let T=min(h_0+h_1,p_2+p_3) and m=min(h_0,h_1,p_2,p_3).
If the four-input activity runs at x in[0,m], the remaining complete bipartite
pair technology produces T-2x. Total value is T+x/8, so
  F(N)=T+m/8.
On every proper coalition the four-input activity is unavailable, so its value
is exactly the original two-pool worth.

Use three report profiles, with the SAME four owners and technology:
  c=(1,1|1,1),  a=(1,1|1,2),  b=(2,1|1,1).
All owners have positive reports. All grand values are17/8. At a, coverage of
{0,2},{1,2},{0,1,3} gives2Y(a)-y_3(a)>=4. At b, coverage of
{1,2},{1,3},{0,2,3} gives2Y(b)-y_0(b)>=4. At c, coverage of{0,3} and own-report
monotonicity on the two indicated one-owner increases give
  1<=y_0(c)+y_3(c)<=y_0(b)+y_3(a)<=2Y(b)+2Y(a)-8.
If total funding were uniformly at most C times production, then
  1<=2C(17/8)+2C(17/8)-8=(17/2)C-8,
so necessarily C>=18/17>1. Efficient truthful funding is impossible despite
the join-tree support condition and the presence of ordinary static LP cores.

This is an adaptation of the already established four-owner9/8 certificate,
not an improved unrestricted lower bound. Replacing17/8 by2+delta for any
0<delta<1/4 gives the same proof with C>=9/(8+4delta)>1. The boundary is the
incentive relevance of separators: a tree of hyperedge bags can have multi-owner
intersections, and it cannot automatically be treated as a hierarchy on actual
owners with all negative terms independent of the reporting owner.

CONCLUSION OF THIS BOUNDED ATTACK
Fixed pair deletion cannot cure dense eligibility while preserving the original
all-profile game. Higher arity does admit the clean fixed-owner-forest support
condition, including Berge-acyclic and interval support families. Hyperedge
join-tree acyclicity alone is too weak, even with all activity profits positive
and every activity nonredundant. These are exact structural boundaries; they
do not settle the unrestricted mixed-ownership forest subsidy problem.
