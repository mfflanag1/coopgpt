VERDICT
No substantive mathematical gap found. The binary theorem is exact on its
stated positive binary domain. The ternary theorem is exact uniformly over
arbitrary fixed owner counts, including mixed owners, on the common finite
grid {0,1,2}. Neither is an upper bound for the continuous report domain.
All statements below retain the actual owners; none permits identity changes.

1. POSITIVE BINARY THEOREM
The matching bound C(m,H)=1+(a-1)/(2ma), a=min(H,m), is correct for m>=2
and integer H>=2. The following load-bearing details were independently checked.

Coverage: if the short pool has grand total T and coalition short amount A_S,
then A_S<=T and sum of capped long reports is at least min(B_S,T), which is
at least min(A_S,B_S). The convex weighted coverage argument follows.

Incentives: a long/tied owner's 1-to-H increment multiplies its capped amount
by a_k=min(H,T) and divides q by precisely a_k, leaving its payment unchanged.
A strictly short owner cannot jump past the opposite integer high count when
one high count is added. Its new payment is at least H/2>=1, while its old
payment is at most1. Tie conventions agree owner by owner.

Budget: for integer d>=1 and a_k>=2, d/a_k^d<=1/a_k. The three cases
H<=m, H>m with k=0, and H>m with k>=1 have the stated cap sums and bounds.
The last polynomial reduction is exact:
 (m-1)H^2+(-2m+1)H+m^2
 = (m-1)(H-m)^2+(2m^2-4m+1)(H-m)+m(m-1)^2>=0.
The profile k=0,l=1 attains the upper.

Lower bound: fixed-population permutation symmetrization preserves all
inequalities. At the one-high power profile, the identity
 mx+(m-1)z+y=(m-1)(x+z)+(ax+y)/a+(1-1/a)y
is exact. Its coalition bounds and own-report comparison imply
B_P>=m+(1-1/a)v, and the symmetric hardware profile gives
B_A>=m+(1-1/a)u. Using u+v>=1 yields the stated lower. Integer a is essential
for the coalition containing exactly a unit hardware owners; the source
correctly does not claim this lower for noninteger H. Growing this symmetric
positive-binary population cannot produce a lower exceeding9/8.

2. PURE TERNARY COVERAGE AND BUDGET
For T>=2, every report is at most T and the payments are the convex combination
(1-q) times short resource and q times long resource, q=2^(-d-1).
Every coalition is therefore covered. Total funding is T+d/2^(d+1).
For integer d>=1, d/2^(d+1)<=1/4, and T>=2 gives factor<=9/8.
For d=0 the rule is efficient.

For T=1, the short pool has one unit owner. If the other pool has one active
owner, every productive coalition contains both, so half each covers value1.
If the other pool has at least two active owners, paying1 to the sole short
owner covers every productive coalition. T=0 is correctly treated as all-zero
payments. There are no omitted coalitions at these boundary profiles.

3. EVERY PURE OWN-REPORT TRANSITION
Let an owner stay on its fixed residual side while its amount changes 0-to-1
or1-to-2. Others' reports are fixed. The following exhausts all possibilities.

Zero-to-one: zero owners receive0. At every new profile the owner's payment
lies between0 and its amount1: this is explicit for T=0 and T=1, and follows
from q in[0,1] for T>=2. Therefore the increment lies in[0,1]. This argument
includes activation into a previously empty pool and activation as a second
long-side owner under the T=1 exception.

One-to-two, opposite total0: both payments0.
One-to-two, own pool long/tied, T>=2: q halves while own amount doubles,
so own payment stays fixed.
One-to-two, own pool long/tied, T=1: opposite total1 remains fixed and own
active-count does not change. A sole active owner keeps payment1/2; an owner
in a pool with at least two active owners keeps payment0.
One-to-two, own pool strictly short, T>=2: integrality prevents crossing from
short to strictly long. The new q is2q, including reaching a tie, and own
increment is1-3q. Since old q<=1/4, this lies in[1/4,1].
One-to-two, own pool strictly short, T=1: own owner is the unique short unit
owner. If the opposite pool has one active owner it must report2; old payment
1/2 becomes1 at the new tie. If it has at least two active owners, old payment
1 becomes an amount in[1,2]. Both increments lie in[0,1].
Thus the primitive is own-monotone and discretely1-Lipschitz for all fixed
opponent profiles, with arbitrary unequal population counts and zeros. These
are finite differences on the grid, not a continuous derivative conclusion.

4. MIXED ACTUAL-OWNER NETTING
For each actual owner define t_i=min(a_i,b_i), u_i=(a_i-b_i)+,
w_i=(b_i-a_i)+. Residual coordinates are ternary and never simultaneously
positive for one owner. The same owner receives y_i=t_i+phi_i(u,w).
For every coalition S,
 min(sum_S a_i,sum_S b_i)=sum_S t_i+min(sum_S u_i,sum_S w_i).
This identity proves coverage and the9/8 budget immediately. It does not
represent strategic splitting, and the pure formula is evaluated on the same
owner set even when a residual is zero.

An increment in a_i with a_i>=b_i leaves t_i fixed and increases u_i by1;
primitive monotonicity applies. With a_i<b_i, it increases t_i by1 and decreases
w_i by1; the discrete Lipschitz bound limits the primitive loss to1. Integer
steps may reach zero but cannot cross from a negative to a strictly positive
residual in one step. The argument for b_i is symmetric. Repeating coordinate
steps proves all whole-bundle comparisons on the full rectangular grid.
Indeed this proof also gives a full payment increment in[0,1] for each unit
coordinate increase, although only nondecrease is required.

The pure four-owner {1,2} subdomain forces9/8 by the audited binary lower.
The correct quantifier is the sharp uniform factor over the ternary class;
particular fixed eligibility patterns can admit smaller factors.

6. EXACT OBSTRUCTION TO UNBIASED OWNER-PRESERVING ROUNDING
This is a narrow obstruction to one transfer route, not a lower bound against
continuous truthful rules and not a disproof of a continuous9/8 upper.

Take FIVE fixed pure owners: three hardware owners each hold1; one power owner
holds1, and another power owner holds3. Suppose these resources are rounded
randomly, allowing completely correlated and profile-dependent randomness,
to a common scaled ternary grid {0,s(omega),2s(omega)}. The scale itself may be
random. Preserve each owner's resource role and assume each rounded amount is
integrable and unbiased. Denote rounded hardware amounts U1,U2,U3, rounded
unit-power amount V, and rounded amount3 power W. Thus E Uj=E V=1 and E W=3.

Suppose expected rounded value is at least original value for every actual
coalition. For the balanced two-owner coalition {hardware j,unit power},
 E min(Uj,V)>=1=(E Uj+E V)/2.
But min(Uj,V)=(Uj+V-|Uj-V|)/2, so this forces Uj=V almost surely for j=1,2,3.
For the balanced coalition of all three hardware owners and the amount3 power
owner, the same identity forces W=U1+U2+U3=3V almost surely. Because V and W
belong to one common scaled ternary grid, W=3V is possible only when V=W=0:
if s>0 and V>0 then V>=s and W>=3s>2s; if s=0 both are already zero.
This contradicts E V=1. Therefore such rounding cannot preserve all coalition
values even in expectation.

The obstruction allows arbitrary correlation and random common scale; it is
not restricted to independent rounding. It applies even at one fixed profile,
so choosing the rounding law using the full profile cannot rescue the stated
properties. More generally, any common bounded integer grid {0,s,...,Ks}
fails on K+1 unit hardware owners, one unit power owner and one amount(K+1)
power owner by the same proof.

What this leaves open: biased rounding, additional compensation, a transfer
argument using payments rather than equality of rounded production games,
unbounded-grid representations, or a direct continuous mechanism. Those would
need new coverage and incentive arguments. The stopping lemma alone supplies
none of them.
