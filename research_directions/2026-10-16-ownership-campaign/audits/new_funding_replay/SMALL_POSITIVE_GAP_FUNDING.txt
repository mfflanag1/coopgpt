QUADRATIC RESPONSE CHARGE FOR SMALL POSITIVE GAPS
16 October 2026. Candidate theorem for independent review.
Original exponential nested envelope; fixed actual owners and all-real reports.

THEOREM
Normalize full hardware H=1 and assume the physical hardware bottleneck
P+M>=1. Let c=1/(2e), C=1+c, K=e-1, and use the full-profile notation
a_i,z_i,d_i,S,q0 from POSITIVE_GAP_DUAL_AGGREGATION.txt. Assume1<=S<=2.
If a full positive-gap recipient has
 g_i=d_i-a_i in(0,1/8] and w_i=(p_i+z_i-1)_+=0,
then its actual envelope gain over its full raw payment N_i satisfies
 0<=E_i-N_i<=8K^2 c z_i g_i^2.                       (SP1)
This estimate permits independently chosen whole-owner underreports and
counterfactual pool switches. There is no owner-count or branch-count bound.

If EVERY positive-gap recipient satisfies these two conditions, then
 sum_i E_i <699/500<C^2.                              (SP2)
Thus this is an unbounded class of mixed-owner profiles with a dimension-free
funding guarantee. The hypotheses concern only the full profile; deviations
need not stay in the class. In particular(SP2) holds for no-full-overflow
profiles with all positive gaps at most H/8 before normalization.
No global C^2 theorem, unrestricted optimum, or novelty claim is asserted.

1. POSITIVE RECIPIENT: QUADRATIC LOCAL RESPONSE
The scalar coefficient k from the dual note is globally4c-Lipschitz on[0,infinity).
For0<t<=1 its derivative magnitude is
 (1/2)exp(1-1/t)/t^2.
With y=1/t>=1, this is(e/2)y^2 exp(-y), whose maximum occurs at y=2 and
equals2/e=4c. For t>=1 the derivative magnitude is at most1/2<4c.
The two formulas agree at1, and continuity handles0.

Consider an arbitrary actual own underreport and the same outer restoration
as in the dual note. If its restored payment is at most the raw full payment,
there is nothing to prove. Otherwise t<S, its remaining positive gap is
r=g_i-x>0, and0<=x<=g_i, where x is its own capped account loss. Because
w_i=0, x is also its actual own local loss after the outer grid restoration.
The sharp nonlinear outgoing bound gives
 S-t<=x+K sqrt(z_i x).
Using r<=g_i and the Lipschitz bound, with eta=4c g_i, gives
 restored payment-N_i
 <=[k(t)-q0]r-q0x
 <=eta(S-t)-q0x
 <=eta K sqrt(z_i x)-(q0-eta)x.
SinceS<=2, q0>=c. Sinceg_i<=1/8, eta<=c/2<=q0/2. Completing the square
therefore gives
 E_i-N_i<=K^2 eta^2 z_i/[4(q0-eta)]
 <=8K^2 c^2 z_i g_i^2/q0<=8K^2 c z_i g_i^2.
All inequalities hold before taking the actual own-report supremum. Full
truthful reporting belongs to that domain, so E_i>=N_i. This proves(SP1).

2. A UNIFORM NEGATIVE-RECIPIENT GAIN BOUND
Let J be the full negative-gap recipients and write
 R=sum_J(a_i-d_i), Z_-=sum_J z_i, delta=CM-Z.
Each negative recipient is uncapped, and hence Z_-+R<=1. The corrected
negative-gap theorem implies
 sum_J(E_i-N_i)
 <=q0 R[1-exp(-delta)L(K^2 Z_-R)]
 <=(1/2)R[1-exp(-c)L(K^2 R(1-R))],                    (SP3)
where L(v)=2exp(sqrt(1-v)-1)/(1+sqrt(1-v)). Here delta<=c because
Z>=M and M<=1. The second step uses q0<=1/2 and decreasing L.

We now prove the right side of(SP3) is less than13/100 using elementary
exact inequalities. The exponential series gives
 1957/720<e<1957/720+1/4410<30/11,
and1957/720>125/46. Consequently
 c<23/125, c>11/60>1/6, K<7/4, and K^2<3.
For0<=v<=3/4, differentiating L gives
 -L'(v)=L(v)/[2(1+sqrt(1-v))]<=1/3,
sinceL<=1 and sqrt(1-v)>=1/2. Thus L(v)>=1-v/3.
Also the signed cubic Taylor bound gives
 exp(-c)>1-23/125+(23/125)^2/2-(23/125)^3/6>83/100.
Finally exp(c)>1+c>7/6, so exp(-c)<6/7<7/8. Therefore
 R[1-exp(-c)L(K^2 R(1-R))]
 <=(17/100)R+(7/8)R^2(1-R).                          (SP4)

For0<=R<=1/2, the right side is at most17/200+7/64<13/50.
For1/2<=R<=1 set s=R-3/4. Its exact expansion is
 3207/12800+(19/3200)s-(35/32)s^2-(7/8)s^3.
Since s>=-1/4, this is at most
 3207/12800+(19/3200)s-(7/8)s^2
 <=3207/12800+(19/3200)^2/[4(7/8)]
 =8979961/35840000<13/50.
Combining(SP3)--(SP4) proves
 sum_J(E_i-N_i)<13/100.                              (SP5)
The casesR=0 orZ_-=0 are included by continuity. The corrected negative
theorem permits arbitrary overflow at other recipients, so(SP5) does too.

3. FUNDING CONSEQUENCE
The full raw outer total is1+q0(S-1)<=C. Tied recipients have E_i<=a_i=N_i.
If every positive recipient satisfies the theorem's hypotheses, (SP1) gives
 sum_positive(E_i-N_i)
 <=8K^2 c sum_positive z_i g_i^2
 <=K^2 c Z/8<=K^2 c C/8.
Using(SP5) and the exact bounds from section2,
 sum E_i <148/125+(49/16)(23/125)(148/125)/8+13/100
 =698699/500000<699/500.
On the other hand c>11/60 gives C^2>(71/60)^2=5041/3600>699/500.
This proves(SP2). Restoring scale multiplies all payments and the bound by H.

4. WHAT THIS LEAVES OPEN
For arbitrary no-overflow profiles in1<=S<2, (SP1) still handles every
recipient with positive gap at most1/8, even when other gaps are larger.
Their TOTAL extra payment is controlled by the weighted quadratic quantity
sum z_i g_i^2, rather than by their count. Since sum_positive g_i<=S<2,
there are at most15 recipients with gap greater than1/8. This is a structural
reduction of the difficult response contribution, not a finite-family proof
for those exceptional recipients. An overflowing positive recipient also
requires separate handling; S<2 allows at most one such full capped owner.
The global mixed-owner C^2 funding problem remains open.
