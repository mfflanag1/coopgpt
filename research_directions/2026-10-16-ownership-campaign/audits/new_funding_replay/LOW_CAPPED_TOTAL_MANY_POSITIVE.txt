MANY POSITIVE GAPS AT LOW CAPPED TOTAL
16 October 2026. Candidate theorem; original exponential nested envelope.

THEOREM
Normalize full hardware H=1 and assume the physical hardware bottleneck.
Let S be the full capped opposing total and let
 R=sum_i(H_i-min(p_i+z_i,1))_+.
If1<=S<=3/2 and R<=1/2, then the actual whole-owner envelope satisfies
 sum_i E_i<7/5<C^2, C=1+1/(2e).                       (LC1)
Arbitrary full overflow, mixed owners, real reports, and unbounded actual owner
and branch counts are allowed. Deviations need not satisfy the full-profile
conditions. In particular R<=1/2 is automatic when M=H, so every full-utilization
profile with S/H<=3/2 satisfies(LC1), regardless of the number of positive gaps.

PROOF
Let c=1/(2e), g_i=d_i-a_i for each positive recipient, and G=sum_positive g_i.
Then G=S-1+R. The fixed-opponent complement-surplus lemma gives
 Z-z_i-M_-i<=M/2<=1/2.
Since S-d_i<=P_-i+Z-z_i, coalition coverage under every actual i-underreport
gives its other CAPPED opposing accounts a floor at least
 min{1,(S-d_i-1/2)_+}.
Put T=S-1/2 in[1/2,1]. Crucially this floor implies that after algebraically
restoring the recipient's own account to d_i, total opposing mass is at least
 max(d_i,T): if T>d_i then T-d_i<=1, so the displayed min does not truncate it.
The positive-gap envelope consequence of the coalition-floor theorem therefore
gives
 E_i-a_i<=g_i k(max(d_i,T))<=g_i k(T).                 (LC2)
This argument retains the cap. It does not assert the same implication when
T>1; the companion cap-truncation obstruction explains that distinction.

The corrected negative-gap theorem gives total negative saving at least
 q0 exp(-delta)L(K^2/4)R,
where q0=(1/2)exp(1-S), delta=CM-Z<=c and K=e-1.
The elementary e<30/11 implies K^2<3. Hence for v<=K^2/4, sqrt(1-v)>1/2,
so decreasing L satisfies
 L(v)>=L(3/4)=4/(3sqrt(e))>4/5,
using e<25/9. Thus negative saving is at least
 (2/5)exp(1-S-c)R.                                   (LC3)
Tied recipients have E_i<=a_i. Summing(LC2)--(LC3),
 sum E_i<=1+k(T)(S-1+R)-(2/5)exp(1-S-c)R.
The coefficient of R is positive, because k(T)>=1/2 and the subtracted
coefficient is less than2/5. Maximizing over0<=R<=1/2 yields
 sum E_i<=1+T k(T)-(1/5)exp(1/2-T-c).                 (LC4)

The right side increases with T on[1/2,1]. Indeed
 [T k(T)]'=1-(1/2)exp(1-1/T)(1+1/T)>=0,
because (1+y)exp(1-y)<=2 for y=1/T>=1; the derivative of the negative
exponential term in(LC4) is strictly positive. The maximum therefore occurs
at T=1 and is
 3/2-(1/5)exp(-1/2-c)<7/5.
For the last inequality it suffices that exp(1/2+c)<2. Since c<3/16,
exp(1/2+c)<exp(11/16)<2; the final strict comparison follows from the
finite positive Taylor sum through degree6 with geometric tail upper bound,
as recorded by verify_nonlinear_funding_exact.py. Also c>11/60 gives
C^2>5041/3600>7/5. This proves(LC1).

WHY FULL UTILIZATION IS SUFFICIENT
If M=H=1, every positive-output branch is hardware-short or tied, and each
owner's hardware-role local payment is at least half its hardware in that
branch. Equivalently the subset hardware-role lemma gives Z_- >=R. More
directly, the total local-power role L satisfies L<=1/2, Z>=1, and
 R<=sum_negative(a_i-U_i)<=1-(Z-L)<=1/2.
Here every hardware-role payment U_i<=a_i, so enlarging the sum from negative
recipients to all recipients is valid. This proves the stated sufficient
condition without restricting ownership or the number of positive gaps.

SCOPE
The high-S full-utilization case is NOT settled by this note. Removing the
min in the fixed-opponent floor in that region is invalid, as shown by the
actual fixed-owner family in COMPLEMENT_CAP_TRUNCATION_OBSTRUCTION.txt.
