CLOSED-FORM MAXIMIZATION
For n>=2, the best own account at fixed short-pool total T is
c(T)=min(beta,T-Pminus), because its coefficient1-q(T)>0. Every T in[P,U]
is feasible; n>=2 allows other accounts to absorb the residual. Up to
T0=beta+Pminus only the own opposing account increases, so primitive monotonicity
applies. Beyond T0 the own account is fixed, q(T) increases, and the sign of
min(a,T)-beta yields exactly the two stated Tstar cases. Both stars lie in
the feasible interval; equality beta=a gives the same optimum.

In the long-pool positive-gap region, the smallest capped total at own c is
max(H,min(c,H)+Sminus). If a raw total>=H is already present, its cap sum is
also>=H. Otherwise every baseline account is below H, and adding just the
missing total reaches H within budget because K>=H. The positive excess is
nondecreasing in the own cap since its derivative has factor
1-(d-a)rho_min' >=0. Thus maximal own c=beta is optimal.

For strictly negative gap d_c<a, necessarily c<H; the largest attainable capped

The candidate cap is>=H: if Wminus>0 an already capped other account contributes

is nondecreasing, and a-c decreases, so the payment increases with c. The own
maximum beta is therefore optimal unless it crosses into the positive case,
which dominates. At d_c=a, payment is identically a. The n=1 exception is
correctly separated. Precomputed cap sums and sorted hardware prefix sums give
