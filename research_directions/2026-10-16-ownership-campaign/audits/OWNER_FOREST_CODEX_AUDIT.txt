GENERAL HIERARCHY
Nonnegativity uses disjoint superadditivity of the child subtrees together with
the root singleton. Efficiency telescopes in each owner-tree component. For a
connected actual coalition S, its topmost owner r and the first outside child
subtrees partition T_r, giving the displayed coverage inequality. Additivity
over induced components handles arbitrary coalitions. In the own-coordinate
argument every subtracted subtree coalition excludes the reporting owner, so
all negative terms are fixed; the positive term increases. No virtual owner
splitting or counterfactual population change is present.

SHARED-ROW PRODUCTION DECOMPOSITION
The rowwise transportation demands have exactly the available aggregate bounds.
Normalizing by the positive activity input coefficients makes both input-owner
marginals sum to the same activity level. The product coupling for positive
level has these marginals, and the zero-level case is explicitly zero. Each
piece uses either one actual owner or an eligible owner pair connected in the
fixed graph. Grouping by actual coalition components respects every owner's
row budgets even when different owners share a physical row. Conversely their
plans add because capacities add by actual owner. Thus the decomposition is
valid for all real reports, not just the unit bipartite test cases.

LIFT AND FOREST COVER
The lifted rows are accounting constraints owned by the same actual owners;
they do not add agents. Both directions of the exact coalition-value equality
are correct. Retaining variants by their definite owner pair gives a subgraph
of the selected forest. A forest supergraph with unused edges remains harmless
for induced-component additivity. Same-owner and one-input variants are kept
in every forest, and Lambda>=1 follows when G has an edge; the explicit empty-
graph convention handles the other case. Coverage by restricting a fixed
optimal plan, and the funding bound by restricted value<=full value, are valid.

EDMONDS REPLICATION ARGUMENT
If a_f=p/q is reduced, a maximizing induced vertex set gives p<=|E| and q<=n-1.
For a copy subset A' with original support A, |A'|<=q|A|. Decomposing the graph
(N,A) into its nontrivial components proves |A|<=a_f rank(A), and parallel
copies preserve that rank. Thus |A'|<=p rank(A'), exactly the needed matroid
partition hypothesis. Each of p independent copy sets contains at most one
copy of an original edge, so projection preserves its forest property and
each original edge appears in exactly q projected forests. Assigning weight
1/q yields total p/q with exact edge coverage1. Empty projected forests are
harmless; retaining them does not invalidate the upper bound. The construction
size and the stated rational LP-oracle scope are correct. Source verification
and historical attribution are being handled by root; this audit checks the
application of the stated standard matroid theorem, not its original-source
transcription independently.

SPARSIFICATION NOTE
The pair-only capacity profile proves the stated H=G result because all
activities have exactly two distinct genuine inputs and positive coefficients.
Deleting every variant of a productive pair leaves no activity using both
nonzero owner-resource rows. Arbitrary same-owner variants cannot rescue that
profile. The proof even applies to grand-value preservation alone. Its limits
for one-input substitutions, linked report domains and deleting only redundant
individual variants are correctly stated. The following separate note adds
