A31/27 MIXED-OWNER TWO-POOL RULE FROM A WATER-FILLING PRODUCT
16 October2026. Candidate conventional proof, frozen for independent review. No historical-priority claim. This is an ALL-REAL-REPORT theorem, not an interpolation of the ternary grid theorem.

MODEL. Fix any finite actual owner set N. Owner i reports arbitrary nonnegative real capacities(a_i,b_i) in two resource pools, within any fixed eligibility restrictions. Reports bind feasible coalition production v(S)=min(sum_S a_i,sum_S b_i). An owner may underreport its entire eligible bundle coordinatewise. Utility is payment, without private costs or extra marginal-payment floors. Identities and eligibility never change. Counts of owners are unrestricted.

THEOREM. The rule below has nonnegative payments, covers every actual coalition at every report profile, is coordinatewise monotone in each actual owner's own report, and has total payment at most(31/27)v(N). It is a direct mixed-owner mechanism; no cloning or generic netting theorem is used. The worst factor of this rule is31/27, attained by one unit short-only owner and two unit long-only owners. That example does not assert optimality among arbitrary mechanisms.

1. DEFINITION
Let A<=B be the two aggregate pool amounts, orienting the smaller pool as short. Write x_i for owner i's short account and z_i for its long account, and put
 A=sum_i x_i, M=max_i x_i, d_i=min(z_i,A), D=sum_i d_i.
If A=0 pay everyone0. Otherwise M>0 and D>=A. Adjoin one accounting cap M, which is NOT a strategic owner or a coalition member. Choose the water level lambda satisfying
 min(M,lambda)+sum_i min(d_i,lambda)=A+M.                  (1)
When D=A choose lambda=max(M,max_i d_i); when D>A equation(1) has a unique solution below that maximum. Define
 p=(1/2)*[min(M,lambda)/M]
          *product_{i:d_i>0}[min(d_i,lambda)/d_i],        (2)
 y_i=(1-p)x_i+p d_i.                                    (3)
Zero caps contribute product factor1. The added cap only determines a scalar coefficient; all payments and all coalition constraints refer to the original actual owners.

Every factor in(2) lies in[0,1], so0<p<=1/2 for A>0. At A=B there is no long cap loss, D=A, p=1/2 and y_i=(a_i+b_i)/2. Thus the formula is independent of the tie orientation. The water-fill coefficient and payments are continuous for A>0, including changes of maximum-owner identity, caps, active sets and the equality D=A. At A->0, y_i<=x_i+d_i/2<=3A/2, so the zero case is continuous too.

2. COALITION COVERAGE
For a coalition S, let X=sum_S x_i and Z=sum_S z_i. Since X<=A,
 sum_{i in S}d_i=sum_S min(z_i,A)>=min(Z,A)>=min(X,Z).
Also X>=min(X,Z). Equation(3), a convex combination of these two dominating quantities, gives sum_S y_i>=min(X,Z). Nonnegativity is immediate.

3. OWN LONG-ACCOUNT MONOTONICITY
Fix A, the short vector and all other long accounts. Increasing owner i's long report above A changes no d_i or payment. Before that cap, d_i=z_i varies.
Work inside a cell with unchanged cap and water-fill active sets. If d_i>lambda, its capped-in-water contribution is lambda, so the water level is independent of d_i, and
 (partial p/partial d_i)/p=-1/d_i.
If d_i<lambda, its contribution to(1) is d_i. Let r be the number of caps strictly above lambda, counting the added cap if it is active. Then lambda'=-1/r, and the r active product factors give
 (partial p/partial d_i)/p=-1/lambda.
Therefore the derivative of the SAME actual owner's payment is
 p+(d_i-x_i)p'.
In the first case this equals p*x_i/d_i>=0. In the second it equals p[1-d_i/lambda+x_i/lambda]>=0 because d_i<lambda. This calculation includes the owner's unchanged short account and does not discard it.

4. OWN SHORT-ACCOUNT MONOTONICITY
It suffices to check derivatives on cells and then use continuity. By homogeneity normalize the current short total A to1; let s=M, so0<s<=1, and write x=x_i and d=d_i for the changing owner's two accounts at that profile. Increasing x_i increases A at rate1. Let epsilon=1 if i is the current strict maximum short owner and epsilon=0 otherwise, so M changes at rate epsilon; ties are handled by the adjacent cells. Let c be the number of long reports strictly above A. Each of those long caps d_j=A changes at rate1. Other long accounts stay fixed. The owner's own d has derivative d' in{0,1} accordingly.

Let k be the number of long caps strictly above lambda, and u the sum of long caps strictly below lambda. At D>A one has lambda<A=1. Thus every d_j=A is in the water-active set and c<=k. Put beta=(partial p/partial x_i)/p.

(a) Added cap inactive: lambda>=s. The water equation is k*lambda+u=1, and differentiation gives
 beta=1/lambda-c>=0,
because c<=k<=1/lambda. Also x<=s<=lambda and p<=1/2. The payment derivative is
 y_i'=1-p+p*d'+p*beta*(d-x)
      >=1-p-p*beta*x
      >=1-p(1+x/lambda)>=0.                            (4)

(b) Added cap active: lambda<s. The water equation is
 (k+1)lambda+u=1+s,
and differentiating(2) gives the exact logarithmic derivative
 beta=(1+epsilon)/lambda-epsilon/s-c.                  (5)
Furthermore p<=lambda/(2s).
If beta>=0 and epsilon=0, the same lower bound as(4) is nonnegative because
 p(1+x/lambda)<= (lambda+x)/(2s)<=1.
If beta>=0 and epsilon=1, then x=s and
 p(1+beta*x)<=p*(2s/lambda)<=1,
where subtracting the c term only improves the inequality.

If beta<0, then epsilon>=0 and lambda<s imply
 beta>=1/lambda-c>=1-s/lambda,
using c<=k<=(1+s-lambda)/lambda. Hence -beta<=s/lambda-1. If d<=x, the beta term in the payment derivative is nonnegative. If d>x, use d-x<=1 and d'>=0 to obtain
 y_i'>=1-p-p*(-beta)*(d-x)
       >=1-p[1+(s/lambda-1)]
       =1-p*s/lambda>=1/2.                             (6)
This handles the adverse sign of the coefficient: p need NOT increase with a short account. The actual owner's complete payment still does.

All short-account derivatives are nonnegative. Along a single coordinate change, maxima, caps and water-fill inequalities define finitely many linear cells; the payments are continuous across their boundaries. Swapping which aggregate is short at A=B is also continuous and both orientations have the proved one-sided monotonicity. Thus all actual-owner coordinate increases are globally nondecreasing, and sequential coordinate changes prove every whole-bundle own-report comparison.

5. FUNDING BOUND
Total payment is A+p(D-A). Normalize A=1 and keep s=M in(0,1]. It remains to prove p(D-1)<=4/27.

Fix s,lambda and the inactive long caps, and vary a water-active long cap d_j over[lambda,1]. Its contribution to the water equation stays lambda, so feasibility and the water level are unchanged. Up to a positive factor independent of d_j, the excess p(D-1) is of the form
 (d_j+R)/d_j=1+R/d_j.
This is monotone or constant in d_j, so its maximum on that interval is at an endpoint. Repeating this operation reduces to active long caps all equal1; caps reaching lambda are absorbed into the inactive sum. This reduction preserves D>=1 because the water equation itself implies sum min(d_j,lambda)>=1. It never changes the short vector or s.

Let k>=0 be the remaining number of active caps equal1. All formulae below also cover boundary coincidences by continuity.

(a) Added cap inactive, lambda>=s. Here k*lambda+u=1. For k0, p(D-1)=0. For k>=1,
 p(D-1)=(k/2)*lambda^k*(1-lambda),  0<=lambda<=1/k.
For k1 this is at most1/8. For k>=2 it increases on[0,1/k], giving (k-1)/(2*k^k)<=1/8. One elementary bound is k^k>=2^k>=4(k-1) for k>=2.

(b) Added cap active, lambda<s. Here (k+1)lambda+u=1+s, so
 p(D-1)=lambda^(k+1)[k+s-(k+1)lambda]/(2s),
 0<=lambda<=min(s,(1+s)/(k+1)).                         (7)
For k0 this is lambda(s-lambda)/(2s)<=s/8<=1/8.
For k1 the unconstrained maximizing lambda is(1+s)/3. If s<=1/2 the cap lambda<=s gives a bound s(1-s)/2<=1/8. If s>=1/2, substitution gives
 (1+s)^3/(54s)<=4/27;
the left side increases on[1/2,1].
For k>=2, the unconstrained maximum occurs at lambda=(k+s)/(k+2), which is at least(1+s)/(k+1), since k^2-2>=s. Thus(7) increases up to its feasible upper endpoint.
If s<=1/k, that endpoint is lambda=s, giving
 (k/2)s^k(1-s)<= (k-1)/(2*k^k)<=1/8.
If s>=1/k, the endpoint is lambda=(1+s)/(k+1), giving
 (k-1)(1+s)^(k+1)/[2s(k+1)^(k+1)].
This increases with s on[1/k,1], since its logarithmic derivative has sign ks-1. At s1 its value is
 (k-1)2^k/(k+1)^(k+1).
For k2 this equals4/27. For k>=3 it is at most(2/(k+1))^k<=1/8<4/27.
This completes all cases and proves total funding<=31A/27.

6. ATTAINMENT, EVALUATION AND LIMITS
At the profile consisting of one short-only owner of amount1 and two long-only owners each of amount1, A=M1,D2 and equation(1) gives lambda2/3. Therefore p=(1/2)(2/3)^3=4/27 and total funding is31/27. Adding arbitrary fixed zero-report owners leaves the formula unchanged, but the theorem already allows arbitrary active populations and bundles.

For rational reports, water filling can be computed exactly by sorting the positive caps M,d_i and solving a linear equation within the corresponding interval. Lambda, p and every payment are rational. This avoids transcendental evaluation. Sorting takes O(n log n) comparisons and the product takes O(n) arithmetic operations; a bit-complexity claim would also account for rational input/output lengths.

The primitive need not be1-Lipschitz in each pure owner's own report, so a generic netting extension is not invoked. Section4 proves the mixed-owner mechanism directly and keeps the own opposite account and moving cap term. The result does not add private-cost truthfulness, coalition strategyproofness, false-name resistance, allocation utilities or grand-marginal fairness floors. It improves the preceding1+1/(2e) TWO-POOL payment bound if the proof survives independent review. Historical novelty remains unconfirmed.

DEPENDENCY WARNING. This rule does not automatically replace the exponential primitive inside the proved7/4 depth-two construction. That construction uses extra componentwise role bounds, including total local-power payment<=H/2, which need not hold for this water-fill rule. The existing7/4 theorem and its primitive remain separate. A positive report-independent sum of two-pool games can instead compose this rule directly: if component resource amounts are nonnegative linear functions of each fixed actual owner's original report and the coalition value is exactly the nonnegative weighted sum of component minima, sum the corresponding payments. Coverage, whole-owner monotonicity and the31/27 budget transfer term by term. This is a routine composition corollary, not a new decomposition claim.
