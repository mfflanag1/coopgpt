THE EXACT CONCENTRATION ENVELOPE OF THE WATER-FILL RULE
16 October 2026. Corollary of the frozen WATERFILL_PRODUCT_THEOREM.txt.
This sharpens its profilewise funding analysis; it does not change the rule.

THEOREM
Fix any actual-owner set and any nonnegative real mixed-owner report profile.
Let A be the smaller total pool amount. If A=0, all payments are zero. If A>0,
let M be the largest actual owner's account in that short pool and put s=M/A.
The existing globally truthful, coalition-covering water-fill rule satisfies
  sum_i y_i <= A[1+g(s)],
where
  g(s)=1/8                         for 0<s<=1/2,
  g(s)=(1+s)^3/(54s)               for 1/2<=s<=1.             (1)
This is the exact worst-case bound at every concentration s: for every real
s in (0,1] there is a finite fixed actual-owner profile attaining equality.
Counts are unrestricted, and owners may own both resources. The upper bound
also applies under any fixed eligibility restrictions; attainment need not be
available within every particular restricted ownership domain or owner count.

Consequently a funding ratio above9/8 can occur only if a single actual owner
holds strictly more than half of the short resource. Quantitatively,
  sum_i y_i <= 9A/8
     + A * ((2s-1)_+)^2 (s+4)/(216s).                     (2)
The concentration penalty is quadratic near s=1/2. This conclusion is about
the same globally incentive-compatible rule on all reports: no incentive proof
is restricted to profiles satisfying a concentration condition.

PROOF OF THE UPPER BOUND
Normalize A=1 and write s=M. The endpoint reduction in the frozen proof keeps
s and the water level lambda fixed and moves each water-active long cap to
lambda or1. The excess p(D-1) is fractional-linear in the cap being moved,
so this cannot decrease its maximum. Absorb caps equal to lambda into the
inactive sum u. It suffices to bound the following expressions, where k is
the number of remaining water-active long caps equal1.

I. Dummy cap inactive, lambda>=s.
The water equation is k lambda+u=1. For k=0 the excess is zero. For k>=1,
  E_k=(k/2)lambda^k(1-lambda),  s<=lambda<=1/k.
For k=1 the unrestricted maximum is1/8 at lambda=1/2; imposing lambda>=s
can only decrease it. For k>=2 the expression increases up to lambda=1/k,
giving (k-1)/(2k^k)<=1/8. Thus all dummy-inactive cases are bounded by1/8.

II. Dummy cap active, lambda<s.
Here (k+1)lambda+u=1+s and
  E_k=lambda^(k+1)[k+s-(k+1)lambda]/(2s),
  0<=lambda<=min(s,(1+s)/(k+1)).                          (3)
For k=0 its maximum is s/8<=1/8.
For k=1 the critical point is lambda=(1+s)/3. If s<=1/2 the feasible maximum
is at lambda=s and equals s(1-s)/2<=1/8. If s>=1/2 the critical point is
feasible and gives (1+s)^3/(54s).
For k=2 the expression increases to its feasible upper endpoint. For s<=1/2
that endpoint is lambda=s, giving s^2(1-s)<=1/8. For s>=1/2 the endpoint is
lambda=(1+s)/3, giving EXACTLY the same value (1+s)^3/(54s).
For k>=3, the maximum is at lambda=min(s,(1+s)/(k+1)): the unconstrained
critical point (k+s)/(k+2) is no smaller than (1+s)/(k+1), since k^2-2>=s.
If s<=1/k this gives (k/2)s^k(1-s)<=(k-1)/(2k^k)<=1/8.
If s>=1/k it gives
  (k-1)(1+s)^(k+1)/[2s(k+1)^(k+1)].
This expression increases with s on [1/k,1]; at s=1 it is
  (k-1)2^k/(k+1)^(k+1) <= (2/(k+1))^k <=1/8.
Thus k>=3 never exceeds1/8. The function (1+s)^3/(54s) is1/8 at s=1/2
and has derivative (1+s)^2(2s-1)/(54s^2)>=0 for s>=1/2. Equations (1)
and (2) follow, using the exact factorization
  (1+s)^3/(54s)-1/8 = (2s-1)^2(s+4)/(216s).

SHARPNESS FOR EVERY REAL s
Choose finitely many short accounts with sum1 and maximum s. Explicitly take
ceil(1/s)-1 accounts of size s and one account of size1-(ceil(1/s)-1)s.
Supply two long accounts of size1 each and all remaining long accounts zero.
These may belong to two of the same actual owners; if necessary include one
additional fixed owner with short account zero. Alternatively use separate
pure-role actual owners. Each is a single predetermined ownership domain and
report profile; there is no strategic cloning or merging operation.
Then D=2 and the water equation is
  min(s,lambda)+2min(1,lambda)=1+s.
For s<=1/2 its solution is lambda=1/2, the dummy is inactive, and p=1/8.
For s>=1/2 its solution is lambda=(1+s)/3, the dummy is active (with equality
at1/2), and p=lambda^3/(2s)=(1+s)^3/(54s). Because D-1=1, the exact excess
is p. This attains (1). Scaling all accounts by any A>0 preserves the ratio.

FURTHER PRECISE CONSEQUENCES
The ratio g(s) is constant on (0,1/2] and strictly increases on (1/2,1].
If s<=1/2+epsilon for 0<=epsilon<=1/2, then
  sum_i y_i/A <=9/8+epsilon^2(9+2epsilon)/[54(1+2epsilon)]
             <=9/8+epsilon^2/6.
The lower population requirement for attaining very small s is automatic:
at least ceil(1/s) positive short accounts are required. Both owner and type
counts remain unrestricted. No inference is made about optimality among all
mechanisms on a restricted concentration domain or historical priority.
