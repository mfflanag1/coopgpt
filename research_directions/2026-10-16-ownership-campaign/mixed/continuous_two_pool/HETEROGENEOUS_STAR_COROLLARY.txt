HETEROGENEOUS HARDWARE AND ONE ADDITIVE POWER POOL: EXACT 31/27 RULE
16 October 2026. Corollary of WATERFILL_PRODUCT_THEOREM.txt. The fractional-
knapsack identity and positive-sum composition are established elementary
ingredients; no historical novelty is claimed for those ingredients.

MODEL
Fix finitely many actual owners i=1,...,n and hardware types j=1,...,m, with
no bound on n or m. Public activity values v_j>0 and power intensities e_j>0
are fixed across all reports. Owner i reports hardware g_ij>=0 and power q_i>=0
in predetermined eligible coordinates. Reports are binding componentwise
underreports. Owners may hold arbitrary mixed hardware and power bundles.
For a coalition S, let g_j(S)=sum_{i in S}g_ij and q(S)=sum_{i in S}q_i. Its
production value is
  F(S)=max sum_j v_j x_j,
       0<=x_j<=g_j(S),  sum_j e_j x_j<=q(S).
Payoffs are the mechanism's nonnegative payments. There are no private
opportunity costs, energy costs, utilities from withheld resources, or request-
level latency claims in this model.

THEOREM
There is an all-profile truthful, coalition-covering rule with
  sum_i y_i <= (31/27) F(N).
It applies to all nonnegative real reports and arbitrary fixed mixed owners.
For rational reports and rational public v_j,e_j, its payments can be computed
exactly as rationals in time polynomial in the full binary input length.

PROOF: FIXED POSITIVE DECOMPOSITION
Set a_j=v_j/e_j and reorder types once by a_1>=...>=a_m>0; a_{m+1}=0.
Ties use any fixed order independent of reports. Write delta_k=a_k-a_{k+1}>=0.
Changing variables u_j=e_j x_j gives a fractional-knapsack problem. An optimal
greedy solution fills the higher a_j first, so its cumulative allocated power
through k is min(q(S),sum_{j<=k}e_j g_j(S)). Summation by parts yields
  F(S)=sum_{k=1}^m delta_k min(q(S),sum_{j<=k}e_j g_j(S)).             (1)
The statement remains valid with ties, zero capacities and unused power.

For each k retain EXACTLY the same actual-owner set. Give actual owner i the
two accounting inputs
  h_ik=sum_{j<=k}e_j g_ij,  q_i.
Apply the water-fill product mechanism to these two accounts and denote its
actual-owner payment y_ik. Define y_i=sum_k delta_k y_ik; omit zero weights.
There are no strategic clones and no identity changes between components.
Every owner's hardware coordinate increase weakly increases its own h_ik in
the appropriate components and leaves all opponents' inputs fixed. Its power
increase raises its own second input in every component. The component theorem
therefore proves own-coordinate monotonicity of y_i. Sequential increases
prove monotonicity under arbitrary simultaneous whole-bundle increases.
Every coalition is covered componentwise, and equation (1) gives
  sum_{i in S}y_i >=sum_k delta_k min(q(S),sum_{j<=k}e_j g_j(S))=F(S).
Likewise sum_i y_i<=sum_k delta_k(31/27)F_k(N)=(31/27)F(N).

EXACT EVALUATION AND BIT COMPLEXITY
The component rule contains no exponentials or irrational operations. For a
nonzero component, sort its n long-account caps together with the single dummy
cap M. On each resulting interval its water equation is linear. Its level is
  lambda=(A+M-sum of caps already saturated)/(number of unsaturated caps).
The exact rule uses that rational level, at most n+1 rational factors for its
coefficient, and rational additions/multiplications for the n payments.
All-zero components return zero; the D=A boundary uses the rational maximal
cap and coefficient 1/2. Sorting and comparisons can use integer cross-products.

Let L be the binary input length of the explicit lists of all public numbers
and owner reports. Then n,m<=L. The transformed inputs e_j g_ij, prefix sums,
public ratios and differences have polynomial bit length: a common denominator
can be the product of the input denominators, whose bit lengths add, and the
numerator gains at most polynomially many bits from sums and products.
Every cap and water level is a rational expression involving one such prefix
sum, sums of at most n caps, and division by an integer at most n+1. Each has
polynomial bit length. The product defining a coefficient contains at most
n+1 such rational ratios, so its numerator and denominator bit lengths again
add and remain polynomial. Each final payment is a sum of at most m weighted
component payments; using the product of their denominators still gives only
a polynomial bit bound. This argument bounds unreduced fractions as well, so
exact fraction reduction cannot invalidate it.

Sorting activities requires O(m log m) rational comparisons, prefix accounts
require O(mn) arithmetic operations, and the component computations require
O(mn log n) arithmetic operations and comparisons in total (with the usual
constant-size interpretation when n=1). All operands have polynomial bit length;
standard integer arithmetic therefore yields a polynomial-time exact algorithm.

INTERPRETATION AND LIMITS
Hardware types may have different public work values and energy intensities.
The common power entitlement is additive across actual owners. This gives a
precise divisible production abstraction for heterogeneous compute and power,
not a claim that measured AI-serving workloads satisfy that abstraction.
The old nested shared/local-power theorem must retain its original exponential
primitive: its separate role inequalities are not consequences of this corollary.
