GROUPED LOCAL ENERGY WITH UNIVERSALLY USABLE SHARED POWER
16 October 2026. Exact model equivalence and theorem corollary; no claim that aggregation creates a harder graph class or independently establishes novelty.

PUBLIC TECHNOLOGY. Partition hardware resource vertices into fixed groups H_g. For each group g, a set L_g of local-energy resource vertices can serve every hardware vertex in H_g and none outside it. A set U of shared-energy resource vertices can serve every hardware vertex in every group. There are no other activities. In the unit model, each activity uses one unit of its hardware resource and one unit of its energy resource and produces one unit of value. All capacity reports are nonnegative reals.

There is one predetermined finite set N of actual owners. Owner i may simultaneously own arbitrary amounts of any hardware, local-energy and shared-energy resources. Reports only reduce that owner's capacities coordinatewise and bind the capacity available to the production plan. Payment is the modeled utility; unreported external uses or privately changing opportunity costs are outside this statement. Both |N| and the numbers of groups/resources may grow without bound.

EXACT OWNER-PRESERVING AGGREGATION. For each actual owner i define
 h_ig=sum_{h in H_g} c_ih,
 l_ig=sum_{p in L_g} c_ip,
 p_i=sum_{p in U} c_ip.
These are fixed positive linear transformations of the owner's report. Actual owners are never split, removed, duplicated or reassigned. For a coalition S write H_g(S)=sum_{i in S}h_ig, L_g(S)=sum_{i in S}l_ig and P(S)=sum_{i in S}p_i. The production value is exactly
 F(S)=min{ H(S), P(S)+sum_g min(H_g(S),L_g(S)) },
 H(S)=sum_g H_g(S).

For the upper bound, local energy can process at most min(H_g,L_g) in group g, shared energy processes at most P, and total hardware processes at most H. For attainment, first process min(H_g,L_g) locally in every group, then allocate up to P shared energy to the remaining hardware. Complete bipartite incidence within each eligible block implements each aggregate flow using the original capacities. This argument holds for every coalition and every real report profile.

Consequently the depth-two range mechanism R, its exact-local-ceiling refinement Rbar, or its counterfactual-floor refinement Rfloor gives an all-profile, whole-owner truthful coalition-covering rule with total payments at most7F/4. The proof and explicit optimizer are the same as in the depth-two theorem. This includes many incidence graphs with cycles: for example two energy resources linked to several hardware resources form K_{2,t}. These cycles are harmless because identical eligibility permits exact aggregation; this is not a solution for general cyclic production networks.

PUBLIC HARDWARE VALUES AND ENERGY ACCOUNTING PRICES. Let a hardware vertex h have a fixed public value v_h per completed unit and let an energy vertex p have a fixed public accounting cost c_p per energy unit. Let each hardware unit h consume a fixed public amount e_h>0 of energy. Activity hp consumes one hardware unit and e_h energy units, with public net objective coefficient v_h-e_h c_p. Assume this coefficient is strictly positive on every allowed activity. The values and costs may differ across vertices; they are not strategic reports.

Set y_hp=e_h x_hp. The hardware capacity becomes e_h c_h, energy capacity remains c_p^{cap}, and both endpoint coefficients become one. The normalized net profit is
 w_hp=v_h/e_h-c_p>0.
This row/activity scaling is valid on the cyclic grouped graph because the intensity depends only on hardware h. It is not the arbitrary-endpoint-coefficient normalization available for trees; arbitrary cyclic coefficients need not admit such a scaling. Throughout this paragraph c_p denotes a public accounting price, whereas c_p^{cap} denotes an energy capacity.

The normalized profits have vertex potentials r_h=-v_h/e_h and r_p=-c_p, so w_hp=r_p-r_h. The positive-sum threshold identity from

applies to every bipartite graph with these potentials, not just forests:
 F_w(c)=sum_t Delta_t F_{G_t}(c),
where Delta_t are fixed positive lengths between successive distinct potentials and
 G_t={hp: r_h<theta_t<r_p}.
The cited note proves the identity by an improving residual augmenting path, for arbitrary real capacities, without strategic-owner splitting.

In the present graph each threshold retains hardware h precisely when v_h/e_h>-theta_t and energy p precisely when c_p<-theta_t. It therefore retains a subset of each hardware group, a subset of its local energy and a subset of universal shared energy. All allowed edges between these surviving resources remain present. Thus each threshold graph has exactly the same grouped structure and the unit-value aggregation above applies. Compose any of the7/4 mechanisms with the fixed positive weights Delta_t. Coalition coverage, whole-owner truthfulness and the same7/4 funding factor follow on every real report profile.

FIXED-OWNER CAVEAT. Every component and threshold mechanism uses the same full actual set N, including zero-resource owners at that threshold. The range mechanism is not asserted to be invariant to adding dummy identities. No false-name or owner-merger robustness is claimed.

ECONOMIC READING AND LIMIT. A group can represent a campus whose local power is usable by any server there; U can represent transferable shared energy or an aggregate capacity entitlement genuinely usable across all campuses under the model. The theorem supports public hardware-specific productivity and fixed energy intensity, including heterogeneous public energy accounting prices. Shared power needs a real operational interpretation: capacities must be simultaneously usable within the production interval and the model's incidence must match that permission. This is a static divisible-production theorem. It does not establish request-level scheduling, latency feasibility, power-flow feasibility, measured energy savings, truthful private opportunity costs or net utility with unreported outside options. Those require separate model/data work.
