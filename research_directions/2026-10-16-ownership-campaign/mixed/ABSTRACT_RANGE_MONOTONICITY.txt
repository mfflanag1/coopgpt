ABSTRACT RANGE-RELAXATION LEMMA: TRUTHFULNESS IS SEPARATE FROM BUDGET
16 October2026. Conventional proof candidate; historical priority unassessed.

DATA. Fix actual owners and their coordinatewise partially ordered report domains. For each owner i, let a_i(b_i)>=0 and p_i(b_i)>=0 depend only on that owner's report and be coordinatewise nondecreasing. Let z_i(b)>=0 be own-report nondecreasing (it may depend arbitrarily on others). Let L(b)>=0 be coordinatewise nondecreasing in EVERY owner's report and satisfy sum_i z_i(b)<=L(b). Let Phi_i(a,c) be any continuous nonnegative payment primitive, coordinatewise nondecreasing in owner i's own accounts(a_i,c_i), with the other accounts held fixed.

Define the auxiliary feasible set for owner i
 U_i(b)={u>=0: u_i<=z_i(b), sum_j u_j<=L(b)},
and the rule
 R_i(b)=max_{u in U_i(b)} Phi_i(a(b),p(b)+u).
The feasible set is nonempty and compact, so the maximum exists. Then R_i is own-report nondecreasing. Moreover R_i(b)>=Phi_i(a(b),p(b)+z(b)).

PROOF. Under an increase of owner i's report, its own z_i and the common ceiling L do not decrease, so U_i only expands. At any previously feasible u, only owner i's coordinates of a and p can change, and both increase. Own monotonicity of Phi implies that i's payment at that u cannot decrease. Maximizing preserves the inequality. The vector u=z(b) is feasible because its sum is at most L, proving domination of the unrelaxed primitive. This proof does not modify ownership or identify u with an actual physical report.

COVERAGE COMPOSITION. If z(S)>=M(b_S) for every coalition and Phi covers the two-input game min(a(S),c(S)), then R covers
 F(b_S)=min(a(S),p(S)+M(b_S)).
Indeed the dominated base rule covers min(a(S),p(S)+z(S)), which is at least F(b_S). This implication requires the stated coalition-value representation; it is not a representation theorem for arbitrary production technologies.

WHAT MONOTONICITY ALONE DOES NOT PROVE. No uniform funding factor follows merely from the lemma. The7/4 result for this campaign additionally uses M<=H=sum a_i, the local factorC=1+1/(2e), and a role decomposition z_i=z_i^h+z_i^l with z_i^h<=a_i and sum z_i^l<=H/2. These are checked for the specific local two-pool construction. They cannot be assumed for a new inner game.

WHY A MONOTONE CEILING MATTERS. Setting L=sum z_i is generally invalid because total local exponential payments can decrease when some resources increase, even though every owner's own payment is monotone. A tighter L may replace CM only after its coordinatewise monotonicity and domination of the total are proved. Merely knowing K>=sum(p+z) and that K is nondecreasing is also insufficient to justify arbitrary floor shifts; the parameterization K=P+L (or an equivalent translation property) makes that step explicit.
