THE CLAIM CHECKED
For fixed actual owner i, any whole-owner coordinatewise underreport, and any
finite but unrestricted number of original exponential branches,
 sum_{k!=i}(z_k-z_k^i)_+ <=(e-1)z_i,
with z_i the FULL own local payment, not its decrement. Both mixed-coordinate
changes and an intervening bottleneck switch are permitted. The theorem is an
outgoing local influence inequality, not itself an outer C^2 budget theorem.

1. SHORT-FIRST PATH
Reducing the owner's original shorter coordinate cannot switch the original
bottleneck. On each cell, q'=q(rho-m)/T>=0 and, for another recipient k,
 z'_k=q'(d_k-s_k)+q 1_{l_k>T}.
Bounding each positive derivative by q'd_k+q 1_{l_k>T}, then summing, gives
 q rho(rho-m)+qm=q[rho^2-m(rho-1)]<=q rho^2<=2/e.
This remains conservative if the cap count includes the deviator, because the
added terms are nonnegative. Integration bounds actual positive losses by
(2/e)s_i <=(4/e)U_i. The owner's opposite-role payment q min(l_i,T) is
nondecreasing with T on each cap cell and continuous, so it weakly decreases
through the short-reduction stage. This justifies V_i^*<=V_i without splitting
actual owners or changing the role labels strategically.

2. LONG PATH BEFORE AND AFTER THE SWITCH
Normalize the surviving original short total to1. With the owner's capped
long amount d, reducing uncapped overflow has no effect. Before a switch,
only q changes for the other recipients, and its multiplier is at most e^d.
Their total positive loss is <=q0(e^d-1)<=(e-1)q0d by the chord inequality on
[0,1]. If a switch occurs, the other actual long reports total v<1, so none
is capped initially and d lies in[1-v,1]. This is the exact parameter domain
needed for the endpoint argument, including the case the original own report
exceeded1.

At the tie, pre-switch loss is <=1/2-q0. After the tie the newly short total
t decreases from1 toward v, while the opposite pool's actual total remains1.
Its capped ratio obeys1<=r<=1/t. The short-stage derivative bound therefore
integrates to the displayed J(v). I independently rederived both integrals:
 J(v)=.5[1-exp(1-1/v)] for v>=.5;
 J(v)=.5+1/(2e)-(2/e)v for v<=.5.
For a partial own reduction, integrating to v rather than the actual endpoint
only enlarges the positive-loss bound. No monotonicity of each recipient's
payment across the switch is assumed.

3. EXACT ENDPOINT INEQUALITIES
Dividing the candidate bound .5-q0+J(v)<=(e-1)q0d by
q0=.5 exp(1-d-v) gives exactly the convex function F_v(d) in the source.
At d=1-v, J's integrand bound2/e suffices because4/e<e-1.
At d=1, the required inequality is .5+J(v)<=.5 exp(1-v).
For v>=.5 it becomes exp(1-v)+exp(1-1/v)>=2; the displayed derivative
ratio and the monotonicity of v-1/v-2log(v) establish it on the entire interval.
For v<=.5, the transformed scalar is exp(2-v)-2e-1+4v. It decreases to a
strictly positive value at.5. The factorization
 exp(3/2)-2e+1=(sqrt(e)-1)(e-sqrt(e)-1)
is exact. The rational bounds e>8/3 and sqrt(e)>13/8 give all signs asserted;
(t^2-t-1) at t=13/8 equals1/64. Convexity in d then proves the whole allowed
rectangle, not just a report grid.

4. ACTUAL-OWNER AND MULTIBRANCH AGGREGATION
The two successive coordinate decreases belong to the same actual owner.
For each other recipient, positive loss along their concatenation is no
larger than the sum of the two stagewise positive losses. Since4/e<e-1 and
V_i^*<=V_i, this yields(e-1)(U_i+V_i). Applying the branchwise inequality and
positive-part subadditivity proves the aggregate theorem. Zero-output full
branches have zero initial payment and cannot contribute any positive loss;
other zero endpoints follow by continuity. Each coordinate path has finitely
many cap and pool boundaries for each finite profile.

5. SHARPNESS
The full reports(1,0),(0,1),(0,1) give payments(1-c,c,c). Deleting the second
owner's local resource gives(.5,0,.5). The other-recipient positive loss is
.5-c=(e-1)c exactly, while the deviator's full payment is c. This confirms
sharpness for the stated outgoing inequality with three fixed pure owners.
It supplies no lower bound against an alternative truthful mechanism.
