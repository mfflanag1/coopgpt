LOCAL-ROLE SHARPENING OF THE SIGNED OTHER-RECIPIENT LOSS THEOREM
17 October 2026. Exact corollary of the reviewed combined theorem.
Original symmetric primitive; fixed actual owners; all hardware held fixed.

For an actual owner's local-only underreport, let U denote the sum of its FULL
LOCAL-role payments on branches where local input was originally short, and
V the sum of its FULL LOCAL-role payments on originally long branches. Then

 [sum_{k!=i}(z_k-z'_k)]_+ <= U + Lambda V,

where Lambda=max_{0<=v<=1}(1-v)(2exp(v)-1). No hardware-role payment is charged.
Either orientation can be chosen at full ties. The constants one and Lambda
are separately exact, by the two examples in the reviewed theorem.

PROOF OF THE ONLY ADDITIONAL POINT
The short theorem already bounds by the original-short local role alone.
On a long branch normalize hardware total to one; use the combined theorem's
notation s,d,v,q. Its full LOCAL role is qd, whether or not it owns hardware.
If d=0 no effective local underreport exists. Otherwise qd>0.
Before a switch, positive signed loss is q(e^u-1)(1-s-v), bounded above by
q(e^u-1)(1-v). Dividing by qd, the same u<=dv argument gives K v(1-v)<1.
After a switch, if other hardware A=1-s<=v the signed loss is nonpositive.
If A>=v, the exact opposing hardware cap inequality yields
 L <=(1-q)(A-v)<=(1-q)(1-v).
Dividing directly by qd gives
 L/(qd)<=(1-v)(2exp(d+v-1)-1)/d<=Lambda.
The latter scalar endpoint argument is unchanged. Sum these role bounds over
the original branches; every role is nonnegative, so taking the final positive
part is valid. The attaining distinguished owner has no hardware, preserving
sharpness of the smaller charge.

SCOPE
This is still a signed, unclipped local-only response bound. It does not by
itself bound positive-part outgoing losses or opposing outer capped totals.
It does not extend to simultaneous hardware withholding without an additional
argument. It makes no novelty or new universal funding-factor claim.
