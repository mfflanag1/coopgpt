INDIVIDUAL LOCAL PAYMENT, OWN HARDWARE, AND OUTER PAYMENT GAP
17 October 2026. Candidate proof for independent review.
Original symmetric exponential local rule and its actual-owner envelope.
No novelty claim. This is not an unrestricted8/5 theorem.

1. INDIVIDUAL COUPLING, ALL FULL PROFILES
All actual owners and their eligible resources remain fixed. Let H be total
hardware, M=sum_j min(H_j,L_j), and z_i the original local payment of owner i,
summed over branches. Put Z=sum_i z_i, c=1/(2e), C=1+c, K=e-1,
delta=CM-Z>=0, and let a_i be owner i's full hardware amount. Then

 z_i <=(1-c)a_i+C(H-M)+cM+K sqrt(c M delta).             (IG1)

This holds for arbitrary mixed owners, reports, positive branch count and owner
count. At H=1, a positive outer gap g_i=d_i-a_i, where
d_i=min(p_i+z_i,1), satisfies g_i<=1-a_i. Consequently

 z_i+(1-c)g_i <=2-M+K sqrt(c M delta).                  (IG2)

The standard z_i<=M remains available separately. In particular, on the local
saturation face H=M=1, delta=0, one has

                   z_i+(1-c)g_i<=1.                   (IG3)

This excludes assigning an arbitrarily large local payment and an arbitrarily
large positive gap to the same owner while keeping the local budget saturated.

2. BRANCH PROOF
On a productive branch let T=min(h,l)>0, rho be its capped long-pool ratio,
q=exp(1-rho)/2, and delta_j=T[c-q(rho-1)]. The previously proved coefficient
stability and power-short scalar estimate are

 (q-c)_+ <=K sqrt(c delta_j/T),
 B(rho):=2-q-C rho <=K sqrt(c delta_j/T).               (IG4)

The first is MARGINAL_FLOOR_STABILITY.txt, equation(4). The second is proved
in sections3-4 of SUBSET_HARDWARE_DEFICIT_COUPLING.txt, including rho=1 and
all cap boundaries. These are scalar facts, not virtual-owner incentive claims.

If hardware is short, h=T, and the owner's payment is
z_ij=(1-q)h_i+q d_i, with 0<=h_i,d_i<=T. The residual is
 z_ij-(1-c)h_i-cT=(c-q)h_i+q d_i-cT.
Maximizing its two linear coordinates separately bounds it by (q-c)_+T.
Thus z_ij<=(1-c)h_i+cT+K sqrt(c T delta_j).

If power is short, the owner's short power report l_i<=T and capped hardware
is min(h_i,T). Since q<=1/2<1-c,
 z_ij=(1-q)l_i+q min(h_i,T) <=(1-c)h_i+(1-q)T.
Subtract cT+C(h-T). The residual is at most
 T[2-q-C h/T] <=T B(rho), since h/T>=rho.
Use(IG4). A zero-output branch has z_ij=0 and satisfies the desired bound
without an error. Summing branches, sum T=M, sum(h-T)=H-M, and
sum sqrt(T delta_j)<=sqrt(M delta) prove(IG1). The formula also covers M=0.

3. DEMONSTRATED FUNDING CONSEQUENCE
Suppose the full profile has a physical hardware bottleneck and satisfies
H=M>0 and Z=CM. Normalize H=1. Define
 S=sum_i d_i>=1, r=S-1, q0=exp(-r)/2,
 v=sum_{d_i<a_i}(a_i-d_i),
 Zplus=sum_{d_i>a_i}z_i, Zminus=sum_{d_i<a_i}z_i.
The original ACTUAL own-report envelope E obeys

 sum_i E_i <=1+exp[-1+3C/8] <8/5.                     (IG5)

This allows arbitrary grid holdings and full outer overflow, and arbitrary
mixed owners and branches. The condition is imposed only on the full profile;
the envelope still ranges over every actual owner's binding real underreports.
For H unnormalized, multiply the right side by H=F.

The accepted individual envelope comparisons, valid with all switches and
caps, give on positive gaps
 E_i<=a_i+q0 exp(K z_i)g_i,
and the accepted negative-gap theorem gives, because delta=0,
 E_i<=a_i-(4/5)q0(a_i-d_i).
Zero-gap recipients satisfy E_i<=a_i. By(IG3) and the scalar lemma below,

 g_i exp(K z_i) <=2g_i+(3/4)z_i.                      (IG6)

Furthermore SD2 in SUBSET_HARDWARE_DEFICIT_COUPLING.txt, at M=1,delta=0,
implies Zminus>=(1-c)v/c. Since Zplus<=C-Zminus and sum positive gaps=r+v,

 sum E_i <=1+q0[2r+(3/4)C+
                    {6/5-(3/4)(1-c)/c}v]
          <=1+q0[2r+(3/4)C].                         (IG7)

The coefficient of v is negative: c<3/16 gives (1-c)/c>13/3.
Maximizing exp(-r)[r+3C/8] over r>=0 gives exp(-1+3C/8).
Finally C<19/16, so this excess is less than exp(-71/128)<3/5;
the latter follows already from 1+71/128+(71/128)^2/2>5/3.

4. EXACT SCALAR SUPPORT LEMMA
For 0<=z<=1, 0<=g and z+(1-c)g<=1,

                g(exp(Kz)-2)_+ <=(3/4)z.             (IG8)

Since K<7/4 and c<3/16, it suffices to show
 (1-z)[exp(7z/4)-2] <=(39/64)z,  0<=z<=1.            (IG9)
The endpoint z=1 is immediate. For z<1 put B=39/64 and
 h(z)=log[2+Bz/(1-z)]-(7/4)z.
Its derivative is
 h'(z)=B/[(1-z)(2-(2-B)z)]-7/4,
which increases strictly on [0,1). Thus h is convex. Direct rational arithmetic
gives h'(2/3)=-19/412 and h'(7/10)>0. The minimum lies in [0,7/10], and the
tangent at2/3 gives throughout that interval
 h(z)>=log(103/32)-7/6-19/12360
      =log(103/32)-4813/4120>0.
The final strict inequality has this finite rational certificate, x=4813/4120:
 exp(x)<=sum_{j=0}^{18}x^j/j!+
              (x^19/19!)/(1-x/20)<103/32.
Above7/10, h is increasing and remains positive. This proves(IG9).
If exp(Kz)<=2, (IG8) is immediate. Otherwise multiply (IG9) by
g/(1-z)<=1/(1-c), using (3/4)(1-c)>39/64; z=1 forces g=0.

5. SCOPE AND LIVE GAP
IG1-IG2 are general structural inequalities. IG5 closes the exact saturation
face that remained loose in the aggregate positive dual. It is not a new rule,
does not change the original exponential mechanism, and does not imply a
global8/5 or C^2 budget. Away from saturation, the owner-dependent polygon in
IG2 must be retained jointly with the coalition-shifted positive dual; simply
summing the per-owner error would introduce an invalid owner-count factor.
The exact checker corroborates only the finite scalar constants in this note;
the conventional proof establishes the unbounded-owner theorem.
