1. EXACT SCOPE
The object is the ACTUAL own-report envelope of the ORIGINAL symmetric
exponential nested primitive. W(epsilon) is a supremum over physical
hardware-bottleneck profiles, normalized H=1, with max_i z_i<=epsilon.
All actual owners and their report permissions remain fixed within each
instance. Mixed ownership, arbitrary grid overflow, different deviations for
different owners, and unbounded finite owner/branch counts are permitted.
The conclusion is
 W(epsilon)=C+c(e-1)epsilon[log(1/epsilon)-loglog(1/epsilon)]
              +O(epsilon).
It is not unrestricted C^2 funding, not a bound against every truthful rule,
and not a claim about the remaining order-epsilon coefficient.

2. LOCAL DERIVATIVE ESTIMATES
At a short-coordinate increase, q'=q(rho-m)/T>=0. For every other recipient,
the only negative term in its derivative is q'(d_k-s_k); summing negative
parts gives <=q'T<=q rho<=1/2. For all recipients, summing positive parts gives
 <=1-q+qm+q' sum d
 =1-q+q[rho^2-m(rho-1)]<=1+q rho^2<=1+2/e.
At an uncapped long-coordinate increase, the derivative is
 q 1_{k=i}-(q/T)(d_k-s_k), so the corresponding bounds are <=q rho<=1/2
and <=2q<=1. Above the physical cap the derivative is zero.
Own-coordinate monotonicity ensures the own derivative is nonnegative, so
these estimates also control any selected subset of local-payment derivatives:
its sum lies in [-1/2,1+2/e]. This last fact is essential for outer cap clipping.

3. RESTORATION TAKES PLACE INSIDE THE ACTUAL REPORT DOMAIN
After restoring only the deviator's grid, every intermediate hardware report
still is an actual underreport. With its local payments z', its outer ratio is
 >=sum_k min(p_k+z'_k,1)
 >=S-(z_i-z'_i)-sum_{k!=i}(z_k-z'_k)_+
 >=S-e z_i>=3/2
whenever S>=3/2+e epsilon. This uses a cap-Lipschitz estimate, not the false
claim that all opposing local payments decrease. The same bound applies at
EVERY point along restoration because it refers to the original full profile.

On an increasing physical hardware coordinate, let W be the sum of local
derivatives of currently uncapped opposing accounts. Then
 Q'=Q(rho-m-W)/H,
 N_i'=1-Q+Q d_i'+Q'(d_i-a_i),
with d_i'>=0 and -1/2<=W<=1+2/e. If d_i-a_i=-g<=0, use g<=H and m>=0 to get
 N_i'>=1-Q(rho+3/2)>0 for rho>=3/2.
If d_i-a_i=g>=0, use rho>=m and g<=H to get
 N_i'>=1-Q(2+2/e)>0 for rho>=3/2.
The first coefficient is bounded above by 3/(2 sqrt(e)); the second by
(2+2/e)/(2 sqrt(e)). Both are strictly below one. These inequalities account
for the local payments changing DURING hardware restoration; they are stronger
than a comparison which artificially restores only the outer hardware account.

Persistent equality clarification: an opposing account equal to the outer
hardware total on a positive-measure set must be assigned to one cap branch.
The patched convention counts it in m, and leaves W strictly below the cap.
On such equality sets its cap derivative equals 1 almost everywhere. This

The author's absolute-continuity addendum correctly avoids assuming finitely

4. ENTROPY MASS AND NEGATIVE-GAP MASS ARE COUPLED
Classify each branch at its FULL profile, with hardware short at ties, and let
X be the sum of production over those branches. Then 0<=X<=M<=1.
For a local-only decrease on an originally local-short branch, incoming is
<=half the withheld amount<=the full local-short role. On an originally
local-long branch, the accepted sharp long-coordinate estimate applies even
if that branch subsequently switches orientation. Summing and applying log-sum
gives
 I_i<=z_i-v_i+K v_i[1+log(X/(2v_i))].
Here v_i is the owner's full local-long role on the X branches; v_i<=z_i<=epsilon
and v_i<=X/2. Thus the author's two cases are valid:
 X>=epsilon: I_i<=epsilon[1+K+K log(X/epsilon)];
 X<epsilon: I_i<=e epsilon.
The original orientation is used solely to account for full payments. No
virtual-owner relabeling, intermediate role preservation, or branchwise
smallness of epsilon is assumed.

Full hardware-role payments U_i satisfy U_i<=a_i and U_i<=z_i<=d_i, while
sum_i U_i>=X/2. Hence the FULL negative-gap sum obeys
 G_-=sum_i(a_i-d_i)_+<=1-X/2.
This is valid with arbitrary grid overflow. It is the information absent from
the older leading-order diffuse bound and is what creates the loglog term.

After actual hardware/grid restoration, a negative-gap recipient has fixed

6. UNIFORM SECOND-ORDER CALCULATION
For epsilon<=X<=1, divide the entropy-mass expression by K and set Y=X/2.
The objective is (1-Y)(L+log Y+log2+1+1/K). Its second derivative is
-1/Y-1/Y^2<0. At its unique interior maximum x=1/Y satisfies
 x+log x=L+log2+2+1/K.
Thus x=L-log L+log2+2+1/K+o(1) and the objective value is x-2+1/x.
This verifies
 F_epsilon=K(L-log L)+1+K log2+o(1).
The X<epsilon branch contributes a bounded constant and cannot maximize for
small epsilon. With t=A-1+epsilon F_epsilon, the exact relaxed ratio maximum is
 1+c A exp(t/A)
for all sufficiently small epsilon, since t/A<1. Its expansion gives exactly
the stated upper coefficient c[2K+1+K log2] at order epsilon.
The remainder is UNIFORM over profiles: the reduction is to a single explicit
scalar function of epsilon, and epsilon^2 L^2=o(epsilon). There is no exchange
of an uncontrolled profilewise remainder with an unbounded supremum.

For S<3/2+e epsilon, the older profilewise bound converges uniformly on
0<=r<=1/2+e epsilon to 1+r exp(-r)/2. Its limiting supremum is
1+exp(-1/2)/4<C, since sqrt(e)<2. A fixed limiting gap suffices to exclude all
such profiles from the asymptotic upper; an explicit epsilon cutoff is not
needed for the claimed asymptotic theorem.
