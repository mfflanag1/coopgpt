1. BEFORE SWITCH
The signed OTHER loss is exactly q(e^u-1)(1-s-v). If its last factor is
positive, v<1 and the other original local caps equal their actual reports.
Feasibility gives u<=d+v-1<=dv. Thus division by the actual full payment
z=(1-q)s+qd, which is at least qd, gives loss/z<=K v(1-v)<=K/4<1.
The reduction remains valid when the deviator's original raw report exceeds
one: its cap does not move until that report has fallen below one. The cap
change thereafter is precisely u. A zero local cap cannot have an effective
local underreport. The upper e-1<2 is enough for the strict comparison.

2. AFTER SWITCH AND MIXED HARDWARE
The exact other-payment difference is
 (1-q)(A-v)+q_new(v-W),
where A=1-s and W=sum_other min(h_k,t). This identity accounts for every
actual owner's two roles without changing ownership.
If A>=v, then W>=min(A,t)>=v, giving the stated upper (1-q)(A-v).
The resulting positive ratio is maximized by s=0: the numerator weakly
decreases and the strictly positive denominator weakly increases with s.
If A<=v, then each other hardware amount is <=A<=v<=t. Therefore W=A
exactly, and the signed loss is (1-q-q_new)(A-v)<=0. This exact branch is
necessary: the coalition floor alone would give a looser positive expression.

3. SCALAR ENDPOINTS
For fixed v in(0,1), d lies in[1-v,1]. The derivative numerator of
 (1-v)(2exp(d+v-1)-1)/d
is increasing with d, because its derivative is 2exp(d+v-1)d>=0.
There is no interior maximum: the endpoint values are 1 and
f(v)=(1-v)(2exp(v)-1). This is a derivative sign argument, not an unsupported
convexity assertion. The v=0 case forces d=1, and v>=1 precludes a strict
switch. A zero resulting total has the separately specified zero-payment
convention.

4. EXACT OPTIMUM AND ATTAINMENT
f'(v)=1-2v exp(v); hence the unique maximum is at v_* exp(v_*)=1/2.
f'(0)>0 establishes Lambda>1, so it dominates both the before-switch bound
and the other endpoint. Substitution gives Lambda=(1-v_*)^2/v_*.
In the stated two-owner example, q=exp(-v_*)/2. After the distinguished
local owner withdraws, the mixed opponent owns all positive resources,
with min(1,v_*)=v_* and payment exactly v_*. Thus the signed loss is
(1-q)(1-v_*), whose ratio to the deviator's full q is exactly Lambda.
The report change does not move resources or change owners.

5. SUMMING AND SCOPE
Each branch inequality uses the same deviator's full branch payment; summing
across branches and then taking the positive part is valid. Arbitrarily many
branches and owners are permitted. The result holds only for local-resource
withholding with all hardware fixed. It bounds the signed sum, not the sum
of positive losses, and not an outer capped total. The text preserves these
limitations. No novelty or unrestricted funding-optimality claim is made.
