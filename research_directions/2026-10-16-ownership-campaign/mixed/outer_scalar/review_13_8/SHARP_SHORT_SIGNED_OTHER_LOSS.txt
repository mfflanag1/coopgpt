SHARP SIGNED OTHER-PAYMENT LOSS UNDER AN ORIGINAL-SHORT LOCAL REPORT
17 October2026. Candidate exact theorem, independent review requested.
Original symmetric exponential two-pool local rule; fixed actual owners,
arbitrary mixed holdings and nonnegative real binding underreports.
No outer-cap funding or novelty claim is made.

THEOREM
Fix a branch whose LOCAL-resource pool is short or tied at the full profile.
Hold every hardware report and every other owner's local report fixed. Under
any decrease of actual owner i's local report, the SIGNED sum of all OTHER
actual owners' payment losses is at most owner i's FULL original-short LOCAL
role payment. In particular it is at most its full branch payment. The constant
one is exact and cannot be decreased.

This concerns the signed sum sum_{k!=i}(z_k-z'_k), not the sum of positive
losses sum_{k!=i}(z_k-z'_k)_+. The two quantities have different sharp constants.
The theorem holds with arbitrary own hardware, any finite opposing hardware
distribution, and every real local report decrease. It introduces no virtual
strategic player or additional incentive constraint.

1. NORMALIZATION AND ACTUAL-OWNER ACCOUNTING
If full short total T=0, all branch payments remain zero. Otherwise normalize
T=1. Let own local amount be d in[0,1], other local total v=1-d, full capped
hardware total r>=1, and full coefficient q=exp(1-r)/2.
The full original-short LOCAL role of owner i is (1-q)d.

At its underreport the local total is t in[v,1]. Hardware remains long or
tied, since its raw total was at least1. Put R=sum_k min(h_k,t).
For t>0 the new coefficient is q_t=exp(1-R/t)/2. Every full hardware cap
c_k=min(h_k,1) belongs to[0,1], and min(h_k,t)=min(c_k,t). Consequently

                            tr<=R<=r.                 (SS1)

It follows that q_t<=q. Owner i's own hardware-role payment weakly decreases:
q_t min(h_i,t)<=q min(h_i,1). Add this nonnegative own hardware-role loss to
the signed loss of all other actual owners. The resulting ACCOUNTING sum
contains every hardware role and only the OTHER local roles. Its loss is

             L_aug=q(r-v)-q_t(R-v).                   (SS2)

Thus actual signed OTHER loss <=L_aug. No owner identities or feasible report
domains are changed by this bookkeeping comparison. It only adds a
nonnegative amount to the quantity being bounded. We prove
L_aug<=(1-q)d, which is stronger than the stated full-payment comparison.

2. ZERO-CHANGE AND ZERO-TOTAL BOUNDARIES
If d=0, no local change is possible and the actual signed loss is zero.
If v=0, the opposing local roles are zero; their augmented new payment is
nonnegative even at t=0. Thus
 L_aug<=q r<=(1-q)d,
because d=1 and q(r+1)<=1 for r>=1.
At an actual new zero total the primitive is defined to pay zero. The formulas
below can alternatively be interpreted by continuous limits.

Assume now0<v<1, so t>=v>0.

3. REDUCTION TO TWO CAPPED-HARDWARE ENDPOINTS
At fixed t, the new opposing accounting term is
 (1/2)exp(1)(R-v)exp(-R/t).
As a function of R its derivative has the sign of1-(R-v)/t. It therefore
increases and then decreases, with its only possible interior critical point
a maximum. Its MINIMUM on the closed interval[tr,r] occurs at an endpoint.
Using the entire interval is a safe relaxation even when a particular endpoint
cannot be attained by the fixed finite hardware vector.

At R=tr, q_t=q and
 L_aug=q r(1-t)<=q r(1-v)<= (1-q)d.                    (SS3)
The last inequality follows from
(r+1)exp(1-r)<=2, whose left side decreases for r>=1 and equals2 at r=1.

At R=r,
 L_aug=q(r-v)[1-exp(-r(1/t-1))].
This expression decreases as t increases, so it is bounded by its value at
t=v. Put y=r(1-v)/v>0. Then

 L_aug/d <=q r[1+(r-1)/y](1-exp(-y))
         <=q r^2
         <=1-q.                                      (SS4)

For the middle inequality use1-exp(-y)<=1 and (1-exp(-y))/y<=1 separately;
their coefficients are nonnegative because r>=1. The last inequality is
equivalent to r^2+1<=2exp(r-1), which follows from the first three
nonnegative Taylor terms of exp(r-1):
2exp(r-1)>=2+2(r-1)+(r-1)^2=r^2+1.
Both endpoint bounds establish the theorem for every admissible R.

4. RESTORE SCALE, SUM BRANCHES, AND EXACT SHARPNESS
Multiplying all payments and capacities by T restores the original units.
For any collection of branches whose local pools were originally short,
sum the theorem branchwise. The signed OTHER loss on those branches is at
most the sum of the deviator's FULL original-short LOCAL role payments.
The recipient may own hardware on those and other branches; identities remain
fixed. This is stronger than charging all its mixed branch payments.

For sharpness take two actual owners: one holds local amount1 and no hardware;
the other holds hardware1 and no local resource. At the full balanced profile,
each is paid1/2. The local owner reports zero and both payments become zero.
The other's signed loss is1/2, equal to the deviator's full short-role payment.
Splitting the balanced construction into arbitrary independent branches gives
the same equality with growing branch counts. No asymptotic or numerical
argument is needed for the sharp constant.

5. PRECISE LIMITATION
The theorem controls an UNCLIPPED signed local-payment sum. Subsequent outer
grid caps may hide gains and thereby remove cancellation. Therefore it cannot
be substituted for a bound on positive outgoing loss or on a capped opposing
total without a separate cap-accounting argument. It complements, rather than
replaces, the sharp nonlinear and positive-loss theorems.
