DIRECT OUTER BOUND FOR EVERY FULL POSITIVE-GAP RECIPIENT
16 October2026. Candidate structural lemma; independent audit required.
Fixed actual owners, original exponential primitive, all-real binding reports.

Normalize full hardware H=1, assume P+M>=1, and define
 a_i=H_i, b_i=p_i+z_i, d_i=min(b_i,1), S=sum_i d_i>=1,
 q0=.5 exp(1-S), g_i=d_i-a_i.
For every full positive-gap recipient g_i>0, its actual envelope satisfies
 E_i <=a_i+k(max(d_i,S-kappa_i))*g_i,                  (1)
where kappa_i=(e-1)z_i and
 k(t)=1-.5 exp(1-1/t) for0<t<=1,
 k(t)=.5 exp(1-t) fort>=1.
The stronger outgoing charge (4/e)U_i+(e-1)V_i may replace kappa_i, where U,V
are original SHORT/LONG local roles summed branchwise, not hardware/power roles.
For d_i=1, (1) recovers FULLY_CAPPED_RECIPIENT_BOUND.txt. Formula(1) genuinely
covers noncapped recipients and outer switches. It is an upper bound, not a
claim of simultaneous attainment across recipients or a new payment formula.

PROOF
For an arbitrary actual own underreport, restore only its outer hardware and
grid reports after local recomputation. This weakly raises its outer payment.
The own capped account b' is at most d=d_i, while other owners' positive capped
account losses sum to at most kappa_i by SHARP_OUTGOING_LOSS.txt. Thus their
remaining capped mass is at least R=max(0,S-d-kappa_i).
Let t be the resulting total capped opposing mass. For t<1, all opposing
accounts are uncapped and this is also their actual total. For t>=1 the outer
pool is hardware-short. Its payment W(t,b') therefore is
 (1-q_h(t))b'+q_h(t)min(a_i,t) for t<=1,
 a_i+.5exp(1-t)(b'-a_i) for t>=1,
where q_h(t)=.5exp(1-sum_j min(a_j,t)/t).
The cap threshold is always full H=1 after hardware restoration.

If b'<=a_i, W<=a_i and the target upper bound is automatic. Otherwise a_i<b'
<=t, so the own hardware cap is inactive. For fixed b', W decreases with t:
on the short side it is b'-q_h(t)(b'-a_i), with q_h nondecreasing; on the long
side it is a_i+q(t)(b'-a_i), with q decreasing. Hence replace t by b'+R to
increase the payment. This is an analytical comparison, not an actual report.

Along the path t=b+R, increase b from b' to d. The payment is nondecreasing.
For t<=1, q_h'=q_h(rho-h)/t, and its derivative along this path is
 1-q_h-q_h'(b-a_i)>=1-q_h(rho+1)>=0,
since b-a_i<=t and q_h(rho+1)<=1 for rho>=1. For t>=1 the derivative is
 q(t)[1-(b-a_i)]>=0 because b<=d<=1. The formulas match continuously at1.
Thus the payment is at most W(d+R,d), with d+R=max(d,S-kappa_i).
If this endpoint is at least1, it equals the right side of(1). If it is below1,
its hardware capped sum is at most1, so q_h(t)>=.5 exp(1-1/t). Since d>a_i,
this only lowers W compared with the displayed k(t) expression, proving(1).
Taking the supremum over actual deviations is legitimate because the bound
uses only the full profile. The same proof works for any valid outgoing charge.

EXACT OBSTRUCTION TO THE NAIVE NEGATIVE-GAP CEILING SUM
It is valid that E_i<=a_i for a full negative-gap owner. Nevertheless, summing
that ceiling with(1) cannot prove C^2, even on a family with unbounded owners
and branches and positive grid supply.

For any m>=1, create m branches. Branch j has hardware1/m owned by owner j,
and local resource1/m owned by one additional owner B. B also owns grid p,
where0<p<=1/2. All other reports are zero. H=M=F=1 and every local primitive
is balanced, so z_j=1/(2m), z_B=1/2. Thus every hardware recipient is a full
negative gap, and their ceilings sum to1. For B,
 a_B=0, d_B=1/2+p, S=1+p, kappa_B=(e-1)/2>1/2.
Therefore max(d_B,S-kappa_B)=d_B and(1) gives d_B*k(d_B). The combined bound is
                         1+d_B*k(d_B).
The function d*k(d) is nondecreasing on[1/2,1], because its derivative is
1-q(1+1/d)>=0. The combined bound is consequently at least
 1+(1-c)/2, c=1/(2e),
which is strictly greater than C^2=(1+c)^2. Indeed its excess is
1/2-(5/2)c-c^2. The exact partial sum e>1957/720>125/46 gives c<23/125, and
substitution gives excess>96/15625>0.
This is an obstruction to these independently maximized accounting bounds,
NOT an actual-envelope counterexample. The hardware ceilings discard the
negative-gap savings needed to fund positive recipients. Both branch and owner
counts grow within one fixed actual-owner domain for each member of the family.
