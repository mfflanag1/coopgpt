WEIGHTED OPPONENT-PRODUCTION ROBUSTNESS: A GENERAL HARDWARE FUNDING REGIME
16 October 2026. Candidate theorem, independent review pending.
Original exponential primitive and its actual own-report envelope. Fixed actual

No novelty claim for weighted coalition coverage or the elementary scalar steps.

1. STATEMENT AND COMPUTABLE STRUCTURAL PARAMETER
At a physical hardware bottleneck H>0, H<=P+M, normalize full H=1. Let m=M/H,
z_i denote normalized full aggregate local payments, and Z=sum z_i. For each
actual recipient choose a valid common ceiling U_i on its local payment after
ANY one actual owner's underreport. One explicit useful choice is
 U_i=min{m,(1+z_i)/2,sum_j min(T_j,max(h_ij,l_ij))}.
The half-gap incoming-response lemma proves the middle ceiling; the other two
follow directly from primitive payment ceilings. All quantities here are
normalized by H. Put u_i=min(p_i,1), and set
 w_i=min(1,(1-u_i)/U_i) if U_i>0, and w_i=1 if U_i=0.

For every actual owner i define the weighted opponent production
 L_i=integral_0^1 m({k!=i:w_k>=t})dt,
where m(A) is the actual coalition's unchanged local production. Set
                 eta=max_i (m-L_i).                       (R1)
The integrand is between zero and m, so 0<=eta<=m<=1. In original units eta is
max_i[M-integral m(A_i(t))dt]/H. No marginal-contribution assumption on U_i
is needed: this theorem uses the exact weighted coalition floor itself.

If eta<=1/4, then the same actual envelope satisfies
           sum_i E_i/H <1+(1/2)exp(-1/4)<7/5<C^2.          (R2)
The number of owners, positive-gap recipients and branches is arbitrary. Full
and deviated grid overflow are allowed. Unlike the headroom theorem, M=H and
Gamma=0 are not required. The all-profile truthful mechanism is unchanged;
only the funding bound is conditioned on this full-profile statistic.

Immediate consequence: every physical hardware-bottleneck profile with
M<=H/4 satisfies(R2), regardless of grid/local ownership concentration, since
eta<=M/H. Productive branches and active owner counts can both grow without
limit in this statement. It is a theorem on the entire stated class, not a
numerical observation on a chosen family.

2. CAP-SAFE POSITIVE COMPARISON
Write a_i=H_i/H, d_i=min(p_i+z_i,1), S=sum d_i>=1, r=S-1,
pi=sum u_i, c=1/(2e), C=1+c, epsilon=Z-m in[0,cm].
The weighted actual-coalition floor gives opponents' capped opposing total
at every i-underreport at least pi-u_i+L_i. Restore i's own outer hardware,
grid, and capped opposing account to full values as monotonic payment
comparisons. The resulting opposing capped total is therefore at least
 d_i+pi-u_i+L_i >=pi+m-eta >=S-epsilon-eta.               (R3)
Here d_i>=u_i and S<=pi+Z. None of these inequalities assumes cap linearity,
and L_i may vanish; the opponents' capped total may exceed one. The right side may be negative, which
causes no difficulty in the following exponential comparison.

For a positive full gap g_i=d_i-a_i>0, the established scalar comparison is
 E_i<=a_i+k(t_i)g_i, where t_i is a valid restored capped-total lower bound.
The all-orientation majorant k(t)<=exp(1-t)/2 for t>=0, together with(R3), gives
 E_i<=a_i+q0*A*g_i, q0=exp(-r)/2, A=exp(epsilon+eta).     (R4)
If(R3) is negative, use the actual nonnegative total directly and then the
monotonicity of exp(1-t); no evaluation of k at a negative argument is needed.
The positive-account restoration and this comparison do not assert attainability
of a common counterprofile for different owners.

3. NEGATIVE SAVINGS AND THE AVAILABLE HARDWARE MASS
The previously audited nonlinear negative-gap theorem gives, for
v=sum_i(a_i-d_i)_+,
 total negative saving >=q0*B*v,
 B=(4/5)exp(epsilon-cm).                                (R5)
Indeed delta=Cm-Z=cm-epsilon. For a strict negative recipient,
z_i+g_i=a_i-p_i<=1, hence z_i*g_i<=1/4. Since K^2<3, its savings argument
K^2*z_i*g_i is below3/4. The decreasing function L therefore obeys
L(K^2*z_i*g_i)>L(3/4)=4/(3*sqrt(e))>4/5, using e<25/9.
This theorem already handles arbitrary full/deviated overflow and switches.

Decompose each full local payment into physical hardware and local-power roles,
z_i=U_i^hw+V_i^power. The notation here is separate from the payment ceilings.
Every U_i^hw<=a_i and sum V_i^power<=min(m,1/2). To verify the latter
branchwise: on a hardware-short branch, power is long and its total role is
q*rho*T<=T/2=H_j/2, since rho*exp(1-rho)<=1 for rho>=1. On a power-short
branch its total role is (1-q)T<=H_j/2: writing rho<=H_j/T, the inequality
2-exp(1-rho)<=rho follows because rho-2+exp(1-rho) is zero at1 and has
nonnegative derivative thereafter. In both orientations the total power role
is also at most T (q*rho<=1/2 or1-q<=1). Zero outputs contribute zero.
Summing proves the asserted min(m,1/2) bound. Hence
 v<=sum_i(a_i-U_i^hw)=1-Z+sum_i V_i^power
   <=b-epsilon, where b=min(1,3/2-m).                    (R6)
This uses actual owners and nonnegative gaps; no merged player is introduced.
Since epsilon<=cm and c<1/2, b-epsilon>=0. With Gplus=r+v, summing(R4)-(R5)
gives
 sum E_i<=1+(exp(-r)/2)[A*r+(A-B)*v]
         <=1+(exp(-r)/2)[A*r+(A-B)(b-epsilon)].          (R7)
Here A>=1>=B, so the substitution has the correct direction.

4. EXACT SCALAR MAXIMIZATION
Put theta=B/A=(4/5)exp(-cm-eta). The positive term in(R7) has its maximum over
r>=0 at r*=1-(1-theta)(b-epsilon)>=theta>0, because 0<=b-epsilon<=1. Its value is
 c*exp[eta+b-theta*(b-epsilon)].                         (R8)
For fixed m,eta the exponent increases in epsilon, so set epsilon=cm. It also
increases in eta. Under eta<=1/4 it is therefore at most
 f(m)=1/4+b-(4/5)exp(-1/4-cm)(b-cm).                     (R9)

For m<=1/2, b=1 and f'(m)=(4c/5)exp(-1/4-cm)(2-cm)>0.
For m>=1/2, b=3/2-m and
 f'(m)=-1+(4/5)exp(-1/4-cm)[1+c+c(b-cm)].
Since b-cm<=1, c<3/16, and exp(-1/4)<4/5,
 f'(m)<-1+(16/25)(1+2c)<-1+(16/25)(11/8)=-3/25<0.
Thus the maximum is at m=1/2. Put x=c/2<3/32. Then
 f(1/2)=5/4-(4/5)exp(-1/4-x)(1-x)<3/4,
because
 exp(-1/4-x)(1-x)
 > (29/32)[1-11/32+(11/32)^2/2-(11/32)^3/6] >5/8.        (R10)
The first inequality uses x<3/32 and the signed cubic Taylor lower bound.
Both comparisons in(R10) are exact; the rational checker records the latter.
Consequently(R8) is less than c*exp(3/4)=exp(-1/4)/2.
Finally exp(1/4)>1+1/4 implies exp(-1/4)/2<2/5. This proves(R2).

The case m=0 is included by eta=epsilon=0 and zero local payments. H=0 has
zero output and payments and is omitted only to avoid normalization. At eta=1/4
all conclusions remain strict because the exponential and c bounds are strict.

5. FINITE CAP REDUCTION AND REMAINING FRONTIER
For the displayed ceiling U_i<= (1+z_i)/2, a nontrivial weight w_i<1 implies
d_i>1/2. Thus S<2 gives at most three such recipients; each L_i needs at most
four original coalition values. This is exactly the independently established
THREE_CAP_RECIPIENT_REDUCTION, now with a demonstrated funding consequence.

More generally the known grid-tail bound already proves C^2 whenever
pi>=15/7. Each exceptional recipient k satisfies 2u_k+z_k>1, because
u_k+U_k>1 and U_k<=(1+z_k)/2. Thus in the remaining cell pi<15/7,
 |J|<2pi+Z<30/7+C<6.
There are at most FIVE cap-sensitive recipients, as established independently
in GLOBAL_FIVE_CAP_FRONTIER_REDUCTION.txt. Each L_i in the unresolved cell
therefore needs at most six original coalition values, independently of owner
and branch counts. After branch totals are precomputed, removing i and at most
five named owners
computes these values in O(number_of_branches) arithmetic operations per i.
This is a real-arithmetic statement, not rational bit complexity for exponentials.

Combining prior results, the general C^2 hardware question can now be restricted
to profiles with pi<15/7 and eta>1/4 (necessarily M/H>1/4). Those profiles may
still have arbitrarily many ordinary owners and positive gaps, but their
cap-sensitive recipient set has size at most five. No claim is made that this
remaining finite-cap structural problem is solved or that eta is necessary.

A SIMPLE PRODUCTION-RESERVE SUFFICIENT CONDITION
Every threshold coalition contains N\(J union {i}), since all weights outside
J equal one. Consequently L_i>=m(N\(J union {i})). Thus eta<=1/4 is guaranteed
if deleting all cap-sensitive recipients and any one actual owner preserves
local production at least M-H/4. This condition is sufficient, not necessary:
the exact weighted floor retains some exceptional owners over positive portions
of the integral and can be strictly stronger. It gives a direct production
interpretation of the parameter without replacing fixed owners or claiming
that separate worst-case reports can be jointly attained.

GENERAL CERTIFIED-FLOOR FORM
The proof only uses the inequality R_i>=pi_{-i}+m-eta. Hence any certified
opponents' capped-total floors R_i>=pi_{-i}, uniform over every actual i-report,
may replace the weighted floor. Define
 eta=max(0,max_i[m+pi_{-i}-R_i]).
Then eta<=m and exactly the same conclusion follows when eta<=1/4. In
particular the finite cap-choice LP floor in FINITE_CAP_CHOICE_COALITION_FLOOR
dominates the weighted floor and can only improve this parameter. Taking the
maximum of several independently valid floors is also allowed. The word
certified here requires an inequality for every actual report, not a computed
minimum over a finite sample; the LP is a coverage relaxation with a proved
containing polytope. No assertion of simultaneous attainment enters this proof.
