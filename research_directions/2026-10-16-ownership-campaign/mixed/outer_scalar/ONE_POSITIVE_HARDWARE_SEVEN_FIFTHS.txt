ONE POSITIVE GAP AT A PHYSICAL HARDWARE BOTTLENECK: 7/5
16 October2026. Analytical theorem, arbitrary full grid overflow.
Original exponential nested primitive and actual own-report envelope only.
Fixed actual owners, all nonnegative real reports, unbounded owners and branches.
Internally independently derived; cross-model review pending. No novelty claim.

THEOREM
Let H=sum_i H_i, M=sum_j min(H_j,L_j), P=sum_i p_i, and F=min(H,P+M).
At a full profile, local payments are z_i, Z=sum_i z_i, and the capped outer
account is d_i=min(p_i+z_i,H). Assume
                  F=H, and #{i:d_i>H_i}<=1.
Then the ACTUAL own-report envelope of the original nested exponential rule obeys
                         sum_i E_i <=(7/5)H <C^2 H,
where C=1+1/(2e). Arbitrary full overflow p_i+z_i>H is allowed. No assumption
restricts which coordinates an owner may jointly own or underreport.

This is a HARDWARE-bottleneck theorem. It does not assert7F/5 at physical power
bottlenecks; the existing separate theorem gives C^2 F in that other regime.
The constant7/5 is not claimed optimal. Exact computation of E is not asserted.

Normalize H=1; H=0 is immediate. Set a_i=H_i, d_i=min(p_i+z_i,1), S=sum_i d_i,
M<=1, c=1/(2e), delta=CM-Z in[0,cM], and q0=.5exp(1-S).
The physical bottleneck implies S>=1: if some p_i+z_i>=1 this is immediate;
otherwise S=P+Z>=P+M>=1.

1. OPPONENT COALITION SURPLUS IS AT MOST HALF THE LOCAL OUTPUT
On a single branch of value T, remove one ACTUAL owner i and let T_{-i} be the
unchanged opponents' coalition value. For the full local exponential payments,
                     sum_{k!=i}phi_k-T_{-i} <=T/2.          (1)
If T=0 it is trivial. Otherwise normalize T=1 and label the short and long
pools according to their FULL orientation. Let x be opponents' short total,
y their raw long total, and d their capped long total. Thus0<=x<=1, d<=y,
and T_{-i}=min(x,y). The full coefficient is q=.5exp(1-rho), where rho=d+d_i
and the omitted single owner's capped long account d_i lies in[0,1].

If y<x, then y<1, so d=y. Hence rho<=1+y, q>=.5exp(-y), and the surplus is
 (1-q)(x-y) <=(1-y)(1-.5exp(-y)) <=1/2.
The final function decreases on0<=y<=1: its derivative is
 -1+.5exp(-y)(2-y)<=0, and its value at0 is1/2.
If y>=x, the surplus is q(d-x). It is nonpositive if d<=x. Otherwise rho>=d,
so it is at most .5exp(1-d)(d-x). Maximizing over d>=x gives .5exp(-x)<=1/2.
This proves(1), including mixed owners and pool ties. The SINGLE omitted actual
owner matters: this is not asserted for an arbitrary omitted coalition.

Sum(1) over all branches. Define
 M_{-i}=sum_j min(H_j-h_ij,L_j-l_ij).
Then
                         Z-z_i-M_{-i} <=M/2.               (2)

2. FIXED OPPONENT COVERAGE PROVIDES A RESIDUAL OUTER FLOOR
At ANY own underreport by i, the opponents' physical resources are unchanged.
Local coalition coverage therefore gives sum_{k!=i}z'_k>=M_{-i}. After restoring
i's full OUTER hardware and grid, other accounts remain p_k+z'_k. Consequently
 sum_{k!=i}min(p_k+z'_k,1) >=min{1,P_{-i}+M_{-i}}.         (3)
This follows from sum min(b_k,1)>=min(sum b_k,1). It is an actual-coalition
constraint, with no virtual owners or reassignment of resources.

Let i be the sole positive-gap owner, put a=a_i, d=d_i, g=d-a>0, and r=S-1.
All opponents satisfy d_k<=a_k, so
            0<=r<=g<=1,        S-d<=1-a<=1.                (4)
At full reports, P_{-i}>=S-d-(Z-z_i), even if an opponent has full overflow.
Combining with(2),
 P_{-i}+M_{-i} >=S-d-M/2.
Since the positive part of this lower bound is at most1, the min in(3) preserves
 R=(S-d-M/2)_+ as a valid lower bound on opposing capped accounts at every own
underreport.

Restore i's outer hardware/grid, then increase its capped own opposing account
back to d. Own-coordinate monotonicity raises its payment. With d>a, increasing
the opponents' total lowers the payment on either side of the outer switch.
For
 k(t)=1-.5exp(1-1/t), 0<t<=1;
 k(t)=.5exp(1-t), t>=1; k(0)=1,
this gives
 E_i <=a+g k(d+R)
      =a+g k(max{d,S-M/2})
      <=a+g k(max{g,r+1/2}).                              (5)
The final step uses d>=g, M<=1, and the fact that k is nonincreasing. Below1,
hardware caps increase the outer q and thus lower a positive-gap payment, so
k is a valid upper envelope there. At or above1 the expression is exact for
the abstract capped-account total. Algebraic restoration is not claimed to be
an actual feasible report.

3. RETAIN THE OPPONENTS' NEGATIVE-GAP SAVINGS
Use NEGATIVE_GAP_SAVINGS.txt, whose hypotheses permit arbitrary full overflow.
For every negative-gap owner k, put g_k=a_k-d_k>=0. That theorem gives
 E_k<=a_k-q0 exp(-delta) g_k L(K^2 z_k g_k),
 L(v)=2exp(sqrt(1-v)-1)/(1+sqrt(1-v)), K=e-1.
Here z_k+g_k<=1 and hence K^2z_kg_k<=K^2/4. Zero gaps contribute zero savings;
the E_k<=a_k ceiling remains valid even at a zero-gap full-capped owner.

Uniformly,
                            L(v)>4/5                    (6)
for0<=v<=K^2/4. A short exact justification is included below. Since delta<=cM<=c
and sum_{k!=i}g_k=1-S+g=g-r, summing the negative owners gives the valid saving
                      (2/5)exp(-r-c)(g-r).
No common underreport is assumed across owners. Every recipient inequality is
uniform over its own full report box.

4. THE REMAINING TWO-VARIABLE INEQUALITY IS ANALYTICAL
Put B=(2/5)exp(-c), so0<B<2/5. Combining(5)--(6),
 sum_i E_i <=1+g k(max{g,r+1/2})-B exp(-r)(g-r),           (7)
for0<=r<=g<=1.

First suppose g>=r+1/2. The positive term g k(g) is independent of r and the
right side increases in r, because the derivative of its negative term is
B exp(-r)(g-r+1)>0. Set r=g-1/2. Along this boundary the excess is
 g k(g)-(B/2)exp(1/2-g),  1/2<=g<=1,
which increases in g. Indeed x k(x) is nondecreasing on[0,1]: its derivative is
1-.5exp(1-1/x)(1+1/x)>=0, by (u+2)exp(-u)<=2 for u=1/x-1>=0. The second
boundary term also increases in g. The maximum is g=1,r=1/2.

Now suppose g<=r+1/2. For fixed r, expression(7) is linear increasing in g.
If r<=1/2, its coefficient is at least1/2-Bexp(-r)>0; if r>=1/2 it is
exp(-r)[sqrt(e)/2-B]>0. Thus set g=min(1,r+1/2). The first case is the same
boundary as above. In the second case, g=1 and r>=1/2; its excess is
 exp(-r)[sqrt(e)/2-B+Br].
Its derivative is exp(-r)[B(2-r)-sqrt(e)/2]<0, since B<2/5, 2-r<=3/2, and
sqrt(e)/2>4/5. Thus again the maximum is g=1,r=1/2.

At this common maximum,
 sum_i E_i <=3/2-(1/5)exp(-1/2-c) <7/5,                  (8)
because exp(-1/2-c)>1/2. Replacing the strict conclusion by <=7/5 gives the
stated convenient rational bound. If no positive gap exists, S=1 and every
E_i<=a_i, so the budget is at most1 directly.

5. EXACT ELEMENTARY CONSTANT CHECKS
For r_v=sqrt(1-v), direct differentiation gives
 L''(v)=-L(v)(1-r_v)/[4r_v(1+r_v)^2]<=0.
By concavity and the endpoints L(0)=1,L(1)=2/e,
 L(v)>=1-(1-2/e)v.
The positive Taylor series and a geometric tail give
               1957/720 < e <11743/4320 <68/25.
Thus K<43/25, 1-2/e<9/34, and for v<=K^2/4,
 L(v)>1-(9/34)(1849/2500)=68359/85000>4/5.
Also e>125/46, hence c<23/125 and1/2+c<171/250. The exact standard-library
checker verify_one_positive_analytic_constants.py bounds exp(171/250)<2 using
a degree8 positive Taylor sum plus its geometric tail. It also verifies
sqrt(e)/2>4/5 and7/5<C^2 by rational comparisons using the same bounds on e.
There is no grid, continuous optimizer, or floating proof decision in this
analytical theorem. All checks pass both normally and with Python -O.
