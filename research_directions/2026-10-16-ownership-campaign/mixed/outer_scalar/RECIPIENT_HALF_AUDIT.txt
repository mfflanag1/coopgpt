No claim below relies on finite report sampling.

1. ACTUAL-OWNER RESTORATION
For a fixed recipient with lesser own coordinate a and greater b, increasing
opponents' lesser-type reports raises its payment on both sides of the pool
tie, whereas increasing opponents' greater-type reports lowers it. In the
lesser-pool-short formula the nonnegative gap min(b,T)-a and the coefficient
q both increase with T. In the other orientation, the own lesser coordinate
is uncapped and payment is b-q(b-a). Cap and pool boundaries match.
Consequently restoring one deviating owner's adverse coordinate to full,
then deleting that SAME owner's beneficial coordinate, upper-bounds its
recipient effect. Both are feasible reports within the fixed original owner's
domain. The diagonal recipient deviation is zero by own monotonicity.
There is no need for virtual-player incentive constraints.

2. HARDWARE-LARGER AND RESOURCE-LARGER EASY CASES
When recipient hardware is b>=a, every opponent hardware deletion changes its
payment by at most half the deleted hardware, on both long and short pool
segments. Thus the sum is at most(H-b)/2<=(H-z_k)/2.
When hardware is smaller, normalize H=1. For b>=1, deletion of capped amount
d changes payment by q(1-a)(exp(d)-1), d<=1. The scalar bound needed is
q[1+2r(e-1)]<1. Maximizing(1+2A r)exp(-r), A=e-1 in(1,2), gives
2A exp(-1+1/(2A))<4exp(-3/4)<2. Division by2 proves exactly the required
inequality. The a=1 boundary has zero response.
For0<b<1 and full resource total<=1, the global derivative bound u/(2b)
through caps and ties gives at most u(1-b)/(2b)<=(1-z_k)/2.

3. FULL RESOURCE TOTAL ABOVE1: CAPPING AND CONCENTRATION
Replacing each OTHER resource report by min(report,1) preserves both the
full payment and the payment after deleting that opponent. If any remaining
report exceeds1, the pool stays long and only caps matter; otherwise its
actual remaining total already equals its capped sum. Hence the reduction
is valid even for a deletion crossing the pool tie.
Writing D=1+r and u=b-a>0, the upper response function g(d) is exponential
until d=r and affine afterward. The left slope at the join is1/2 and the
right slope1/(2b)>=1/2, so it is convex with g(0)=0.
At fixed sum of coordinates in[0,1], convex pair consolidation gives unit
coordinates and at most one fractional coordinate. This is a scalar upper
bound; it neither changes the owner set in the model nor asserts an attaining
strategic deviation. Existing fixed coordinates suffice for the consolidation.
For0<r<1 their sum is1+r-b<2:
 * If r<=b, the concentrated coordinate gives1/(2b)-q, below the desired
   (1/b-q)/2.
 * If r>=b, the two coordinates1 and r-b give the stated expression. Its
   difference from the target is half of
   r/b-1-exp(-b)+(3/2)exp(-r),
   increasing in r>=b, and equal to exp(-b)/2>0 at r=b.
For r>=1, convex secants give q(e-1)(1+r-b). This decreases in r while the
target increases. At r=1 the residual polynomial is
(1-2c)b^2-(2-3c)b+1, decreasing on[0,1] and at least c>0.
Finally the conversion from the scalar target to(H-z_k)/2 uses
(1-z_k)/(2u)=(1-b)/(2u)+(1-q)/2>=(1/b-q)/2.
All zero and equality boundaries are valid by continuity or the explicit
separate cases. Multibranch aggregation uses positive-part subadditivity,
then sums over the independently deviating actual owners.

4. GRID OVERFLOW AND CAPPED RETENTION
For fixed recipient k, sum_i v_i<=U_k/2. Thresholding its possible increases
at U_k-p_k gives min{U_k/2,(p_k-U_k/2)_+}, including both baseline-overflow
and zero-overflow cases. This bound is at most p_k/2 and min(p_k,U_k)=p_k-w_k.
Restoring each deviator's grid is an allowed upper comparison because only its
own outer coordinate is increased; the other grid reports already stay full.
The full hardware threshold H is fixed after the corresponding outer hardware
restoration. None of these statements imposes feasibility of a joint profile
realizing every independently selected counterfactual simultaneously.
For the capped-own variant, the own overflow loss L_i is nonnegative and at
most min(w_i,z_i). The identity
 capped retention defect
 =ordinary retention defect
  +(1/3)sum_{k!=i}(w_k^i-w_k)+(2/3)L_i
is correct. Thus G/3 and(2/3)L are the correct compensation coefficients;
there is no lost diagonal or missing owner-count factor.

SCOPE CHECK
These lemmas hold for arbitrary real mixed reports and arbitrary counts.
They supply a grid-sensitive charge for capped response. They do not by
themselves convert that charge into a uniform C^2 funding guarantee when
physical hardware is the bottleneck; the source correctly leaves that step
open. The sharper power-bottleneck candidate in the neighboring file is a
separate theorem and does not establish this missing implication.
