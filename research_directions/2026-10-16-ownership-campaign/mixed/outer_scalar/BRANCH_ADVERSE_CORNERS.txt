FINITE ADVERSE CORNERS FOR BRANCH-LOCAL OUTGOING RESPONSE
16 October2026. Structural upper bound using the original exponential primitive.
No uniform envelope budget or novelty claim is asserted here.

Fix one local two-pool branch and actual owner i. Write full hardware/local-power
reports h_k,l_k, with full payments phi_k. Define two profiles on exactly the
same actual owner set:
 C_power: retain every full report except replace l_i by0;
 C_hardware: retain every full report except replace h_i by0.
Use the same exponential primitive at both profiles. Put
 B_i=sum_{k!=i:h_k>l_k}[phi_k-phi_k(C_power)]
     +sum_{k!=i:h_k<l_k}[phi_k-phi_k(C_hardware)].          (1)
Every summand is nonnegative. Then for EVERY simultaneous binding underreport
(h'_i,l'_i)<= (h_i,l_i),
                    sum_{k!=i}(phi_k-phi'_k)_+ <=B_i.     (2)
Moreover each summand in(1) is the exact maximum loss of that individual
recipient over i's rectangular report box. The sum need not be jointly attained:
the adverse corners differ for the two recipient groups.

CROSS-SIGN PROOF
For an unchanged recipient k!=i, its payment is nondecreasing in other owners'
LOCAL POWER and nonincreasing in their HARDWARE when h_k>l_k. Both signs reverse
when h_k<l_k. If h_k=l_k its payment is exactly h_k at every opposing profile.
These signs persist across pool ties and cap changes.

For example, when hardware is short with total T and coefficient q, phi_k is
(1-q)h_k+q min(l_k,T). Increasing another owner's long local power decreases q;
this raises phi_k exactly when h_k>=l_k. Increasing another owner's short
hardware increases T and q. If l_k<=T, the derivative has the sign of l_k-h_k.
If l_k>T, then l_k>h_k (because h_k<=T), and both derivative contributions are
nonnegative. The opposite orientation is symmetric. Finite cap/role cells and
continuity extend the signs to arbitrary finite changes, including zero values.

For h_k>l_k, reducing i's hardware can only HELP k; restoring that hardware
weakly lowers k's counterfactual payment. Then reducing i's local power all the
way to0 can only lower it further. Thus phi'_k>=phi_k(C_power). The h_k<l_k case
uses the opposite corner. Equality is attained for that recipient at its own
specified adverse corner. This proves(1)--(2).

MULTIPLE BRANCHES AND USE WITH EXISTING CHARGES
For arbitrary mixed bundles over unboundedly many branches, define B_ij by(1)
on each branch and sum. Since the positive part of a sum is at most the sum
of positive parts,
 sum_{k!=i}(z_k-z'_k)_+ <=sum_j B_ij.
More sharply, on each branch take the minimum of B_ij and every independently
valid local allowance, such as K sqrt(z_ij D_ij), K z_ij, Z_j-z_ij, or the
hardware-directed allowance U_{-i,j}+eta h_ij. Summing those minima remains
valid. This prevents hardware on an unrelated branch from financing outgoing
harm on a branch where owner i cannot alter either input.

The bound requires only two additional primitive evaluations per branch and
owner. It changes neither the allocation rule nor the actual ownership model.
For a power-only deviator on a branch, the C_hardware profile is the full
profile, so all power-heavy recipients contribute0; only hardware-heavy
recipients can lose. The analogous statement holds for hardware-only deviators.

DEPENDENCIES
The explicit exponential primitive, its fixed-owner report domain, and continuity.
The proof is a direct cross-sign calculation. Application to a complete envelope
funding theorem still requires summing the resulting owner-specific effects.
