SHORT-STAGE RETENTION AND THE TWO-LONG-OWNER COUNT
16 October2026. Root proof note for the universal retention campaign.
Uses the original exponential two-input primitive, not water filling.

Normalize full short total to1, full capped long ratio rho>=1. Put
q(r)=exp(1-r)/2, c=1/(2e), C=1+c, lambda=1/3,
Z=T[1+q(r)(r-1)], delta=CT-Z, and potential F_i=z_i-Z/3.
Every owner has a separately chosen own underreport. Its path first reduces
its short account, then its long account. Actual owners remain fixed.

A. AGGREGATE SHORT-STAGE POTENTIAL GAIN <=delta/3
On a short-coordinate cell, let t be the current short total, r its capped
long ratio, m the number of capped long accounts, x the owner's short share,
and d its capped long share. Let e be1 if its own opposite account is capped,
0 otherwise. Then
 z_i'=1-q+q(r-m)(d-x)+qe,
 Z'=1+q[r(r-1)-m(r-2)-1].
Here prime is with respect to INCREASING own short report. All long reports
are fixed. We have0<=m<=r,0<=x,d<=1 and e>=0. Thus
 z_i'>=1-q-q(r-m)x.

If x<=2/3, direct subtraction gives
 3F_i'>=2-q(r^2+r+2)+qmr>=0.
Indeed q(r)(r^2+r+2) decreases on r>=1 (derivative q[-r^2+r-1]) and
has value2 at r=1. The owner's current short fraction decreases during
its short underreport, so x never exceeds its FULL short share s_i.
Thus only owners with s_i>2/3 can have any short-stage potential increase;
there is at most one such actual owner.

For arbitrary x<=1 and r>=2,
 3F_i'>=2-q(r^2+2r+2)+qm(r+1)>=0.
The displayed lower bound follows by setting x=1; its m coefficient is
r+1. The product q(r)(r^2+2r+2) decreases (derivative -qr^2), and at2
it is10c<2 because e>5/2. Hence short-stage gains occur only while r<2.

On1<=r<=2, delta'=C-Z'>=c-q(r-1)>=0, since the expression defining Z'
is largest at m=r. Moreover
 2Z'-3z_i'-C <=-(1+C)+q[2r^2+r+1+m(1-2r)]
             <=-(1+C)+7/(2sqrt(e))<0.
The scalar product q(r)(2r^2+r+1) is maximized at r=3/2. For the strict
comparison use e>8/3>441/169 and e<3, giving
7/(2sqrt(e))<13/6<2+c=1+C.
Rearranging gives -F_i'<=delta'/3 throughout1<=r<=2.

During a short underreport r is nondecreasing. Integrate the possible
negative derivatives only on the interval r<=2: its total potential gain
is at most one third of the decrease of nonnegative slack delta, hence at
most the ORIGINAL delta/3. The later r>=2 part cannot help. At most one
owner can have positive gain, so summing positive short-stage gains over
all separately chosen own underreports still gives at most delta/3.
Continuous cap boundaries and a zero final short total follow by limits.

B. AT MOST TWO OWNERS CAN GAIN IN THE FOLLOWING LONG STAGE
Assume full rho<2. Write s_i for full short share and d_i=min(full long_i,1),
so sum_i(s_i+d_i)=1+rho. After its own short reduction let t>=1-s_i be
the remaining short total. At t>0 normalize this intermediate point by t:
let R be its capped long ratio, x the own capped long share and y its own
short share. All other long coordinates remain at full reports, so
  R>=x+rho-d_i, x<=d_i/t, x<=1.
The initial derivative of F_i with respect to INCREASING its long account
is q[1-x+y-(2-R)/3]. Its bracket increases as the long report is reduced
under the artificial original-orientation continuation. The independent
pool-switch lemma shows that continuation bounds any actual post-switch
maximum as well. A positive long-stage gain therefore requires
  3x-3y>1+R,
which implies 2x>1+rho-d_i. For s_i<1, using t>=1-s_i gives
  d_i(3-s_i)>(1+rho)(1-s_i).
Consequently
 d_i+s_i>(1+rho)/3,
because
 (1+rho)(1-s_i)/(3-s_i)+s_i-(1+rho)/3
 =s_i[1-2(1+rho)/(3(3-s_i))]>=0
when rho<=2 and s_i<=1. If s_i=1, the same strict inequality follows
from1>(1+rho)/3. Thus at most TWO actual owners can have positive long-stage
gain. This count remains valid when each owner selected a different preceding
short underreport. For full rho>=2 the existing retention theorem gives
no potential gain at all, so that case needs no count.

The exact artificial long-stage gain bound is at most delta_intermediate/3;

If R>=2 there is no gain. Combining this with A and B yields

sum of an owner's two stage changes is at most the sum of their positive
