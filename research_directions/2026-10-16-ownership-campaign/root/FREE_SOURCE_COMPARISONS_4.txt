Lou and Luo, Strategy-proof Multi-Resource Allocation in Cloud Computing via
Adaptive-Speed Fairness, arXiv2609.38034v1,29September2026.
https://arxiv.org/html/2609.38034v1
Read model definitions and strategy-proof framework in the original, plus its
stated contributions and related work. It allocates divisible resources to
agents with reported Leontief demands, without money, and compares welfare
with an SI/EF-constrained benchmark. Our agents own binding endowments and
receive monetary payments covering every coalition, with total subsidy as
objective. Its adaptive-speed design is a potential methodological analogy;
its theorem does not directly prove our ownership guarantee. Water filling
itself is established. Different models are not proof of historical novelty.

Economics Bulletin33(4),2013,pp2938-2947,original IRS surplus-sharing paper.
https://www.accessecon.com/Pubs/EB/2013/Volume33/EB-13-V33-I4-P274.pdf
Read Sections2-3 in the original: one scalar contribution per agent, coalition
value f(sum contributions), increasing average returns, proportional core
selection and resource monotonicity. This is important prior work on payment
monotonicity under endowment changes. It does not establish a complementarity
subsidy theorem for arbitrary two-coordinate bundles. Cover metadata was not
reliably extracted, so title/authorship are not guessed here. Earlier related
Izquierdo/Rafels comparison remains in the source ledger.
