AN ASYMMETRIC OUTER PRIMITIVE AND A GENERAL171/100 FUNDING BOUND
16 October2026. Candidate theorem. Independent audit pending; no novelty claim.

Scope. This constructs a NEW truthful range mechanism for the same fixed-owner
depth-two physical game. It does not improve the proved bound on the OLD
symmetric nested envelope merely by comparison. Counts of owners and branches
are unrestricted, all reports are nonnegative reals, and every coordinatewise
whole-owner underreport is allowed. Utility is the payment; no private costs,
overreports, false identities, or extra public marginal floors are included.

MODEL AND FIXED LOCAL RULE
Owner i has(h_ij,l_ij,p_i). Put H_i=sum_j h_ij,H=sum_i H_i,P=sum_i p_i,
M=sum_j min(sum_i h_ij,sum_i l_ij),F=min(H,P+M).
On every local branch use the existing symmetric exponential primitive Phi,
with coefficient exp(1-rho)/2, and sum its actual-owner payments to z_i.
Write Z=sum_i z_i,c=1/(2e),C=1+c,B_i=p_i+z_i,K=P+CM.
The established local properties are actual-owner own-coordinate monotonicity,
coalition coverage, Z<=CM, and a role decomposition z_i=z_i^h+z_i^l with
z_i^h<=H_i, sum_i z_i^l<=min(M,H/2). Local coverage gives Z>=M.
Write L=sum_i z_i^l. These are accounting roles, not separate strategic owners.

1. ASYMMETRIC OUTER PRIMITIVE
Fix w=49/100,v=51/100,alpha=49/51=w/v. For two vectors(a,b), a denotes
hardware and b denotes the opposing resource; this distinction is public.
If their minimum total T is zero, pay zero. Otherwise orient the shorter
vector as s and longer as l, set d_i=min(l_i,T),rho=sum_i d_i/T, and pay
  Psi_i=(1-q)s_i+q d_i,
where q=w exp(1-rho) if hardware a is short, and
      q=v exp(alpha(1-rho)) if opposing resource b is short.
At a pool tie, rho=1 and both formulas give v a_i+w b_i. Thus Psi is continuous.

Coverage follows because both s(S) and sum_(i in S)min(l_i,T) are at least
min(a(S),b(S)); Psi is their convex combination with0<=q<=1.
For own-coordinate monotonicity, write q=q_*exp(beta(1-rho)), where the
orientation gives(q_*,beta)=(w,1) or(v,alpha). In both cases beta<=1 and
q_*(1+beta)<=1. On a smooth cell, increasing an uncapped own long account
gives derivative q[1-beta(d_i-s_i)/T]>=q(1-beta)>=0; a capped own long
increase has derivative zero. For an own short increase, let m count capped
long accounts and e_i be the own cap indicator. Direct differentiation gives
  Psi_i'=1-q+q e_i+beta q(rho-m)(d_i-s_i)/T
         >=1-q[1+beta rho]>=0.
The last step uses that exp(beta(1-rho))(1+beta rho) decreases on rho>=1,
with maximum1+beta at1. Integration over finitely many cap cells, continuity
at ties, and the zero-output limit prove all own-coordinate comparisons.
Coordinate telescoping gives every mixed whole-owner underreport comparison.

2. NEW RANGE MECHANISM
For each actual owner i use the compact domain
  D_i={b>=p: b_i<=B_i, sum_k b_k<=K}
and define R_i^w=max_(b in D_i) Psi_i(Hvector,b).
The full vector p+z belongs to every D_i, so the nested raw rule is majorized.
Local coalition coverage followed by outer coverage proves coalition coverage.
For an increase in own h or l, only own H_i changes in the hardware vector,
while B_i and K weakly increase and the lower bounds p stay fixed: the domain
expands and pointwise own monotonicity applies. For own grid increase eta,
the map b->b+eta e_i carries old feasible vectors into the new domain and
Psi_i is own monotone. Thus R^w is truthful on the full report domain.
If F=0 every feasible payment is zero. The one-owner case pays exactlyF.
The earlier explicit range evaluator extends by replacing the two orientation
coefficients; its endpoint arguments use beta<=1 and remain valid. This gives
O(nm+n log n) real operations plus exponentials, not a finite-bit implementation
claim. The mathematical maximization definition alone suffices for the theorem.

3. LOW K: K<=H
Put d_i=min(H_i,K),D=sum_i d_i,rho=D/K, and Zh=sum_i z_i^h. Every chosen
opposing vector has total at mostK, so the opposing pool is short throughout.
The same monotonic-total comparison as for the old range gives
  payment_i<=B_i+v exp(alpha(1-rho))(d_i-B_i)_+
            <=B_i+v exp(alpha(1-rho))(d_i-z_i^h).
Here z_i^h<=min(H_i,K). Summing and maximizing over rho>=1 gives
  sum R_i^w<=P+Z+(Kv/alpha)exp(alpha-1-alpha Zh/K).
The scalar maximum occurs at rho=Zh/K+1/alpha>=1. Since Zh>=Z-M, the
right side is at most P+Z+(Kv/alpha)exp(alpha-1-alpha(Z-M)/K).
Its derivative in Z is1-v exp(alpha-1-alpha(Z-M)/K)>0, so replace Z byCM.
Now F=P+M and K/F=1+t with t=cM/F in[0,c]. The resulting ratio is
  (1+t)[1+(v/alpha)exp(alpha/(1+t)-1)].
Its derivative is1+(v/alpha)exp(alpha/(1+t)-1)[1-alpha/(1+t)]>0.
Therefore the ratio is at most
  C[1+(v/alpha)exp(alpha/C-1)]<171/100.                 (A_low)
The strict final comparison is certified with rational Taylor bounds below.

4. HIGH P: P>=H
Normalize H=1. Every feasible opposing total is at least1, so hardware is
short and its coefficient is w exp(1-rho). The previously proved negative-gap
coupling argument applies with coefficient w in place of1/2. For completeness,
let u_i=min(p_i,1),v_i=min(B_i,1),U=sum u_i>=1,
D_+=sum(v_i-H_i)_+,D_-=sum(H_i-v_i)_+, and t=CM<=C.
Positive gaps pay at most w exp(1-U)(v_i-H_i)_+; negative gaps retain at
least w exp(1-U-t)(H_i-v_i)_+. Baseline overflow P-U is unavoidable and
belongs to opponents of each negative-gap owner, proving the latter bound.
Thus total<=1+w exp(1-U)[D_+-exp(-t)D_-]. The role bound gives
D_+<=U+1/2, and D_- >=max(0,D_+-U-Z+1). Optimizing these coupled gaps,
using Z<=t<=C<3/2, and then U>=1 gives
  sum R_i^w<=1+w[3/2-exp(-C)(3/2-C)]
             <1+(57/40)w<171/100.                    (A_high)
Indeed C<6/5,exp(C)<4 imply exp(-C)(3/2-C)>3/40.

5. INTERMEDIATE P<H<K
Normalize H=1,p=P/H,m=M/H,k=p+Cm,f=p+m. Define
  q0=w exp(1-k),
  q1=v exp(alpha(1-1/p)) for p>0, and q1=0 at p=0,
  Q=1-q1.
Then Q>=w>=q0. For a positive full account gap B_i-H_i, every opposing-short
choice has excess at most Q(B_i-H_i), since its total is at leastp and its
coefficient q is at leastq1; a hardware-short choice has coefficient at mostw.
For a negative gap, the deficit is at least q0(H_i-B_i): hardware-short
capped total is at mostk, while opposing-short deficit coefficient1-q is
at leastw. The total positive gap is at most p+L, and positive minus negative
gaps equals p+Z-1. Consequently, uniformly over separate recipient choices,
  sum R_i^w<=1+Qp-q0+(Q-q0)L+q0 Z.                     (A_role)

The established symmetric LOCAL support functions, for Q>=q0>0, are
  (Q-q0)L+q0 Z<=H[q0+(Q/2)exp(-q0/Q)],
  (Q-q0)L+q0 Z<=M[Q+(q0/2)exp(-Q/q0)].
Their full proofs are in root/JOINT_ROLE_TRADEOFF.txt; the local primitive
has not changed, so this invocation is valid.

If F=H, the H-support bound gives
  sum R_i^w<=1+Q[p+(1/2)exp(-q0/Q)].                   (A_W)
For fixedp this increases withm, because q0 decreases. Setm=1. Replacing
C by148/125 further decreases q0 and raises this bound. The exact interval
certificate covers everyp in[0,1] and proves(A_W)<171/100.

If F<H, the M-support bound gives
  sum R_i^w/F<=Q+(1-q0)/f+(m/f)(q0/2)exp(-Q/q0).
Here 1<k<=Cf, q0>=w exp(1-Cf), and the function
[1-w exp(1-x)]/x is nonincreasing forx>=1, since w<=1/2. Hence
(1-q0)/f<=C(1-w)=Cv. AlsoQ<=1,m/f<=1,q0<=w,Q/q0>=1. Therefore
  sum R_i^w/F<=1+Cv+w/(2e)=2-w+c<171/100.            (A_power)
