EXACT OBSTRUCTION TO FIXED POSITIVE MIXTURES OF ATTENUATED MARGINALS
16 October 2026. Candidate structural result; historical priority unassessed.

MOTIVATION AND CLASS
For a homogeneous production value F, an automatically own-report-monotone
payment candidate is
  g_i(t;b)=F(b_i+t b_-i)-F(t b_-i),  t>=0,
where b_-i is the aggregate resource vector of all other actual owners. The
second term is independent of i's report. A fixed positive mixture retains
nonnegativity and own-report monotonicity. Allow a different nonnegative
measure mu_i for each owner, depending on the fixed technology and owner set,
but not on the report profile:
  y_i(b)=integral g_i(t;b) d mu_i(t).
Assume payments are finite on all profiles. This includes fixed attenuations,
finite positive weighted averages, and continuous randomized attenuation.
The weights need not sum to one, need not be anonymous, and t may exceed one.
The domain allows every fixed actual identity to report either input. It is
not an externally imposed partition into permanently pure-role identities.

THEOREM
Already for the two-input technology F(H,P)=min(H,P), any such rule covering
every actual-owner coalition at every nonnegative real report profile has
worst-case total funding factor at least
  2(n-2)/(n-1)=2-2/(n-1)
on every fixed owner set of size n>=3. Consequently this mechanism class
cannot have a uniform funding factor strictly below2 as owner counts grow.
The assertion is an obstruction to this particular class, not to arbitrary
truthful mechanisms: the existing mixed-owner two-input rule achieves
C=1+1/(2e)<2 on this same technology.

PROOF
For each actual owner i set
  a_i=integral min(1,t) d mu_i(t).
This is finite because it is i's payment when i owns one hardware unit,
another owner owns one power unit, and all remaining owners report zero.

Fix i. At the forcing profile, i owns(1,0), while each of the n-1 other
owners owns(0,1/(n-2)). Total power is c=(n-1)/(n-2). For every other
owner j, removing its power leaves total power exactly1 and hardware1.
For every t>=0, the t-scaled other owners therefore already produce t;
adding j's nonnegative power cannot raise output above the unchanged
hardware capacity t. Hence g_j(t)=0, and y_j=0.
For i, the others have no hardware, so g_i(t)=min(1,ct).
Grand-coalition coverage at this profile requires
  1<=integral min(1,ct) d mu_i(t)<=c a_i,
using min(1,ct)<=c min(1,t) for c>=1. Thus a_i>=(n-2)/(n-1).
The same argument applies separately to every owner identity, without
requiring their measures to be equal.

Now take a single profile at which distinct owners i,j own(1,0),(0,1),
and all other actual owners own zero. Its production is1. Directly,
g_i(t)=g_j(t)=min(1,t), and every other owner's marginal is zero.
Total payment is a_i+a_j>=2(n-2)/(n-1), proving the claim.

All profiles use the same actual owner identities, and every requirement is
an all-profile coalition inequality. The proof is exact and does not require
simultaneous attainment of counterfactual payment coordinates, resource-owner
cloning, a numerical limit interchange, or a finite grid extrapolation.

SCOPE AND CONSEQUENCE
The measures may depend on n, so simply retuning a fixed smoothing kernel as
the owner count grows does not evade the obstruction. Report-dependent
kernels, signed mixtures, additional transfers or mechanisms that do not use
these marginals are outside the statement. The two-resource class is a
subclass of broader sparse-production domains; a rule of this form intended
to work on those domains must also survive it. No lower bound on the optimal
subsidy for the whole problem follows. The consequence is to stop the fixed
positive attenuation-mixture route and retain the owner-coupled construction.
