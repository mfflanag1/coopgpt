EXPLICIT RANGE MECHANISM FOR MIXED-OWNER DEPTH-TWO PRODUCTION
16 October2026. Candidate conventional theorem; audit pending. The physical
model and previously proved mixed two-pool primitive are those of
../mixed/RANGE_ROBUST_ENVELOPE_THEOREM.txt. This construction is a new
mechanism, not a claim that the previously false nested mechanism is truthful.

DEFINITIONS
For arbitrary fixed actual owners i=1,...,n and branches j, compute local
mixed-two-pool payments z_i as before. Put H_i=sum_j h_ij, H=sum_i H_i,
P=sum_i p_i, M=sum_j min(h_j,l_j), C0=1+1/(2e),
B_i=p_i+z_i and K=P+C0 M. Let Phi_i(a,b) denote the previously proved
mixed-owner two-pool payment, with zero payment when min(sum a,sum b)=0.
Define the compact range
 D_i={b in R_+^n: b_k>=p_k for all k, b_i<=B_i, sum_k b_k<=K}.
It is nonempty, since p belongs and B_i>=p_i, K>=P. It also contains the
full local-payment accounts p+z, since sum z<=C0 M.
Define
 R_i = max_{b in D_i} Phi_i((H_k)_k,b).
This maximizes over abstract opposite-input accounts; it does not alter
physical owners or assert those accounts arise from feasible underreports.

TRUTHFULNESS
Increase any coordinate of owner's own h/l bundle. Its H_i, B_i and K are
nondecreasing (local primitive own-bundle monotonicity and local production
monotonicity); all other H_k and all p are fixed. Thus D_i expands. At each
old feasible b, increasing only own outer H_i cannot lower Phi_i. Maximizing
shows own-h/l monotonicity. If owner increases p_i by delta>=0, then B_i and
K both increase by delta. The map b -> b+delta e_i carries every old D_i
vector into the new D_i, and Phi_i is nondecreasing in its own b_i.
Coordinate telescoping gives every whole-bundle comparison, with fixed owners.
No assumption of increasing other owners' local payments is used or needed.

COVERAGE AND BUDGET
R_i>=N_i=Phi_i(H,p+z), and the nested rule N covers all real coalitions by
local coverage followed by outer coverage. Thus R covers all coalitions.
It is nonnegative and finite. If an owner's whole resource bundle is zero,
H_i=B_i=p_i=0, so its payment is zero. If grand F=0 all payments vanish.
Every vector in D_i satisfies the range inequalities(2) of the frozen range
proof. That proof bounds each owner's payment uniformly over those vectors,
then sums inequalities using the same full-profile quantities. Consequently
it applies directly to R even though different owners use different vectors.
Together with SEVEN_FOURTHS_SHARPENING.txt it proves
  sum_i R_i <=(7/4)F, F=min(H,P+M),
for every real profile, with owner and branch counts unrestricted.

CLOSED FORM: ZERO AND ONE-OWNER CASES
If H=0 or P+M=0, pay all zero. For n=1, each local primitive pays its full
local output to that owner, so B_1=P+M and R_1=min(H,B_1)=F.
Below assume n>=2 and H>0. For each i put a=H_i, beta=B_i, Pminus=P-p_i.
Note beta+Pminus=P+z_i<=P+C0 M=K. Compute the maximum of the following
short-pool and long-pool options whenever their regions are nonempty.

SHORT OPPOSING POOL (exists if P<=H)
Set U=min(K,H). For total opposing capacity T in[P,U], the largest possible
own account is c(T)=min(beta,T-Pminus). This is attainable, since others can
absorb any residual while staying at least their p values (n>=2). At fixed T,
Phi_i=(1-q(T))c(T)+q(T)min(a,T),
q(T)=(1/2)exp(1-rho(T)), rho(T)=sum_k min(H_k,T)/T.
Its coefficient1-q(T) is positive; therefore the best own account is c(T).
At T=0 interpret the whole payment as zero.

The maximum is attained at the following explicit total:
  Tstar=U if beta<=a;
  Tstar=min(U,beta+Pminus) if beta>=a.
At equality beta=a the choices give equal optima. Evaluate the displayed
Phi formula at Tstar; this is Rshort_i.
Proof: Up to T0=beta+Pminus, set others exactly at p and raise only the own
account c=T-Pminus. Primitive own-account monotonicity proves this part is
nondecreasing. Beyond T0 the own account is beta and q(T) is nondecreasing.
If beta<=a, before T=a the nonnegative difference T-beta and q both increase;
after T=a the nonnegative difference a-beta is constant, so the payment
continues to increase. If beta>=a, all T>=T0 satisfy T>=a, the difference
is a-beta<=0, and payment is nonincreasing. These cover all T and tie cases.

LONG OPPOSING POOL (exists if K>=H)
Put d=min(beta,H),
 Sminus=sum_{k!=i}min(p_k,H), Wminus=sum_{k!=i}(p_k-H)_+.
If d>=a, set rho_min=max(1,(d+Sminus)/H) and
 Rlong_i=a+(1/2)exp(1-rho_min)(d-a).
If d<=a, set rho_max=min((d+(n-1)H)/H,(K-Wminus)/H) and
 Rlong_i=a+(1/2)exp(1-rho_max)(d-a).
At d=a either expression is a. The relevant rho is always at least1.

Proof of the long formula: For fixed own opposing account c and d_c=min(c,H),
payment is a+(1/2)exp(1-rho)(d_c-a). When d_c>=a maximize this by minimizing
capped total. Its minimum is max(H,d_c+Sminus). If c+Pminus>=H, leave other
accounts at p; their capped total already is at leastH. If c+Pminus<H, then
c<H and every other p_k<H; add only the missing capacity to reach totalH,
so capped total equalsH. The extra is possible because K>=H and n>=2.
Thus rho_min(d_c)=max(1,(d_c+Sminus)/H). On d_c in[a,H],
exp(1-rho_min)(d_c-a) is nondecreasing: off its single corner the derivative
is exp(1-rho_min)[1-(d_c-a)rho_min']>=0, as rho_min' is0 or1/H.
Hence the best positive-gap choice is c=beta (or any c with the same cap).

The equality case d_c=a is immediate: the payment is a, regardless of rho.
When d_c<a, necessarily c<H, so d_c=c. Maximize capped total by raising
other opposing accounts up toH until their remaining total budget is used.
Their unavoidable wasted mass aboveH is Wminus. The maximum capped total is
min(c+(n-1)H,K-Wminus). It is attainable: start from p and fill unsaturated
other accounts, with budget K-c-Pminus>=0. The resulting total can be kept
at leastH, since K>=H and either the other accounts already include a capped
one or their combined cap allowance plus c is at leastH. This uses n>=2.
The function a-(1/2)exp(1-rho_max(c))(a-c) is nondecreasing in c, because
rho_max(c) is nondecreasing and a-c is nonnegative and decreases. Thus within
the negative-gap region the largest admissible own c is best. If beta crosses
a, a nonnegative-gap payment is at least a and dominates negative-gap choices.
This proves c=beta attains the full long-regime optimum in every case.

EVALUATION COST AND SCOPE
R_i=max of its available short and long options. The regions cover every D_i
vector by its total; they meet consistently at totalH. Precompute the sums of
min(p_k,H) and (p_k-H)_+ for all long options. Sort H_k and form prefix sums:
for any T, sum min(H_k,T) equals the prefix sum belowT plusT times the number
aboveT. Thus all short options take O(n log n) comparisons/arithmetic after
sorting; an unsorted implementation takes O(n^2). Forming all local primitives
from a dense n-by-m report table takes O(nm). Each option uses an exponential.
These are real-arithmetic/exponential-operation counts, not bit-complexity or
finite-precision incentive guarantees. The mathematical payment formula is exact.
No private-cost, overreport, false-name, merging, collusive or strict-truthfulness
guarantee is claimed. This is not a deployment or a measured infrastructure gain.
