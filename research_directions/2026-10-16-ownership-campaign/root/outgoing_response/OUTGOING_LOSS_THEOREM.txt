OUTGOING PAYMENT LOSS IS CONTROLLED BY THE DEVIATOR'S FULL PAYMENT
16 October2026. Root candidate requiring independent audit.
Original exponential local primitive, fixed actual owners, all real reports.

CLAIM
For any actual owner i and any coordinatewise own-bundle underreport, let z_k
and z_k' be full and counterfactual local payments. Then
 sum_{k!=i}(z_k-z_k')_+ <=(e-1)z_i.                    (OL)
The same statement holds after aggregating arbitrarily many branches, where
owner i may change its whole bundle. Branch inputs are fixed nonnegative
coordinates of that bundle (or fixed coordinatewise nondecreasing input maps),
so a bundle underreport induces a coordinatewise underreport on each branch. This bounds one owner's outgoing effect;
it is not a bound on the sum over independently deviating owners relative to
hardware or grid. No novelty or complete nested-funding claim is made.

PRIMITIVE AND ROLE ACCOUNTING
At full short total T>0 and capped long ratio r>=1, q=exp(1-r)/2,
z_k=(1-q)x_k+q min(l_k,T). Regard the two displayed nonnegative terms as
accounting contributions. This does not create strategic players. Every
actual recipient's positive loss is at most the sum of its two role losses.
Coordinates in the long input can be treated as pure-role accounts for this
accounting comparison because splitting the two input labels changes neither
pool total nor any cap within either input. Actual owner i stays fixed.

1. DECREASING A SHORT-INPUT COORDINATE
Keep the long reports fixed and decrease the short total from T to T-a, with
0<=a<=x_i. The input stays short. Other short-role payments weakly increase,
since q decreases; only long-role contributions can lose. Their total is
q(U)D(U), where D=sum_k min(l_k,U), r=D/U, and m counts long coordinates
strictly capped by U. On any cap cell,
 d[qD]/dU=q[r^2-m(r-1)] <=q r^2<=2/e.
The maximum of r^2 exp(1-r)/2 on r>=1 is2/e at r=2. All cap boundaries and
zero limits are continuous. Thus the sum of all long-role losses, even if
owner i's own long-role loss is included, is at most(2/e)a. Since q<=1/2,
this is at most(4/e)(1-q)x_i.

2. DECREASING A LONG-INPUT COORDINATE
Keep short reports fixed. It suffices to bound complete deletion of i's long
amount b, because every other short-role payment decreases monotonically as
this coordinate falls, whereas every other long-role contribution increases.
The latter statements also hold after the pools switch: the old long input
then becomes short and its remaining accounts acquire coefficient1-q_new;
the old short input becomes long with capped payments q_new min(x_k,T_new).
The cross-input monotonicity follows directly from q'(U)=q(r-m)/U>=0.

Normalize T=1 and let d=min(b,1), so i's full long-role payment is qd.
If the remaining long total is at least1, there is no pool switch. Deleting
b lowers the capped long ratio by d, so total short-role loss is
 q(exp(d)-1)<=q(e-1)d,
using the convex secant exp(d)-1<=(e-1)d for0<=d<=1.

If the remaining long total is r<1, all those accounts are uncapped at the
full profile and the full capped ratio is d+r>=1. Put q=exp(1-d-r)/2.
After full deletion, the new capped ratio of the old short input is R in
[1,1/r], and its aggregate payment is q_new R r, where
q_new=exp(1-R)/2. Since R exp(1-R) decreases for R>=1, this aggregate is
at least exp(1-1/r)/2. At r=0 use its continuous limit0.
The total short-role loss is therefore at most
 1-q-exp(1-1/r)/2.
We claim it is at most(e-1)qd. Equivalently,
 A(r)exp(d)<=1+(e-1)d,
 A(r)=2exp(r-1)-exp(r-1/r).
For0<r<=1, A(r)<=1. To prove this set x=1-r and multiply the inequality by
exp(1-r); it becomes
 exp(x)+exp(-x/(1-x))>=2, 0<=x<1.
Its derivative is nonnegative because the log of the ratio of its positive
and negative derivative terms is
 x+x/(1-x)+2log(1-x)=1/r-r+2log r>=0.
The last function equals0 at r=1 and has derivative
 -(1-r)^2/r^2<=0. Hence the exponential sum is at least its value2 at x=0.
At r=0, A(0)=2/e<=1 directly. Finally exp(d)<=1+(e-1)d for0<=d<=1,
which proves the claimed long-stage loss bound through the switch.
Restoring T gives loss<=(e-1)q min(b,T).

3. WHOLE-OWNER REPORTS
For an arbitrary actual owner's two-coordinate underreport, first lower its
full-profile short coordinate to the reported amount, then lower its long
coordinate. The first stage keeps the original orientation. Its external
positive loss is at most(4/e) times the owner's FULL short-role payment.
At the intermediate profile, the owner's long-role payment is at most its
full long-role payment, since both q(U) and min(b,U) increase with U.
The second-stage bound is therefore at most(e-1) times its FULL long-role
payment. Since4/e<=e-1 (for example e>8/3 implies e^2-e-4>0), positive-part
subadditivity across the two stages proves(OL). Ties may use either initial
orientation. Zero-output branches have zero at every underreport.

For multiple branches, apply this argument on every branch and use positive-
part subadditivity over branch contributions for each actual recipient. The
right side sums to(e-1)z_i. No owner is cloned or reselected, and no finite
report-grid hypothesis or bounded owner count appears.

POTENTIAL USE AND LIMIT
For a saturated grid owner, the outer cap can hide its own local payment loss.
The theorem bounds how much that same owner can nevertheless lower other local
accounts, using its full local payment as a scale. It may complement the
half-gap incoming-response lemma. Converting this to C^2 funding remains open.

SHARPNESS OF THE OUTGOING CONSTANT
One hardware owner has report1 and two pure local-resource owners each have
report1. At the full profile q=c=1/(2e), so each resource owner receives c.
After one resource owner deletes its report, the two remaining inputs balance
and the hardware owner's payment falls from1-c to1/2. The other resource
owner's payment increases. Thus the total external positive loss is
1/2-c=(e-1)c, attaining(OL) exactly. This is sharpness of the response lemma,
not a subsidy lower bound or a bounded example offered as the main result.
