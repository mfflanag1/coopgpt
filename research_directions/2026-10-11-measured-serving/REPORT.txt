Measured serving and energy study — 11 October 2026

DECISION
The full promotion gate is NOT met. We obtained free measured evidence for two
hardware pairs, implemented a request-level online scheduler, and ran the frozen
study. The primary conditional replay improves on-time completions from 69/92 to
71/92 (2.17 percentage points of offered demand), below the declared 5-point gate.
It is useful engineering evidence, not validation of the earlier synthetic gain,
a pooled deployment, an energy saving, or a new theorem. Do not extend this result
to scarce peak power or private opportunity costs yet.

WHAT WAS ACQUIRED
WattGPU / Watt Counts supplies 30 measured runs of the same reported
meta-llama/Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct model on a single A100-SXM4-40GB and single H100NVL.
The authors report a common FP16/vLLM protocol: five repeats each at 0.017 requests/s,
0.33 requests/s, and offline batch. Energy is whole-run GPU board joules, including
idle in server mode. The 20 server runs contain 1,016 completed-request timing
observations across both GPUs; batch timings are not per-request observations.

This is a credible within-source hardware comparison with limitations: missing
exact software/checkpoint revision, arrival timestamps, request identities, raw
power timeline and isolated resident-idle measurements. High-load arrays cannot
be paired by index. Prompts are very short; the low-load sample has only 30 timings
per GPU with repeated questions. It does not identify a realistic long-context
concurrent server or transferable per-request costs.

MELODI supplies 5,099 exact-prompt pairs on two complete reported gemma:7b/Ollama
configurations: Quadro RTX 4000 laptop and RTX A2000 workstation. It has request
execution times and request-window GPU energy, but not pinned checkpoint,
quantization, engine, offload or power-limit metadata. These are NOT clean
GPU-only interventions or interchangeable measurements of the A100/H100 system.
Only 461 pairs have identical response text. The frozen warm-and-comparable filter
retains 460; a prompt-hash split gives 368 training and 92 held-out requests. This
retrospectively selected 9.02% subset is not representative production demand or a
prospective eligibility rule. Identity of responses does not establish model
identity, quality, or functional equivalence on excluded prompts.

The source collector's contemporaneous code integrates from 0.5 seconds before
request start through 0.5 seconds after finish; its exact deployed version is
unrecorded. Source requests are serial and have gaps exceeding 3 seconds. Our
replay therefore assumes an API-duration + 4-second slot: 0.5 seconds before, 0.5 seconds after, and 3 seconds of
cooldown. Response completion occurs before the 3.5-second trailing tail. Stored
window energy is charged once. Between-window idle and cooldown energy remain
unmeasured and EXCLUDED. A diagnostic on all matched rows bounds clock-minus-API
overhead below 0.014 seconds; this small client overhead is not in the replay.
CPU energy is not used: historical subprocess accounting is uncertain. The paper
also reports background GPU processes on the workstation. No facility-energy,
carbon, GPU-only efficiency, or real economic-budget interpretation is established.

Other free candidates were checked, not silently substituted: Splitwise has zero
H100 power placeholders; BEAM has incompatible hardware bundles plus energy/latency
window and GPU-inventory issues; ML.ENERGY public summaries lack the required raw
request associations and its fuller current dataset is gated. No paid/gated data,
new GPU rentals, or actual hardware experiment were used.

HELD-OUT A100/H100 CHECK
For each GPU, the first three source-ID repeats at each server rate train
E_run = P_proxy * observed_duration + e_proxy * observed_completed_requests.
The last two repeats per rate are held out, giving four test runs per GPU.
The fit is ordinary least squares with no extra intercept and no coefficient
clipping. A100 gives 66.148 W and 426.145 J/request; H100 gives 89.073 W and 356.078 J/request.
All eight held-out errors are under 10%; maximum 5.163% for A100 and 2.433% for H100.
These are CONDITIONAL energy reconstructions given realized count and duration,
not advance workload forecasts, independently measured idle power or job energy.
The heldout runs repeat the same two offered-load regimes; this checks repeat-to-repeat stability, not generalization to new loads. The fitted coefficients cannot be carried unverified into the MELODI replay.

All 508 recorded server requests per GPU satisfy the illustrative TTFT <= 1 second
AND completion <= 15 seconds thresholds. Among the 175 held-out completions per GPU,
high-load p95 completion is 3.313 seconds for A100 and 1.530 seconds for H100. These
are descriptive completed-request samples, not an offered-demand loss audit,
stable population p99, independent natural requests, or a pooled deployment SLA.

REQUEST-LEVEL EXPERIMENT
Two fixed owners retain one serial configuration each. Both arms share exactly
the same demand queue, forecasts, admission policy, hardware and deadlines.
Autarky means separate energy accounts, not disconnected demand queues; pooling
allows their request-window energy allowances to transfer. Every unique request
arrives once, with no duplicated standalone demand. Synthetic fixed-spacing
arrivals are declared rather than inferred from service-dependent source times.

The controller sees 90th-percentile training reservations by input-token bin for
service and energy. It does NOT see held-out output lengths, true service costs,
or future completion events. It chooses the oldest currently feasible request;
among eligible idle configurations it chooses the lower forecast energy. At
slot completion, realized energy settles the reservation. Lateness and debt remain
in the outcome; no actual request is clipped or retrospectively rejected.

This conservative forecast covers 89–91% of held-out duration/energy observations.
Its aggregate energy reservations exceed actual totals by 48–49%, as one would
expect of quantile reservations rather than mean estimates. That is not evidence
that every predictor fails, and not a 10% accurate advance total-energy model.
It also offers no deterministic guarantee against unseen long jobs or budget debt.

PRIMARY RESULT
92 unique requests, 10 seconds apart, 60-second response deadline, equal owner shares,
and total measured-window allowance 5,426.966 J. The allowance is 0.75 times 92 times
the TRAINING average uniform-routing energy 78.651684 J; no held-out costs tune it.
                              Separate accounts       Pooled account
On-time completions                  69                     71
Late completions                      1                      0
Rejected without starting            22                     21
Request-window GPU joules        5,287.235              5,384.530
Actual budget overrun                 0                      0

Pooling adds two on-time completions (+2.17 points of offered demand, +2.90% relative
to 69), using 97.295 J more measured-window energy. This is not an energy saving.
The separate-account late response took 61.633 seconds from arrival despite a
50.231-second forecast. Pooling meets this case's response/budget constraints but
misses the 5-point gain threshold; the stricter two-arm screen also retains that
baseline lateness. No finite-job payment or core guarantee is asserted.

POST-REVIEW DIAGNOSTIC (EXPLORATORY)
With the same primary arrivals and deadlines but nonbinding energy accounts,
both arms complete all 92 requests on time. With the original budgets and a
nonbinding deadline, separate accounts complete 69 and pooling 71. Thus the
primary restriction is the artificial window-energy allowance, with residual
reservations and routing affecting the result; it is not evidence that pooling
solves a difficult physical-capacity or tail-latency bottleneck. The original
+2 on-time difference contains one extra admission and one fewer late completion.
Changing deadlines changes dispatch order, so this ablation does not identify a
pure causal contribution of each mechanism. See PRIMARY_DIAGNOSTICS.json and
run python3 diagnose_primary.py. These checks followed independent review and
were not part of the frozen sensitivity family.

PRESPECIFIED SENSITIVITY FAMILY
Cross product: interarrival 30/10/3 seconds; deadline 30/60/120 seconds; total allowance
factor 0.5/0.75/1; owner 0 share 0.2/0.5/0.8: 81 cases, each with both arms.
Positive on-time gain in 58 cases, zero in 18, negative in 5. Differences range from -5 to +52
requests. Nine pass the conditional 5-point/zero-lateness/no-debt screen in both
arms. Pooling has late completions in 12 cases; separate accounts in 21. At least
one arm exceeds its actual account budget in 4 cases. All adverse cases are saved.
These are sensitivities of the SAME 92 held-out records, not 81 independent replications.
Skewed allowance ownership can create large artificial stranding; cherry-picking
the maximum would not validate a consortium. Negative cases show this greedy
online policy need not improve when constraints relax; they do not prove that an
optimal pooling scheduler has lower feasible value than an optimal separate one.

INTERPRETATION AND NEXT DECISIVE EXPERIMENT
The earlier synthetic pooling story survives only as a possibility, not a verified
practical effect. Request uncertainty, demand timing, scheduler choices and quota
placement materially change it. The immediate frontier is trustworthy measurement
and a stronger deadline-aware scheduler, before more elaborate economic theory.

A closing experiment needs two documented nodes running the SAME pinned checkpoint,
tokenizer, quantization, engine and decoding policy; explicit CPU/GPU offload and
power limits; separate warmup; resident-idle measurements before/after; timestamped
board power over the entire run (preferably wall power too); and unique request
arrival/start/first-token/token/finish timestamps. Run identical prompt/generation
policies first serially, then in held-out representative concurrent arrival windows.
Hide realized output length from the controller. Compare both quota rules on the
same offered demand, including rejections, late requests, idle and drain energy.
A new source release with those fields could substitute for new hardware access.
No external contact or spending was initiated.

VERIFICATION / ARTIFACTS
22 focused unit tests pass, including hidden-outcome guards, nonoverlap, unique
requests, exact event ordering, retained debt/lateness, response-vs-cooldown timing,
quantile bins, unit conversion, held-out budget exclusion and signed OLS recovery.
The driver independently reconciles request IDs, slots, measured costs, terminal
outcomes, chronological energy reservations and final account balances for all 162 replays. A separate agent checked
primary physical records, the training-only forecasts and budget reference.
Completed cross-model review and clean-directory reproduction are recorded in
REVIEW.txt and VERIFICATION.json. Internal model audit is not human peer review.
PROTOCOL.txt was frozen before replay outcomes; source descriptive inspection was
already known. Source-lane methods were frozen before their own fitting. All
outcomes, extraction/replay code, licenses and immutable data hashes are retained.
