Verdict: revise.

The candidate has a strong central diagnosis—the universal indispensable power owner trivializes the stated weak truthfulness problem—but several source characterizations and the proposed gap need correction before acceptance.

1. High: P4 materially overstates the assignment-game correspondence. Assignment games are a subclass of \(r=2\) sparse LP games: bipartite matching games with unit vertex capacities and one owner per vertex. They are not “exactly \(r=2\), unit-capacity sparse LP games”; general two-row-support packing LPs need not have bipartite matching structure. Moreover, Solymosi’s main result is Theorem 9, and the paper gives three component types plus compositions—not simply “PMAS essentially requires veto structure.” The paper itself includes a dominant-veto-mixed-pair class. See [Solymosi, Theorem 9 and §3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2210.17373).

2. High: P5’s mechanism implication is overstated. Immorlica–Mahdian–Mirrokni do not establish the blanket statement “group-strategyproof + upper-continuity ⇒ cross-monotone” for the original mechanism. Their Theorem 4.1 constructs a semi-cross-monotone scheme from a group-strategyproof mechanism; Theorem 4.2 is an existence equivalence for upper-continuous mechanisms and cross-monotone schemes. The exact profit-sharing result closest to this project is omitted: Theorem 3.6 rules out \(o(n)\)-budget-balanced cross-monotone profit sharing for maximum-flow games even though their core is nonempty. That is more relevant than the cited facility-location, vertex-cover, and set-cover cost-recovery factors. See [original paper, §§3.5 and 4.3](https://immorlica.com/pubs/costshare.pdf).

3. Medium: the veto citation is not an exact theorem match. Bachrach et al. Theorem 1 concerns simple \(\{0,1\}\)-valued games. The sparse-LP game is generally not simple. The all-to-power-owner rule is nevertheless correct by the candidate’s direct proof, but it should be labeled an elementary extension/analogy, not “the TU-LP version of P1 Theorem 1.” [Bachrach et al., Theorem 1](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2016/02/CoS-SAGT-2009.pdf).

4. Medium: P3 does not satisfy the candidate’s own verification representation. It says all listed texts were “fetched and read,” but later admits §6—the part invoked for the aggregator’s maximum share—was not read. The paper actually gives the maximum aggregator share in §6: in the balanced case it is \(v(N)-|U|\varepsilon^*\), hence \(v(N)\) when \(\varepsilon^*=0\). Cite that exact statement or remove the comparison. Also explain that the aggregator is a gatekeeper, not necessarily a supplier of a physical resource row. See [Bigi et al., §6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.05291v2).

5. Medium: the recommended \(\beta(2)\) gap is not yet precisely defined. It must specify whether \(A,p\) are fixed or ranged over, whether rules may depend on them, the report domain, whether reports are only coordinatewise underreports, nonnegative transfers, treatment of profiles with \(F(c)=0\), and whether the supremum ranges over games as well as reports. “Binding underreports” is not a definition. As written, \(\sum_iU_i/F(c)\) is undefined at zero-output profiles.

6. Low: the common-pool and cost-of-stability checks are substantially accurate. The common-pool paper indeed assumes a priced external resource required by every product and proves Theorems 8, 18, and 23 under the conditions reported. The \(v(S)=1\) example is exactly the \(n-1\) CoS construction in Bachrach et al. §3.1. [Common-pool paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1602.00525), [CoS paper](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2016/02/CoS-SAGT-2009.pdf).

The trivialization result should be retained:

\[
U_{i^*}(b)=F\!\left(\sum_i b_i\right),\qquad U_i(b)=0\quad(i\ne i^*)
\]

is exactly budget-balanced, coalition-covering, and weakly own-report monotone when every activity requires a resource owned entirely by \(i^*\). This relies critically on weak payment-only truthfulness: all other owners are indifferent to withholding. It does not yield strict truthfulness, efficient implementation under outside options, or group-strategyproofness.

A better single live gap is:

> For fixed bipartite two-resource production—activities consume one unit from each side, capacities are additively owned by multiple agents, and no agent is a veto player—determine the least \(\alpha\) such that a nonnegative payment rule can cover every coalition at every capacity profile, be coordinatewise monotone in each owner’s reported capacity under binding underreports, and satisfy \(\sum_iU_i(b)\le \alpha F(c(b))\) whenever \(F(c(b))>0\).

Then ask first whether \(\alpha=1\) is possible. This avoids the assignment-game equivalence error, removes the universal-owner trivialization, and isolates the own-capacity incentive premium from static core cost. No novelty conclusion is warranted from the reported searches.
