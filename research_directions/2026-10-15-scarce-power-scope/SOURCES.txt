Conclusion: no historical novelty confirmation. The mixed-owner extension and
star corollary are internally proved here, but the bounded source pass does not
establish that they are absent from all prior work. The earlier exclusive-pool
exponential rule and 9/8 lower witness are PROJECT RESULTS, not newly discovered

1. Bachrach et al., The Cost of Stability in Coalitional Games (SAGT 2009).
https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2016/02/CoS-SAGT-2009.pdf
Coordinator read original PDF, section 3.1, printed p6 (PDF index 5): the game
v(empty)=0 and v(S)=1 for every nonempty S has stability subsidy n-1. Thus our
previous public-power example is an exact instance of an established example,
not merely analogous to a known issue. Its LP formulation is static; it does
not impose one capacity-monotone rule across report profiles. Use B(b) to keep
this established static funding need separate from the incentive premium.

2. Gutierrez, Llorca, Sanchez-Soriano and Mosquera, On the effects of a
common-pool resource on cooperation among firms with linear technologies (2016).
https://arxiv.org/pdf/1602.00525
Coordinator read abstract, introduction and model section 3 (printed pp5-7).
The input is externally managed, priced at a fixed unit cost, essential to every
activity, and not endowed to producers. Coalition access may depend on the
outsider partition. This directly precedes our institutional warning. Their
objective subtracts the input cost and their access convention differs from
our additive ownership model. Do not present common-pool scarcity or resulting
core failure as new, and do not silently replace their partition-dependent
outside options with additive entitlements. The source contains further core
conditions, which this campaign does not need to import as new theorems.

3. Bigi, Fioriti, Frangioni, Passacantando and Poli, Least cores in energy
community games, arXiv:2511.05291v2 (26 February 2026).
https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.05291v2
Coordinator checked abstract, veto definitions (printed p5), sections 3-4 and
the discussion after Theorem 1 (printed p12). The aggregator is indispensable;
without admission fees the game is balanced, and assigning the entire surplus
to the aggregator is recognized as practically unfair. This confirms that
veto-driven efficient allocations in energy games are established. The paper
studies fixed-game core/least-core distribution, not our exact all-profile
capacity-withholding factor. No claim of a new veto observation is warranted.

4. Solymosi, Assignment games with population monotonic allocation schemes,
Social Choice and Welfare 62 (2024).
https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00355-023-01477-z
Free author version: https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.17373
Coordinator read publisher full text, introduction, Theorem 1, Proposition 4
and conclusion. The paper characterizes PMAS-admissible assignment games using
veto-related structure and cites Sprumont's classical all-to-veto construction.
PMAS concerns how shares change when coalition membership grows. Our incentive
condition changes one fixed owner's resource bundle. Neither a PMAS obstruction
nor a PMAS construction transfers automatically to that different condition.
Sprumont's original was not independently accessed in this campaign; distinguish
Solymosi's original results from his historical statements about that source.

5. Ezra, Feldman, Neyman, Talgam-Cohen and Weinberg, arXiv:1811.09871.
https://arxiv.org/pdf/1811.09871
Existing project source check: Lemma 6.2, printed p24, gives the MPH-r
sum-of-grand-marginals inequality. This is the established ingredient behind
the sparse rT baseline, as explicitly proved and credited in
../2026-10-11-ai-infrastructure/SPARSE_PRODUCTION_NOTE.txt.
This campaign relies on that existing proof/source audit; it did not re-audit
the full external paper. Core coverage requires LP core existence in addition
to that marginal-sum inequality. Do not claim the rT baseline as new.

6. Huang, Mei, Yang and Cao, Mechanism Design in Max-Flows (2025 preprint).
https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.08248
Coordinator checked section 2.2, Example 1, printed p7: efficient core selection
can reward capacity underreporting in a four-edge example. This already rules
out treating the basic core/truthfulness incompatibility as new. Their
subsequent mechanism addresses other allocation axioms and should not be
assumed to meet the coalition-covering inequality after our subsidy change.
The exact proposed mixed-owner star constant is not established by that example.
This comparison is with the freely available preprint, not a verified final
publication or every subsequent paper.
