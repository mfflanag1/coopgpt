Scarce-power ownership and sparse-production scoping
15 October 2026

Scope: one bounded scoping pass, not an overnight research goal. Start from the
existing sparse-production and inference-pilot notes. Stop after an institutional
screen, one constructive mathematical lane, reproducible checks, and independent
review. Do not run further physical benchmarks or modify the submission manuscript.

Question: which ownership model makes scarce power a genuine all-profile
incentive problem, and does its structure improve the established rT payment
baseline with arbitrarily many fixed owners and resource types?

Permitted outcomes: a proved structural lemma/corollary; an exact obstruction;
a corrected model; or an explicit unresolved bottleneck with a go/no-go decision.
Neither a fixed-size example nor renaming the existing public-power obstruction
qualifies as a new general contribution.

Separate fixed-profile funding B(b)=min sum z_i subject to z>=0 and
z(S)>=v_b(S) for every coalition, from the premium of one own-report-monotone
payment rule on the entire report domain. Utility initially is payment only;
underreports are binding; owner identities, technology and public coefficients
are fixed. Private costs, population changes and group deviations are excluded.

Independent lanes:
1. Institution screen: additive entitlements, indispensable power owner, common
   access. Explain coalition outside options and distinguish peak kW from kWh.
2. Mathematical structure: sparse-production reduction and heterogeneous star
   technology. Seek a bound independent of owner count and hardware-type count.
3. Free original-source comparison: common-pool production, veto games,
   stability subsidy, strategic capacity reporting and bounded complementarity.

The candidate selected during initial analytic scoping is the positive-sum
decomposition of a heterogeneous compute/power star LP into two-input production
games. The remaining proof obligation is preserving whole-owner incentives when
one real owner holds both inputs. This selection precedes the new numerical
checks, but is not a preregistered empirical experiment or a novelty claim.

Verification: prove the full continuous statement, including zeros and ties;
independently check the ownership argument; enumerate exact small primal
solutions to check the decomposition; reproduce the existing 9/8 lower witness;
use interval arithmetic for sampled nonlinear payment inequalities. Explicitly
distinguish finite checks from the all-profile analytic proof.

Promotion gate: a valid owner-preserving theorem on an unbounded class and a
clear research frontier beyond it. A direct composition of established
ingredients may justify the application model but not a breakthrough claim.
Historical novelty remains unresolved unless exact primary-source comparisons
support a narrower statement. No outreach or purchases are authorized here.
