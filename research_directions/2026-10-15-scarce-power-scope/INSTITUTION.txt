Economic interpretation and what remains unmeasured
15 October 2026

The defensible starting point is a closed consortium of fixed owners with
already leased, transferable hardware reservations and power-capacity rights at
one specified meter and time slot. This is a proposed contract model, not a claim
that current GPU markets or utilities implement it.

Each beneficial owner may hold both hardware and power entitlements. The proof
explicitly handles that ownership; one firm's accounts are not treated as
independent strategic suppliers. A coalition can commit only its members' rights.
A neutral feeder operator enforces the contract and has already consented and
been compensated; otherwise it is an omitted indispensable strategic participant.

Idle/base load is reserved first. The power rights cover incremental draw. Fixed
lease ceilings must sum to no more than remaining feeder capacity, so an extra
public feeder limit is redundant on the feasible reporting box. Power rights
at different meters or times cannot be silently pooled. A hardware reservation
must not already include power that is counted again as a separate entitlement.

The theorem uses continuous activity rates and fixed public linear service-value
and power coefficients. A kW quota must bound simultaneous draw, rather than
average energy. GPU-hours subject to a kWh budget constitute a different model.
The earlier inference pilot measured neither scarce peak-power operation nor
these rights. The Mac retrieval experiment has no independently owned pools.

Using this abstraction for actual infrastructure requires a safe, executable
dispatch interpretation of fractional activity. In the current star theorem each
hardware row has ONE activity/mode, and capacities are independent reservations.
Alternative modes competing for the same hardware, fixed setup costs, shared
memory/bandwidth, indivisible requests and latency deadlines introduce additional
constraints. They cannot be hidden in independently additive hardware capacities.

The current incentives are weak underreport incentives with payment-only utility.
Within the modeled committed window, withheld resources cannot earn a private
outside payoff. A real owner who can run its own profitable jobs violates this
assumption. Public audited usage-cost reimbursement is not a substitute for a
private opportunity-cost theorem. Likewise, coefficients p_j are fixed public
distributable values, not measured tenant values or elicited bids.

The ~18.394% bound is a theoretical external-payment ceiling relative to produced
value. It is NOT measured power savings, operator profit, an estimated market
subsidy, or a demonstrated affordable contract. A source of external funds is
not specified. The 12.5% lower bound is a worst-case necessity over the class,
not a statement that every real consortium needs that amount.

Common access is a separate institution. Giving each deviating coalition the
entire feeder capacity defines optimistic outside options. Several coalitions
cannot simultaneously exercise those outside options. If outsiders retain
access or an operator rations among coalitions, specify the partition-dependent
deviation convention before claiming coalition stability. Do not assign invented
power rights merely to recover the additive theorem.
