Mac bounded-concurrency queueing comparison — 14 October 2026 UTC

Question and scope
On the user's single shared-memory Mac, does allowing two complete matrix-job
pipelines in flight improve verified completion latency relative to one pipeline?
This is a controlled artificial-workload engineering comparison. It is not an
LLM-serving benchmark, a new scheduler, an economic welfare estimate, an energy
measurement or independent-owner pooling. Earlier packages remain frozen.

Mechanisms
Two live jobs may overlap CPU preparation/validation with GPU work and reduce
queue delay. They may instead add CPU, memory or device contention. A single Metal
command queue does not imply simultaneous GPU kernels; measure whole-pipeline
concurrency and report device intervals separately. FCFS refers to admission from
the common FIFO, not guaranteed GPU submission/completion order with two workers.

Treatments and workload
Exactly one or two worker threads admit jobs from a common FIFO. A slot is held
through buffer preparation, GPU encoding/submission/completion, CPU reference,
complete output comparison, fingerprints and resource cleanup. A dedicated
producer follows the offered schedule independently of worker availability.
It must not wait for a free worker before recording new arrivals.

Every window has 60 jobs, dimensions 256,512,1024 repeated 20 times, with identical
input rules to the previous calibration:
 A[i,k]=((i+3k) mod 7)-3; B[k,j]=((5k+j) mod 11)-5.
Nominal offers occur at i/r seconds from the measured epoch for i=0..59, at fixed
rates r in {10,40,100} requests/second. Deadline is 0.100 seconds after each nominal
offer, common to all jobs. The producer records actual enqueue time and lateness.
Primary latency starts at nominal offer, so producer delay cannot be hidden by
resetting request clocks when a worker is ready. Also report actual-enqueue latency.

Each new native process first executes three serial correctness-checked warmups,
dimensions 256,512,1024, outside the measured epoch. Warmup timestamps use their
declared separate origin. It then executes the entire 60-job window. All output
entries must match CPU Accelerate SGEMM exactly; independent integer reconstruction
must verify the full GPU and CPU output digests. Signed zeros are normalized in
the canonical row-major Float32-LE hashes. Inputs and partial sums fit exactly in
Float32 for this special integer family; this is not general accuracy evidence.

Fixed paired sequence
Four rounds, each with all three rates and both configurations:
 round 1: rates 10,40,100; within each rate concurrency 1 then 2;
 round 2: rates 100,40,10; within each rate concurrency 2 then 1;
 round 3: rates 40,10,100; within each rate concurrency 1 then 2;
 round 4: rates 100,10,40; within each rate concurrency 2 then 1.
Thus 24 windows, 1,440 planned measured jobs and 72 separate warmup jobs.
Use a one-second inter-window idle interval and drain every window fully. No
outcome-based reordering, extra repeats, changed rates, discarded slow windows or
rerunning a bad comparison. These rates are selected using the earlier tiny
calibration as development information, not as a guaranteed service-time model.

Timing, accounting and preservation
Record nominal offer, actual enqueue, locked FIFO admission/sequence, submit,
observed GPU completion, validated completion and post-cleanup resource release.
All host offsets share the window monotonic epoch. GPU start/end timestamps use
their own documented timebase and must not be subtracted from host offsets.
Primary completion includes allocation, preparation, CPU validation and hashing.
CPU reference cost is part of this checked service, not a free offline oracle.
Record admission/release/enqueue events and active counts under the queue lock.
Independently reconstruct the maximum active pipelines and FIFO order.

Per-job explicit buffer/reference/hash-scratch cap is 64 MiB; at most two active
slots bound this tracked sum by 128 MiB. The expected largest explicit job uses
about 16 MiB. Framework/private allocations and whole-process peaks are outside
that cap. Retain global Metal allocation samples without attributing overlapping
global counts to individual jobs. Unified RAM is counted once. No global memory
availability, safe production concurrency or independent-device partition follows.

Record selected hardware, power-source and thermal diagnostics before/after.
An active desktop has uncontrolled background load. AC is not required for this
same-machine comparison, but each paired comparison must have the same known
power source at all four before/after observations. Record battery percentages.
Thermal diagnostics are descriptive, not measured temperatures or exclusion rules.

Native windows retain all requested rows and failures. Root stops subsequent
windows after a process/timeout, incomplete window, correctness or source-integrity
failure; planned but unrun windows remain named. Each native process has a
90-second watchdog and the collection has a 600-second monotonic work budget.
Before/after diagnostics and native execution share that deadline; no further
subprocess starts once it expires. Selected inventory calls are individually
bounded to 15 seconds and counted against the budget. Process termination and
final record serialization may finish after the work deadline; report actual
elapsed time rather than claiming a hard real-time wall bound.
Timeout/missing records are failures or incompleteness, never zero latency. No
privileged settings, live server, paid API, model download or energy sampler runs.

Prespecified analysis and promotion gate
Compute useful on-time fraction out of all 60 offered jobs in each completed
window; invalid, missing or late jobs are not on-time successes. Any correctness
failure closes the comparison's validity gate. Preserve all jobs and report
incomplete/unrun denominators separately. Use nearest-rank p95 latency (sorted
element ceil(0.95*n)) and ordinary median (mean of middle two for even n).

Validity requires all 24 fixed windows complete; all measured/warmup correctness,
digest, ordering, timing and active-count checks pass; all 12 power-source pairs
are comparable; and every window's p95 producer lateness is <=0.010 seconds.
Treat larger producer delay as distorted delivery, not evidence for faster service.
Timeline consistency allows 1 microsecond numerical tolerance; deadline and gate
comparisons use the recorded times without rounding. Preserve borderline cases.

Primary contrast: at rate 100, paired ratios R_k=p95(cap2)/p95(cap1), k=1..4.
Qualify the configuration for a larger follow-up only if ALL hold:
 1. validity gate above passes;
 2. median(R_k)<=0.90 (at least ten-percent median paired p95 improvement);
 3. cap2 p95 is lower in at least three of four rate-100 pairs;
 4. total cap2 on-time successes at rate 100 >= cap1;
 5. at rate 10, median paired p95 ratio <=1.10 (no >10% low-load regression).
Rate 40 and mean/max latency, deadline counts, queue delays, producer jitter,
observed overlaps and tracked memory are secondary descriptive diagnostics.
Report every pair and rate, not just the primary winner. No multiple exploratory
specifications, p-value, population confidence interval or causal generalization
is claimed from four paired windows on one active machine.

Permitted conclusions: engineering gate passes for this fixed cohort; the tested
configuration fails the material-improvement gate; or the comparison is invalid/
incomplete with the exact reason. A pass justifies a longer independently frozen
follow-up, not energy/AI-serving/pooling claims. A failure is useful evidence
against this specific proposed optimization and does not license tuning on the
same captured stream until it passes.

Freeze protocol, plan, sources, preparation checks and executed binary before
physical comparison. Review design and concurrency logic independently. The known
Fable/Opus quota failure has not reset; use independent Codex review and executed
audits, disclosing that cross-model approval remains unavailable.
