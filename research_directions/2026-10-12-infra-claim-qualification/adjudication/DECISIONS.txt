No candidate responses exist at this point. Initial source excerpts, builder,
cases and blind view are archived here. The final inputs are frozen separately.

Scope decision: the independent design critique proposed 12 development plus 36
confirmation requests. We instead choose one bounded 24-case engineering screen:
eight of each label, four per source section, no confirmation or unseen-population
claim. One packet has four excerpt blocks; block-level references avoid forcing
arbitrary answer-span matching. This is a disclosed simplification, not completion
of the critique's proposed larger study or a matched difficulty benchmark.

Both initial blind annotation reviews agreed on all 24 author labels. Revisions:
- Kubernetes driver/plugin setup is a premise, so D2 alone proves the GPU-limit
  conclusion. D1 can corroborate but is not mandatory.
- APC's supported claim was rewritten to grant a shared prefix and ask only about
  prompt versus decoding benefit. D3 suffices, D1 may corroborate.
- Sleep D4 regained its missing sleep_preserve_parameter_names antecedent and
  use cases, preserving the scope of the level-2 exception.
- Initially every insufficient claim requested current local observations. Five
  were replaced with missing qualitative behaviors (GPU fractional granularity,
  cache persistence across restart, allocator selection, live actor migration,
  backup compression). Several decisive claims now use hypothetical local wording.
  No model outcomes were consulted. Remaining three observational claims stay.

Final Fable review was blind to author labels and found 8/8/8. Final Codex review
read packets without gold/rationale, but its change message revealed intended
labels for the five replacements. We disclose it as packet re-adjudication,
not a fully blind second round. All 24 final labels agree with both reviewers.

Fable's proposed APC D1-alone negative proof was independently checked and rejected:

agrees; no unresolved label disagreement remains. Accepted proof stays D3, with
D1 optional. This is a verified logical adjudication, not review-by-majority.
The PyTorch performance claim remains insufficient: faster memory allocation is
not a measured or guaranteed whole-training-job comparison.
