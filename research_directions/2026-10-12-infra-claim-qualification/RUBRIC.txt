Infrastructure documentation claim verification — prospective rubric

Task: a human engineer reviews a proposed GPU-serving/configuration claim against
an explicitly supplied documentation packet. The model provides an informational
verdict and citations. It neither changes a cluster nor provides execution approval.
Each request is independent; only the supplied packet is evidence. Outside training
knowledge cannot fill omitted deployment facts. Source commits are pinned snapshots,
not assertions that a released product or the local machine matches those versions.

Supported: the complete claim follows from the packet with the same scope and
conditions. Contradicted: the packet establishes the claim's negation or an
incompatible value/condition. Insufficient: neither conclusion follows. A documented
requirement is not evidence it is satisfied by a named deployment; lack of mention
is not a documented prohibition. Intended benefits are not achieved measurements.

Output exactly {"verdict":"supported|contradicted|insufficient","evidence":["D1",...]}.
Use real enum members, not the pipe-joined example. Evidence IDs name excerpt blocks,
not arbitrary generated text. List at most three unique valid IDs. For supported
or contradicted, cite a complete proof, accepting any prospectively reviewed minimal
sufficient set and optional relevant corroboration. Citation order and JSON key
order do not matter. Irrelevant citations and incomplete proofs fail. For insufficient,
return [] because topical context alone is not decisive evidence of the missing fact.

Gold evidence alternatives and relevant corroboration are fixed by independent
pre-inference review. All materially valid alternatives found by review are accepted;
model outputs cannot add alternatives retroactively. Score schema validity, verdict
correctness, citation correctness and joint success separately. The primary useful
completion is joint success, not a verdict with broken evidence. No word-for-word
answer span is generated or evaluated.

Study: one hand-authored, balanced 24-case prospective qualification cohort, eight
per verdict, four per source section. No development/confirmation split or heldout
accuracy claim. The previous public QA outcomes informed this new task design;
these claims and packets are fresh and have no candidate responses yet. Source
knowledge may be in pretraining. Cases share source packets, are dependent and
are not representative random operator traffic. It is a narrow engineering screen.

Qualification requires all 24 schemas valid, >=22/24 joint successes, >=7/8 in
supported and contradicted, 8/8 in insufficient, and ZERO predicted-supported
verdicts for gold contradicted/insufficient claims. The latter is a sample gate,
not proof of a zero population false-support rate. All 24 are offered once, even
if an early quality failure makes the gate impossible; infrastructure failure
aborts. One pinned Qwen3:32b prompt/configuration, no fallback or retuning.

Independent label review sees packet+claim without author labels/rationales and
records alternatives/ambiguities. Reconcile all disagreements before inference;
unresolved cases stop the launch rather than invite post-response repair.
Public schema/instruction contains labels and citation format, never task-specific
gold label/proof/rationale. Case IDs and order are deterministic hashes of claims,
not labels. No result-dependent selection or omission is permitted.

Cheap comparisons: always-insufficient is only a 1/3 weak verdict floor. A fixed
lexical ranker retrieves up to three excerpt IDs, evaluated for coverage of a
sufficient proof on answerable cases; it does not supply a valid three-way decision.
A specialist searching the source and reviewing it, and configuration validators
where applicable, remain serious alternatives. No time savings or economic value
is measured. If lexical retrieval already solves citation location, disclose it.

A pass allows considering a separately frozen service study on fresh cases; it does
not automatically start concurrency, energy or pooling tests, establish human
specialist validation, justify operational automation or demonstrate economic gain.
