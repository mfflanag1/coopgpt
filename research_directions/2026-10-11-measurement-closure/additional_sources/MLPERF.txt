MLPERF INFERENCE SERVER POWER — BOUNDED PUBLIC SOURCE AUDIT
Access date: 2026-10-11. Search began 17:36:31 UTC and stopped within ten minutes.

DISPOSITION
Public MLPerf submissions do contain directly measured, sampled whole-system
power associated with LLM Server performance runs. They are substantially richer
than aggregate energy tables. However, the three representative power submissions
inspected below do not expose request-level arrival/first-token/completion events.
Their public performance subtrees lack mlperf_log_trace.json. Their detailed logs
contain run metadata, dataset sample sets, counts and latency summaries, not the
individual query timing observations needed for request-level scheduling replay.
The requested measurement gate remains open. This is an inspected-example result,
not a claim that no MLPerf submission anywhere contains usable request traces.

OFFICIAL SEMANTICS AND RULES
https://mlcommons.org/benchmarks/inference-datacenter/
The official benchmark page identifies reported power as full-system AC power
measured at the wall during the accompanying performance benchmark. This is not
GPU-board energy, and neither idle nor CPU/fan/memory consumption should silently
be subtracted. A whole-system energy accounting adapter would be legitimate if
the study explicitly adopted that scope; TensorRT rather than vLLM is not itself
a reason to reject these observations.

https://github.com/mlcommons/inference_policies/blob/master/power_measurement.adoc
The inspected official historical document is labeled v2.0, updated January 2022;
it must not be presented as a fully audited v5.1 rulebook. It explicitly requires
power and performance from the same run, uses LoadGen start/end timestamps, and
separates performance from accuracy/compliance. Current official power workflow
documentation describes distinct ranging and testing phases:
https://github.com/mlcommons/inference/blob/3fbc329939999c13d0a7b5e67fb2092287e06047/docs/power/index.md
No proprietary power software was obtained and no EULA was accepted. Inspecting
public result logs did not require either action.

SAME-RUN AND REQUEST IDENTITY AUDIT
For all three examples, the SHA-1 of the fetched run_1 detail file matched the
corresponding entry in power/client.json. Sample timestamps bracket the detail
log's power_begin/power_end, and samples are marked as the testing phase.
This establishes documented session association; it is not an independent audit
of the original instruments. The pre/post samples are observed power, but were
not independently certified here as a clean dedicated-idle calibration interval.
The ranging files are a separate meter-ranging run and must not be substituted
for testing power. Separate accuracy/compliance outputs must not be paired to
performance timing by position. Each inspected run_1 accuracy file is an empty
JSON array.

The detail logs expose loaded_qsl_set (dataset indices) and random seeds. Those
are not an observed per-request dispatch/completion table. Seeds or source-code
reconstruction cannot recover missing realized latency events. No per-query
arrival, first-token or completion records were present in the inspected logs.
Percentiles also cannot recover the joint distribution of first-token and total
latency per request. GPU execution-start timestamps are not required for this
study; their absence is not the decisive obstacle.

COMPATIBILITY AND USE
All three use the same named MLPerf model/accuracy benchmark, but different
release versions, traffic rates, sample RNG seeds and declared precisions. The
H200 software differs from H100/B200; the B200 CUDA metadata also needs resolving.
Thus these examples do not establish a controlled paired workload with identical
execution settings. They may support carefully scoped comparisons of complete
submitted configurations under the MLPerf quality contract; they cannot isolate
GPU hardware effects or supply the missing request-level observations.

No new throughput, energy-saving, or pooling-benefit estimates were calculated.
The useful result is a concrete source of same-run wall-power samples and the
precise remaining barrier: public per-request timing traces for a compatible
pair. An adapter cannot reconstruct unobserved timing data.
