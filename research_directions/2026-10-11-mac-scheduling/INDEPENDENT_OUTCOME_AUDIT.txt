Scope and independence
I independently parsed TRACE.json, OFFERED_TRACE.json, all eight RUN.json,
REQUESTS.json, WARMUP.json and SERVER.log files, both freezes, PROTOCOL.txt,
AMENDMENT_01.txt, and retained preflight-abort metadata. I computed endpoints
with Python standard-library expressions entered separately at the shell; I did
not read, import, or run analyze.py. I subsequently compared the independently
computed endpoints with METRICS.json and read REPORT.txt. No hardware/model/API
calls, capture edits, extra workload, or external review occurred.

Decision
The captured data support a narrow descriptive statement: under this fixed
64-token-capped workload, two server slots produced fewer deadline misses and
shorter recorded response latencies than one slot. The predeclared engineering
follow-up screen FAILS. The average extra on-time response count is 1.25 per 16,
below the frozen threshold of 2, and the first window does not satisfy the strict
immediate post-unload observation check. There is no basis here for energy,
owner-pooling, production-quality, population, or causal claims.

Strict-validity analysis
Window1 has a nonempty loaded_after_unload.models snapshot. Windows2-8 have
empty post-unload snapshots. On the specified strict interpretation, window1
fails and pair1 is not a wholly valid pair. Its 16 observations remain visible.
The seven individually valid windows contain 112 offered requests, but they
are not a balanced four-pair experiment: P1 contributes 48 and P2 contributes
64. Comparing those unmatched totals is not the paired estimand.

Use the three fully valid pairs 2, 3, and 4 for the strict paired sensitivity:
P1 45/48 on time; P2 48/48 on time; extra counts +1, 0, +2; mean 1 per 16,
or 6.25 percentage points. This also falls below the numerical threshold.
These three pairs do not turn the original four-pair validity screen into a
pass, and the omitted pair was not replaced. Both analyses must be labeled.

Configuration and timing checks
All raw logs show actual llama_context n_seq_max equal to the assigned P,
n_ctx_seq=2048, and 29/29 layers offloaded to the Apple M3 Max GPU. The engine
commit-load records and runner-parallel records corroborate these settings.
P1 total context is 2048; P2 total context is 4096. The API's per-sequence
context remains 2048 in both arms. The literal runner model-blob hash is the
same dde5aa...ccdff in every window and belongs to MODEL_LOCK.json. All tag and
before/after loaded-model snapshots match manifest a80c4f17...b5b8b72, model
llama3.2:3b, and Q4_K_M; all version observations are Ollama 0.20.0.

The stored option objects are identical: num_ctx=2048, num_predict=64, seed=0,
temperature=0. Server-setting dictionaries differ only in OLLAMA_NUM_PARALLEL.
No discrepancy was found in the retained model/engine/config records. Actual
HTTP request bodies are not separately persisted in REQUESTS.json: full prompt
and option transmission is supported by the frozen collector/trace design and
server evidence rather than an independent wire capture. This audit does not
re-hash the live model cache or binary or attest to live machine inventory.
The raw server logs independently identify M3 Max; the 30-core/96-GB inventory
is prior project provenance rather than a fresh hardware measurement here.

All eight windows have two successful nontrace warmups, both offered at zero.
Warmup dispatch offsets are below 0.6 ms. Every workload begins at least 2 s
after the recorded warmup client finishes (2.005963-2.011831 s from UTC records).
Each after snapshot is at least 2 s after workload drain (2.022470-2.033133 s).
The first warmup window takes 20.222 s; all later warmups take 2.086-2.460 s.
That is evidence of an unbalanced initial cache/startup history, despite fixed
warmup request counts and fresh runners. Workload timing correctly excludes
warmup, but fresh runners do not reset OS, Metal, filesystem, or thermal state.

All request clocks inspected are finite and ordered; nonempty first-content
and terminal timestamps agree with corresponding stored chunk offsets.
The largest dispatch lag is 10.318250 ms, below the frozen 100-ms condition.
No request dispatch precedes its own offered offset. The trace copied into
capture is structurally identical to TRACE.json. All retained original-endpoint
idle-before/after booleans are true. Boundary observations are not continuous
proof of host exclusivity.

All before/after snapshots report AC power and no recorded thermal or performance
warning. The first begins AC-attached/not-charging and ends charging; subsequent
snapshots report charging. One-minute load averages range from 8.887695 to
16.032715 and vary during collection. These observations neither measure energy
nor eliminate desktop/Codex background work, transient throttling, or charging
power changes. No energy telemetry exists in this pilot.

Comparison with root-generated analysis
After my independent recount, I compared METRICS.json against raw calculations.
There are zero discrepancies in per-window offered/complete/on-time/late/error
counts, nearest-rank completion and first-content quantiles, prompt/output token

classification. REPORT.txt states the failed screen and distinguishes strict
validity. The parent revised its initial broad success wording to make capped

finishing this audit. This is a resolved wording issue, not a new readiness
block or a reason to discard measurements. Conventional statistical medians would
average the eighth and ninth observations; the report uses and labels nearest
rank instead. This is a definition difference, not a discovered arithmetic bug.

Claim boundary
Keep four paired windows as the unit of the full descriptive comparison and
three as the strictly valid paired sensitivity. Do not treat 128 repeated jobs
as independent experiments; do not infer the latency effect causally from the
fixed order on a shared host. Do not convert the five avoided deadline misses
into welfare, energy savings, owner pooling, or output-quality benefit. The
failed engineering threshold is informative and must not be retuned. A later
load/length/concurrency study would require a new prospective design, sustained
windows, and an appropriate quality objective. Energy and separate-resource
pooling require the still-missing corresponding hardware measurements.
