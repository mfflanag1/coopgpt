The mathematical object here is a finite packing/admission problem, not the
earlier homogeneous continuous production model.

For each job j enumerate its permitted placements P_j. A placement names one
node and exactly g_j distinct GPUs on that node, all of one allowed family,
each satisfying the device VRAM minimum, with an allowed resource owner.
Binary x_{jp} chooses placement p. Each job has sum_p x_{jp} <= 1; each GPU
occurs in at most one chosen placement. CPU and RAM sums are bounded per node.
Lexicographically maximize (sum_{j,p} priority_j x_{jp}, sum_{j,p} x_{jp}).
Every unchosen job is explicitly deferred in the output. This is conventional
finite integer optimization; the bounded exact search is not a novelty claim.

Exact solver argument: process jobs in fixed order. Every feasible plan either
defers the next job or chooses one of its enumerated permitted placements. The
remaining GPUs and per-node CPU/RAM determine all feasible continuations. The
recurrence exhausts those alternatives and optimizes the additive lexicographic
objective. Memoization preserves its optimum. The independent oracle instead
orders all admitted subsets by their objective and exhaustively asks whether
each subset has a feasible placement; the first feasible subset is optimal.
Both searches are exponential and bounded to at most 8 jobs, 8 GPUs and 4 nodes.

Why previous production assumptions fail automatically: take one job of value 1
requiring 2 identical same-node GPUs, with CPU/RAM nonbinding. For available GPU
capacity b, its integer value is F(0)=F(1)=0 and F(2)=1. Any extension agreeing
at these points violates concavity, since F(1) < (F(0)+F(2))/2. Homogeneity also
fails: F(2) != 2F(1). A fractional admission relaxation can admit x=1/2 at b=1
and return value 1/2, while the integer optimum is 0. The relaxation's value is
not an executable partial placement. With finite pending demand the value also
saturates. Owner-dependent permissions and physical node identity add constraints
that cannot be suppressed into a fungible sum of scalar capacities.

These elementary examples establish an assumption boundary, not an impossibility
theorem for cooperation or a new theorem. Earlier incentives/payment guarantees
need a separately proved model-specific extension before reuse. Priority here
is an administrative score, neither money nor willingness to pay.
