GPU snapshot placement: completed bounded engineering reference
13 October 2026 UTC

The fixed comparison passed its software gate: all 420 primary policy plans were
valid, exact optimization agreed with the independently authored oracle on all
140 manifests, all 420 transformed-instance checks passed, and all 41 applicable
GPU-reuse corruptions were rejected. A separate set of 99 preparation tests passed
in both normal and optimized Python. The optimized saved-data reproduction matches
every row, objective, summary and gate; it is not another set of observations.

What was built
A command-line planner and separate strict verifier for whole-GPU snapshot
admission. Inputs declare node ownership, device identities/families/VRAM,
available CPU and host RAM, and job demands, priorities and owner permissions.
Each multi-GPU job stays on one node and one GPU family; VRAM cannot be pooled
across devices. Every job is placed or explicitly deferred. Objective is total
administrative priority, with admitted job count breaking ties. This is ordinary
integer packing, not a new optimization method. Bounds: 8 jobs, 8 GPUs, 4 nodes.

A concrete placement failure
In scarce_memory_placement, a flexible job has priority 6 and a second job has
priority 5 but needs the only 32 GiB GPU. First-fit priority order puts the flexible
job on that GPU and achieves 6. Exact placement sends it to the 16 GiB GPU and
places the restricted job on the 32 GiB GPU, achieving 11. The manifest and both
plans are in examples/. This is a simple constructed witness of memory stranding,
not a measured GPU workload. HEURISTIC_MISSES.json retains every mismatch.

Interpretation and limits
The engineering reference is usable within its formal snapshot contract. The
stronger practical conclusion is that a conventional exact algorithm already
provides the objective ceiling on these small instances; an LLM has no objective
improvement to offer over that ceiling. Both heuristics often reach the same
answer, and neither is claimed to implement or outperform a production scheduler.
At larger scales the first comparison should be appropriate CP-SAT/MIP and
established scheduling methods; exponential enumeration has no scalability claim.

No live cluster was changed, no local inference benchmark ran, and no serving or energy
experiment followed. Resource quantities, owner declarations and priorities are
synthetic. Same-node/family fit does not establish real library, interconnect,
memory-peak or runtime compatibility. State freshness, atomic reservations,
durations/deadlines, fairness, private costs and peak power remain unmodeled.
The Mac's unified-memory GPU is not a validation of this discrete multi-GPU model.

This is not a field-level breakthrough or evidence of economic pooling gains.
THEORY_BOUNDARY.txt gives the simple two-GPU job example that violates automatic
concavity/homogeneity. Earlier continuous-production payment and coalition
guarantees cannot simply be carried across. A future economic model must specify
actual rights, feasible deviations and the value/cost of completed jobs.

Next decision
Obtain one authoritative real GPU inventory plus pending-job snapshot, initially
read-only, with validated per-job requirements and permissions. Apply this planner
within its domain and inspect disagreements with the existing operator/scheduler.
Then validate actual execution fit with fresh reservations before measuring useful
completions, latency or full-horizon energy. A practical discovery could be a
repeated avoidable placement failure under real memory, topology or power limits;
the current synthetic counts do not establish its prevalence or value. A language
model is optional for normalizing genuinely unstructured job requirements, only
if a separate evaluation demonstrates value relative to explicit forms/importers.
