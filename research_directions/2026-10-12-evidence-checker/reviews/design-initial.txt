Independent prospective evidence-checker design critique
12 October 2026. Reviewed the prior qualification PROTOCOL, REPORT, RUBRIC and
contract.py. No new cases authored, future outcomes inspected, inference run,
network access, or canonical files changed.

Recommendation: one fixed paired native cohort plus one separate challenge
Freeze 24 new packet-relative claims, eight per gold verdict, with the existing
primary prompt/model/configuration. Independently adjudicate labels and all valid
citation alternatives before any response. Do not reuse the failed fractional-GPU
claim as a new test success or merely rename its entities. Reused source families
are permissible but disclose dependence and adaptation to the prior failure.

The baseline answer is generated once. A fixed stateless verifier then sees the
same packet and claim plus the exact primary verdict/evidence, without author gold,
case source label, rationale, reviewer identity, confidence, or an error category.
It returns only a strict accept/reject decision. Accept means BOTH verdict and
citations are justified under the original three-way packet-relative rubric.
For insufficient, it must assess whether neither decisive direction is established;
checking that the candidate's citation list is empty is not substantive checking.
Reject means route the unchanged case to human review. Do not relabel it
'insufficient', repair the answer, or count the unperformed human review as correct.

Also freeze 12 additional challenge claims, each with ONE prospectively invalid
proposal and ONE matched valid proposal: 24 verifier calls, not 24 independent
claims. Use four challenges per true verdict. Suggested error allocation:
  * Four insufficient claims given unjustified decisive proposals: two supported,
    two contradicted. These test the missing-premise issue in both directions.
  * Four decisive claims given the wrong decisive verdict: two per true verdict.
  * Four decisive claims given the correct verdict but deficient/irrelevant proof:
    two per true verdict. These prevent a verdict-only checker passing.
Adjudicate the invalidity and valid control before inference; keep all proposals
schema-valid so this tests semantics beyond a JSON validator. Separate challenge
claims from native claims. Never derive challenge defects from the new primary's
observed mistakes. Fixed hash ordering should intermix valid/invalid proposals,
with no pair ID, type indicator, or adjacent-pair instruction in the payload.
The checker receives no shared conversation or preceding answers. Reusing weights,
packet prefixes or caches does not make its errors statistically independent.

This is at most 72 scored generations: 24 primary + 24 native verifier + 24
challenge verifier. Always run the challenge, even when the primary makes errors;
adding it only after observing zero errors would make the design adaptive. Freeze
one verifier prompt, model, schema, output cap and order. No prompt search, retries,
third stage or outcome-triggered expansion. A generic 'reject anything dubious'
checker is tested against equally visible matched valid controls.

Metrics: native and challenge results must remain separate
For native case i let C_i indicate the primary's joint verdict/citation correctness,
and A_i indicate that its unchanged proposal is accepted. Report all counts:
  Baseline correct = sum C_i / 24.
  Incorrect automatic outputs = sum A_i(1-C_i) / 24.
  Correct automatic outputs = sum A_i C_i / 24.
  Coverage = sum A_i / 24; manual-review load = sum (1-A_i) / 24.
  Correct-answer retention = sum A_i C_i / sum C_i, undefined if denominator zero.
  Observed error catch rate = sum (1-A_i)(1-C_i) / sum (1-C_i), undefined if no errors.
Also show the four paired accept/reject x correct/incorrect counts and break out
false support, false contradiction, wrong insufficient and citation-only failures.
Accepted-only accuracy can be secondary, with numerator and denominator explicit.

An accept/reject-only workflow CANNOT increase correct automatic outputs on the
all-offered denominator: sum A_i C_i <= sum C_i. Its possible contribution is fewer
incorrect automatic outputs in exchange for review load and checker cost. Do not
claim 'accuracy improved' merely by discarding rejected cases. Rejected cases are
not successful useful completions, and unperformed human review is not free labor.
If the baseline makes zero native errors, natural error-detection sensitivity is
unobserved; constructed challenge success cannot be substituted for that result.

Small, fixed engineering gates
Native screen: require complete valid collection; valid verifier schemas for every
attempted verification; zero incorrect accepted outputs; at least 18/24 correct
automatic outputs; and at least 90% retention of the baseline's correct answers (use exact integer
cross multiplication, not rounded percentages: 10*retained_correct >= 9*baseline_correct).
Report native coverage and errors by true verdict, without an additional per-class
coverage gate: routing a harder class more often to review is permissible unless
a per-class service promise was made. The 18/24 floor explicitly permits
at most six cases requiring human review, not a measured staffing-capacity claim.
Primary schema-invalid answers are deterministic human-review referrals, never
repaired or claimed caught by the semantic verifier; retain them in the 24-offered
denominator and record any deliberately skipped verifier call. Primary/verifier
transport or host/clock integrity failure instead leaves collection incomplete
and prohibits promotion; retain partial data and orphan captures.

Challenge screen: reject 12/12 invalid proposals; accept at least 11/12 valid
controls. With four valid controls per class, 11/12 already implies at least 3/4
accepted in each true-verdict class. These thresholds stop
reject-all, accept-all, and a checker that rejects an entire verdict class. Report
results by the three prespecified error mechanisms and each matched pair. They
are a small constructed sensitivity screen, not error-prevalence or production
reliability estimates. No confidence bound should treat matched proposals or
repeated packets as independent samples.

Both screens must pass before describing this fixed workflow as having passed its
narrow screen. Actual naturally occurring errors may be claimed caught only when
observed among the 24 baseline answers. Passing with no baseline errors supports
retention plus challenge sensitivity; it does not establish real error reduction.
Any screen failure ends the experiment. Complete all planned quality cases unless
an infrastructure or operational-budget stop intervenes; do not stop early after
obtaining a favorable tally or continue until enough failures are caught.

Operational bounds and remaining limits
Use one prospective work budget (45 minutes plus existing bounded cleanup is ample
as a proposed cap, not a predicted duration), with an explicit per-call timeout and
cleanup reserve. All expected jobs, deterministic skips, attempted IDs and preserved
raw responses must reconcile. Budget exhaustion means incomplete, not a sample
size reduction or permission to retry. Preserve actual primary-to-verifier payload
binding via hashes. An independent scorer should reconstruct native candidate
correctness and checker decisions from raw payloads before accepting the gate.
Reuse established host, model, context, clock and owned-process controls. No energy
or latency superiority claim follows from this serial quality experiment; the
previous host-load findings especially discourage treating elapsed time as clean
capacity. The extra verifier generation and human-review burden are real costs,
but no dollars, energy savings, safety guarantee or independent-owner pooling
benefit are identified here. A pass enables a separately scoped service evaluation,
not automatic deployment or a retrospective reopening of the failed 23/24 study.
