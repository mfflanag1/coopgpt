Design reconciliation: controlled proposals on a frozen native-case subset
12 October 2026. Addendum to /tmp/coop-checker-design-review.txt.
No model outputs inspected, cases authored, or canonical files changed.

Decision
Endorse the narrower implementation. Twelve prospectively selected claims from
the 24 fresh native claims can each receive one invalid and one matched valid
proposal. This still yields at most 72 scored calls: 24 primary, 24 native checker,
and 24 controlled checker. There is no identification blocker under the stated
frozen selection, stateless calls and limited claims. The original review's use
of 12 additional claims was a suggested separation, not a necessary requirement.
This version contains 24 distinct native claim problems; the control component
reuses 12 of them. It does not contain 36 independent claim problems.

What is identified
The native component describes how the frozen checker accepts/rejects the actual
primary answers on the 24 fresh cases. The controlled component describes its
response to prospectively specified valid and invalid proposals on a deliberately
selected 12-case subset. Matching on packet and claim is useful: within each pair,
accepting the valid proposal while rejecting the invalid one demonstrates the
required discrimination for that same semantic problem. Because invalid proposal
construction and subset selection precede all new responses, the observed primary
mistakes are not used to select the checker tests.

Reuse does not invalidate either descriptive count. It does prevent treating the
two components as independent confirmations, treating the controls as held out
from the native cohort, or adding them into one larger accuracy denominator. The
controls sample chosen error mechanisms, not the frequency or distribution of
natural model mistakes. Their success cannot identify native error-detection
sensitivity when the primary makes no errors. No causally independent second
opinion is created by using the same model twice with stateless calls.

Additional accounting, without expanding the benchmark
Freeze the 12 case IDs and both proposed responses before primary inference.
Keep the subset selection rule, error categories and reviewer labels in source
artifacts only. The verifier receives packet, claim and candidate response, with
no group/validity/source-label metadata. Valid and invalid proposals use the same
serialization contract. Preserve the fixed intermixture/order and do not reorder
or select after seeing native outputs. Exact IDs need not enter prompt text.

A native primary answer may equal a prewritten valid or invalid control. Preserve
both planned calls, but report how many canonical checker inputs are identical
across native and controlled arms, alongside the number of unique claim problems.
Do not count such repetition as a new semantic test or independent evidence.
Identical payloads can still yield separate retained generation records; neither
a difference nor equality of their responses identifies an independence claim.
Do not rerun, deduplicate away failures, or alter controls when a duplicate occurs.

Statelessness means no accumulated dialogue or previously generated answers in
later requests. Repeated packet prefixes and implementation cache reuse are not
themselves dialogue history. They do leave timing/cache dependence, so this study
remains a quality screen and does not support a performance comparison.

Gates and termination
Keep the reconciled gates unchanged: controlled invalid rejected 12/12, matched
valid accepted >=11/12, no incorrect native automatic acceptance, native correct
retention >=90% by integer comparison, at least 18/24 correct automatic outputs,
all required checker schemas valid, and complete verified collection. Report
errors/coverage by label and each controlled error mechanism separately. A screen
pass is not a deployment or energy-study promotion. The original 23/24 failed
qualification remains closed and unchanged.

Adopt the specified 1800-second work budget and 300-second headroom reserve,
replacing my earlier suggested 45-minute cap. This is a fixed operational stop,
not a prediction that all 72 calls will fit. Keep per-call bounds and bounded
owned-process cleanup. If time expires, preserve all attempted, partial and orphan
records and explicitly mark unattempted planned calls. The campaign is incomplete
and cannot pass its gates; do not shrink the denominator, move controls into a
favorable completed subset, extend the cap after outcomes, or retry the remaining
calls under the same frozen experiment. Deterministic primary-schema referrals
and their predeclared skipped verifier calls remain separately accounted for.
