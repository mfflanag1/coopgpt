A. ENERGY OF THE UNCHANGED SAVED SCHEDULE
Keep both configurations resident over the SAME horizon [0,H] for both arms.
H=max(last OFFERED arrival + original deadline length, both arms' last slot end).
The measured energy window is [saved start, saved slot end - 3], length API+1.
Use union length L_ah (verify no overlaps) and recorded window energy W_ah on
owner h in arm a. Omitted duration I_ah=H-L_ah includes cooldown. One-rate model:
 E_ah(p_h)=W_ah+p_h I_ah, p_h>=0.
p_h is an assumed mean board power OUTSIDE measured windows, not a measured idle
power or a calibrated plausible range. Do not add it inside measured windows.
Also save omitted time split into non-slot resident time Z and cooldown time C,
where I=Z+C and C=3 times started count. This enables later separate idle/cooldown
rates; this audit's grid assumes those two rates equal on each owner.

Hypothetically reuse the original window-only B_h as GROSS full-horizon budgets:
separate accounts require E_aut,h<=B_h individually; pooling requires sum E_pool,h
<=sum B_h. These are ex-post fixed-schedule funding constraints, not online
reservation guarantees, and do NOT correct or refute the original window contract.
Compute exact uniform-p upper frontiers, handling negative slack and zero omitted
time explicitly. Save full two-rate affine constraints rather than a scalar-only
result. Extra funding preserving each saved outcome is respectively
 sum_h max(0,E_aut,h-B_h) and max(0,sum_h E_pool,h-sum_h B_h).
For p0,p1 in {0,1,5,10,25,50} watts save all 36 scenarios per case.
The comparison is Delta E=Delta W-sum_h p_h Delta L_h: common p_h H cancels.
Save nonnegative uniform-p energy-equality crossings or their absence. More
completions and more energy do not themselves identify economic welfare.

B. TIMING ONLY: FIXED ADMISSIONS, ASSIGNMENTS, ORDER AND RELEASE FLOORS
On each owner, saved start t_j is a release floor, not a forced overlapping start.
For a common API duration multiplier r>=1 and unchanged 4-second overhead:
 S_j(r)=max(t_j,C_{j-1}(r)); C_j(r)=S_j(r)+4+r*d_j;
 R_j(r)=C_j(r)-3.5.
Deadlines tighten relative to arrival: D_j(q)=A_j+q*(D_j-A_j), 0<q<=1.
Evaluate r in {1,1.05,1.10,1.25,1.5,2}, q in {1,.9,.8,.5}.
No energy extrapolation is made for changed duration. Timing and energy analyses
are SEPARATE conditional calculations, not a joint physical model.

Unrolling the recurrence gives an exact deadline threshold:
 R_j(r)=max_{k<=j}[t_k+4(j-k)+.5+r*sum_{ell=k..j}d_ell];
 r*_j(q)=min_{k<=j}[(D_j(q)-t_k-4(j-k)-.5)/sum_{ell=k..j}d_ell].
Then j is on time iff r<=r*_j(q), equality included. Save a minimizing k, its
numerator and duration denominator for every admitted job and q. Negative or
sub-unit thresholds preserve already-late jobs. Do not round before classifying.
Report counts and on-time difference; each arm's count is nonincreasing with
stress, but their difference need not be. Show survival of the old strict screen
using its UNCHANGED original window-energy debt flag only, never a stressed-energy
claim. Do not identify this fixed-plan calculation as rerunning the online policy.
