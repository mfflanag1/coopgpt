CoopGPT directions in AI infrastructure and energy
11 October 2026

DECISION
The best first project is incentive-compatible revenue sharing for a consortium
of committed GPU, facility-power and network reservations. Start with warm local
inference replicas and an auditable production model, then investigate shared
infrastructure rights and private flexibility costs. This combines a direct use
of CoopGPT with genuinely unresolved modeling and mathematical questions.

The broad opportunity is to determine when independent infrastructure owners
can share capacity without rewarding withholding or causing coalitions to leave,
and what funding or relaxation of stability is necessary. A scheduling speedup,
a deployment of GPU sharing, and a truthful supplier-payment mechanism are three
different contributions. None of the current results establishes an operating
market or a validated energy application.

The source screen found substantial prior art in cloud federations, fairness,
energy communities, truthful demand response and common-pool production. The
strongest proposed contributions concern their intersection with fixed-owner,
all-profile supplier incentives and coalitional outside options. Historical
novelty is not confirmed. All sources used were freely inspectable; no article
was purchased, gated dataset obtained, outside researcher contacted, deployment
made, or long research goal started.

1. Why this is a credible application

Real institutions already pool compute, and power flexibility is a real
engineering capability. The National Research Platform lets institutions
contribute nodes while retaining owner-priority reservations and using spare
cycles opportunistically [S01]. This gives a concrete ownership setting, but
also exposes the value of keeping capacity for one's own jobs. It validates
pooling, not the payment-only incentive assumptions of our theorem.

OpenTela documents a research serving federation across three administratively
independent subclusters within CSCS Alps, with low intercluster latency [S02].
It is not evidence that VRAM is freely pooled across distant data centers.
Petals has measured multi-institution, geographically distributed inference by
placing model stages on independent servers; each stage retains its own attention
cache [S03]. Its contributor rewards are a proposal rather than a validated
financial settlement. These are different production technologies and should
not share a single undifferentiated GPU-capacity variable.

Google reports that 1 GW of demand-response capacity has been included in
long-term utility contracts [S10]. That is contracted capacity, not proof of
1 GW simultaneously delivered. A June 2026 Emerald-and-partners preprint studies
a 96-GPU, 130 kW test cluster over five days, with 22 dispatch events derived from
historical cases and more than 200 targets/ramp constraints [S11]. This is useful
physical evidence; its geography experiment uses generated inference demand,
and it is not an estimate of systemwide welfare or causal emissions savings.

2. The institution and exact ownership model

Use a short, precommitted operating window. Participants hold enforceable rights
to deploy particular hardware, consume reserved facility power, and use specified
network paths. Resources are indexed by site, hardware type, time and entitlement
where those distinctions constrain production. A power entitlement at one site
cannot power an unconnected GPU at another site.

The clean initial institution is a consortium with public contractual output
prices and a sufficiently backlogged workload within its declared physical
capacity domain. Providers contribute already committed reservations, with no
concurrent own-use or outside rental opportunity during the window. Prices can
also be declared common experimental weights, but then the results concern that
objective, not measured willingness to pay. Monetary payments and service credits
are not interchangeable without a utility model for the credits.

A feasible unilateral deviation is to offer less usable owned capacity to the
pool. It must respect the contract: do not describe breaking an accepted delivery
obligation as an available strategic action. Overreporting is excluded by the
binding-report assumption and would require measurement or enforcement in an
implementation. Hardware identity, feasible configurations and quality coefficients
are initially verified/public; hidden quality and false identities are extensions.

This baseline is narrower than a general marketplace for idle GPUs. If a provider
can keep withheld GPUs for its own profitable jobs, utility is instead
  utility_i = payment_i + outside_i(withheld capacity) - cost_i(actual dispatch).
Monotonicity of payment alone does not make truthful contribution optimal there.
Audited reimbursement can cancel a realized operating cost if the contract truly
provides it; it cannot cancel private foregone job value. Private costs and delay
preferences are a major next project, not an innocuous parameter change.

3. A physical production model that connects to CoopGPT

Catalogue admissible service activities j: a fixed model and request-size bucket,
a hardware/configuration type, site, batching policy and any allowed network route.
Let A_ej>=0 be its resource requirement and p_j>0 its fixed distributable value.
For aggregate committed resources c, start with
  F(c)=max {p.x : x>=0, Ax<=c}.
For coalition S, v_b(S)=F(sum_(i in S)b_i). No coalition receives another owner's
hardware, licenses, power entitlement or fabric capacity without consent.

Every column must consume a positive resource. LP duality then gives
  F(c)=min {w.c : w>=0, A^T w>=p},
and a finite representation by dual vertices. This is precisely the existing
min-linear production model. The existing sharp form-count theorem applies to
this abstraction. Constructing the LP alone is an application of established
linear-production and systems optimization, not a new mathematical discovery.

Use warm complete replicas at first, with cross-owner request routing. Actual
memory constraints, tensor parallelism, placement, batching, startup and tail
latency must determine which activities are admissible. Do not assume aggregate
GB and FLOPs certify physical feasibility. Gavel already separates fractional
allocations from actual placement/scheduling [S04]. A configuration master LP can
encode simultaneous feasible schedules, but the catalogue may be exponentially
large and each column may use many distinct resource rows.

Disaggregated prefill/decode is a later extension inside measured high-bandwidth
locality domains. DistServe and Mooncake demonstrate why transfer traffic, cache
location and topology matter [S05,S06]. Pooling HBM on a GPU, host DRAM, and a
remote KV cache are different physical claims. A fluid LP must be calibrated and
validated against an executable schedule; it is not itself a serving system.

Fixed demand caps, public power caps, fixed setup costs, integer replicas and
storage balance equations do not automatically fit this nonnegative homogeneous
packing model. Demand caps can be harmless on a bounded feasible-report domain
only after establishing that backlog never binds there. Alternatively, demand
or license rights can be endowed to explicit actors, but this changes the game
and requires an honest outside-option interpretation. Profits p_j must use a
consistent cost accounting convention; subtracting private costs from p does not
by itself establish truthful reporting of those costs.

4. A concrete computable mathematical baseline

This exploration derives the following elementary structural corollary. Let r be
the maximum number of resource rows used by one activity, i.e. maximum column
support of A. Pay each owner its grand-coalition marginal contribution:
  U_i(b)=F(c)-F(c-b_i), c=sum_i b_i.
Then, for the model above and all nonnegative real reports,
  U_i>=0,
  sum_(i in S)U_i >= v_b(S) for every coalition S,
  U_i is nondecreasing in the owner's whole bundle,
  sum_i U_i <= min(n,r) F(c).
Only n+1 explicit LP value computations are needed. For rational explicitly
encoded data these have polynomial bit complexity. This does not solve implicit
exponential configuration enumeration, integer scheduling, or the sharp general
form-count algorithmic problem.

The core guarantee follows from an optimal dual-price core allocation z:
U_i>=z_i. The budget proof removes each owner's resource share from an optimal
production plan; each activity's lost value is charged across at most r resource
rows. SPARSE_PRODUCTION_NOTE.txt supplies the complete proof including zeros.

The same marginal-sum inequality has an established antecedent in MPH-r
(bounded-complementarity) valuations: Ezra et al., Lemma 6.2 [S19]. A sparse LP
game has such a representation, but MPH membership alone does not imply core
coverage. The LP-core property is the separate essential ingredient. We claim
an elementary usable baseline, not a new general theorem or a breakthrough.

This is nevertheless useful: the budget can depend on a serving plan's resource
requirements rather than every owner, site or dual price in the system. r is NOT
just the count of words GPU/memory/bandwidth/power. A route across many links, a
plan spanning many named GPUs or a long time-expanded schedule may have large r.
The rule can pay rT, requiring subsidy (r-1)T; this is a worst-case ceiling, not
an economically acceptable funding estimate. The r bound is tight for this rule,
not proved necessary for every rule.

For a fixed domain-wide scalar lambda, lambda U remains own-monotone and covers
lambda v(S) with budget <=lambda rT. In particular U/r spends at most T and
provides 1/r coalition coverage. That is a baseline funding/stability tradeoff,
not exact stability or guaranteed exhaustion of the revenue. Profile-dependent
normalization requires its own incentive proof.

A promising new mathematical target is the optimal factor for sparse production:
allow arbitrarily many owners, resources and activities while each column uses
at most r resources. The established rT baseline may be far from optimal. Try
to improve the general r dependence, or resolve a nontrivial fixed-r class while
all other counts grow. A sharp classification or polynomial mechanism improving
this baseline would advance the application more than another small example.
The existing multi-glove lower bounds supply logarithmic-over-loglog growth in r
when one activity uses r essential resources; they do not match the linear upper.

5. Three exact examples explain the real issues

Complementarity: with F(G,P)=min(G,P), two owners holding (8,4) and (4,8) can
produce 4 each alone and 12 together. The pooling surplus is4. This is normalized
compute/power arithmetic, not a calibrated hardware measurement.

Withholding under dual prices: GPU owners have capacities2 and1; the power owner
has capacity2. Grand output is2 and an efficient binding-price allocation pays
(0,0,2). If the first GPU owner reports1/2, output becomes3/2 and the same
minimum-resource price rule pays (1/2,1,0). Both allocations cover every coalition
at their own profiles, but withholding earns that owner an extra1/2. This is a
counterexample to the rule, not to all efficient mechanisms on every domain.

Common-access obstruction: n GPU owners each have one GPU, while every nonempty
coalition can separately claim the same unit of public power or customer demand.
Then v(S)=1 for every nonempty S, T=1, and singleton coverage forces payment>=n.
For n>=2 every grand marginal is zero and fails coverage. No report manipulation
is needed. This is a classical kind of common-pool/externality issue [S16], not
a new novelty claim. Assigning access rights changes the outside options; one
must not invent rights solely to recover a convenient theorem.

Coverage also differs from coordinated incentive resistance. With F=min(G,P),
GPU owners1 and2 each own1 and power owner3 owns1. Grand marginals are(0,0,1).
Owner1 withholding makes payments(0,1,1), so the GPU coalition benefits even
though no individual can improve its OWN payment by withholding alone.

6. Ranked research projects

A. Committed-capacity supplier payments: best starting point.
Question: under realistic local serving configurations, what payment and funding
rules keep contributions truthful and make shared production attractive?
Deliverable: explicit contract model, physical configuration benchmark, n+1-LP
marginal baseline, exact small-instance coalition/deviation checker, and a
measured budget-versus-coalition-protection frontier. A research contribution
requires a better general guarantee, robust implementation theorem, or a new
empirical finding with genuine owner data; a rebranded LP does not suffice.

B. Shared power, fabric and interconnection rights: strongest economic extension.
Question: how does the institution assigning a common bottleneck change the
additional price of truthful participation?
Compare fixed additive entitlements, an infrastructure owner whose consent is
needed, and genuine common access with outsider-dependent rationing. First
measure the static minimum covering cost at each profile, then the extra cost
of choosing payments consistently across reports. Cost-of-stability and common-
pool LP games are established [S16,S17]; the candidate addition is the incentive
premium under a specified realistic access rule. Seek an unbounded separation
or structural bound, not the elementary public-capacity example alone.

C. Flexibility procurement from AI tenants: highest direct energy relevance.
Question: how to pay for deliverable load reduction and recovery when tenants
have private delay costs and own jobs?
Start with one site, short events, a public workload model and one private scalar
cost per tenant. Explicitly include deferred-job recovery, SLA losses, private
outside value and the operator's utility agreement. Existing FairDR/MEDR and
core-selecting electricity mechanisms already address costly strategic responses
[S14,S15,S18]. A new target could be complementary flexibility across time/sites,
coalition stability, or a sharp cost/coverage/incentive tradeoff. Generic truthful
curtailment procurement would duplicate existing work.

D. Cooperation among local inference services: strong systems collaboration.
Question: which routing or cache-sharing arrangements generate robust surplus
once ownership and outside options are counted?
Start with warm complete replicas, then within-site prefill/decode separation.
Study who gets the value created by a model provider, KV-cache host, GPU provider
and network operator. Fixed data/model-license rights must be explicit. Prefix
cache data can support physical replay; public traces do not reveal the values
or legal rights of these participants. A topology-sensitive production and
payment result would be more meaningful than optimizing raw token throughput.

E. Repeated lending and contribution credits: plausible but already occupied.
Question: can multi-period contribution accounting stabilize participation when
hardware availability fluctuates? Credit Fairness (IJCAI2026) already gives an
axiomatic model and an impossibility under anonymity, efficiency and strategy-
proofness [S21]. A new project must distinguish supplier contributions, coalition
coverage and intertemporal outside options. Credits are promises on future scarce
service, not free money that eliminates a budget constraint.

F. Investment, reliability and energy infrastructure: high-value later program.
Question: should a consortium acquire GPUs, additional power rights, cooling or
battery capacity, and how should it divide the risk and future value? This needs
capex, stochastic availability, reliability, financing and long-run contracts.
It is a different stage from allocating today's surplus. A reliability guarantee
or fixed interconnection right cannot be treated as a freely additive present
resource without its physical and contractual conditions. Do not begin with a
full power-market equilibrium and GPU investment model before the smaller
participation model is credible.

7. Data we can actually use

Azure LLM2024 has direct public CSVs with invocation times and context/output
token counts [S07]. It is a good initial arrival/length workload. It lacks owner
endowments, payments, values, energy and counterfactual hardware service rates.

Mooncake's FAST25 release has two real one-hour request traces with prefix hashes
and a separately labeled synthetic trace [S08]. These can test cache locality;
short traces do not establish long-run welfare or representative demand.

Alibaba GPU2023 supplies a tractable node/task release, with hardware and QoS
information [S09]. Requested resources and observed runtime are not counterfactual
performance profiles on every GPU. The much larger2026 release deliberately
removes calendar dates and owner organizations; its pod-hourly archive is about
352GB. It cannot be naively joined to historical electricity prices or weather.
We have not downloaded it and it is unnecessary for a first experiment.

ML.ENERGY provides measured inference power/performance data, but its v3 raw
Hugging Face dataset is free-but-gated and requires login/contact-data agreement
[S12]. It has not been accessed here. Use openly reported configurations first,
and distinguish an access dependency from available data.

OpenG2G is an Apache-licensed GPU-to-grid simulation platform with power controls
and feeder constraints [S13]. Its inference replay uses measured benchmark data;
its training power traces are synthetic. It is useful for a later physical
stress test, not proof of commercial demand-response savings. No installation
or full benchmark was run during this exploration.

The first study would be a calibrated simulation with real workload shapes and
explicitly synthetic owner/endowment scenarios. Calling trace user IDs hardware
owners, or interpreting tokens as willingness to pay, would be invalid. Causal
claims about grid reliability, electricity prices or emissions require a separate
identification design and additional data.

8. The first bounded pilot and its promotion gates

Step1: freeze the contract and outside options. Choose committed colocated
reservations, an explicit power-entitlement rule, public output values and a
bounded physical report domain with sufficient backlog. List which actor holds
every right. If valuable withheld-resource use cannot be excluded, switch to
projectC rather than claim the current theorem applies.

Step2: build a small configuration catalogue with 2 hardware types and a handful
of local service plans. Preserve memory and placement constraints. Start with
3-6 synthetic owners so every coalition and finite-grid unilateral deviation
can be checked exactly. Declare the grid a finite diagnostic, not a proof over
all real reports; the mathematical guarantees require the written argument.

Step3: calibrate on one workload segment and freeze the held-out segments before
headline comparisons. Retain nonsharing, contributor-priority scheduling and a
strong heterogeneity-aware planner. On the SAME production plans compare
proportional payments, LP dual prices, Shapley where computable, grand marginals,
and small-instance exact versions of the existing sharp mechanism. Distinguish
the production gain from the allocation of that gain.

Step4: report completed SLO-compliant requests, dollar/objective value, peak kW,
kWh, recovery/rebound load, worst coalition deficit, maximum individual incentive
gain, total subsidy, and runtime. Carbon is a separate scenario-dependent metric;
carbon intensity and electricity price are not interchangeable. Show sensitivity
to valuation assumptions, hardware coefficients, topology and ownership rights.

Go only if the physical model predicts held-out feasible service adequately under
thresholds fixed before that test, and the economic conclusion survives plausible
outside options and coefficient uncertainty. A failed test can still yield a
valuable model-boundary result. Do not retrofit the contract after observing the
headline outcome just to make the payment theorem apply.

The mathematical promotion gate is an improved uniform bound or a structural
separation for a growing class, or an implementation theorem beyond the baseline.
For sparse production, try r=2/3 while owner/resource/activity counts grow, then
seek a general r dependence. For common access, isolate the incentive premium
beyond the static instability. None of these questions is declared a recognized
major open problem or a confirmed novel theorem yet.

9. Priority and evidence boundaries

DRF and Themis concern tenant requirements and scheduling fairness [S20,S23].
Truthful double auctions for federated clouds already combine VM trading and
electricity costs [S22]. Energy-community stability is established under explicit
tariff/scheduling assumptions [S25]. Population cross-monotonic profit sharing
faces strong existing budget barriers [S24]; this is a different condition from
our own-report monotonicity. Adding private consumer values as well as provider
costs introduces classical bilateral-trade impossibilities [S26]. These sources
make broad novelty claims untenable without an exact comparison of types,
feasible deviations, outside options, budget definitions and efficiency goals.

This is a scoped source exploration, not an exhaustive priority clearance. The
report contains proposed designs, not measured cost savings. check_models.py
reproduces exact finite examples and tests two sparse LP families on1024 binary
profiles in total:8192 coalition inequalities and7296 strict unilateral report
pairs, with independent primal/dual vertex values. Checks remain active under
Python -O. These corroborate the formulas; the universal sparse-production bound
is supported by the conventional proof and separately reviewed as recorded in
REVIEW.txt. No paid data, human outreach or full serving benchmark was used.

Reproduce finite checks from this directory:
  python3 -B -O check_models.py
Read SPARSE_PRODUCTION_NOTE.txt for assumptions and proof, SOURCE_LEDGER.tsv
for direct primary links/access limits, and CHECK_RESULTS.json for bounded output.

RECOMMENDATION
Lead with committed-capacity GPU/power cooperation, using the sparse-production
payment rule as a transparent calibrated baseline. Make common-infrastructure
access rights the main economic theory extension, and private costly flexibility
the next stage once the institutional model is fixed. This gives a coherent
research program connecting useful AI systems to coalition stability, strategic
participation and energy constraints without promising that a resource-allocation
theorem already solves an electricity market.
