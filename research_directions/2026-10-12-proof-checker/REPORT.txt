BLIND COMPONENT EVIDENCE INVENTORY — COMPLETED NEGATIVE QUALIFICATION
12 October 2026

The frozen workflow did not qualify. All 48 scored calls completed in one valid
collection: 24 blind evidence inventories followed by 24 primary answers. Filtering
retained 15 correct original answers, accepted zero observed incorrect answers,
and referred 9 cases. The nine referrals comprise all 7 answers that failed the
frozen joint verdict/citation rubric plus 2 correct answers. Referrals were not
corrected, completed by a human, or counted as useful automatic answers.

The primary model got 22/24 verdicts right and 17/24 verdict-and-citation answers
right. The distinction matters: only 2 primary errors were wrong conclusions;
5 had the right verdict but violated the frozen citation-relevance rule. That
rule permits only evidence belonging to a decisive whole-claim proof. Citing
support for the true half of a refuted conjunction therefore fails even when
the decisive refutation is also cited. The frozen score is unchanged, but this
citation convention is stricter than many practical documentation workflows.
The result does not mean that 7 unsupported conclusions were generated.

WHAT FAILED
Two inventories contained both support and contradiction for one component.
Two used IDs outside the allowed D1/D2/D3 excerpt set; one of these also conflicted.
Thus 3 distinct inventories were unusable, despite all 24 being schema-valid JSON.
One used documentation anchor names as IDs. Another invented D4 while listing
both support and contradiction for the prohibited remote async-task claim.

The two wrong primary verdicts were:
- b0cb35844fe9a7384577: '400m' memory was called 400 mebibytes despite the explicit
  0.4-byte explanation. The inventory placed D1 on both polarities, triggering
  an uncredited conflict referral.
- 1ed27c633bc3c2d2129c: the answer treated an in-place resize API description as
  proof that every VPA recommendation uses that API. The inventory correctly
  left that component unknown, causing a credited verdict-mismatch referral.

The five other native joint-quality failures added citations outside the frozen
whole-claim contradiction proof family. Four were rejected with usable inventories;
one was referred through an unusable inventory. Combined with the VPA case,
5/7 native joint-quality errors receive credited semantic-catch status; all 7
were actually withheld. No native format failures occurred.

Two correct answers were lost:
- b4d53981f8cef6bc7568: a correct insufficient answer was referred because its
  inventory used source-anchor names instead of permitted excerpt IDs.
- f764cbc9ba492cfdea21: a valid contradicted answer cited D1 and D2. Both are
  independently decisive, but the inventory omitted the D2 alternative. The
  fixed acceptance rule therefore rejected the correct extra citation.

By gold class, baseline joint-quality / retained correct were 8/8 ->8/8 for
supported;2/8 ->1/8 for contradicted;7/8 ->6/8 for insufficient. Seven of the
nine referrals arose on contradicted cases. Correct automatic yield fell from
17/24=70.83% before filtering to 15/24=62.5% afterward. Conditional correctness
among the 15 accepted answers was 15/15 in this sample; it is not a population
safety guarantee or a measure of net operator benefit.

INVENTORY DIAGNOSTICS
Twenty of 24 inventories passed the frozen semantic-quality rubric; 18 recovered
all annotated minimal alternatives. The rubric credited 41/48 component-proof
judgments. It assigns zero credit to BOTH components of an unusable inventory,
so 41/48 must not be read as seven independently verified false component claims.
Two otherwise correct inventories omitted alternatives (vLLM process separation
and Slurm afterok). A usable vLLM encoder-DP inventory included D1 alongside the
sufficient D2, violating the frozen minimal-proof relevance convention. This is
another strict proof/citation failure, not necessarily a wrong factual verdict.
Exact packets, gold, decisions and diagnostics are preserved in FAILURES.json;
raw answer text is recoverable without repair from capture/collection/*.json.

DECISION
Close this bounded attempt at its failed gate. No serving-performance, latency
sweep, energy measurement or pooling conclusion follows. Earlier cohorts used
different claims and instructions, so score changes do not establish improvement
or deterioration caused by the checker. Filtering cannot increase completed
correct original answers, and human referral/decomposition costs remain unmeasured.

The next project decision should specify one real infrastructure workflow, an
executable correctness criterion where possible, and its acceptable error/review
cost. A concrete candidate is GPU-job placement against an explicit resource
manifest, checked by capacity and compatibility constraints. That would be a
materially different task, with separately frozen quality and economic targets;
it is a planning option, not a study launched here. Avoid another automatic
24-case documentation-checker redesign. Compatible deadline-aware measurements
and full-horizon energy on two configurations, then real independent-owner
pooling, remain additional requirements for the broader research program.
