Primary-source and institution notes, accessed16 October2026. Free access only.
This is model design, not legal advice, market validation, or empirical evidence.

1. Static stability funding is established prior art.
Bachrach, Elkind, Meir, Pasechnik, Zuckerman, Rothe, Rosenschein (2009),
The Cost of Stability in Coalitional Games, arXiv0907.4385v1:
https://arxiv.org/pdf/0907.4385
Read Definitions3.1–3.2 and LP(1)–(4), printedpp4–5 / PDFpp5–6. They formulate
external payments supporting coalition stability and the minimum-payment LP.
Our B_f adds a public floor to that established static formulation. The
cross-profile comparisons defining K_f are additional and not supplied by
this reference. No claim that minimum coalition-cover funding is new.

2. Grand marginal contribution is an established quantity, not a novel floor.
Tamás Solymosi (2023), Assignment games with population monotonic allocation
schemes, Social Choice and Welfare, DOI10.1007/s00355-023-01477-z:
https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00355-023-01477-z
Freely readable original article; Section5 explicitly defines
M_i=v(N)-v(N\{i}) as the upper marginal vector and attributes the tau-value
construction to Tijs1981. It states these core upper bounds need not be sharp.
Thus requiring a positive fraction of M_A is a substantive fairness condition,
not standard individual rationality. The article's PMAS monotonicity concerns
coalition expansion; our own-report condition keeps owners fixed. Tijs1981
was not obtained directly in this bounded free-source check. Historical
novelty of our floor-plus-capacity incentive separation remains unconfirmed.

3. GPU reservations support the commitment abstraction, with strong limits.
AWS official EC2 Capacity Blocks documentation:
https://docs.aws.amazon.com/AWSEC2/latest/UserGuide/ec2-capacity-blocks.html
https://docs.aws.amazon.com/AWSEC2/latest/UserGuide/capacity-blocks-pricing-billing.html
https://docs.aws.amazon.com/AWSEC2/latest/UserGuide/capacity-blocks-share.html
The current documents describe future fixed-window GPU reservations, an
upfront price fixed at reservation, and account-level sharing within one AWS
Organization. The owner remains responsible for the reservation; consumers
do not acquire an unrestricted resale right. Sharing is first-come access,
not our proposed coalition payment mechanism. This supports distinguishing
sunk reservation cost from subsequent utilization, but does not establish
independent-firm GPU/power entitlement trading or our physical campus model.
An actual proposed deployment needs its own transferable-rights contract.

4. Power and energy cannot be pooled as interchangeable coordinates.
US Department of Energy, Solar Integration: Solar Energy and Storage Basics:
https://www.energy.gov/cmei/systems/solar-integration-solar-energy-and-storage-basics
The primary explainer distinguishes energy capacity from discharge power and
notes conversion losses. Accordingly L_j means deliverable power for a fixed
window Delta; separately enforce available energy>=L_j Delta, appropriate
efficiency losses, state-of-charge constraints, and outage reserves. One-period
rights cannot represent multi-period battery arbitrage without a new model.

5. Grid service rights and cost obligations must be documented, not presumed.
FERC Commissioner Chang's18 June2026 concurrence, EL26-70-000:
https://www.ferc.gov/news-events/news/e-8-commissioner-changs-concurrence-midcontinent-independent-system-operator-inc
Read sections on transmission services and customer protection. The statement
describes region-specific proceedings, proposed firm/non-firm products for
co-located/flexible loads, and cost-recovery concerns. This is evidence that
deliverability and cost responsibility are institutional choices, not proof
that a freely transferable right P currently exists at any selected campus.
The model therefore assumes an existing authorized, enforceable campus-level
reservation and does not claim a newly proposed national rule makes it valid.

RECOMMENDED CONTRACT FOR A FIRST APPLICATION

Use one campus, one short committed window, a qualified-workload service
envelope, and a neutral operator with a pre-existing right to schedule these
reservations. Document actual owners, independent withholding rights, hardware
location, local battery deliverability, and shared grid deliverability. A local
battery remains local. The graph encodes complementary inputs, not GPU movement.

Separate three ledgers before optimizing:
(i) public fixed availability retainers and precommitted capital/reservation cost;
(ii) verifiable variable operating costs and net output value;
(iii) incremental coalition surplus and any external stabilization/fairness money.
Never count the same energy bill both as a deducted cost and as a payoff floor.
The mathematical total-payment denominator is NET production value T. Gross
cash and the amount of externally funded retainers should also be reported.

Fixed public retainers are the credible participation starting point. A floor
theta M_A is a distinct bargaining provision one could negotiate and test; it
is not an estimate of private battery opportunity cost. It restores positive
surplus payment only when A has positive marginal contribution. A floor theta L
pays for useful local capacity even when grid substitution eliminates A's
grand marginal contribution; its unavoidable cost is static and can be large.

FALSIFICATION GATE
Reject the payment-only application if withheld hardware can run outside jobs,
withheld battery energy can be privately resold/carried forward profitably, or
grid purchase costs are private and not settled by the contract. Also reject a
common-row model if deliverability depends on coalition composition beyond owned
rights. These failures call for a new utility/feasibility model; a payment floor
alone does not fix them. No pilot or Mac measurement establishes these assumptions.
