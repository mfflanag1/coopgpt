RULE AND MODEL CHECKED
Actual owners are A(all local capacities L_i), C(grid capacity P), and one
hardware owner H_i for each h_i, for any finite m>=1. Reports are arbitrary
nonnegative real capacities and utility is payment only, with binding
coordinatewise underreports. Put
  ell_i=min(h_i,L_i), r_i=h_i-ell_i,
  L=sum ell_i, Q=sum r_i, H=L+Q,
  G=min(P,H), R=min(P,Q), T=L+R, M=T-G.
For theta=1 and L>0, define
  x_i=ell_i clip((P-Q+r_i)/L), X=sum x_i,
  y_A=M, y_C=G-(X-L/4)_+, y_Hi=x_i.
At L=0, use y_A=0,y_C=G,all x_i=0. For arbitrary theta, mix this rule with
the efficient rule(0,R,ell_i), using fixed weight theta on the new rule.

1. OWN-REPORT MONOTONICITY AND ZERO BOUNDARIES
Identity M=min(L,(H-P)_+) holds in all three regimes P<=Q, Q<P<H, P>=H.
When A changes any or all local reports, H and P stay fixed and L increases
coordinatewise. Therefore y_A is own-bundle monotone. This argument does
not mistakenly hold Q fixed when A changes its reports.

For hardware i, the original numerator P-Q+r_i equals
P-sum_{j!=i}r_j and is independent of h_i. Likewise L_-i is fixed. Writing
p_i=(P-sum_{j!=i}r_j)_+, its payment equals
  min{ell_i, p_i ell_i/(L_-i+ell_i)}.
Both terms are nondecreasing in ell_i. For the second, the derivative is
p_i L_-i/(L_-i+ell_i)^2 wherever the denominator is positive. If L_-i=0,
the expression is min{ell_i,p_i} for positive ell_i and tends to zero as
ell_i decreases to zero. Thus the L=0 boundary is continuous in own payment.
After h_i reaches L_i, ell_i remains constant and so does its payment;
the increase of residual r_i cancels from the numerator. Whole own-report
monotonicity therefore holds through every clipping threshold.

For grid owner C, every ell_i,r_i,L,Q,H is fixed. Each x_i(P) is increasing
and ell_i/L-Lipschitz, so X(P) is increasing and1-Lipschitz. By P=H,
P-Q+r_i=L+r_i>=L, hence X has saturated at L. Therefore for P'>=P,
  0<=X(P')-X(P)<=min(P',H)-min(P,H)=G(P')-G(P).
The positive-part function is increasing and1-Lipschitz, proving that
G-(X-L/4)_+ is nondecreasing. This also covers intervals crossing P=H.
When L=0, C simply receives the increasing function G.

The inequality P-Q+r_i<=P gives X<=min(L,P)<=G. Hence y_C>=0.
All other payments are nonnegative. If T=0 then L=0 and G=T=0, so every
payment is zero. Hardware h_i=0 forces x_i=0; grid P=0 forces y_C=0;
all A-owned local capacities zero forces M=0. Mixing preserves these facts.

2. LOCAL-ENERGY COALITIONS
Set D=G-R. In every regime,
  D/L=clip((P-Q)/L) for L>0, and M=L-D.
Since r_i>=0, x_i>=ell_i D/L and x_i<=ell_i. For any hardware subset I,
  ell(I)-x(I)<=ell(I)(1-D/L)<=L-D=M.
Thus A plus I receives at least ell(I), its exact local-only production.
At L=0 all such worths vanish.

3. GRID COALITIONS: INDEPENDENT SUBSET-ENVELOPE DERIVATION
For fixed hardware and local reports, define
  d_I(p)=min(p,H_I)-x_I(p), H_I=sum_{i in I}h_i.
On p<=H_I, d_I is increasing because x_I is s-Lipschitz, where
s=ell(I)/L<=1. On p>=H_I it is decreasing. Therefore its maximum over
past grid reports0<=p<=P occurs at t=min(P,H_I). Put
  c_I=d_I(t), c_env=max_I c_I.
The empty subset gives0, and c_I<=t<=G, so0<=c_env<=G.

It suffices to show X+c_I-G<=L/4 for every I. Current P may be replaced
by p=G: if P>H, all x_i have already saturated and t=min(P,H_I)=H_I
does not change. Otherwise p=P.

Case H_I>=p. Here t=p and X+c_I-p=x_outside(p). For j outside I,
Q-r_j>=r(I), hence
  (p-Q+r_j)/L <= (p-r(I))/L <= ell(I)/L=s.
Clipping is monotone and s lies in[0,1], so outside payment is at most
L(1-s)s<=L/4. Negative numerators cause no problem.

Case H_I<p. Write delta=(p-H_I)/L>0, so t=H_I. For every outside j,
its clipping argument is at most s+delta, and inside payments increase
between t and p by at most s(p-t)=Ls delta. Consequently
  X+c_I-p
  =x_outside(p)+[x_inside(p)-x_inside(t)]-(p-t)
  <=L(1-s)[clip(s+delta)-delta]
  <=Ls(1-s)<=L/4.
The middle inequality uses clip(s+delta)-delta<=s: it is equality below
the upper clip and becomes1-delta<=s above it. The edge cases s=0,s=1
are included. Thus c_env<=G-X+L/4 as well as c_env<=G, giving
  c_env<=min(G,G-X+L/4)=y_C.
Since d_I(P)<=c_env, every C-plus-hardware coalition is covered.

4. COALITIONS CONTAINING BOTH ENERGY OWNERS
For a hardware subset I, the difference between its joint-energy worth and
grid-only worth is exactly
  ell(I)+min(P,r(I))-min(P,H_I)
    =min(ell(I),(H_I-P)_+)=M_I.
This is at most M=min(L,(H-P)_+), since both arguments increase when I is
enlarged to all hardware. Adding A's payment M to the already established
grid-coalition inequality covers every coalition containing both energy
owners. Coalitions with neither energy owner have zero production. Together
with Sections2--3, this exhausts all coalitions, including empty hardware sets.

5. BUDGET, INTERPOLATION, AND SHARPNESS
The exact theta=1 identity is
  total=M+G+X-(X-L/4)_+=T+min(X,L/4)<=T+L/4<=5T/4.
Fixed convex interpolation with the efficient rule preserves every proved
eligibility property, gives A exactly theta M, and gives exact total
  T+theta min(X,L/4)<=(1+theta/4)T.
The public parameter theta is fixed independently of the report profile.

For any positive even m, choose h_i=L_i=1 and P=m/2. Then T=m,M=m/2.
Let a,c,X denote payments to A,C,and all hardware. Coalition coverage gives
  a+X>=m,
and averaging grid-plus-I constraints over all m/2-element hardware subsets
gives c+X/2>=m/2. The floor is a>=theta m/2. Applying weights1/2,1,1/2
to these inequalities yields
  a+c+X>=m+theta m/4.
This proves uniform sharpness with growing active owner/resource counts.
It does not assert optimality of this implementation on every fixed m.
All reports in this lower family belong to the same specified model; within
each problem the actual owners/roles remain fixed.
