95: Theorem. On all nonnegative real profiles, (2) is nonnegative, covers every
96: coalition, is whole-owner underreport truthful, and satisfies
97:   sum_i y_i <= (1+1/(2e)) F(sum_i g_i,sum_i q_i).
98: The bound is independent of the numbers of owners and hardware types. Mixed
99: ownership of hardware and power is permitted. Explicit evaluation takes
100: O(m log m + nm) arithmetic operations and O(m) exponential evaluations after
101: prefix aggregation; this is a real-arithmetic statement, not an exact finite-bit
102: evaluation guarantee for transcendental numbers.

104: Proof. Identity (1) holds for every coalition, so summing component coverage
105: with fixed nonnegative Delta_k proves coverage. Each owner's own coordinate
106: increase raises its derived H_i,k and/or q_i, leaving other owners' derived
107: reports fixed. The lemma and positive weights prove incentives. Summing the
108: component budget bounds and applying (1) proves the budget. QED.

122: 4. Existing lower bound and exact live gap
123: 
124: One type is a subclass, so the existing 9/8 lower bound applies. To make the
125: owner-preserving certificate explicit, use two hardware-only owners 0,1 and
126: two power-only owners 2,3 at profiles
127:   c=(1,1 | 1,1), a=(1,1 | 1,2), b=(2,1 | 1,1).
128: At a, coverage of {0,2},{1,2},{0,1,3} gives 2Y(a)-y_3(a)>=4.
129: At b, coverage of {1,2},{1,3},{0,2,3} gives 2Y(b)-y_0(b)>=4.
130: At c, coverage of {0,3} plus monotonicity gives
131: 1<=y_0(c)+y_3(c)<=y_0(b)+y_3(a)<=2Y(a)+2Y(b)-8.
132: All three profiles have T=2. A uniform Y<=C T therefore requires 1<=8C-8,
133: or C>=9/8. This is the earlier lower witness, reproduced rather than claimed
134: as new. In particular the optimal uniform factor for the star class lies in
135:   [9/8, 1+1/(2e)].
136: 
137: More precisely, positive-sum decomposition shows that the optimal uniform
138: factor over arbitrary mixed-owner star technologies EQUALS that over arbitrary
139: mixed-owner two-input technologies: a rule for the latter composes prefixwise,
140: and the former contains the latter as m=1. Thus increasing heterogeneity in
141: this star model cannot itself open a harder minimax problem. Closing this
142: interval is exactly a two-input problem, not a new independent frontier.
143: If the infimum is not attained, compose rules within any positive epsilon of
144: the two-input infimum, then let epsilon decrease to zero.

146: 5. Institutional obstructions and reduction
147: 
148: Veto power. If one fixed owner is required for every positive-output coalition,
149: paying it T and everybody else zero is efficient, covering and own-monotone.
150: This is the conventional veto-player construction. For m compute owners,
151: v({0} union S)=min(q,sum_{i in S}g_i), imposing y_0<=alpha T for fixed alpha
152: in [0,1] instead gives optimal uniform total factor m-(m-1)alpha. The matching
153: rule is y_0=alpha T, y_i=(1-alpha)min(g_i,q). Pair coverage at g_i=q=1 proves
154: the lower bound. This extra funding is forced already at one profile by the
155: distributional cap, not by incentives.
156: 
157: Full common access. For v(S)=min(sum_{i in S}g_i,P), fixed public P,
158: B(b)=sum_i min(g_i,P), attained on every profile by singleton payments
159: y_i=min(g_i,P), which are own-monotone. Static funding can be nT while Gamma=1.
160: More generally, suppose each activity consumes exactly one private resource
161: row (strictly positive coefficient), plus any number of nonnegative common
162: resource rows with fixed capacities. Every coalition plan can be split among
163: owners in proportion to their contribution of that activity's private row.
164: Each owner's allocated subplan respects its private capacities and consumes
165: no more of any public row than the original plan. Thus coalition value is at
166: most the sum of singleton values; paying singleton values is covering,
167: statically optimal and own-monotone. Exclude activities consuming no private
168: row and assume finite optima. Full public access for each deviating coalition
169: is an explicit optimistic outside-option convention, not simultaneous physical
170: feasibility for all coalitions or a claim about actual rationed markets.
171: 
172: Broad additive power retains sparse hardness. Given any sparse nonnegative LP
173: F(c)=max{p.x:Ax<=c,x>=0}, with p>0, nonzero columns of A and finite nonnegative
174: capacities, append a row p.x<=q. The new production function
175: G(c,q)=min(F(c),q), by scaling an optimal plan. Choose fixed w>=0 satisfying
176: A^T w>=p and embed each original report b_i as (b_i,w.b_i). This preserves
177: owner identities, coordinatewise underreport order and every coalition value,
178: because F(c)<=w.c. Every column gains just one positive entry. A truthful
179: covering rule for G therefore restricts to one for F with the same factor.
180: Choosing w dual-optimal at a specified positive target profile makes the
181: power row binding there; lowering q below T lowers output one-for-one. The
182: chosen w is then held FIXED on the full embedded domain. This establishes a
183: mathematical reduction, not an assertion that energy per activity equals price
184: in real hardware. A generic additive-power label alone does not simplify the
185: general sparse problem.

187: 6. Exact obstruction to a fixed greedy order beyond the star
188: 
189: There are two hardware rows H1,H2, two power rows P1,P2 and three activities:
190:   a=(H1,P1), value 3; b=(H1,P2), value 2; c=(H2,P1), value 2.
191: Every activity consumes one unit of each incident resource. The resource graph
192: is a path, hence acyclic. Consider a greedy procedure that fixes one order of
193: these activities independently of reports and fills each activity as much as
194: remaining resources permit.
195: 
196: At capacities (H1,H2,P1,P2)=(1,0,1,1), optimal value is 3, so such an optimal
197: greedy order must put a before b. At (1,1,1,0), it must put a before c. But at
198: (1,1,1,1), a first yields value 3 whereas b=c=1 yields 4. Therefore no fixed
199: activity order is optimal at all profiles, even on this three-edge path.
200: This obstructs copying the star's fixed merit-order proof to arbitrary
201: compatibility trees. It does NOT show that a different decomposition or payment
202: rule cannot achieve the same factor, or that the general 2T bound is tight.
