1. BASE MODEL AND EFFICIENT RULE
For fixed hardware set I and fixed ownership, the stated coalition values are
consistent with the resource-flow model. With both energy owners present,
local service ell_j=min(h_j,L_j) may be maximized independently before shared
grid serves residual demand. Hence worth is ell(I)+min(P,r(I)).

The all-profile rule (0,R,ell_1,...,ell_m) covers every coalition. With A alone,
hardware payments equal local coalition output. With both energy owners,
R=min(P,r([m]))>=min(P,r(I)). With C alone:
  if P<=r([m]), then R=P already covers its coalition output;
  otherwise R=r([m])>=r(I), so R+ell(I)>=h(I)>=min(P,h(I)).
Without either energy owner coalition worth is zero. Total payment equals T.
Own-A payment is constant zero; own-C payment increases with P; own-H_j
payment min(h_j,L_j) increases with h_j. No owner identities change.

For the local-output floor theta L, the proposed addition to A is own-A
monotone and leaves other own-payment maps unchanged. It gives total
T+theta L<=(1+theta)T, with zero payments when T=0. The profile h_j=L_j=1,
P=m gives T=L=m, while the coalition excluding A also produces m. Adding
A's floor proves the matching static lower (1+theta)m. This proves equality
of worst-case T-normalized funding and worst-case static requirement; it
does not prove pointwise equality to B_f or K_f=1. The source states this
distinction correctly. For literal completeness, specify m>=1 rather than
the potentially ambiguous phrase 'every finite m'; m=0 is a degenerate
zero-production domain and not the intended institution.

2. MARGINAL FLOOR: STATIC BASELINES
Payments below are ordered (a,c,x,y), for owners(A,C,H1,H2).
For theta in[0,1], the three displayed baselines are exact.

O=(P,h1,h2,L1,L2)=(1,1,1,1,1): T=2, M_A=1.
The witness(theta,theta/2,1-theta/2,1-theta/2) is nonnegative.
For A plus one hardware owner it pays1+theta/2>=1; for A plus both it
pays2; for C plus one it pays1; for C plus both it pays2-theta/2>=1.
With both energy owners and one hardware owner it pays1+theta>=1;
the grand coalition receives2+theta/2>=2. Coalitions without hardware or
without either energy owner have zero worth. Thus every coalition is covered.
For a matching lower, add the constraints A+H1+H2, C+H1, C+H2 and A's
floor. Their left side is2(a+c+x+y), right side4+theta. Therefore
  B_f(O)=2+theta/2.

V=(2,1,1,1,1): T=2, M_A=0. The witness(0,0,1,1) pays each present hardware
owner1. Every coalition's production is at most its number of hardware
owners, so every coalition is covered. The grand coalition forces total2:
  B_f(V)=2.

U=(1,2,1,1,1): T=3, M_A=2. The witness(2,1,0,0) meets the floor2theta.
A alone with any hardware produces at most2, covered by its payment2;
C alone with any hardware produces at most1, covered by its payment1;
both energy owners with any hardware produce at most3, covered by their
combined payment3. Without either energy owner production is zero.
Grand-coalition coverage forces total3, hence
  B_f(U)=3.

3. OWN-REPORT INEQUALITIES AND CONTINUOUS LOWER BOUNDS
At O, adding the two C+H_j constraints gives
  total_O+c_O-a_O=2c_O+x_O+y_O>=2.
With a_O>=theta and total_O<=K(2+theta/2),
  c_O>=2+theta-K(2+theta/2).
At V, the coalition A+H1+H2 has worth2, so c_V<=2K-2.
Only C's report changes from O to V, increasing P from1 to2. Hence
c_O<=c_V, giving
  K(4+theta/2)>=4+theta,
  K>=(8+2theta)/(8+theta).

For the second bound, c_O+x_O>=1 by C+H1 coverage. At U,
c_U+y_U>=1 by C+H2 coverage, a_U>=2theta, and total_U<=3K.
Thus x_U<=3K-1-2theta. Only H1 changes from O to U, increasing h1 from1
to2. Therefore x_O<=x_U, while c_O<=c_V<=2K-2 as above. Combining gives
  1<=c_O+x_O<=5K-3-2theta,
  K>=(4+2theta)/5.
Every comparison is between actual reports of the same one owner, with all
other owners' reports identical. Both lower bounds apply to any continuous
all-profile rule because all three profiles belong to its required domain.
They are strictly incentive-based after normalization by the exact static
baseline; the first exceeds1 for every theta>0.

The continuous upper (theta M_A,R,ell_1,...,ell_m) is valid: M_A=T-min(P,H)
is nonnegative and nondecreasing in A's report, because the subtracted term
does not depend on A. Its total is T+theta M_A<=(1+theta)T<=(1+theta)B_f.
When B_f=0, T=0 and every payment is zero. Thus zero-baseline profiles are
handled, rather than discarded.

5. ECONOMIC AND SCOPE WORDING
The marginal contribution floor is explicitly an added public normative
constraint, not conventional private-cost individual rationality. It can
vanish when the owner supplies useful substitutes. The text correctly avoids
calling it an observed outside option. Positive constant retainers or per-kW
floors cannot have finite T-normalized factors on a domain containing zero
production, while fixed report-independent retainers preserve own-payment
monotonicity when added to an eligible net-surplus rule. The participation
timing caveat is necessary and correctly supplied.

This audit does not establish growth with m, a continuous optimum18/17 or6/5,
a result for the original floor-free arbitrary mixed-owner problem, or novelty.
No institution or canonical file was changed. The coordinator subsequently
authorized a separate reproducible replay script and results in this lane.

6. ADDITIONAL GLOBAL STATIC RATIO -- CHECKED AFTER THE FIRST AUDIT
The new exact claim sup_{b:T>0} B_f(b)/T=1+theta/4 is correct over the class
of all finite m>=1, for0<=theta<=1. Here is an independent derivation.

The efficient vector e=(0,R,ell_j) and the energy-only vector g=(L,P,0,...,0)
both cover every coalition. For g, coalitions with only A among energy owners
produce at most L; those with only C produce at most P; those with both
produce at most L+P. Coalitions with neither have zero worth. Also
0<=M_A<=L. Therefore lambda=theta M_A/L lies in[0,1] when L>0. When L=0,
M_A=0 and lambda=0. The convex mixture (1-lambda)e+lambda g meets A's
floor and has total T+lambda(P-R).

If P<=H-L, then R=P and the excess is zero. If P>=H, then T=H and M_A=0,
so the excess is again zero. For H-L<P<H, necessarily L>0 and H>0, and
R=H-L,T=H. Write t=(P-(H-L))/L, so0<t<1. Then
  M_A=H-P=L(1-t), lambda=theta(1-t), P-R=Lt,
  excess/T=theta(L/H)t(1-t)<=theta/4.
The two endpoint cases are already included in the zero-excess branches.
At T=0 both the floor and static optimum are zero. For O, L=H=2,P=1,
so L/H=1,t=1/2 and the exact baseline B_f(O)=2+theta/2 attains the upper.
Hence the global static constant is sharp, without asserting sharpness for
every fixed m. The theta=1 static mixture pays H1 one half at O and zero
at U; since O->U only increases H1's report, this construction is indeed
not an own-report-monotone rule.

The distinction K_f>1 versus C_f exceeding the global static ratio is vital.
The current text now explicitly says the former does not imply the latter;
that is correct because K_f and C_f have different profile-dependent
denominators and may attain their worst cases at different profiles.
