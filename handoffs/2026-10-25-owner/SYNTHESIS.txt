1. WHAT THE PROGRAM IS ABOUT

The program began with a question about fairness within stable cooperation:
if every coalition can leave and produce value on its own, how much freedom is
left to allocate the common surplus, and how differently can reasonable fairness
rules use that freedom? It then moved from a single allocation to a rule used
across many resource reports: can stability coexist with incentives to contribute
resources, and what additional funding is unavoidable? The application branch
asks whether those contracts have a useful interpretation in compute, power,
financial risk or investment. Those are three different evidentiary problems.

Game theory specifies players, actions and payoffs. Mechanism design chooses the
institution: reports, feasible actions, allocation and payments. Algorithmic game
theory adds computability, information and approximation. A payment rule can
exist without being efficiently evaluable; an exact solver for one small example
does not supply a general algorithm.

In a transferable-utility cooperative game, v(S) is what coalition S can create
without the others. Its core is
  Core(v)={x: sum_i x_i=v(N), sum_{i in S}x_i>=v(S) for every S}.
These inequalities express stability against coalition departure. They do not
select a unique fair allocation. The individual ideal H_i is the largest payment
owner i receives anywhere in the original core; different H_i can be attained
at different allocations and need not be simultaneously attainable.

Leximin raises the poorest sorted payoff first. The project's opposing Lmax rule
minimizes the descending sorted payoff vector; its name must not be read as
maximizing one person's income. An exact game is one in which every coalition's
constraint is attained at some core point. Exactness does not mean all those
constraints are attained simultaneously. A construction using a segment must
show that it is the entire core, not merely a convenient feasible subset.

In the production papers, actual owner i holds a resource bundle and reports
b_i in a fixed coordinatewise box. The technology F gives output
T=F(B), B=sum_i b_i; coalition S can produce F(sum_{i in S}b_i).
Nonnegative payments cover every coalition at every profile. A total funding
factor K means sum_i y_i(b)<=K F(B). The sponsor's extra payment is at most
(K-1)F(B), not K F(B).

Individual payment incentives require y_i to be nondecreasing in owner i's own
report, with all other owners unchanged. Coalition TCWM instead requires the
SUM of a protected coalition's payments to be nondecreasing when its members
restore resources. Full OUT requires the increase to cover the removed bundle's
joint stand-alone output. In the sparse program, only incentive coalitions have
size at most s; static coverage is still required for every coalition. Protected
members may keep their reports unchanged. This is not the same contract as
individual population monotonicity, arbitrary private-cost truthfulness,
false-name resistance or general group strategyproofness.

The distinction matters economically. GPU capacity, memory, bandwidth, firm
electrical capacity and service output are not automatically interchangeable
money. A coalition's physical exit rights, how its removed resources can work
together, and who funds a stronger guarantee are part of the model. The program
has repeatedly rejected applications that supplied telemetry but not those rights.

3. HOW THE MATHEMATICS ADVANCED

The static phase moved beyond repeated local counterexample searches by
describing whole cores, building asymptotic families and proving graph-dependent
classifications. The important output is the exact theorem and its domain, not
the number of searches or draft titles. Earlier chain-cycle and graph-protection
branches are subsumed by P1; pair/triple comparison notes by P3; many boundary
calculations by P4/P5. P6 illustrates why an internally correct result can still
have a much narrower contribution after source comparison.

The individual-resource phase exposed the funding cost of making one rule work
over all reports. P8's leading constant is sharp in supporting-form count K:
  B_K=(1+o(1)) log K/log log K.
The lower already occurs on simple DAGs with strategic auxiliary owners; those
owners cannot be removed from the contract. K can be exponential in the network
description. The broader upper
  min{d, n sqrt(2/(n+1)), 1+2 sqrt(d)}
does not close the unrestricted n/d gap.

Ownership campaigns then tested whether physical topology could survive
strategic owners holding several kinds of resource. On the depth-two
hardware/local-resource/grid technology, the actual own-report envelope has
funding at most 323/200. Exact interval certificates and analytic response
lemmas support the bound; an attempted general 8/5 strengthening stopped at a
relaxation obstruction, not a mechanism counterexample. A misplaced C^2 shortcut
was withdrawn. Later weighted-graph TCWM factor 3/2 supersedes the old sub-two
EXISTENCE target: the depth-two technology is the fractional resource graph
with edges H_j--L_j and H_j--P, whose value is min(H,P+sum_j min(H_j,L_j)).
Owners retain all their original resource rows, and coalition TCWM implies
individual monotonicity. This does not say the earlier envelope attains 3/2.

The overnight program established broader coalition contracts, resource-graph
lifts, common-capacity growth laws, reserve-cone guarantees and several exact
architecture barriers. Its 66 root adjudications and chronological headline
ledger are indexed in SOURCE_INDEX.json. These are supporting results and
corollaries, not 66 papers. Their old suggested next goals are superseded by
later decisions.

P9's sharp universal dimension factors are
  D_d=(d+1)/2,
  D_d^OUT=1+c(d-1), c(2-exp(-1/c))=1, c approximately .5430633898.
They range over finite owner institutions and the stated monotone concave
degree-one technologies. Coalition protection is stronger than individual
protection; these lower bounds cannot be imported into the individual problem.

For finite packing technologies of activity arity at most r, the full-OUT
upper is r s^(r-1), independent of owner and resource counts. The October 18
campaign improved this project's earlier arity-three lower to
  sqrt(s/6)<=K_3(s)<=K_3^OUT(s)<=3s^2.
The sharper ladder B_s is asymptotic to sqrt(2*pi*s)/(3*sqrt(e)), and B_6=493/243.
Higher arity gives Omega_r(s^((r-2)/(r-1))). Lower witnesses keep actual owners
fixed through comparisons; the fully eligible and pure-owner realizations have
different counts. Resource dimension is exponential. These are proposed
quantitative contributions pending specialist correctness and priority review,
not a statement that the earlier internal quarter-power bound was the published
state of the art.

The next upper-bound gate found useful exact structure but no subquadratic
mechanism. Balanced two-terminal comparison regions contract. A component with
k genuinely varying owners admits a max(k,r) bound; valuable frozen owners do
not by themselves yield a stronger lower. Efficient-price selection at every
profile fails on an exact finite optimum, while terminal-budget relaxation can
escape it. That price freedom does not control general outgoing reserves.
Repeated positive finite examples did not establish the required universal lemma.

The original minimum-norm own-report envelope route is now substantially closed.
Every efficient-core selector suffers linear envelope inflation on a specified
pool family. Every fixed cap and fixed power r>1 has a linear lower sequence
and matching owner-order upper. The uncapped quadratic envelope exceeds any
constant times sqrt(N). Growing obstructions require baseline domination, so
they are not unrestricted growing lower bounds. The separate immediate
unrestricted factor-two implication is explicitly isolated in the final draft.
Reopening the old missing-diagonal estimate would now repeat a false target.

The constructive graph return succeeded: full OUT with total factor
1+1/sqrt(2) on unit-profit four-resource paths, complete blow-ups and disjoint
unions, for arbitrary fixed mixed owners and all real profiles. Its complete
proof is in P9 Section 6. The attempted tree extension stopped: fixed original-
leaf-layer reserves fail on the six-vertex path P6, and an output-maximizing split can preserve
production and budget while violating incentives. Arbitrary sequential orders
and all tree mechanisms are not ruled out.

The latest October 25 reset sharpened the stopping boundaries:
- Preserving designated coverage rows can force arbitrarily costly one-layer
  repair, even for a chosen optimal zero-slack certificate with singleton
  coverage. Another optimum in that example is already one-layer.
- Choosing a different optimum does not always permit LOSSLESS laminar
  coverage: a fixed four-owner/three-profile instance has optimum 9/8 versus
  laminar cap 1, with a strict positive-report version as well.
- Irreversible individual reserves fail even on a fixed two-owner min game:
  an L-step chain needs exact restricted factor 1+3L/4. The original full-OUT
  problem on that same box has factor 1 via y=(F,0), and efficient-price domination with reserve release
  has factor 2. The obstruction is to irreversible reserves, not all mechanisms.

These results explain why the current decision is review and consolidation.
A new construction must explicitly allow and control reserve release/reassignment
across protected coalition sums. Merely writing the required inequality, adding
a fixed cap, preserving a convenient certificate representation, or obtaining
another bounded numerical example does not meet the general-progress gate.

4. OTHER MATHEMATICAL LANES THAT REMAIN PRESERVED

ROOT_TRACKS.json records each lane separately with exact source and review paths.
The major parked questions are:
- Floor-free full OUT on arbitrary weighted resource graphs, still between
  1+c and 2. The four-resource-path advance does not close that class.
- Payment-only pair TCWM funded from gains, distinct from pair full OUT whose
  owner coefficient is already linear.
- Two heterogeneous common resources with an unbounded private block. A single
  common resource admits sharp 3/2 TCWM under private diminishing returns;
  natural three-block or logarithmic completions fail mixed-owner tests.
- The unrestricted individual owner/resource-dimension gap using a genuinely
  contextual rule or transport argument, not the blocked norm envelope.
- Reserve norms and matrix production: broad conditional theorems plus narrowly
  scoped implementation/architecture limits, not direct infrastructure evidence.

The gain baseline is S+KG, where S=sum_i F(b_i) and G=F(B)-S. A theorem funded
from total F(B) does not imply a constant gains-only coefficient. Similarly,
resource trees, owner-compatibility trees and fixed coalition-generator trees
are different objects. Classical flow duality, principal partitions, semivalues,
supporting prices, norm embeddings and monotone majorants are credited ingredients.

The universal exact-game monotone-selector project asks for efficient core
selection under changes to characteristic-function coordinates. Its leximin
counterexample and finite dual/structural exclusions are relevant neighbors;
the universal question remains open in the inspected record. No equivalence
to resource withholding or transfer of funding constants was established.

The joint-investment result shows why a bad fairness selector need not imply

A different veto, commitment or liquidity institution is needed for a genuinely
different financing question. Accepted Output supplies an appropriate service
denominator: verification, repair and fallback costs count, and routing may use
only information available before assignment. Historical benchmark estimates
are not current-model workplace effects.
