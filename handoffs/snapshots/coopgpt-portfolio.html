P1
Structural classification / assignment markets

Acyclicity and simultaneous protection of individual core maxima

The pattern of interaction between sectors determines whether a stable allocation can protect everyone relative to their own best stable payoff. Trees permit a uniform guarantee. Cycles can defeat even the best choice of stable allocation as markets grow.

Contribution. A tree–cycle classification that applies both to the leximin rule and to the best simultaneous protection achievable anywhere in the entire core.

The mathematics and its scope
c(G) = R(G) = 1/K for trees;
c(G) = R(G) = 0 for cyclic connected graphs.

G is a fixed connected allowed interaction graph on K ≥ 2 sectors. Sector sizes are equal; pair matrices are nonnegative real matrices, with zero matrices allowed. Only complete teams produce; coalition values come from integral matchings. Require positive total worth and a nonempty entire core. With Hᵢ the individual core maximum, c(G) is the worst leximin ideal fraction and R(G) the worst best-achievable simultaneous ideal fraction. The triangle family has exact best fraction 1/(2q+3), and selective subdivision transfers the obstruction to general cyclic graphs.

Boundary of the claim. Zero is an infimum over growing finite markets. Every single finite market still has positive best simultaneous protection. Integral matching does not mean integer-valued input weights. This theorem does not cover arbitrary productive partial teams.

Review and priority. The October 14 source and 11-page PDF match the release hashes. Scoped internal audits are recorded. Bilateral half-ideal protection, matching duality and vanishing guarantees in broader classes have antecedents; the strict-model classification is the proposed contribution. Priority and human specialist review remain open.

P2
Fairness selectors / exact games

Sharp asymptotic separation of opposing egalitarian core selections

Two rules that both favor equality can distribute almost all the same surplus to different people. This paper determines how quickly their largest possible disagreement approaches that extreme as the population grows.

Contribution. A sharp first-order quantitative comparison between opposing fairness selectors, combining a universal convex overlap inequality with explicit whole-core constructions.

The mathematics and its scope
sₙ = sup ‖Lmin(v) − Lmax(v)‖₁ / T
2 − sₙ = 2/n + O(log n / n²),
so n(2 − sₙ) → 2.

The supremum is over singleton-normalized exact transferable-utility games with n players and T = v(N) > 0. The upper argument applies to nonnegative compact convex constant-total sets; whole-core Fibonacci segments realize the matching asymptotic lower. Lmax here minimizes descending sorted payoffs. Half the normalized L1 distance is the fraction of surplus that changes recipients.

Boundary of the claim. This is not an exact finite-n formula, a unique-extremizer theorem or an aggregate welfare-loss claim. An explicit subset of the core would not suffice: the entire-core characterization is essential.

Review and priority. Current packaged September source/PDF hashes agree. Internal correctness and priority audits are available. Whole-core segments and Fibonacci matrix ingredients have precedents; the precise opposing-selector comparison is the candidate contribution. Global priority and human review remain unconfirmed.

P3
Matching markets / fairness distance

Fair core selections: a sharp boundary between pairs and triples

Moving from pairwise cooperation to three-way cooperation changes the possible disagreement between fairness rules. Pair markets have a sharp finite bound; sparse triple markets can approach complete redistribution.

Contribution. A sharp finite distance theorem for graph matching, together with a sparse three-sided construction and a distinct additive benchmark.

The mathematics and its scope
max ρgraph = ⌊n/2⌋ / (⌊n/2⌋ + 1), n ≥ 4
sup ρtriples = 2;  sup ρadditive = 4/3.

ρ = ‖Lmin − Lmax‖₁/T. The graph class has unit capacities, nonnegative values, positive total and nonempty entire core; all players, including isolates, count toward n. Sparse strict triples permit arbitrary arrays and use bounded team values in {0,1,2,3}. The additive benchmark requires complete equal-sided markets and retains a one-third ideal guarantee.

Boundary of the claim. The triple result is a supremum over growing populations, not a finite-n classification. These matching examples need not be exact TU games. Arbitrary triple arrays and the complete additive benchmark must not be confused with P1’s pair-additive interaction-graph model.

Review and priority. Current packaged September source/PDF hashes agree. The half-ideal guarantee has prior algorithmic antecedents; classical matching reductions are credited. The finite distance comparison and sparse triple realization are principal candidates. Some original-source comparisons and human review remain incomplete.

P4
Core geometry / balanced transport

Balanced transport at the egalitarian core: a complete boundary phase diagram

At a fairness-optimal allocation, the richer players may receive strictly more than they can produce alone. This paper identifies exactly how many poorer and richer players are needed for that slack to be possible.

Contribution. A complete two-population existence threshold, with tight-coalition geometry, balanced transport and explicit realizing families.

The mathematics and its scope
Slack is possible iff m ≥ mmin(h), where
mmin(1) = 4;
mmin(2) = mmin(3) = 3;
mmin(h) = 2 for h ≥ 4.

For a singleton-normalized exact game, let φ = Lmin(v). Split players into L and H at a strict payoff boundary, with every player in H richer than every player in L; m = |L| and h = |H|. The question is whether v(H) < φ(H). Poorer players need not all have the same payoff. Necessity uses normal-cone/tight-coalition structure; sufficiency gives families at every admissible parameter.

Boundary of the claim. The principal phase theorem has stronger review coverage than some auxiliary finite-certificate claims. Restricted searches over five-player split shapes are not a universal uniqueness theorem.

Review and priority. The main theorem survived the portfolio audit. Exact partitions, Lorenz implications and qualitative geometry have earlier antecedents. The existing PDF is not identified as superseded, but a current source/PDF hash binding was not located; use the TeX for authoritative text. Human review and priority remain open.

P5
Core geometry / companion to P4

Boundary partitions at the leximin core: a two-parameter characterization

When exactly two players lie above a slack fairness boundary, this paper determines when two tight coalitions can partition everyone, placing one richer player in each coalition.

Contribution. A closed-form characterization, including the (2,2) construction that corrected an earlier conjecture.

The mathematics and its scope
BP(p,q) exists iff p ≥ 1, q ≥ 1 and p + q ≥ 4.

In a singleton-normalized exact game at φ = Lmin(v), the richer set is H = {f,g}. Complementary tight blocks are {f} ∪ P and {g} ∪ Q, with p = |P| and q = |Q| poorer players. The characterization assumes a strict slack boundary. Both necessity and sufficiency have explicit proofs and constructions.

Boundary of the claim. The statement is specifically about two richer players and two blocks. It gives no general multi-block theorem, prevalence statement or generator minimality. An auxiliary (1,3) frontier claim uses a separate finite stratification check.

Review and priority. A completed specialized companion to P4, rather than a separate broad program. The README’s old opening draft warning predates its later recorded internal audit. Existing PDF/source abstracts match, but a fresh version hash binding was not established here. External novelty confirmation remains pending.

P6
Core stability / procedural egalitarian rule

Core stability does not imply egalitarian stability: a sharp six-player counterexample

A game can have an entire core that is stable as a set, while a procedural egalitarian rule still selects an allocation outside that core. The paper makes this separation explicit with six players.

Contribution. A transparent sharp-dimensional witness, direct stability proof and complete three-regime additive family; the broad existence claim has a narrower novelty status than originally proposed.

The mathematics and its scope
C(v) = [A,B],  v(S) = min{A(S),B(S)}
A = (1,1,0,1,3,0),  B = (2,0,1,0,1,2).
Terminal claims sum to 13/2 > T = 6.

The game is exact, monotone and superadditive, with a von Neumann–Morgenstern stable core, but it is not singleton-normalized. Terminal procedural claims differ from the final efficient allocation. The final PES allocation is (3/2,1/2,1,1/2,3/2,1); coalition {5,6} receives 5/2, below its value 3. The rule here is distinct from the Lmin/Lmax comparison in P2 and P3.

Boundary of the claim. Do not describe the terminal 13/2 claims as the final efficient allocation. Six-player minimality uses established low-player implications. An earlier exact-game counterexample in Mauri’s thesis is not claimed new.

Review and priority. Current packaged September source/PDF hashes agree. Priority review downgraded bare existence to a short synthesis of classical ingredients. The explicit example and complete ray remain narrower worked-result candidates. No human endorsement or general novelty certification is recorded.

P7
Static protection / three-sided assignment

Sharp subsidies for protecting core maxima in three-sided assignment markets

External money can protect each participant at one third of their best original stable payoff while still covering every coalition. The worst necessary subsidy approaches one third of the original surplus, but no finite market reaches that ratio.

Contribution. The sharp static subsidy constant, supported by a fractional proof and an explicit growing family with full coordinate-attainment witnesses.

The mathematics and its scope
supv s1/3(v)/T = 1/3;
s1/3(v) < T/3 for every finite admissible market.

Freeze Hᵢ at the individual maxima in the original efficient core. Minimize Σᵢyᵢ − T subject to y(S) ≥ v(S) and yᵢ ≥ Hᵢ/3. The domain is strict equal-sized three-sector nonnegative pair-additive markets with positive total worth and a nonempty original entire core, with coalition worth defined by integral matchings of full teams. Six comparisons at separately attaining core points control a fractional packing dual. A {0,1,2}-valued family proves sharpness; simultaneous attainment of all ideals is not assumed. The separate integral-obstruction bound is 2(k−1)T/[3(2k−1)] for k unmatched players per sector, sharp for k ≥ 2; it is not the exact full fractional subsidy at every fixed market size.

Boundary of the claim. This is a fixed-game result, with no reporting-incentive guarantee. The endpoint incompatibility example in the extensions requires zero subsidy; it does not refute the one-third theorem. General-sector sharpness and unrestricted rational short-flow bounds remain open.

Review and priority. Current TeX includes the October 7 static-protection appendices: general sectors, short flow/quota, transportation embedding, sparse cores, coattainment, perturbation and cyclic-dual obstructions. The October 3 PDF predates those additions. Internal proof reviews and exact checks are recorded; historical priority and specialist review remain pending.

P8
Mechanism design / general production / networks

Sharp subsidies for truthful production and network cooperation

Protecting every coalition while preventing gains from hidden resources requires a funding budget that grows with production complexity. This paper proves the sharp growth rate, with a matching lower bound even for ordinary simple directed acyclic networks.

Contribution. A general capped hybrid mechanism and weighted report-transition lower bounds with a matching leading constant. New owner/dimension bounds expand the general concave-production theory.

The mathematics and its scope
BK = (1 + o(1)) log K / log log K.
General concave production: Σᵢyᵢ ≤ min{d, n√(2/(n+1)), 1 + 2√d} · T.

For the sharp K theorem, f(c) = minₖ wᵏ·c has at most K fixed nonnegative supporting forms. Fixed owners report whole nonnegative resource bundles, which may overlap across resource types. One deterministic rule covers every coalition and is monotone in each owner’s own bundle on all real profiles. The matching lower uses simple DAGs with strategic auxiliary owners. The new n-owner/d-resource upper bounds cover finite-valued monotone concave homogeneous production, including boundary discontinuities. The choice among these general bounds uses fixed public parameters before reports; it is not a pointwise minimum of payment vectors.

Boundary of the claim. Truthfulness is for binding coordinatewise underreports and payment-only utility. It does not cover overreports, private costs, collusion or identity splitting. K can be exponential in a compact network description. Restricted square-root architecture barriers are not unrestricted lower bounds. Owner/dimension rates remain open; general evaluation is not guaranteed efficient.

Review and priority. Main, supplement and brief were updated and compiled on October 16. The current source/check packet is authoritative; the October 14 PDFs and submission package are historical relative to those additions. The finite-sampling algorithm can require exponential certification preprocessing. Novelty and human specialist assessment remain unconfirmed; no outreach or submission was sent.

Research results beyond the paper titles

Major supporting theorems, recent extensions, measured outcomes and stopping results. Results already integrated into a manuscript are labelled accordingly.

Integrated in P8 / mechanism foundations

From small pools to an unrestricted hierarchy

The two-pool exponential mechanism bounds extra subsidy by 1/(2e) ≈ 18.394%, with a one-half original-ideal floor. A one-eighth subsidy lower bound leaves a real gap. Nonlinear one/two-altered-pool constructions show why proportional-payment obstructions do not apply to unrestricted rules. Weighted transition hierarchies ultimately close the multi-pool leading-constant gap at one.

What remains unresolved. Small altered-pool domains are scoped constructions, not constant-budget solutions for arbitrarily many altered pools. The multi-pool sharp theorem does not settle the exact two-pool constant.

Integrated in P8 / successful subclasses and obstructions

Networks, Wheatstone bridges and the old envelope

An effective-capacity rule works on two-terminal series-parallel networks and on a bundled bridge, but composition can break incentives. The original rule’s own-report monotone envelope restores incentives and coverage and has a KT upper bound; exact growing obstructions prevent it from achieving the sharp logarithmic rate. The successful general hybrid uses a different rule.

What remains unresolved. This older network-envelope obstruction is distinct from the still-open mixed-owner depth-two envelope. Neither a failed rule nor a failed proof estimate is an unrestricted mechanism impossibility.

Integrated in P8 / algorithmic result

Exact all-profile certification

For rational finite technologies, a frozen finite mask sample can preserve exact own-report incentives and cover every coalition on every real report profile. A rational arrangement/LP procedure can certify the seed, including tie faces. Repeated sampling and certification gives a Las Vegas preprocessing scheme.

What remains unresolved. Preprocessing may be exponential. Polynomial evaluation after successful certification is not an end-to-end polynomial-time or practical deployment guarantee. Fixed-profile tests alone cannot certify all-profile coverage.

Integrated in P7 / supporting static theorems

General sectors, flow and geometric obstructions

For K ≥ 3 sectors, the static bound is s1/K(v) < 2(K−2)T/[K(K−1)]. Unit-capacity DAGs whose source–sink paths have length at most three have sharp bound (T−1)/3. What remains unresolved. These are static claims. The endpoint-attainment obstruction needs no subsidy; growing-market sensitivity does not mean fixed-market discontinuity. The 33-player result is a finite computer-assisted proposition, not a general graph classification.

Heterogeneous hardware sharing scarce power

With mixed owners allowed to hold both hardware and power, a continuous rule covers every coalition and deters binding withholding with total funding at most (1 + 1/(2e))T ≈ 1.183940T. 16 October / sharp research supplement

A public energy-participation floor costs exactly θ/4

One owner controls local energy, one shared grid capacity, and separate owners control hardware pods. Requiring the local-energy owner to receive at least θ of its grand-coalition marginal contribution gives sharp uniform total factor 1 + θ/4 for 0 ≤ θ ≤ 1. The result holds on all real reports and arbitrarily many pods; fixed public pod values also work if local and grid service have the same value. The unit-value rule is O(m), including its fixed-θ mixture of two linear-time rules; the weighted construction is O(m²) after sorting.

What remains unresolved. The floor is a public contractual share, not an inferred private outside option. The separate static-premium optimum is open: at θ = 1 it lies in [6/5, 5/4]. The same-rule tradeoff C + K ≥ 2 + θ/2 requires at least two pods. This result is not yet a new integrated manuscript.

A short mathematical glossary

Coalition value
v(S) is what a group S can produce alone. T = v(N) is grand-coalition value.
Core
Allocations x with Σᵢxᵢ = T and Σᵢ∈S xᵢ ≥ v(S) for every coalition. Each group receives at least its stand-alone value.
Exact game
Every coalition’s constraint is attained at some core allocation. Different coalitions may need different allocations.
Individual ideal
Hᵢ = max{xᵢ : x is in the original core}. These individual best payoffs need not be simultaneously attainable.
Lmin and Lmax
Lmin maximizes the increasingly sorted payoff vector lexicographically. The project’s Lmax minimizes the decreasingly sorted vector lexicographically. Both favor equality; Lmax does not maximize one person’s payoff.
Funding vs subsidy
Total funding ≤ BT means extra subsidy ≤ (B − 1)T. A 4/3 funding factor means a one-third subsidy, not a 4/3 subsidy.
All-profile incentives
The same payment rule must deter withholding at every allowed report profile, with actual owners fixed. Separate core allocations at each profile do not automatically form a truthful mechanism.
n, d, K and θ
Usually n counts owners and d resource dimensions. K means sectors in graph/static results, or supporting forms/cuts in production results; these are different parameters. θ is the public contribution-share floor in the new energy contract.

  1. Lead with the truthful-production and network theorem (P8). It gives a sharp general asymptotic rate and a matching simple-DAG lower bound. Ask a specialist to assess the model, proof and priority.
  2. Keep the structural and static stories distinct. P1 classifies ideal protection by the sector graph. P7 prices static ideal protection. Neither supplies the all-profile incentive guarantee in P8.
  3. Test the new energy contract’s economic assumptions. Multiple local-energy owners or mode-dependent public costs are substantive next targets. The existing public-value extension already handles mode-independent heterogeneity.
  4. Keep open general problems explicit. Sharp owner/dimension rates, a factor below 2 for arbitrary mixed-owner depth-two production, and the unrestricted rational short-flow static bound remain unresolved.
