The ownership question became a general theorem.

The early test was whether splitting local-energy ownership required new mathematics. It did not: the sharp pure-role result extended as a corollary. The campaign therefore moved to owners who can simultaneously hold hardware, local resources and shared grid capacity.

What the production model actually describes.

Each branch has hardware and a local resource such as an energy allowance. Shared grid capacity can supply hardware that its local resource cannot serve. An owner may hold resources across any number of branches and roles; more than one owner may contribute to the same resource pool.

F = min(H, P + M)H = total hardware, P = total shared grid capacity,
M = Σj min(Hj, Lj).

Reports are binding capacity commitments. An owner may withhold its own resources coordinatewise, and its utility is its payment. Other owners’ reports remain fixed during a unilateral deviation.

This is a stylized production model. The funding guarantee is not a measured GPU throughput gain, energy saving, scheduling result or latency guarantee. Private operating costs, overreports, false names and coordinated deviations require different incentive constraints.

The precise payment construction and why incentives are automatic

The two-input primitive pays each owner a mixture of its short-pool contribution and its capped long-pool contribution. At short total T > 0, set di = min(longi, T), ρ = Σdi/T and q = e1−ρ/2:

Φi = (1 − q) shorti + qdi.

At T = 0 all primitive payments are zero. Sum the branch payments to obtain zi; apply the same primitive to Hi and pi + zi to obtain raw payment Ni. Then define

Ei(b) = sup0 ≤ b′i ≤ bi Ni(b′i, b−i).

A larger own report contains every previous underreport option, so Ei cannot decrease. Since Ei ≥ Ni, it preserves the raw rule’s proved coalition coverage. The challenge is controlling the total of these separately optimized payments. Different owners’ best deviations do not have to occur simultaneously.

The general mixed-owner funding theorem.

Σi Ei(b) ≤ (323/200) F(b) = 1.615 F(b)Every nonnegative real report profile; fixed actual owners; arbitrary finite owner and branch counts.

Economic meaning

For every coalition of owners, its members receive at least the production they could generate using only their reported resources. Each owner maximizes its payment by revealing its full feasible bundle.

The total funding requirement is at most 161.5% of production value; the extra funding above production is at most 61.5%. This is an upper guarantee, not a claim that every profile needs that subsidy.

The pure-role public grand-marginal floors are not among this mixed-owner theorem’s hypotheses. Its contract differs from the earlier 1 + θ/4 pure-role theorem.

What made the proof advance

The analysis retains the relationship between an owner’s hardware, local payment, capped account and response to underreporting. Subset entropy and deficit constraints rule out combinations that loose aggregate estimates permit.

All valid positive-excess inequalities constrain the same actual sum of envelope gains. Negative-gap savings, overflow accounting and owner-role constraints then reduce the funding question to a continuous moment region.

Sharp diffuse-payment asymptoticsSpecified envelope · hardware bottlenecks

When every full local payment is small relative to total hardware, the worst funding premium has an exact leading and logarithmic correction. Let W(ε) be the supremum of total envelope payments divided by H, over physical hardware bottlenecks with maxi zi/H ≤ ε.

W(ε) = C + cKε[L − log L] + O(ε)c = 1/(2e), C = 1 + c, K = e − 1, L = log(1/ε)

The matching family works for every sufficiently small real ε, with both owner and productive-branch counts growing. Small local payments do not require small hardware holdings. The ε condition restricts the full profile; counterreports still range over the original underreport boxes.

The next coefficient remains open. For R(ε) = [W − C − cKε(L − log L)]/ε, the current bounds are

lim inf R ≥ −0.777825046106238…
lim sup R ≤ −c(2 + K log 2) ≈ −0.586955733.

No existence or value of a residual limit is claimed. The improved lower uses the cap-hiding obstruction discussed below.

A sharp signed response constantLocal-only withholding · hardware retained

Suppose one actual owner withholds any part of its local-resource bundle while retaining its hardware. The signed loss to all other local recipients, with gains offsetting losses, is at most Λ times that owner’s full local payment:

[Σk ≠ i(zk − z′k)]+ ≤ Λziv*ev* = 1/2,   Λ = (1 − v*)²/v* ≈ 1.194793583.

An actual two-owner configuration attains the constant. The stronger role-sensitive version charges only local-resource payment components: coefficient 1 when originally short and Λ when originally long. Own hardware-role payments are excluded from that sharper charge.

This does not directly bound the sum of positive losses or cap-truncated outer losses. A separate earlier result gives the sharp positive-loss bound K√(ziDi), where Di is the deviating owner’s local-payment loss.

An exact obstruction to adding two signed chargesProof-method obstruction · unbounded consequence

The sharp one-owner signed bound cannot simply be added across two owners’ separate deviations after outer caps. An offsetting local gain can be hidden by a cap, while the associated loss remains visible.

Three fixed owners, with hardware 1 and local contributions 1 and 2/5, give the exact ratio

(11e2/5 − 8)/7 > 6/5 > Λ.

This disproves the proposed joint Λ charge. Its unbounded lift improves the actual diffuse-envelope lower bound, so the obstruction has a demonstrated consequence. It does not prove that every eligible mechanism needs that funding.

After one deviation, another owner’s local payment can exceed ε. This is permitted because diffuseness is a condition on the full profile only.

A sharper owner-specific compatibility inequalityStructural lemma · actual owners preserved

Normalize H = 1. Write ai = Hi, di = min(pi + zi, 1), Z = Σzi, δ = CM − Z and α = 1 − c. For a positive gap gi = di − ai,

zi + αgi ≤ 2 − c − M + M Q(δ/M).

Here Q is the increasing inverse of c + q log(2q) on [c, 1/2]; define M Q(δ/M) = 0 when M = 0. The inequality is attained on the full-utilization face M = 1.

Combining this with the actual ceiling Ei ≤ 1 gives a stronger positive-dual bound for S < 2, where at most one actual owner can be fully capped. It preserves the association between an owner’s payment and gap instead of allocating aggregate overflow freely.

The count assumption is strict: the one-cap argument does not apply at S = 2.

The next effort should expand the theorem’s reach.

The useful next mathematical step is a funding guarantee for deeper resource networks. Further tuning of the order-ε lower-bound gadgets would refine this envelope’s constants, but would not supply the broader method the research program is seeking.

Recommended next goal: a depth-independent ownership theorem

Construct a truthful, coalition-covering mechanism with a uniform funding factor κ < 2 on unit-production resource forests, independent of owner count, resource count and tree depth. Allow each fixed actual owner to hold arbitrary bundles across branches and levels.

Precisely: resource rows are graph vertices; each edge activity consumes one unit of each of its two endpoint resources and earns one unit. Row capacities aggregate owner reports. The resource graph is acyclic and eligibility is fixed.

This excludes general min-linear production and unbounded-arity serial complementarity, where the project already establishes that no universal constant funding factor suffices.

Package the present result

Prepare the general funding theorem, sharp diffuse law and essential response lemmas for specialist review. Keep architecture-specific and conditional computational statements distinct.

Prove a useful separator lemma

Couple baseline output and boundary resources so a change in one actual owner’s whole bundle preserves incentives without multiplying funding factors across levels.

Keep an explicit stopping gate

Accept a recursive invariant with a proved one-step consequence, or an exact actual-owner obstruction to the proposed invariant. Another fixed-depth example does not pass.

The mathematical first target and the approaches already blocked

When child components meet a new shared resource, the exact production identity is

F = A + min(H, P + M).

Here A is production without the boundary resources and M is their aggregate marginal contribution. M need not be own-report monotone: on a child path h–ℓ–u,

A = min(ℓ, u),   M = min(h, (ℓ − u)+).

Increasing an owner’s internal substitute u can increase A while decreasing M. Feeding M into an individually monotone primitive is insufficient. A compensated separator lemma must keep both effects in the same owner’s payment accounting.

The induction must maintain one constant at every depth. It must not reuse the already blocked virtual-vertex-core majorant, fixed component covers, or independent signed charges that ignore cap hiding. A counterexample to one invariant is a method obstruction; an unrestricted lower bound requires coalition constraints linked by actual unilateral report transitions.

The C² question for the current depth-two envelope remains a useful secondary target. Even an actual counterexample would reject that bound for one rule, not every possible mechanism.
