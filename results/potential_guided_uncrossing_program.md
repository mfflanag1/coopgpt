# Threshold repair conjecture and proof program

## Canonical reduced capacity dual

For a both-sensitive circuit, let \(q\) be its aggregate balanced collection
and let \(\rho<1\) be its recoloring capacity. The base color is redundant:
any balanced coloring \((\mu_0,\mu_1,\mu_2)\) can be replaced by
\[
 w=\mu_1+\theta\mu_0,\qquad q-w=\mu_2+(1-\theta)\mu_0
 \quad(0\leq\theta\leq1),
\]
without decreasing either distinguished retained mass. Thus \(\rho\) is a
two-color box problem.

Its exact dual uses one zero-sum player potential \(z\) and two nonnegative
retention prices. Put
\[
 \Phi(r_A,r_B,z)=r_Bq_B+
 \sum_Sq_S[z(S)+r_A\mathbf1_{\{S=A\}}-r_B\mathbf1_{\{S=B\}}]_+.
\]
Then
\[
 \rho=\min\{\Phi(r_A,r_B,z):
 r_A\lambda_{1,A}+r_B\lambda_{2,B}=1,
 \ r_A,r_B\geq0,\ z(N)=0\}.
\]
This removes the coalition prices and the three gauge-dependent player
potentials from the earlier formulation.

## Two-extreme-set repair conjecture

Every both-sensitive positive circuit with disjoint arms admits an optimal
minimizer with nonconstant \(z\), orthogonal to the support nullspace, such
that, writing
\[
 P^+=\operatorname{argmax}_{i\in N}z_i,
 \qquad
 P^-=\operatorname{argmin}_{i\in N}z_i,
\]
both coalitions \(P^+\) and \(P^-\) satisfy the two-color transfer criterion.

By the positive-circuit formulation and the two-level certificate
characterization, this conjecture implies that every base-plus-two-bump family
of exact games with disjoint arms is feasible, in every dimension.

## Single-witness recoloring system

The conjecture can be stated without endpoint-game variables. Fix \(U\) and
seek \(\mu_{g,S}\geq0\), \(\alpha_g\), and \(\delta_g\) satisfying
\[
 \sum_{S\ni i}\mu_{g,S}=\alpha_g+\delta_g\mathbf1_U(i),
 \qquad
 \sum_g\mu_{g,S}=q_S+\Big(\sum_g\delta_g\Big)\mathbf1_{\{S=U\}},
\]
with \(\sum_g\alpha_g=\sum_ga_g\) and the two adjusted bump-retention
conditions. Summing the three two-level exactness inequalities cancels the
inserted base term \((\sum_g\delta_g)b(U)\). This is precisely the
single-witness recoloring criterion now proved in the manuscript. Balanced
recoloring is the subcase \(\delta_g=0\).

Thus the global problem has been reduced to a two-cut alternative: prove that
the fixed-witness system is feasible for \(P^+\) or \(P^-\).

## Two-color box reduction

The exact audit permits a stronger target. In every known case the successful
top-set repair can be chosen with
\[
 \mu_0=0,\qquad \delta_0=0,\qquad \delta_1+\delta_2=0.
\]
Put \(w=\mu_1\). Since the aggregate collection \(q\) is balanced of total
weight \(a=\sum_ga_g\), the entire certificate is then equivalent to
\[
 0\leq w_S\leq q_S,
 \qquad
 \sum_{S\ni i}w_S=\alpha+\delta\mathbf1_U(i),
\]
together with
\[
 w_A-\delta\mathbf1_{\{U=A\}}\geq\lambda_{1,A},
 \qquad
 q_B-w_B+\delta\mathbf1_{\{U=B\}}\geq\lambda_{2,B}.
\]
The second color is simply \(q-w\) and has coverage
\((a-\alpha)\mathbf1-\delta\mathbf1_U\). Thus the three-color exactness
problem collapses to intersecting the degree zonotope
\[
 Z(q)=\sum_S[0,q_S]\mathbf1_S
\]
with the plane \(\operatorname{span}\{\mathbf1,\mathbf1_U\}\), subject to two
distinguished-coordinate bounds.

This is now the preferred theorem target: for a support-orthogonal optimal
reduced dual, both \(U=P^+,P^-\) make the displayed box system feasible. It is
strictly more
structured than the general single-witness formulation and should admit a
cut, zonotope, or polymatroid separation argument.

## Layer-cake lemma

If the distinct coordinate values of \(u\in\mathbb R^N\) are
\(c_1<\cdots<c_k\), and
\(U_j=\{i:u_i\geq c_j\}\), then
\[
 u=c_1\mathbf1+
   \sum_{j=2}^k(c_j-c_{j-1})\mathbf1_{U_j}.
\]

This follows coordinatewise: for a coordinate at level \(c_m\), the right
side telescopes to \(c_1+\sum_{j=2}^m(c_j-c_{j-1})=c_m\). Hence every player
potential is generated by two-level directions indexed by its threshold
chain. This explains why threshold coalitions are the canonical exactness
witnesses. It does not alone guarantee the nonnegative coalition collections
required by a certificate; that is the remaining substantive step.

## Proposed contradiction proof

1. Choose an ordinary optimal minimizer \((r_A^*,r_B^*,z^*)\) and form its
   extreme coordinate classes \(P^+,P^-\).
2. Assume \(\tau_{P^+}<1\) and \(\tau_{P^-}<1\). The reduced fixed-witness
   dual then supplies normalized minimizers \((r^+,z^+)\) and \((r^-,z^-)\)
   with objective below one and, respectively,
   \[
   z^+(P^+)+r_A^+\mathbf1_{\{P^+=A\}}
      -r_B^+\mathbf1_{\{P^+=B\}}=0,
   \]
   and the analogous equality at \(P^-\).
3. Uncross the positive-part supports in \(\Phi(r^+,z^+)\) and
   \(\Phi(r^-,z^-)\). The strict coordinate ordering of \(z^*\) outside its
   extreme classes should give the sign needed to average the two separators
   into a reduced ordinary dual with objective strictly below the value
   permitted by the positive-circuit normalization.
4. Equivalently on the primal side, use the two failed extreme cuts to build
   an augmenting direction for the optimal balanced subcollection \(w^*\),
   increasing both distinguished retained masses and contradicting
   optimality of \(\rho\).

The remaining delicate point is the positive-part nonlinearity in \(\Phi\).
The next symbolic audit should classify the active sets
\(\{S:z(S)+r_A1_{S=A}-r_B1_{S=B}>0\}\) and test which submodular uncrossing
identity is common to all deficient circuits.

## Exact evidence

`results/potential_guided_source_ablation.json` checks the threshold witness
family on 31/31 known recoloring obstructions through representative probes of
all six disjoint \(n=7\) geometries.
The checks solve the restricted endpoint primal and verify its rational dual
coefficientwise. `results/potential_guided_minimal_tights.json` then tests
every individual arm-potential threshold: at least one single \(U\) works in
all 31 cases. Twenty-eight certificates use two game blocks and three use
three. A single \(u_1\) supplies some successful \(U\) in 27/31 cases and a
single \(u_2\) in 19/31, so allowing both arms is currently the minimal uniform
formulation.

More sharply, `results/potential_guided_minimal_tights.json` checks the two
top sets directly. \(P_1\) works in 24/31 cases and \(P_2\) in 16/31; their
union covers all 31. The generalized top-set capacities satisfy the sharper
identity
\[
 \max\{\sigma_{P_1},\sigma_{P_2}\}=1
\]
in every case (ignoring a constant potential whose top set is all of \(N\)).
The same identity remains true after imposing the two-color restrictions
\(\mu_0=0\) and \(\delta_1+\delta_2=0\).
Hence the stated two-top-set conjecture matches every known obstruction through
the complete representative seven-player geometry sweep.

The canonical audit `results/canonical_extreme_box_capacity_audit.json`
removes the remaining gauge dependence. Its reduced two-color capacity equals
the original three-color capacity in all 31 cases. The maximum coordinate
class \(P^+\) repairs 29/31, the minimum coordinate class \(P^-\) repairs
29/31, and their union repairs 31/31. In every case
\[
 \max\{\tau_{P^+},\tau_{P^-}\}=1.
\]
All 62 individual extreme-set capacities satisfy the stronger dichotomy
\[
 \tau_{P^\pm}\in\{\rho,1\}.
\]
Exactly four extremes fail, and each failure leaves the capacity at \(\rho\)
rather than producing an intermediate value. This suggests an optimal-face
dichotomy: the added orthogonality hyperplane either meets the original dual
optimal face or forces the minimum directly to the circuit-normalization
level one.

The recession cone gives an exact explanation of the four failures. Define
\[
 K(q)=\{h:h(N)=0,\ h(S)=0\text{ for every }S\in\operatorname{supp}(q)\}.
\]
If \(h(U)\neq0\) for some \(h\in K(q)\), shifting an ordinary minimizer to
\(z+th\) can enforce the fixed-witness equation at no objective cost, so
\(\tau_U=\rho\). Equivalently, improvement is possible only when
\[
 \mathbf1_U\in\operatorname{span}
 \bigl(\mathbf1_N,\{\mathbf1_S:S\in\operatorname{supp}(q)\}\bigr).
\]
The exact optimal-face audit finds precisely four support-flexible extremes;
they are exactly the four failures, their potential-sum ranges are all of
\(\mathbb R\), and no case has both extremes support-flexible. Every
support-rigid extreme reaches capacity one.

Projecting an optimal potential orthogonally onto \(K(q)^\perp\) preserves
every supported coalition sum and hence preserves the dual objective. The
exact quotient audit `results/quotient_canonical_extreme_audit.json` performs
this projection in rational arithmetic. In all six cases with nontrivial
support nullspace it removes the apparent failure; after projection, both
extremes repair in all 31 cases, for 62/62 exact capacities equal to one. The
maximum-set repair always has negative coverage defect and the minimum-set
repair always has positive coverage defect. This sign rule is the clearest
primal clue for constructing the two augmentations directly from the ordered
dual potential. The
conjecture has therefore split into two sharper statements:

1. both extreme sets of a support-orthogonal optimal potential are
   support-rigid;
2. a support-rigid canonical extreme set of a positive circuit has capacity
   one.

The first statement has a natural sufficient condition. If the support
nullspace is generated by differences of players with identical incidence
signatures on \(\operatorname{supp}(q)\), then a support-orthogonal potential
is constant on each twin class and all its level sets are rigid. The exact
structure audit checks all 609 circuits in the exhaustive five-player census
and all 86 representative six-/seven-player circuits. All 695 have
twin-generated nullspace; 14 have nonzero nullspace, always one-dimensional,
and none has a non-twin direction.

## Reduced fixed-witness dual

Fixing \(U\) adds exactly one equation to the canonical reduced dual:
\[
 \tau_U=\min\{\Phi(r_A,r_B,z):
 r_A\lambda_{1,A}+r_B\lambda_{2,B}=1,
 \ r_A,r_B\geq0,\ z(N)=0,
\]
\[
 z(U)+r_A\mathbf1_{\{U=A\}}-r_B\mathbf1_{\{U=B\}}=0\}.
\]
This follows directly from stationarity of the free coverage-defect variable
\(\delta\). Ordinary recoloring omits the last equation, so
\(\tau_U\geq\rho\).

The conjecture is therefore unusually sharp: an ordinary minimizer \(z^*\)
selects two coordinate-extreme coalitions, and imposing the single scalar
orthogonality equation for one of them must raise the dual minimum all
the way from \(\rho<1\) to one. No coalition-price matching or three-color
gauge choice remains. The obstruction is now entirely contained in the
positive-part support function \(\Phi\).
