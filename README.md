The [continuous-report extension](research_campaigns/2026-10-05-continuous-pools/INDEX.txt) now gives an explicit rule for **every real report in [1/r,1]**, with at most two pools altered from all-unit: **total payment ≤7T**, or **≤8T with the original fairness floor**, uniformly in pool count, owner count and report-grid refinement. It revises the earlier binary payments. The formula is own-report monotone and covers all coalitions; a complete proof, independent model reviews and exact labeled checks are recorded. A broader theorem handles any fixed number m of altered pools with a bound linear in m. An exact family shows this rule itself cannot give an unrestricted constant. Historical novelty remains unconfirmed.

The [two-altered-pool extension](research_campaigns/2026-10-05-two-altered-pools/INDEX.txt) now preserves the nonlinear construction at **total payment ≤2T**, or **≤3T with the original fairness floor**, when two pools simultaneously contain mixtures of reports **1/r and 1**. It covers all coalitions, respects every retained own-report change, and agrees exactly with the earlier one-pool boundary. The proof covers all k,r; exhaustive labeled checks and independent model reviews support it. The continuous-report result above removes the first restriction with a looser bound; a constant for arbitrarily many altered pools and historical novelty remain unresolved.

The [beyond-thresholds attempt](research_campaigns/2026-10-05-beyond-thresholds/INDEX.txt) proves that arbitrary report-dependent pool prices still require growing subsidy when payments remain proportional within each pool. The optimal floor-free capped-proportional factor is **(1+o(1)) log k / log log k**. A nonlinear payment assignment covers the same linked-profile family at factor **2**, showing exactly why that obstruction does not extend to unrestricted mechanisms. Fixed random-order values face a separate obstruction. The all-profile constant-versus-divergence question and novelty remain open; proofs, exact checks, failures and model reviews are recorded.

The [constant-subsidy campaign](research_campaigns/2026-10-04-constant-subsidy/INDEX.txt) strengthens the necessary subsidy constant for all stage and owner counts from **50% to 100%**. It also proves that the preceding mechanism has the best growth order within the specified fixed-statistic independent-threshold class. Whether any constant subsidy works remains open. Full proofs, exact certificates, failed constructions and cross-model reviews are recorded; historical novelty is unconfirmed.

The [stage-scaling AGT campaign](research_campaigns/2026-10-04-stage-scaling/INDEX.txt) now gives a truthful, coalition-covering mechanism with original **H/k** floors and subsidy **O(log k / log log k)** for arbitrarily many complementary stages and owners, improving the previous linear scaling. The growth rate is sharp for this particular rule, which applies the established continuous Banzhaf functional after capping. Separate lower-bound families show that four stages require at least **21.43%**, strictly above the two-stage ceiling below. A three-stage construction gives a **16.67%–46.31%** interval. The constant-subsidy question and historical novelty remain open; proofs, exact verifiers and independent model reviews are recorded.

The [new exponential-payment theorem](research_campaigns/2026-10-04-mechanism-frontier/INDEX.txt) reduces the general two-pool subsidy upper bound from **50% to 1/(2e), about 18.394%**, for arbitrary finite owner counts and every nonnegative real capacity profile. The rule covers every coalition, is truthful under binding capacity underreports with no operating costs, and protects half of each original-core ideal. The current minimax interval with H/3 fairness is **12.5%–18.394%**. Full proofs, rational-interval checks, exact lower-bound certificates and cross-model reviews are recorded. Novelty and human specialist review remain unresolved.

The [preceding Shapley audit](research_campaigns/2026-10-04-many-owner-subsidy/INDEX.txt) proves the sharp universal coalition factor **1/2** and shows that scalar Shapley rules need a worst-case **100% subsidy** across owner counts. That obstruction remains valid; the exponential rule changes how rewards are distributed.

The earlier [2+2-owner result](research_campaigns/2026-10-04-ownership-subsidy/INDEX.txt) gives a **20%** upper bound with the stronger **3H/5** fairness floor. The exponential theorem improves its subsidy bound for the H/3 contract, while guaranteeing H/2 rather than 3H/5.
