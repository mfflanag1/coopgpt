TWO ANALYTICAL LOWER-BOUND FAMILIES -- 2026-10-04
Conventional proof extracted from exact finite duals; independently reviewed.
No fairness assumptions. Fixed original owners, two per pool, reports in {1,2}.

THEOREM
Any nonnegative, coalition-covering, own-report-monotone rule with budget factor
1+beta for every real profile on k pools, two owners per pool, must satisfy
 beta >= max{(k-1)/(2(k+3)), (k-2)/(2k+3)} for k>=3.
For k=2 the preceding exact 1/8 bound remains stronger. In particular k=3 needs
at least 1/6 and k=4 at least 3/14. Any uniform bound across k must be at least
1/2 in subsidy ratio. This does NOT prove that the optimal subsidy diverges.

SYMMETRIZATION
Given an arbitrary rule for this fixed ownership structure, average its relabeled
payments over all permutations of the k pools and swaps of their two owners.
Each permutation preserves worth, budgets and one-coordinate order. Linear
averaging therefore preserves nonnegativity, coalition coverage, own monotonicity
and the same beta. We may assume the averaged rule is invariant. No owner is
split or merged; relabeling is only an argument proving a lower bound for ANY rule.

A pool of type (1,1) gives each owner the same payment. In a type (1,2) pool,
all capacity-1 owners have one payment and capacity-2 owners another, within a
profile having specified counts of these types.

SHARED PROFILE X
One (1,1) pool and k-1 (1,2) pools. Write a for each (1,1) owner's payment,
b for each capacity-1 owner in a mixed pool, and d for each capacity-2 owner.
T=2. Coverage and budget give
 a+(k-1)b >=1                       [one capacity-1 owner from each pool],
 2a+(k-1)d >=2                      [both from (1,1); the 2 from each mixed],
 2a+(k-1)(b+d) <=2+2beta.           [budget]
Subtracting the first coverage lower bound from the budget gives
 a+(k-1)d <=1+2beta.
Compare with the second coverage lower bound to obtain a>=1-2beta.
Substitute back to obtain d<=4beta/(k-1).

FAMILY I
Profile Y has two (1,1) pools and k-2 (1,2) pools, T=2.
Write A for each owner's payment in the (1,1) pools, B and D for the capacity-1
and capacity-2 payments in mixed pools. We have
 2A+(k-2)B >=1,
 4A+(k-2)D >=2,
 4A+(k-2)(B+D) <=2+2beta.
Adding the two coverage inequalities and comparing with budget yields
 A>=1/2-beta.
Raise one owner in a (1,1) pool of Y from 1 to 2, obtaining X. Its new payment
d must be at least its old payment A. Therefore
 1/2-beta <= A <= d <=4beta/(k-1),
so beta >=(k-1)/(2(k+3)). The argument uses k>=3 to retain the displayed mixed
pool types; at k=2 the omitted coefficients are zero and the bound is still valid
but weaker than the existing 1/8 certificate.

FAMILY II
Profile Z has k mixed (1,2) pools, T=3. Write b_Z,d_Z for its two payments.
The coalition of all capacity-1 owners and the budget yield
 k b_Z>=1,       k(b_Z+d_Z)<=3+3beta,
so k d_Z<=2+3beta.
Raise one owner in the unique (1,1) pool of X from 1 to 2, obtaining Z.
Own monotonicity gives d_Z>=a>=1-2beta. Hence
 k(1-2beta)<=2+3beta,
which rearranges to beta>=(k-2)/(2k+3). This tends to 1/2 as k increases.

STRICT TWO-VERSUS-FOUR STAGE SEPARATION
The two-pool exponential mechanism already guarantees beta<=1/(2e) while
protecting H/2. The four-pool lower bound is 3/14 with NO floors. Since
 e>1+1+1/2=5/2>7/3,
we have 3/14>1/(2e). Four stages therefore require strictly more subsidy than
a universally valid two-stage ceiling, even though the four-stage lower bound
allows weaker fairness. This is an exact separation, not a numerical comparison.

PROVENANCE AND LIMITS
Full finite-grid LPs at k=2,3,4 have independently verified exact rational duals.
A separate symmetry-reduced discovery experiment at k=2,3,4,5,6,8,10,16,32,64
suggested these two families. The proof above, not the numerical optima, establishes
all k. No completeness or optimality claim is made for those finite searches.
Novelty and human specialist review remain unresolved.
