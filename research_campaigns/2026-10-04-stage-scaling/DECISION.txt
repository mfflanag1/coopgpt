MAIN RESULT
For every number k>=2 of complementary resource pools, arbitrary finite original
owner counts and all nonnegative real capacities, capped owner-level continuous
Banzhaf payments with an M_i/k top-up cover every coalition, are truthful under
binding capacity underreports/no use costs, and protect the original-core H_i/k.
The new subsidy upper bound is R_k+1/log R_k, where R_k>e solves
R_k log(R_k/e)=log k. Its order is log k/log log k instead of the prior linear
(k-1)/2. All logarithms are natural. The old bound remains better at small k;
one may choose the rule by k alone to obtain the minimum of the two bounds.

This particular rule has worst-case subsidy (1+o(1))log k/log log k, taking the
supremum over all finite owner counts at each k. That is a sharp analysis of this
rule, NOT a matching lower bound on every possible mechanism.

STRUCTURAL EXPLANATION
The rule uses the distribution of individual capacities inside each pool.
Rules whose per-unit prices see only capped pool-total ratios need budget factor
at least k/(2sqrt(k)-1) along square k, with sufficiently many owners and zero
capacities allowed. They cannot achieve the sublinear rate established here.
The old ratio-only mechanism is therefore not the route to a constant all-stage
bound. This is a restricted-class obstruction, not an unrestricted impossibility.

TWO OTHER CHECKED RESULTS
- Three pools: normalized exponential payments give beta<=W(2/e)<0.4631 with
  M_i/3>=H_i/3, improving the earlier 100% ceiling to approximately 46.306%.
  An exact lower bound is 1/6, so its interval is [1/6,W(2/e)].
- Two owners per pool: all mechanisms need beta>=max{(k-1)/(2(k+3)),
  (k-2)/(2k+3)} for k>=3, even with no fairness floors. At k=4 this is 3/14,
  strictly larger than the 1/(2e) ceiling for two pools. Across all k, any uniform
  subsidy constant would have to be at least 1/2.
