For k=2 or 3, use d_i=min(c_i,T), C_j=sum_{i in N_j}d_i, rho_j=C_j/T,
u_j=rho_j-1>=0, w_j=exp(-u_j), Z=sum_j w_j, p_j=w_j/Z.
Pay y_i=p_j d_i; T=0 means all payments zero. Some rho_j=1.
The following proves all-profile coverage, own monotonicity, M_i/k
floors (hence H_i/k), and subsidy beta_k=W((k-1)/e), where beta_k is the
unique positive solution beta_k exp(beta_k)=(k-1)/e. For k=3, beta_3<0.4631.
The earlier two-pool exponential rule has the better bound 1/(2e).

MODEL: v(S)=min_j sum_{i in S intersect N_j}c_i on fixed finite disjoint pools,
T=v(N), original efficient core C(v), H_i=max_{x in C(v)}x_i. Binding capacity
underreports only, payment utility, no use costs.

COVERAGE: for any owner subset X, min(c(X),T)=min(d(X),T): if one capacity
exceeds T both sides are T, otherwise no term changes. A raw bottleneck pool
has capped total T, and v(S)<=T. Hence v(S)=min_j d(S intersect N_j).
Nonnegative prices sum to one, so their weighted average covers this minimum.

FAIRNESS: M_i=T-v(N without i) bounds original H_i from above by the core
complement constraint. Fix any owner and let a be its other own-pool total.
Then M_i=(T-a)_+<=d_i. If M_i=0, the floor is trivial. Otherwise a<T, so all
other owners in this pool are uncapped. Put m=d_i; then C_j=a+m,
u_j=(m-M_i)/T, and M_i<=m<=T. Since Z<=k,
 y_i>=m exp(-(m-M_i)/T)/k>=M_i/k,
using that t exp(-t/T) increases on [0,T]. This also covers a capped lone owner
whose capped pool total is T although its raw total exceeds T.

OWN REPORT, LONG REGION: hold all other reports fixed, own pool total x+a>=B,
where B is the least total among the other pools. Then T=B. Write
m=min(x,B), alpha=sum_{same-pool others}min(c_l,B), so C_j=alpha+m.
Other weights fixed. On m<B, derivative of m p_j wrt m is
 p_j[1-(m/B)(1-p_j)]>=0. Saturation makes the payment constant for m>=B.

OWN REPORT, SHORT REGION: 0<x+a<=B, T=x+a, rho_j=1, own payment=x/Z.
For every other pool l, rho_l=sum_{i in N_l}min(c_i/T,1), with
 0>=rho_l'>=-rho_l/T
on each smooth interval. Thus
 y_i'=p_j[1+x sum_{l!=j}p_l rho_l']
       >=p_j[1-sum_{l!=j}p_l rho_l].
Here x<=T. The final bracket is nonnegative iff
 sum_{l!=j}(rho_l-1)exp(-(rho_l-1))<=1.
Each summand is at most 1/e. Therefore it holds for k<=3, since 2/e<1.
For T>0 the formula is continuous, with finitely many clipping points; its
short and long formulas agree at T=B. This completes monotonicity across all
regimes. If T tends to zero along this path, 0<=y_i<=x tends to zero when a=0.
The assigned zero payment is at most every later nonnegative payment.

BUDGET: sum_i y_i/T=1+F, where F=sum_j u_j exp(-u_j)/sum_j exp(-u_j).
Choose an index s with u_s=0. Let b>0 solve b exp(b)=(k-1)/e. For any u>=0,
 (u-b)exp(-u)<=exp(-1-b), with maximum at u=1+b.
Summing over j!=s, sum_j(u_j-b)exp(-u_j)<=-b+(k-1)exp(-1-b)=0.
Hence F<=b. Equality when u_s=0 and all remaining u_j=1+b. This is feasible:
one unit owner in pool s; in each other pool choose three equal owners with
capacity (2+b)/3<1 for k=3. Then C_j=2+b and no owner is clipped.
This is budget optimality FOR THIS RULE ONLY.
