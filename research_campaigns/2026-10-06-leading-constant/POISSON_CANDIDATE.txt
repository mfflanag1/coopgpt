FINITE STATEMENT
Let d>=1, m>d, alpha>0, A=(d+1)m-d^2, lambda=A/alpha,
Z=sum_(h=0)^d lambda^h/h!, and p_h=(lambda^h/h!)/Z.
For any integer k>=2 and every eligible rule on k pools of m fixed owners,
 B >= A / [(1+alpha) E T],
where E T=m+(m-1)sum_(r=1)^d [sum_(h=r)^d p_h]^k.
In particular E T<=m[1+d(1-p_0)^k]<=m[1+d exp(-k p_0)].

PROOF
Symmetrize the arbitrary rule by all pool permutations and within-pool owner
permutations as in the established hierarchy lemma. Preserve all original
coalitions and changing-owner report comparisons. Restrict to reports1 andm.
Typeh has h high owners and m-h low owners, h=0,...,d. Every type retains lows.

At histogram n=(n0,...,nd), sum n_h=k, use x_h(n) and z_h(n) for a present
low/high owner's payment; assign arbitrary zero values if the type is absent.
Two actual coalitions imply, for EVERY histogram, including n0=0 or nd=k:
 sum_h n_h x_h >=1;
 (m-d+1)sum_(h<d)n_h x_h+n_d z_d >=m-d+1.
The second selects m-d+1 lows in every type<h d and one high in every type d.
When all pools have type d its actual worth is m, which is even larger.
Consequently
 A <= A sum_(h<d)n_h x_h + n_d[(m-d)x_d+d z_d].                 (1)

Draw k pool types independently with law p only as an averaging device over
report profiles. Histograms have multinomial weights
 mu(n)=k! product_h p_h^(n_h)/n_h!.
For h<d and n_h>0, increase one actual low owner from1 to m. Destination is
 n'=n-e_h+e_(h+1).
Own-monotonicity gives x_h(n)<=z_(h+1)(n'). We do NOT require T to be constant.
The exact multinomial identity is
 mu(n)n_h = (p_h/p_(h+1)) mu(n') (n'_(h+1)).                  (2)
Summing this changing-owner inequality over all source histograms, which map
bijectively to destination histograms with n'_(h+1)>0, yields
 E[n_h x_h] <= (p_h/p_(h+1)) E[n_(h+1) z_(h+1)].             (3)
Since A p_h/p_(h+1)=alpha(h+1), averaging(1) now gives
 A <= alpha E[sum_(r=1)^d r n_r z_r]
       + E[n_d((m-d)x_d+d z_d)]
   <= (1+alpha) E[y(N)] <=(1+alpha) B E[T].                  (4)
Each bracket is a subset of the nonnegative payment budget. The same
destination budget now absorbs contributions from ALL exporting types after
averaging. No unchanged-owner comparison, proportionality, cloning, or
report-dependent owner identity is used.

Pool total at typeh is m+(m-1)h, so T=m+(m-1) min_j H_j. The tail-sum formula
for the minimum gives the stated exact E T. If any pool has type0, T=m;
otherwise T<=m+(m-1)d<=m(1+d). Thus the displayed simpler bound follows.

ASYMPTOTIC CHOICE AND ALL-k EXTENSION
For d>=2, let q=ceil(log(d+1)), m=dq, alpha=1/q, lambda=Aq.
Take k_d=ceil(2qZ); then for every k>=k_d, k p0>=2q, whence
 B >= (d+1-d/q) / [(1+1/q)(1+d exp(-2q))] = (1-o(1))d.
Here lambda=(d+1)dq^2-d^2 q and log lambda=(2+o(1))log d.
Because lambda>d, the summands lambda^h/h! increase up to d, so
 lambda^d/d! <= Z <= (d+1)lambda^d/d!.
Using log(d!)=d log d-d+O(log d), obtain
 log Z=d log lambda-log(d!)+O(log d)=(1+o(1))d log d.
Therefore log k_d=(1+o(1))d log d.
For any epsilon in(0,1), choose d=floor((1-epsilon)log k/log log k).
Then k>=k_d eventually, and the displayed finite bound applies to that exact
k and ownership m=dq. No monotonicity of B_k is assumed: the probability proof
works for each k>=k_d independently. Let epsilon decrease to0.
This proves liminf B_k log log k/log k>=1. The existing upper proves limsup<=1.
Optional H_i/k floor changes B_k by at most1 and thus has the same coefficient.
