MAIN RESULT: ANY UNIVERSAL CONSTANT MUST BE AT LEAST 100% SUBSIDY
For k>=3 stages and exactly m>=2 original owners per stage, put t=2m-2. Any
nonnegative coalition-covering own-report-monotone rule has
 beta >= (k-1)(t-1)/[t(k-1+2t)].
This uses strictly positive capacities, one owner's report change, and no fairness
floors. With m=n+1 and k=8n^2+1 it equals (2n-1)/(2n+1), tending to 1.
Thus a subsidy constant uniform in BOTH stages and owners must be >=1: at least
100% of produced value must be allowed as subsidy, or a total budget >=2T.
This improves the previous necessary constant of 50%. No finite instance in this
family reaches 100%, and the theorem proves neither existence nor divergence of
the unrestricted optimum. With three owners per stage the limit is already 75%.

SECOND RESULT: THE PRIOR MECHANISM IS ORDER-OPTIMAL WITHIN A BROAD PRECISE CLASS
Choose the winning pool using a fixed finite Borel coordinatewise-monotone
statistic of its independent owner thresholds Z_i=min(c_i,T)U_i, and pay each
owner its capped capacity times that pool's winning probability. The statistic
may depend on fixed labels and k, but cannot separately access reports, d or T.
Every such class member needs Omega(log k/log log k) subsidy over owner counts.
The preceding sum-statistic rule attains O(log k/log log k), including H/k.
Changing sum to max, an order statistic or another fixed statistic cannot give a
constant. Report-dependent statistics, correlated thresholds and nonlinear owner
payments are NOT excluded by this class theorem.

OTHER CHECKED BOUNDARIES
Bounded owner counts permit a bounded subsidy independent of k via d_i/k;
any truly divergent sequence must grow both k and the owner bound. Fairness can
be added at at most T, so H/k does not change constant existence qualitatively.
A minimum-pool core selection's monotone envelope is exactly M and needs kT.
Every softmax envelope with temperature depending only on k also fails to give
a uniform constant; an explicit case split replaces the delegated heuristic.
The knapsack and envelope identities are useful reductions, not solutions.
