RECOMMENDATION
The most useful new research target is the quantitative interaction of fair
payments, coalition stability and truthful capacity reporting in resource-pooling
networks. The strongest exact interpretation already available is a limitation
on bargaining in multi-unit labor/resource markets. Compute pooling is a plausible
motivating application only under an explicitly checked production model. General
machine-learning utility or allocation without money is a larger model change.

1. ALGORITHMIC GAME THEORY: THE SUBSIDY PRICE OF INCENTIVES.
Problem: resource owners can withhold capacity. We want them to report honestly,
remain in the cooperative system, and receive a guaranteed fraction of their
best payoff in the original core. What external budget is sufficient/necessary?
Huang-Mei-Yang-Cao2025, Mechanism Design in Max-Flows, already disproves truthful
core selection and gives a truthful alternative outside the core. Repeating that
impossibility is not a contribution. Read Sections2.1-2.2, Example1, Proposition1,
Theorem1 and conclusion:
https://arxiv.org/html/2502.08248v1

Precise candidate target, NOT a proved general theorem. Fix depth<=3 acyclic directed multigraphs (parallel arcs permitted),
one capacity owner per arc and no edge-use costs. Reports are binding capacities;
over-reporting is infeasible. Utility equals the report-dependent monetary payment,
with no additional utility from unused capacity or delivered flow. Incentives concern
unilateral report deviations, not coordinated misreports. For every reported capacity
vector c, keep original entire-core H(c) fixed before subsidy. Seek a payment
rule y(c)>=0 satisfying:
  y(c)(S)>=v_c(S) for EVERY coalition S;
  y_i(c)>=H_i(c)/3;
  y_i(c_i,c_-i)>=y_i(c'_i,c_-i) whenever0<=c'_i<=c_i;
  sum_i y_i(c)<= (1+beta)*T(c).
The third condition is own-report monotonicity and hence DSIC in the specified
under-report-only, no-cost model. Study the smallest universal beta, or prove
that no graph-size-independent beta exists. Coalitional rationality is imposed
at every profile; the fairness benchmark is never recomputed after adding money.
Computability and split/merge-proofness are later questions, not bundled into
the first target. No claim that beta=1/3 survives truthfulness is made.

Useful entry gate: reproduce the published four-player truthfulness obstruction,
then match or improve a certified lower/upper bound on a narrowly stated graph
family. CALIBRATION_LEMMA.txt gives a hand-derived1/8 lower bound using five
capacity profiles as a sanity check; this is tight ONLY on that finite domain,
with the witness checked by exact arithmetic. Its priority is not asserted. The12-player
endpoint counterexample checks that no proof assumes simultaneous sector maxima.
Stop if the proposed theorem is only a restatement of the2025 impossibility,
or an LP formulation without a new bound/construction. A strong eventual result
would be a sharp general tradeoff with an implementable mechanism, not merely
a renamed transportation example. Current assessment: best substantive AGT fit,
but the exact new target's priority and solvability remain to be established.

2. AI COMPUTE / INFRASTRUCTURE POOLING: A CONCRETE PRODUCTION MODEL.
Take owners of compute capacity g_i and owners of a complementary bandwidth or
power service b_j. Assume fixed conversion rates, a homogeneous workload,
linear per-job revenue p, divisible work, unrestricted compatibility, no costs,
and transferable monetary payments. Then coalition revenue can be modelled as
 p*min(sum g_i, sum b_j) after choosing consistent per-job units.
Under precisely these assumptions the existing counterexample transfers by
scaling, so individually best stable owner revenues need not coexist. This is
an EXACT MODEL INTERPRETATION, not a validated claim about GPU clusters.
A network version can represent separately owned service stages; the graph and
subsidy papers may apply only after proving their production-model hypotheses.

The next substantive question would be a sharp fairness/stability/incentive
bound for owners with heterogeneous capacities and genuine complementary stages,
plus a defensible workload/revenue model. It must account for utilization costs,
location constraints, heterogeneity and demand if those are claimed empirically.
Known comparator: Ghodsi et al2011 Dominant Resource Fairness studies allocating
multiple resources to users and gives strategyproof/sharing guarantees in its
model. It does NOT directly price coalition-owned resources with transferable
money. We are not disproving DRF:
https://www.usenix.org/legacy/events/nsdi11/tech/full_papers/Ghodsi.pdf
Current assessment: appealing application for direction1, with model-validation
work required. Relabelling capacity as GPUs does not create a new theorem.

3. LABOR / PLATFORM BARGAINING: STRONGEST IMMEDIATE INTERPRETATION.
Firms purchase divisible work, workers provide multiple units, and a firm-worker
pair may trade multiple units. With identical per-unit income, coalition output
is the smaller total work supply/demand. The counterexample therefore shows
that no single stable allocation need maximize every firm's payoff simultaneously.
It is about internal conflict on the same side of a market, even before strategic
reports. Source/sink reversal gives the analogous role reversal.
This is the all-productivity-one specialization of Model2 in Nunez2013,
not an extra theorem. PRIORITY_CHECK.txt gives the explicit LP reduction.
It does NOT transfer to one-to-one matching, pair-capacity1 multiple partnerships,
or workers who each supply only one unit. Strongest current significance lead:
compare the exact negative result with the historically open sector-optimal
question, then establish present priority. The same general open question is documented
in the inspected 2016 Robles-Nunez manuscript and Robles2017 thesis. The interpretation requires neither
physical transportation nor a new ML model.
https://archive.illc.uva.nl/COST-IC1205/documents/Budapest-2013-Slides/Nunez.pdf

4. DATA / FEDERATED-LEARNING REWARDS: INTERESTING, MORE DISTANT.
Question: can contributors receive a stable share of jointly created model value
and an individual guarantee without excessive external budget? The relevant
mathematical object is a transferable-utility revenue game only if model quality
can defensibly be mapped to money and coalition retraining values are specified.
The min-of-two-capacities counterexample does not establish a claim about arbitrary
learning curves, nonconvex training or heterogeneous client utilities.
Existing work already uses the least core for data valuation (Yan-Procaccia2021)
and coalition fairness for federated learning (Ray Chaudhury et al2024). A new
contribution would need a structural or statistical theorem beyond those results,
for example verifiable individual guarantees with uncertain coalition values.
No such theorem or new algorithm has been established here.
https://cdn.aaai.org/ojs/16721/16721-13-20215-1-2-20210518.pdf
https://proceedings.mlr.press/v235/ray-chaudhury24a.html
The latter uses ordinal model preferences/proportional veto core, not our TU
money-allocation core. Current assessment: potentially broad relevance but lower
immediate proof fit; do not turn the existing result into an AI claim by analogy.
