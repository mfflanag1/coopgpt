FREE ORIGINAL-SOURCE COMPARISON: CAPACITY TRUTHFULNESS AND SUBSIDIZED COVERAGE
7 October2026. Bounded literature review, not historical novelty confirmation.
Coordinator reopened the originals below and checked the named definitions or
sections. Links/labels replace unstable web-extraction line numbers. The new
hybrid bound is still undergoing mathematical review at this checkpoint.

MODEL BEING COMPARED
Fixed resource owners, binding downward capacity reports, payment-only utility,
nonnegative deterministic payments covering every coalition at every profile.
Capacity reports vary continuously; this is not population entry or cost bidding.
K is structural minimal-cut count for single-commodity flow, dual-vertex count
for binary packing. Proposed contributions are the effective-usage/optimal-face
invariance argument and its combination with conditional masks, marginal
contributions and a general complexity-dependent subsidy bound. Neither LP
duality, random sampling, own-report monotonicity nor self-exclusion is claimed
as a new general idea. A proof audit is separate from this literature comparison.

1. Huang, Mei, Yang and Cao, Mechanism Design in Max-Flows (2025), v1.
https://arxiv.org/html/2502.08248v1
Inspected: Section2.1 (model/DSIC), Section2.2 Example1 and Remark2;
Proposition1; Section4 Definition2 and Theorem1. This is the closest inspected
model: one edge owner, private capacity and binding underreport, no operating
cost. Efficient core selection cannot be truthful. Their minimal-cut mechanism
is efficient and truthful but may violate coalition stability; it averages
structural cuts with capacity-proportional payments. These results do not give
an all-profile truthful coalition-covering subsidy factor in K. Their positive-
capacity convention differs from our explicit zero-report extension. The
general impossibility and minimal-cut mechanism must be credited prominently.
The arXiv landing page was checked again for version context; comparison here
is to the inspected v1, not an assertion about every later or unpublished work.

2. Resnick, Bachrach, Meir and Rosenschein (MFCS2009), network-flow CoS.
https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2009/01/CoS-NFG-MFCS.pdf
Inspected: model definitions and Definitions11-13, PDF page5/printed p5.
The game is thresholded: a coalition is successful when its flow meets a fixed
threshold. Capacities are fixed; there is no all-profile report incentive.
Definition13 explicitly measures total adjusted gains v(N)+Delta, so this
paper's CoS convention must not be silently treated as the added Delta alone.
The original definition was independently checked after the reviewer flagged
the convention as needing verification. This is stabilization background, not
a matching capacity-truthful subsidy theorem.

3. Samet and Zemel (1984), On the core and dual set of linear programming games.
https://www.tau.ac.il/~samet/papers/lp%20games.pdf
Inspected: printed pp309-314, dual payoff construction and Theorems1-4.
Dual prices produce core allocations at a fixed profile; the paper investigates
when the core and dual allocations coincide and refinement/replication effects.
These are established LP-game foundations. They do not supply the all-profile
capacity-monotone selection or complexity-dependent subsidy bound considered
here. Theorem1 credits Owen's earlier result. Owen(1975) full text was not
obtained in this bounded review; bibliographic identification is not inspection.

4. Assignment games with population monotonic allocation schemes.
https://arxiv.org/pdf/2210.17373
Inspected: Section2 Definition1 (printed p4), including efficiency for every
population and monotonicity as a population expands. It credits Sprumont(1990),
whose originating paper was not inspected here. This original research paper
is used for the stated definition, not as the origin of PMAS. The population
parameter differs from changing one fixed owner's capacity; no implication
to the present incentive condition is assumed in either direction.

5. Archer and Tardos (2001), Truthful Mechanisms for One-Parameter Agents.
https://www.cis.upenn.edu/~mkearns/teaching/SponsoredSearch/ArcherTardos.pdf
Inspected: Section4, especially Theorem4.2. The model has work-dependent
private costs and characterizes truthfulness through a monotone allocation
curve with integral payments. This is an important monotonicity antecedent,
but it is a different utility model from binding capacity underreports with
payment-only utility. No coalition-covering K-dependent flow subsidy theorem
is inferred from its characterization.

6. Goldberg, Hartline, Karlin, Saks and Wright, Competitive Auctions.
https://homes.cs.washington.edu/~karlin/papers/comp-journal.pdf
Inspected: Section2.1 Auction1, Theorem2.4/Lemma2.5/Corollary2.8; Section6
random sampling and Auction2. Bid-independent pricing and using other bids
to set an agent's price are established truthfulness techniques. The random
partition is an internal mechanism device, not a distributional assumption
on reports. Therefore conditioning/excluding an agent must not be advertised
as new in itself. The candidate-specific issue is invariance of the entire
optimal dual face after report-dependent effective usage, together with
coalition coverage and the subsidy bound; this source addresses auction revenue.

7. Moulin and Shenker, Strategyproof Sharing of Submodular Costs.
https://conta.uom.gr/conta/phges/netstrategies/courses/Strategy%20proof.pdf
Inspected: the freely available draft's introduction, Section2 model and
discussion leading to Section4's group-strategyproof characterization.
Users report willingness to pay for service, receive binary access decisions,
and pay shares of a nondecreasing submodular cost. This is a different model
from resource suppliers with capacity reports. The draft explains truthful
cost-sharing and marginal-contribution alternatives; it is a methodological
antecedent, not a matching network subsidy theorem. This comparison is to the
free draft, not a claimed inspection of the final published edition.

8. Van Beek, Malmberg, Borm, Quant and Schouten (2023), Competition and
cooperation in linear production and sequencing processes.
https://doi.org/10.1016/j.geb.2023.02.001
Inspected: freely exposed publisher introduction and Section2 model; the full
proofs were not available in the extracted view. The first stage strategically
selects resource locations/bundles, followed by cooperative LP allocation.
The stated result concerns pure Nash equilibrium with two resource locations,
including allocation through the Owen set. It is not the fixed-owner downward-
report mechanism model. Broader strategic LP production already exists; do not
claim to introduce incentives into LP games generally. No detailed theorem
comparison beyond the inspected model and stated result is made.
