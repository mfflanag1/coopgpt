FINITE SAMPLING OF THE RAW-CAPPED OWNER-BUNDLE MECHANISM
7 October 2026. Conventional algorithmic corollary with independent model audit.

SCOPE AND RESULT
This is a finite-sample evaluator for the exact mechanism in
GENERAL_PRODUCTION_THEOREM.txt. It preserves own-report incentives for EVERY
realization of its report-independent randomness. At ANY ONE FIXED profile,
it simultaneously covers every coalition with probability at least 1-delta.
It does not establish that one sampled rule covers all real report profiles
simultaneously. It is not a replacement for the exact deterministic all-profile
coverage theorem.

Let n>=1 fixed owners report resource bundles b_i>=0 in d>=1 resource types.
Fix a finite family V of K>=1 nonnegative forms and a report-independent
priority. Write

 f(x)=min_(w in V) w.x,
 v_b(S)=f(sum_(i in S)b_i),       T(b)=v_b(I).

All supplies, ownership permissions, and technology remain fixed under reports.
Choose an integer j>=2 and accuracy parameters eta>0, 0<delta<1 independently
of the reports. Let

 C_j(K)=j+(log K+2log j+1)/log[j^3/(j^2+j-1)].

The exact mechanism M^j has nonnegative, componentwise own-monotone payments,
covers every coalition at every profile, and sums to at most C_j(K)T.
For

 N >= n^2/(2eta^2) * log(2n/delta),                         (1)

the sampled rule below has all of these guarantees:

 (a) Every fixed random realization is componentwise own-report monotone.
 (b) For any fixed profile b, with probability >=1-delta it simultaneously
     covers all 2^n coalitions and pays at most [C_j(K)+2eta]T(b).
 (c) Its expected total is at most [C_j(K)+eta]T(b).
 (d) It uses at most 1+N(n+1) canonical value-and-minimizer oracle queries,
     counting the grand value as at most one such query.

There is an exact rational sampler with the same expectations, range bounds,
and incentive properties. It needs no real-valued q sample or numerical
quadrature. Oracle and parameter-selection qualifications appear below.

1. ONE CONTINUOUS MIXTURE SAMPLE
First draw q uniformly from (0,1/j), then independently mask OWNERS with
Pr(B_i=0|q)=q. Put S={i:B_i=1}. For each i let

 S_i^-=S\{i},       S_i^+=S union {i},
 W_i^-=first argmin_(w in V) w.(sum_(h in S_i^-)b_h),
 A_i(S,b)=min{W_i^-.b_i,T(b)},
 Delta_i(S,b)=v_b(S_i^+)-v_b(S_i^-),
 X_i(q,S,b)=q A_i(S,b)+(1-q)Delta_i(S,b).                  (2)

The selection of W_i^- uses the RAW linear objective. Its payment contribution
is capped afterward. Since S_i^- excludes i, W_i^- is independent of i's
entire own bundle when the other reports and sampled S are fixed.
Thus A_i is componentwise own-monotone: both W_i^-.b_i and T are monotone.
Delta_i is also own-monotone: its off term omits i, while its on term is
monotone in b_i. Hence each fixed-sample X_i is own-monotone.

Both A_i and Delta_i lie in [0,T]. For Delta, both endpoint coalition values
are nonnegative, the on value is at most T, and it is at least the off value.
Consequently

 0<=X_i(q,S,b)<=T(b).                                     (3)

Conditional on q, the distribution of B_-i is the independent q-mask used in
the exact mechanism. The own sampled bit has no effect on A_i or Delta_i.
Therefore E[X_i|q]=r_i^q(b) and

 E[X_i]=j integral_0^(1/j) r_i^q(b)dq=M_i^j(b).            (4)

2. SAMPLED RULE AND UNIVERSAL OWN-REPORT INCENTIVES
Draw N independent samples of (q,S) by the preceding procedure, all using
report-independent parameters. Define

 Mhat_i(b)=(1/N)sum_(ell=1)^N X_i(q_ell,S_ell,b)+eta*T(b)/n. (5)

Every X_i is own-monotone for each fixed sample, and so is the common-value
cushion eta*T/n. Thus (5) is componentwise own-monotone for EVERY fixed sample
sequence, simultaneously for all report profiles. This is universal individual
truthfulness under componentwise binding underreports and payment-only utility.
It does not mean group-strategyproofness across owners.

The random sequence must be independent of reports and is held fixed when
comparing counterfactual reports. Equivalently, the randomized mechanism is
a distribution over deterministic own-monotone rules. This also implies own-
report monotonicity of expected payment if fresh independent seeds are used.
The theorem does not permit profile-dependent choices of j, N, eta, or a
different active-owner count after removing zero reporters.

3. FIXED-PROFILE HIGH-PROBABILITY COVERAGE AND BUDGET
Fix a profile b independently of the samples. If T=0, every sample and cushion
is zero and all assertions hold surely. Otherwise divide (3) by this fixed
positive T. Hoeffding's inequality, applied across independent samples for
each owner, gives

 Pr(|(1/N)sum_ell X_i - M_i^j|>eta*T/n)
   <=2 exp(-2N eta^2/n^2).

The n coordinates within one sample may be dependent; no independence across
owners is required. A union bound over owners and (1) give an event of
probability at least 1-delta on which ALL coordinate errors are at most eta*T/n.
On that event,

 Mhat_i>=M_i^j for every i,
 sum_i Mhat_i<=sum_i M_i^j+2eta*T<= [C_j(K)+2eta]T.

Coordinatewise domination of the exact covering vector proves every coalition
constraint simultaneously, with no additional 2^n union bound.
For expected total, unbiasedness (4) gives the exact identity

 E sum_i Mhat_i=sum_i M_i^j+eta*T,

which proves (c). Expected payments also cover every coalition at every fixed
profile, since they equal the exact covering payments plus the nonnegative cushion.

The probability statement is pointwise in b. It does not control a profile
chosen adaptively after observing the sampled rule, and does not assert one
sample sequence is covering on the whole uncountable report domain.
For L predetermined profiles, an additional union bound would replace the
logarithm in (1) by log(2nL/delta). No uniform-all-profile statement is inferred.

4. EXACT DISCRETE IMPLEMENTATION
Let b0=1/j and, for integers a,k>=0, define

 I_(a,k)=integral_0^b0 q^a(1-q)^k dq
        =sum_(h=0)^k (-1)^h binom(k,h)b0^(a+h+1)/(a+h+1).  (6)

For a full mask S of size k its marginal probability in the continuous
experiment is

 pi_k=j I_(n-k,k).                                        (7)

This is the probability of ONE PARTICULAR size-k subset, not the probability
of cardinality k. Conditional on that full mask, the posterior mean of q is

 theta_k=I_(n-k+1,k)/I_(n-k,k),                           (8)

which belongs to (0,1/j). In particular it depends only on cardinality, not
on reports or the identity of the owners in S.

To draw one discrete sample:
 1. Draw k in {0,...,n} with probability binom(n,k)pi_k.
 2. Draw a uniform k-subset S of the fixed owner set.
 3. For every i compute

    Xtilde_i(S,b)=theta_k A_i(S,b)+(1-theta_k)Delta_i(S,b).  (9)

The cardinality probabilities sum to one because

 sum_k binom(n,k)pi_k
   =j integral_0^b0 [q+(1-q)]^n dq=1.

For fixed S and profile b, A_i and Delta_i do not depend on q. Therefore (9)
is exactly E[X_i(q,S,b)|S]. Taking expectation proves E[Xtilde_i]=M_i^j.
Each fixed-S estimator is still own-monotone and in [0,T], since its weights
are report-independent, nonnegative, and sum to one. Thus all results above
hold with N iid discrete samples in place of the continuous samples.
Conditioning on the full mask, rather than on only B_-i, is legitimate and
allows the SAME sampled S and theta_k to be used for every owner.

5. RATIONAL BIT LENGTH AND SAMPLING COST
For integer j, all pi_k and theta_k are positive rationals. Put

 H=(n+2)! * j^(n+2).

Every denominator of a summand in either j I_(n-k,k) or j I_(n-k+1,k)
divides H: the integer factor a+h+1 is at most n+2 and the j-power is at
most n+2. Hence

 u_k=H*pi_k,
 t_k=H*j*I_(n-k+1,k)

are integers, with u_k>0 and theta_k=t_k/u_k. The positive cardinality
weights U_k=binom(n,k)u_k satisfy sum_k U_k=H.
All have O(n(log j+log(n+2))) bits, as do the intermediate common-denominator
terms up to the same polynomial order. Formula (6) computes all of them
using O(n^2) exact arithmetic operations on polynomial-length integers.
Alternating terms should be summed exactly, not by unverified floating-point
subtraction.

Draw a uniform integer in {0,...,H-1} and use the cumulative U_k intervals
to select k. Then draw a uniform integer in {0,...,binom(n,k)-1} and unrank a
k-subset, or use any exact uniform-subset algorithm. Uniform integers can be
generated from fair bits by rejection, with fewer than two attempts on average
when using the next power-of-two range. Thus sampling has expected polynomial
random-bit and arithmetic cost in n and log j. Its worst-case number of
rejection attempts is unbounded, with almost-sure termination.

If eta and delta are rational, a conservative integer sample size can also
be selected without evaluating a real logarithm exactly: replace log(2n/delta)
in (1) by ceil(log_2(2n/delta)), then round N upward. The replacement is larger,
and the ceiling of the base-two logarithm is found by exact integer comparisons.

6. ORACLE COUNT AND CANONICAL TIES
For a sampled full set S, the only coalition masks needed are

 S and S symmetric-difference {i}, for i=1,...,n.

These are n+1 distinct masks. When i is in S, the off coalition is S\{i}
and the on coalition is S. When i is outside S, the off coalition is S and
the on coalition is S union {i}. A canonical value-and-minimizer oracle at
these masks therefore supplies every Delta_i and every W_i^-.
The grand value T is queried once for the profile. This proves the bound
1+N(n+1) canonical queries. Caching repeated masks can reduce this count.

For an explicitly listed family V, such a query enumerates the K raw scores
and applies the fixed priority, using O(Kd) arithmetic operations.

For a rational production LP with resource-price polyhedron
Q={w>=0:A^T w>=pi}, a conservative way to obtain a fixed canonical VERTEX is:
first minimize the queried aggregate-resource objective over Q; restrict to
its optimal face; then successively minimize w_1,...,w_d, preserving all prior
optimal values as equalities. Every coordinate objective is bounded below by
zero. At the end the point is unique and is a vertex of Q: any decomposition
into two feasible points would force equality at every successive minimum,
then equality of every coordinate. This returns the lexicographically first
optimal vertex and depends only on the queried aggregate vector and technology.

This procedure takes at most d+1 exact LP optimization calls per canonical
query. Accordingly a conservative bound is

 1+N(n+1)(d+1)

ordinary exact LP calls, allowing the grand value to use one value-only solve.
Rational inputs yield rational vertices and values. Implementations need exact
or suitably certified solves; approximate numerical tie-breaking is not silently
assumed to preserve the universal monotonicity or exact-unbiasedness claims.
An arbitrary nonvertex optimal point is not automatically an allowed substitute
when the complexity K counts dual vertices.

7. PARAMETER AND EFFICIENCY QUALIFICATIONS
The stated evaluator is polynomial in its displayed sampling parameters for
supplied j and a suitable exact canonical oracle. In particular its canonical
query count is O(n^3 eta^(-2) log(n/delta)). No factor K occurs in that query
count, although a naive explicitly listed-form oracle itself costs O(Kd).

The evaluator does NOT compute K. Choosing the sharp j(K) requires knowing
K or otherwise providing that parameter. Counting or enumerating all dual
vertices is a separate task, so this corollary alone is not a polynomial-time
algorithm for finding the sharp K-sensitive tuning from an arbitrary LP input.

One may instead use any report-independent supplied integer j, or choose it
from a known structural upper bound Kbar>=K. For a d-variable dual defined
by m activity inequalities plus d nonnegativity constraints,
K<=binom(m+d,d), since every vertex has a linearly independent active set of
d inequalities. This computable bound can supply Kbar. The resulting guarantee
uses C_j(K)<=C_j(Kbar), and its asymptotic tuning is in Kbar rather than an
uncomputed exact K. It need not retain a sharp bound in the smaller actual K.

This is a useful algorithmic follow-up in the oracle/high-probability sense:
it replaces exponentially many mask evaluations by a polynomial number of
LP queries, retains universal individual incentives, and covers all coalitions
simultaneously at any fixed profile with explicit confidence. It does not
give deterministic realized coverage, uniform coverage over all profiles on
one sampled rule, an efficient exact evaluator of M, or an algorithm for
computing the true structural complexity K.
