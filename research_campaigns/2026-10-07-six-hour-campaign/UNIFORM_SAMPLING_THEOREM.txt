UNIFORM FINITE-SAMPLE IMPLEMENTATION OF THE RAW-CAPPED HYBRID
7 October 2026. Conventional proof; independent cross-model audit completed without a correctness finding.

VERDICT
The proposed simultaneous-in-all-reports guarantee is valid for a FIXED finite
nonnegative min-linear technology, fixed owner identities, and a sampling law
chosen independently of reports. An explicit pseudodimension and sample bound
are given below. The proof counts all hyperplane faces, including ties; it does
not infer an infinite-profile statement from a finite computational experiment.

1. EXACT STATEMENT AND QUANTIFIERS

Fix n,d>=1, a nonempty ordered set V={w^1,...,w^K} contained in R_+^d,
and F(c)=min_{v in V} w^v.c for c in R_+^d. The order on V is fixed before
reports. Owner i reports b_i in R_+^d; total resources are c=sum_i b_i.
Write b=(b_1,...,b_n), T(b)=F(c), and v_b(S)=F(sum_{i in S} b_i).
This includes the finite-LP dual-vertex model already under study.

Fix j>=2, independently of reports. One random observation s=(q,B) is drawn by
  q ~ Uniform(0,1/j),
  conditional on q, B_1,...,B_n independently satisfy Pr(B_h=0)=q.
For each i, ignore B_i and set
  c^-_i(s,b)=sum_{h!=i} B_h b_h,
  c^+_i(s,b)=c^-_i(s,b)+b_i.
Let w^-_i(s,b) be the FIRST vertex minimizing w.c^-_i. Define
  a^-_i(s,b)=min(w^-_i.b_i,T(b)),
  Delta_i(s,b)=F(c^+_i)-F(c^-_i),
  X_i(s,b)=q a^-_i(s,b)+(1-q)Delta_i(s,b).
Thus 0<=X_i(s,b)<=T(b). Its expectation M_i(b)=E_s X_i(s,b) is exactly the
uniformly q-averaged capped hybrid, using a common sample for all coordinates.

Assume the established exact-mechanism theorem:
  M(S;b)>=v_b(S) for every S,b,
  sum_i M_i(b)<=B_j(K) T(b),
where
  B_j(K)=j+[ln K+2 ln j+1]/ln[j^3/(j^2+j-1)].
The new sampling result below is a corollary of those exact guarantees.

Let 0<eta<=1 and 0<delta<1. Put
  p=nd,
  D=ceil(16 p log_2(8pK)),
  epsilon=eta/n,
  A=32e/epsilon=32en/eta.
It suffices to take an integer sample size
  N >= (128/epsilon^2) [ D ln(A ln A)+ln(8n/delta) ].                 (1)
Draw s_1,...,s_N independently ONCE, before any reports. Publish/freeze them.
Define the deterministic rule associated with that seed by
  Mhat_i(b)=N^{-1} sum_l X_i(s_l,b)+(eta/n)T(b).                      (2)

Then:
  (a) EVERY seed gives a nonnegative rule, own-vector componentwise monotone
      at EVERY profile. Thus the payment-only underreporting incentives hold
      exactly and universally over the sampling randomness.
  (b) With probability at least 1-delta over the ONE initial seed, simultaneously
      for EVERY b in (R_+^d)^n and EVERY S subseteq[n],
         Mhat(S;b)>=v_b(S),
         sum_i Mhat_i(b)<=[B_j(K)+2eta]T(b).                        (3)
      In fact the same event gives Mhat_i(b)>=M_i(b) for every i,b.

This is polynomial sample complexity in n,d,log K,1/eta,log(1/delta). In order
notation, with logarithms bounded below by positive constants, (1) is
  O( n^2/eta^2 * [ nd log(8ndK) log(en/eta)+log(n/delta) ] ).
No union bound over the 2^n coalitions or over report profiles is needed.

2. INCENTIVES, RANGE, AND THE ZERO-VALUE CASE

Fix an observation s and all reports except b_i. The off vector c^-_i and its
fixed-priority minimizer w^-_i are entirely independent of b_i, including at
b_i=0 and all tie cases. As b_i increases componentwise, w^-_i.b_i and T(b)
both weakly increase, because every vertex is nonnegative. Their minimum
a^-_i is therefore nondecreasing. Also F(c^+_i) is nondecreasing while
F(c^-_i) is fixed, so Delta_i is nondecreasing. The coefficients q,1-q are
nonnegative. The sample average and the added eta*T/n inherit monotonicity.

The same monotonicity of F gives 0<=Delta_i<=T. By definition 0<=a^-_i<=T,
so X_i is bounded by T. If T(b)=0, every masked/subcoalition value is also zero;
both a^-_i and Delta_i are zero for every observation. Consequently X_i=M_i=
Mhat_i=0 exactly at all such profiles; no division by zero is used operationally.

For analysis define
  h_{b,i}(s)=X_i(s,b)/T(b) if T(b)>0, and h_{b,i}(s)=0 otherwise.
Thus h_{b,i} takes values in [0,1]. Let H_i={h_{b,i}:b in R_+^{nd}}.
The normalization is only a proof device; the implemented payment is (2).

3. A TIE-SAFE SUBGRAPH GROWTH BOUND

Fix coordinate i, m observations s_l=(q_l,B_l), and arbitrary real subgraph
thresholds u_1,...,u_m. Regard the p=nd real report coordinates as parameters.
All the following are homogeneous linear forms in those parameters:
  C_v(b)=w^v.sum_h b_h,
  L^-_{l,v}(b)=w^v.sum_{h!=i} B_{l,h} b_h,
  L^+_{l,v}(b)=L^-_{l,v}(b)+w^v.b_i,
  A_{i,v}(b)=w^v.b_i.

Make a first arrangement consisting of:
  - comparisons C_v-C_t for all v<t;
  - comparisons L^-_{l,v}-L^-_{l,t} for all l and v<t;
  - comparisons L^+_{l,v}-L^+_{l,t} for all l and v<t;
  - cap comparisons A_{i,v}-C_t for all ordered pairs v,t.
The number H0 of hyperplanes is at most
  H0=(2m+1) binom(K,2)+K^2 <= 3mK^2.                              (4)
Identically zero forms may simply be omitted.

Count ALL sign faces, not just full-dimensional open cells. The number of
nonempty ternary sign patterns of H real hyperplanes in R^p is at most
  F(H,p)=sum_{k=0}^p 2^k binom(H,k) <= (1+2H)^p.                   (5)
Here binom(H,k)=0 for k>H. One elementary proof of the first bound uses
F(H,p)<=F(H-1,p)+2F(H-1,p-1), with F(0,p)=F(H,0)=1: inserting a hyperplane
replaces an intersected relative-open face by at most three pieces. Degeneracy
can only reduce the count. The second inequality follows from binom(H,k)<=H^k
and comparison with the binomial expansion of (1+2H)^p. Restricting the
parameters to the nonnegative orthant cannot create new sign patterns.

On each first-arrangement face the fixed-priority minimizers for T, off, and
on are fixed, and every cap branch is fixed. Equality signs specify ties
exactly; the prescribed first-minimizer order resolves them. Therefore, on
that face, each X_i(s_l,b) is a fixed homogeneous linear form U_l(b), and
T(b) is a fixed homogeneous linear form C_t(b).

Restrict momentarily to T(b)>0. Then
  h_{b,i}(s_l)>u_l  iff  U_l(b)-u_l C_t(b)>0.
These are m more linear threshold tests within that face. Their label patterns
are bounded by F(m,p), again including equality faces. Multiplying the two
arrangement bounds and adding the single label pattern supplied by T=0 yields
the uniform subgraph-growth bound
  Pi_i(m) <= 1+F(H0,p)F(m,p)
          <= 1+[(1+6mK^2)(1+2m)]^p
          <= 1+(21m^2K^2)^p
          <= 2(21m^2K^2)^p,     m>=1.                            (6)
The thresholds may differ at every observation, so this is a pseudodimension
argument, not merely a bound for one common threshold.

If m points were pseudo-shattered, (6) would imply
  m <= 1+p log_2 21+2p log_2 K+2p log_2 m.
Put r=m/p and L=log_2(8pK)>=3. This inequality implies
  r < 2L+2log_2 r,
because 1/p+log_2 21-6<0. If r>=16L, then r>=48,
2L<=r/8, and 2log_2 r<=r/2, which contradicts the preceding inequality.
Thus Pdim(H_i)<16p log_2(8pK), and D in (1) is a valid upper bound.
Constants are deliberately conservative; no optimal dimension claim is made.

4. UNIFORM CONCENTRATION AND SOURCE CHECK

Free primary source: David Haussler, "Decision Theoretic Generalizations of
the PAC Model for Neural Net and Other Learning Applications," January 1992
revision, subsequently Information and Computation 100 (1992), 78-150.
https://www.cis.upenn.edu/~mkearns/teaching/COLT/haussler.pdf
Relevant locations in that free manuscript: Corollary 1, PDF page 26;
Theorem 6, PDF page 31; Theorem 12 and Definition 11, PDF pages 54-55.

For permissible [0,1]-valued classes, Corollary 1 bounds uniform empirical-mean
deviation by 4 times the expected L1 covering number at epsilon/16, multiplied
by exp(-N epsilon^2/128). Theorems 6 and 12 bound that covering number by
2[(32e/epsilon)ln(32e/epsilon)]^D when pseudodimension is at most D.
These are the only imported probability/covering results.

The source's measurability condition holds here. Joint evaluation (s,b)->h is
Borel: fixed-priority selection has finitely many polynomial inequality/equality
branches; on T>0 the formula is a ratio of continuous linear/polynomial forms,
and on T=0 it is explicitly zero. Moreover h is invariant under positive
scaling of the entire report vector. Hence the same class can be indexed by
the compact nonnegative simplex sum_{i,h}b_{ih}=1 together with the zero vector.
This meets the permissible-class definition directly. In particular, ties
and unbounded original report magnitudes create no measurability gap.

Applying those results separately to H_i gives
  Pr{sup_b |N^{-1}sum_l h_{b,i}(s_l)-E h_{b,i}|>epsilon}
     <=8[A ln A]^D exp(-N epsilon^2/128).
A union bound over the n coordinates gives a failure probability at most
  8n[A ln A]^D exp(-N epsilon^2/128),
which is at most delta under (1). Coordinates need not be independent;
the observations s_l must be independent across l.

On this ONE event, for every b with T>0 and every i,
  |N^{-1}sum_l X_i(s_l,b)-M_i(b)|<=eta*T(b)/n.                     (7)
At T=0 the same inequality holds exactly. Adding eta*T/n makes each coordinate
at least M_i and at most M_i+2eta*T/n. Summing over a coalition proves coverage;
summing over all owners and using the exact-mechanism budget proves (3).

5. WHAT THIS DOES AND DOES NOT ESTABLISH

The random seed can be public and reused for arbitrarily many, even adaptively
chosen, report profiles: the high-probability event already quantifies over all
profiles. Incentives hold for every seed, whereas simultaneous coverage/budget
hold on the event of probability at least 1-delta. A positive-probability good
seed exists, but this proof gives no deterministic certificate that a particular
observed seed is good and no derandomization procedure. Testing finitely many
profiles is not a certificate of the uniform property.

The technology V, its ordering, n,d,j,eta, and the sampling law must be fixed
independently of reports. The theorem is uniform in reports for that technology;
it does not assert one seed works for every different technology. A certified
upper bound on K can replace K in the dimension/sample/budget bounds. Computing
K or optimizing j is a separate problem; j may simply be supplied ex ante.

Polynomial sample count does not by itself imply polynomial total running time
in log K, or efficient access to V. Each observation requires off/on values
and a canonical off minimizer for each owner; oracle implementation and exact
tie resolution are separate computational obligations. The statement above uses
real-arithmetic sampling. Section6 below supplies an exact finite-law implementation using the
posterior-mean sampler, rather than an unverified discretization of q.

The adjustment in (2) may pay eta*T/n to an owner reporting a zero bundle.
If zero payment to that owner is desired, replace the adjustment by
(eta*T/n) 1{b_i!=0}: X_i=M_i=0 exactly when b_i=0, and the modified adjustment
is still own-vector monotone, so the same conclusions follow.

No claim of historical priority, asymptotic lower-bound novelty, or machine-
checked proof is made. This is a conventional proof of the stated sampling
corollary using a source-verified uniform-convergence theorem.

6. EXACT RATIONAL ONE-SEED IMPLEMENTATION

Use the discrete law in SAMPLING_COROLLARY.txt Section4. For a subset S of
size k, its individual probability is pi_k=j integral_0^(1/j) q^(n-k)(1-q)^k dq.
Conditional on that full mask, replace q by
 theta_k = integral q^(n-k+1)(1-q)^k dq / integral q^(n-k)(1-q)^k dq,
with both integrals over(0,1/j). Define
 Xtilde_i(S,b)=theta_k a_i^-(S,b)+(1-theta_k)Delta_i(S,b).
The full-mask conditional expectation identity gives E Xtilde_i=M_i exactly;
it does not rely on treating theta_k as independent of the mask. Each fixed
mask still has nonnegative report-independent weights and own-off selection,
so every realized rule is own-vector monotone and 0<=Xtilde_i<=T.

The entire Section3 growth bound applies verbatim to observations S_l, with
q_l replaced by the fixed scalar theta_|S_l|. No new report-dependent surface
is introduced. Thus the same sample size(1) gives(3) simultaneously over all
real report profiles for this finite sample space. The canonical mask law is
drawn once independently of reports and reused. A positive-probability good
finite seed exists; no efficient deterministic good-seed certification follows.

The exact law has a common integer denominator H=(n+2)!j^(n+2). Cardinality
weights and posterior coefficients use O(n(log j+log(n+2))) bits. Exact
fair-bit sampling has expected polynomial cost using rejection sampling;
the rejection count is not bounded in the worst case, but terminates almost
surely. Rational reports and technology give rational exact payments.

With a canonical value/minimizer oracle, evaluation uses at most
 1+N(n+1)
queries. For a rational production LP with d resource-price coordinates,
lexicographic optimal-face minimization uses at most d+1 ordinary exact LP
calls per canonical query, hence at most1+N(n+1)(d+1) LP calls overall.
This is polynomial in the displayed input/sample parameters, assuming j and
a certified K upper bound are supplied independently of reports. A computable
bound K<=binom(m+d,d) is available for m activities; using it tunes the result
to that upper bound, not necessarily to the smaller unknown actual K.

Polynomial arithmetic evaluation is distinct from verifying that a randomly
chosen seed is good. No deterministic polynomial construction, practical
sample-size claim, or machine-checked uniform theorem is asserted.
