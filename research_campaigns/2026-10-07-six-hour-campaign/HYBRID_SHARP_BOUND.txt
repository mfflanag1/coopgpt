MIXING CONDITIONED-ZERO AND ENDPOINT-MARGINAL PAYMENTS
7 October 2026. Independently audited proof; exact mechanism checks recorded
in verification/hybrid_result.json after coordinator execution.
No historical novelty, efficient-computation, or field-level claim is made.

RESULT
For a fixed binary resource-packing technology with K dual-cover vertices,
for every integer j>=2 there is a nonnegative deterministic payment rule on
ALL nonnegative real capacity reports that is own-report nondecreasing and
covers every coalition. The rule indexed by j has total payment at most

 T [ j + (log K + 2 log j + 1) / alpha_j ],                    (A)
 alpha_j = log(j^3/(j^2+j-1)) > 0,

where T is the grand coalition's production value and logarithms are natural.
The choice

 L=log K,
 j(K)=max{2, ceil[L/(log(2+L))^2]}                            (B)

is valid for every K>=1, depends only on the fixed technology, and yields

 total payment <= (1+o(1)) T log K / log log K                (C)

as K tends to infinity, uniformly over capacities and owner counts.
When T=0 all payments are exactly zero. The construction applies to directed
single-commodity flow games, where K is the number of inclusion-minimal
STRUCTURAL edge cutsets and each edge has one fixed owner. The upper proof
also applies to arbitrary finite binary resource-packing technologies.

The successful rule is a report-independent mixture of the hybrid rules
r^q. This is not a claim that every individual fixed-q hybrid has bound (C),
nor is it a new bound for the old uniform-noise monotone envelope.

1. MODEL, FIXED TECHNOLOGY, AND DUAL VERTICES
Let E be a finite nonempty resource set, one fixed distinct owner per resource.
Let P be a finite family of nonempty subsets of E. For finite c>=0 define

 F(c)=max {sum_(P in P) x_P : x>=0,
          sum_(P containing e) x_P <= c_e for every e}.

Every activity yields one unit of value and uses one unit of each resource
it contains. Empty P is the trivial zero technology. Empty individual
activities are excluded, since they would make production unbounded.
The coalition value is v_c(S)=F(c*1_S). All reports belong to the SAME fixed
technology and owner set. Owners may underreport their own capacity and have
payment-only utility without usage costs; own-report nondecreasing payments
give the required incentive guarantee.

The dual polyhedron is

 Q={w>=0 : sum_(e in P) w_e >= 1 for all P in P}.

It is nonempty and pointed and has finitely many vertices V. Every nonnegative
linear objective attains its finite minimum at a vertex, so

 F(c)=min_(w in V) c.w.                                     (1)

Every vertex coordinate lies in [0,1]. If w_e>1, all covering constraints
containing e, and the e nonnegativity constraint, are slack. Constraints
not containing e do not change when that coordinate changes. Small changes
of both signs would remain feasible, contradicting extremality.
Set K=|V|>=1. Fix one total priority order on V ONCE, independently of reports,
mask outcomes, and q. In particular zero reports do not delete resources,
activities, vertices, or structural cuts from the definition of K.

2. EFFECTIVE USAGE AND EVERY MASK
Define

 D_e(c)=max {sum_(P containing e) x_P : x feasible at c}.

The bounded packing polytope is compact, so these maxima exist. They satisfy

 0 <= D_e(c) <= min{c_e,F(c)},                               (2)

because resource usage never exceeds total production in a binary activity
packing. Each D_e is nondecreasing in every capacity.

The feasible packing polytopes at c and D(c) are identical. Every packing
at c obeys all effective upper bounds by their definition, and D(c)<=c gives
the converse inclusion. The same fact holds after EVERY binary mask b:
a packing feasible at c*b is feasible at c, hence obeys every D bound, and
has zero usage on every masked-out resource. Conversely D*b<=c*b. Thus

 F(c*b)=F(D(c)*b) for every b in {0,1}^E,                    (3)

and equality holds for the entire feasible packing polytopes, not just values.

For a fixed owner e write D_h(t) for the effective usage at (t,c_-e).
For u>0 and 0<t<=u, convexly mix usage-maximizing feasible plans at 0 and u.
Their mixture is feasible at t and gives

 D_h(t)>=D_h(0)+(t/u)[D_h(u)-D_h(0)].                        (4)

Consequently D_h(t)>D_h(0) if and only if D_h(u)>D_h(0).
By comparing any two positive reports to their maximum, the set of coordinates
strictly above their report-zero values is the SAME for all positive own reports.

3. THE FIXED-q HYBRID
Fix q in (0,1), put s=1-q, and independently sample bits with Pr(B_e=0)=q.
At profile c let w*(c,B) be the first vertex minimizing D(c)*B.w. Define

 z_e^q(c)=D_e(c) E[w*_e(c,B)],
 p_e^q(c)=D_e(c) E[w*_e(c,B) | B_e=0],                     (5)
 m_e^q(c)=E_(B_-e)[F(c*(B_-e,1))-F(c*(B_-e,0))],          (6)
 r_e^q(c)=q p_e^q(c)+(1-q)m_e^q(c).                        (7)

In (6), notation c*(B_-e,b_e) means c_e b_e in the own coordinate and
c_h B_h elsewhere. These are raw-capacity endpoint values. All payments are
expectations, hence deterministic; realized-mask coverage is not required.

3a. Nonnegativity and own-report incentives.
Nonnegativity is immediate. The off value in (6) does not depend on c_e,
whereas the on value is nondecreasing in c_e, so m_e^q is own-monotone.

For p fix a mask b with b_e=0. By (3) its minimum objective is F(c*b), independent
of the own report. Starting from own report zero, every vertex objective is
nondecreasing as the own report rises. A vertex is minimizing at a positive
report t exactly when it was minimizing at zero and every h with b_h w_h>0
has D_h(t)=D_h(0). Equation (4) makes this condition independent of the positive
report t. Thus the ENTIRE minimizing vertex family, and then the fixed-priority
selected vertex, are the same for all positive own reports. The conditional
coefficient E[w*_e|B_e=0] is therefore own-report independent on (0,infinity).
Since D_e is own-monotone and D_e=0 at own report zero, p_e^q is own-monotone
on the entire nonnegative report domain. Equation (7) preserves that property.

3b. Domination and every coalition's coverage.
Fix c and B_-e, keeping D=D(c) fixed in this comparison. Let w1 be the selected
vertex when B_e=1. Dual feasibility gives

 F(D*(B_-e,0)) <= sum_(h!=e) D_h B_h w1_h,
 F(D*(B_-e,1))  = sum_(h!=e) D_h B_h w1_h + D_e w1_e.

Subtracting and applying (3) to the two endpoints shows

 F(c*(B_-e,1))-F(c*(B_-e,0)) >= D_e w1_e.                  (8)

This is the endpoint supergradient inequality for the concave production
value, written directly so no differentiability or tie assumption is needed.
After expectation over B_-e,

 r_e^q >= q D_e E[w*_e|B_e=0]+s D_e E[w*_e|B_e=1]
        = z_e^q.                                          (9)

For each coalition S and every dual vertex w, weak duality and (3) imply

 sum_(e in S) D_e w_e >= F(D*1_S)=F(c*1_S)=v_c(S).

Expectation gives z^q(S)>=v_c(S); hence r^q(S)>=v_c(S) by (9).
No report comparison for unchanged owners is used.

4. EXACT AGGREGATE IDENTITY
For the rest of the budget proof fix c. Write T=F(c), D=D(c), and

 g(q)=E_q F(c*B)=E_q F(D*B),
 Z(q)=sum_e z_e^q(c),
 R(q)=sum_e r_e^q(c).

These functions are finite polynomials in q except that the displayed
conditional-expectation notation for p initially uses q>0. That conditional
expectation itself is a finite product-probability polynomial in the other
bits, so p, m and r extend polynomially to both endpoints as well.
In particular g(0)=T, g(q)>=0, and g(1)=0.

The selected masked objective is exactly the masked production value, so

 E_q sum_e D_e B_e w*_e = g(q),
 q sum_e p_e^q = E_q sum_e D_e(1-B_e)w*_e = Z(q)-g(q).      (10)

Differentiate the multiaffine expectation in the common zero probability q.
Differentiating coordinate e contributes its conditional off value minus
its conditional on value. Summing coordinates gives the exact identity

 g'(q)=-sum_e m_e^q(c).                                    (11)

Equations (7), (10), and (11) yield

 R(q)=Z(q)-g(q)-(1-q)g'(q).                                (12)

There is no interchange of infinite sums or limiting random objects in (11).
It is an identity for finite polynomials. For any b in (0,1), integration
by parts in (12) gives the more precise equality

 integral_0^b R(q)dq
   = integral_0^b Z(q)dq + T -(1-b)g(b)-2 integral_0^b g(q)dq
   <= integral_0^b Z(q)dq + T.                            (13)

The minus sign and the factor 2 in this identity are intentional.

5. A UNIFORM BOUND FOR Z ON A SHORT q INTERVAL
Suppose T>0. For a fixed vertex w let

 a_e(w)=D_e w_e/T in [0,1],   rho_w=sum_e a_e(w).

An unmasked optimum has objective T, and masking cannot increase its objective.
Therefore the selected masked vertex always satisfies

 sum_e a_e(w*)B_e <= 1.                                   (14)

For any lambda>0 set

 alpha(q,lambda)=-log[q+(1-q)exp(-lambda)] > 0.

The function a -> log[q+(1-q)exp(-lambda a)] is convex on [0,1]. Its value
at zero is zero, so its chord from zero to one bounds it above by
-alpha(q,lambda)*a. Independence and Markov's inequality give, for each w,

 Pr_q(sum_e a_e(w)B_e <= 1)
   <= exp(lambda) product_e [q+(1-q)exp(-lambda a_e(w))]
   <= exp(lambda-alpha(q,lambda)rho_w).                    (15)

For every x>=0, the event rho_(w*)>=x is contained in the union of the events
in (15) over vertices with rho_w>=x. There are at most K vertices. Thus

 Pr_q(rho_(w*)>=x) <= min{1, K exp(lambda-alpha(q,lambda)x)}.

Tail integration, splitting at (log K+lambda)/alpha(q,lambda), yields

 Z(q)/T=E_q rho_(w*) <= (log K+lambda+1)/alpha(q,lambda).   (16)

This estimate includes ties because (14) holds for every selected optimum.
It makes no independence assumption about the selected vertex and the bits.

For integer j>=2 set b=1/j and lambda=2 log j. For 0<q<=b,

 q+(1-q)exp(-lambda)
   <= b+(1-b)b^2=(j^2+j-1)/j^3 < 1.

Hence alpha(q,lambda)>=alpha_j=log[j^3/(j^2+j-1)]>0, and (16) is uniformly
bounded on this entire interval by

 Z(q)/T <= (log K+2 log j+1)/alpha_j.                      (17)

6. THE MIXED RULE, FINITE BOUND, AND ALL-PROFILE PARAMETER
Choose j solely from the fixed technology, and define

 M_e(c)=j integral_0^(1/j) r_e^q(c)dq.                     (18)

This is a fixed convex mixture of nonnegative deterministic own-monotone,
coalition-covering rules. It therefore has all those same properties. One
can interpret (18) as first drawing a common q uniformly from (0,1/j) and
then taking all expectations, but the final payment is deterministic.
Selecting q or j from the reported capacities is not part of this mechanism.

Combining (13) and (17) gives precisely

 sum_e M_e(c)
   <= j integral_0^(1/j) Z(q)dq + jT
   <= T [j+(log K+2 log j+1)/alpha_j],

proving (A).

If T=0, (2) gives D=0. Every masked raw profile also has production zero by
monotonicity, so m=p=r=M=0. This deals with the zero-value domain directly,
without division by T. K=1 is also covered by (A) and (B); no log log K is
used in their finite definitions. The asymptotic notation is only for K->infinity.

Now choose j(K) from (B). For L=log K tending to infinity,

 j ~ L/(log L)^2,
 log j = log L - 2 log log L + o(1),
 alpha_j = log j - log(1+1/j-1/j^2) ~ log L.

Thus j=o(L/log L), the numerator L+2log j+1 is (1+o(1))L, and (A) proves (C).
The o(1) depends only on K, since the finite bound contains no report or
owner-count term. The leading coefficient in this upper bound is one.

7. FINITE EXACT REPRESENTATION; COMPUTATIONAL LIMITS
For n=|E| and a subset S of the other n-1 owners, define

 u_e(S)=D_e w*_e(c,(1_S,0)),
 delta_e(S)=F(c*(1_S,1))-F(c*(1_S,0)).

Writing a=n-1-|S| and d=|S| gives

 r_e^q(c)=sum_(S subset E\{e}) q^a(1-q)^d
                       [q u_e(S)+(1-q)delta_e(S)].         (19)

This is a polynomial of degree at most n. For b=1/j, the positive weights
in its mixed representation are

 j integral_0^b q^(a+1)(1-q)^d dq,
 j integral_0^b q^a(1-q)^(d+1) dq.

They can be evaluated by finite binomial expansion; for example

 integral_0^b q^A(1-q)^B dq
   = sum_(h=0)^B (-1)^h binom(B,h) b^(A+h+1)/(A+h+1).

Thus rational reports and fixed integer j admit rational payments by finite
LP and mask enumeration. There is no need for numerical quadrature or a
positive lower cutoff in q. This is an existence/exact-finite representation,
not an efficient algorithm: vertex counts and mask enumeration may be exponential.

8. SINGLE-COMMODITY GRAPHS AND THE LOWER-BOUND COMPARISON
Take the activities to be all simple directed s-t paths of a fixed finite
directed graph, with one fixed owner per edge. Their packing value is maxflow.
The path-cover polyhedron Q is the s-t cut dominant; by maxflow/mincut and LP
duality its vertices are exactly the 0/1 incidence vectors of inclusion-minimal
structural edge cutsets. Therefore the K in this proof is exactly the fixed
structural cut count. This includes parallel-edge DAGs, simple DAGs, and cyclic
graphs, with D defined by path packings rather than arbitrary circulations.
Unused edges have D_e=0 and cause no difficulty.

The larger binary-packing statement also covers fractional unit-value
multicommodity path packing, using all allowable paths as activities and K
equal to the number of fractional cover vertices. It does not identify that
K with an ordinary graph-cut count. Unequal activity values, arbitrary
nonbinary coefficients, and owners controlling several independently reported
resources are outside the stated theorem.

The existing project manuscript states the unrestricted serial-pool minimax
lower bound (1-o(1)) log k/log log k; see
paper5/truthful_cooperation/manuscript.tex,
Lemma labelled lem:weighted-hierarchy and Theorem labelled thm:order (lines
413-515 in the version inspected on 7 October). This is the profile-averaged
hierarchy result with coefficient one, stronger than the earlier one-profile
hierarchy coefficient one-half. It allows arbitrary nonanonymous payment
rules, all nonnegative reports, and arbitrary finite fixed pool ownerships.

Its transfer to the present graph class is exact: realize k pools as k serial
bundles of parallel edges, each owned by the corresponding fixed agent. Every
minimal structural cut is exactly one whole bundle, so K=k. An underreport
changes the same single original edge owner's report. Coalition values and
own-report comparisons are identical to those in the pool theorem.
Consequently that already-established lower, together with the new upper here,
gives the sharp leading coefficient one for the unrestricted worst-case factor
over this general directed-graph class, and for the encompassing binary-packing
class, when complexity is K and arbitrary finite owner counts are allowed.
The lower result is an imported project theorem, not newly proved in this file.

This transfer alone does not establish a matching leading coefficient when
the admissible graph class forbids parallel edges: serial subdivision changes
the owner set and the structural cut count. The present UPPER remains valid
on simple DAGs, but a matching simple-DAG lower needs its own parameter accounting.
Nor is (A) claimed to equal or improve every finite-K pool-specific bound U(K).

AUDIT STATUS
The core proposed advance is (12)-(18): the derivative term from truthful
endpoint marginals has a report-independent integrated budget of at most jT.
The effective-usage and conditioned-zero ingredients have separate exact finite
checks. This integrated theorem has its independent proof audit and
mechanism checks; finite checks alone do not establish its universal bound.
