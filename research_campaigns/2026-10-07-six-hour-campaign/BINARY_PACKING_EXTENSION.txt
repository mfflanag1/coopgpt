CONDITIONED-ZERO PAYMENTS FOR BINARY RESOURCE-PACKING GAMES
7 October2026. Coordinator and independent Codex derivations agree. Exact
checks pass; opposite-model review found no substantive gap. Application
proof and tests were strengthened following its two low-severity suggestions.

MODEL
Let E be a finite set of resources, each owned by one fixed distinct agent.
Let P be a finite family of NONEMPTY subsets of E. A production plan consists
of nonnegative real x_P: one unit of activity P consumes one unit of every
resource in P and yields one unit of value. For reports c>=0 define
 F(c)=max {sum_P x_P: x>=0, sum_{P containing e}x_P<=c_e for every e}.
The coalition value is v_c(S)=F(c*1_S). Owners may underreport their single
resource capacity and receive payment-only utility, with no operating costs.
All resource identities, activities and technology remain fixed across reports.
Nonempty activities ensure bounded primal variables and finite attained F.
An empty activity family gives the trivial zero game and can be treated separately.

The dual cover polyhedron is
 Q={w>=0: sum_{e in P}w_e>=1 for every activity P}.
It is nonempty and pointed; let V be its finite set of vertices and K=|V|.
Every nonnegative objective has an optimal vertex, so
 F(c)=min_{w in V}sum_e c_e w_e.                       (1)
Every vertex satisfies 0<=w_e<=1. If w_e>1, all cover inequalities containing
e and the e-nonnegativity inequality are slack; other constraints ignore e.
Both sufficiently small positive and negative changes of that coordinate
would be feasible, contradicting extremality. K depends only on technology.

THEOREM
There exists an all-profile own-report-monotone nonnegative deterministic
payment rule covering every coalition with total payment
 <= (exp(1)+o(1))F(c) log K as K tends to infinity.
The same explicit finite-K bound (1) of BERNOULLI_LOG_BOUND.txt applies.
No efficient exact computation, priority, or field-level breakthrough claim
is included. The number K counts dual vertices here, not graph cuts unless
the technology is a single-commodity network.

1. EFFECTIVE RESOURCE USAGE
Define
 D_e(c)=max {sum_{P containing e}x_P: x is feasible at c}.
It is attained, nondecreasing in every report, and satisfies0<=D_e<=min(c_e,F(c)):
usage of a resource is at most total production in any feasible plan. The
feasible production polytopes at c and D(c) are identical. Indeed every plan
feasible at c satisfies every effective usage bound by definition, and D<=c.
For every binary mask b the feasible polytopes at c*b and D(c)*b are likewise
identical, by the same inclusion argument. Thus
 F(c*b)=F(D(c)*b).                                    (2)
For a fixed owner e, high report u>0, and0<t<=u, convex mixtures of usage-
maximizing plans at0 and u show
 D_h(t)>=D_h(0)+(t/u)(D_h(u)-D_h(0)).                  (3)
Hence D_h(t)>D_h(0) iff D_h(u)>D_h(0). This is precisely the strict-support
lemma of the graph proof, now applied to the production polytope.

2. MECHANISM AND INCENTIVES
Fix a report-independent total priority on V and q in(0,1). Independently mask
each resource with Pr(B_e=0)=q. Let w*(c,B) be the first vertex minimizing
sum_e D_e(c)B_e w_e. Put
 z_e(c)=D_e(c) E[w*_e(c,B)],
 p_e(c)=D_e(c) E[w*_e(c,B) | B_e=0].                  (4)
The expectation is a deterministic payment; no realized-random guarantee is claimed.

Fix a mask with b_e=0. By (2), the minimum dual value equals F(c*b), which
does not depend on the own report c_e. Every vertex's objective is at least
this value at report0 and has only nonnegative increments as c_e increases.
At a positive report t a vertex attains the value precisely when it attained
it at0 and every h with b_h w_h>0 has D_h(t)=D_h(0). By (3) this is the SAME
condition for all positive own reports. The entire optimal-vertex family,
and then the fixed-priority selected vertex, are invariant. In particular
E[w*_e|B_e=0] is independent of positive own reports. Since D_e is own-monotone
and D_e=0 at ownreport0, p_e is own-report nondecreasing everywhere.

3. DOMINATION AND COALITION COVERAGE
Fix c and B_-e. Let w0,w1 be selected when B_e is0,1. The two optimality
inequalities, written with A(w)=sum_{h!=e}D_h B_h w_h, are
 A(w0)<=A(w1),
 A(w1)+D_e w1_e<=A(w0)+D_e w0_e.
If D_e>0 they imply w1_e<=w0_e. If D_e=0 all related payments vanish. Thus
 z_e<=p_e<=z_e/q.                                    (5)
For every coalition S, every w in Q weak-dually bounds production under
capacities D*1_S:
 sum_{e in S}D_e w_e >= F(D*1_S)=F(c*1_S)=v_c(S).
Taking expectation for w* proves z(S)>=v_c(S), hence p(S)>=v_c(S).
Nonnegativity is immediate. The least own-report envelope of z is also
bounded by p, as in the graph theorem.

4. BUDGET
Let T=F(c)>0. For every vertex w define
 a_e(w)=D_e(c)w_e/T in[0,1], rho_w=sum_e a_e(w).
An unmasked minimizing vertex has D-weight T, so the selected masked objective
is always <=T. For each fixed vertex, the independent-mask Chernoff argument
in BERNOULLI_LOG_BOUND.txt gives
 Pr(sum_e a_e(w)B_e<=1)<=exp(lambda-alpha rho_w),
 alpha=-log(q+(1-q)exp(-lambda)).
A union over K vertices and tail integration yield
 sum_e z_e/T=E rho_{w*}<=(log K+lambda+1)/alpha.
Equation(5) proves the same finite-K bound for p divided byq. With fixed
q=exp(-1) and analysis-only lambda=log(2+log K), total p is
 <=(exp(1)+o(1))T log K. When T=0 all D and payments are zero.

5. APPLICATION SCOPE
For a finite directed single-commodity network, use its finite family of
simple s-t paths as activities. Fractional path packing has value maxflow.
Its dual vertices are exactly characteristic vectors of inclusion-minimal
structural cuts: maxflow/mincut for every nonnegative objective identifies
the dual dominant as the convex hull of those cut vectors plus the nonnegative
orthant. Here are details: both polyhedra are closed upper polyhedra and have
the same minimum for every nonnegative objective by maxflow/mincut. If they
differed, separation from the cut dominant would give a nonnegative objective
with unequal minima, a contradiction. Vertices of a finite convex hull plus
the orthant belong to its generating cut vectors. Conversely each minimal
cut C is uniquely optimal among cut vectors for objective 1 on C and M>|C|
outside: another minimal cut must contain an outside edge. Strictly positive
objectives exclude a nonzero recession direction. Thus every minimal cut
vector is indeed a vertex. Therefore this is the graph theorem, including
graphs with directed
cycles if D is defined through PATH PACKINGS as above. In cyclic graphs do
not define D by allowing arbitrary circulations, and do not import the DAG
prefix/suffix formula without proof.

For unit-value splittable multicommodity throughput, let activities be all
simple paths joining any of the fixed terminal pairs. Value is total units
delivered, with no compulsory per-commodity demand, no unequal revenues,
and no budget/operating costs. The theorem applies with K fractional-cover
vertices; these need not be integral cuts. No graph-cut-count bound is claimed
for the multicommodity case.

More generally, the technology describes fractional production from unit
bundles of complementary resources. Arbitrary nonbinary resource-consumption
coefficients or unequal activity revenues need additional scaling analysis;
they are not silently included. One owner controlling multiple independently
reported resource capacities would be a different incentive model.

Established ingredients are linear-programming duality and classical core
coverage by dual prices. The candidate new mechanism uses effective usage
and conditioning on an owner's zero mask to obtain all-profile incentives,
then bounds the subsidy by logarithmic dual-vertex complexity. Source and
specialist comparisons are pending; a lack of a located match is not priority.
