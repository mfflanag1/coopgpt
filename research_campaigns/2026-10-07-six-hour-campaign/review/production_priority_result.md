### 1. Nishizaki–Hayashida–Sekizaki–Tanaka

Ichiro Nishizaki, Tomohiro Hayashida, Shinya Sekizaki, and Kenta Tanaka, “Averaged dual solution for linear production games and its characterization,” *Central European Journal of Operational Research* 31, 523–555 (2023). Online publication: 15 October 2022. DOI: [10.1007/s10100-022-00820-6](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10100-022-00820-6).

- Access: publisher abstract/metadata only; subscription preview, not full text.
- Inspected: [Springer article page](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10100-022-00820-6), abstract and bibliographic record.
- Exact evidence:
  - Abstract says the authors “revise the definition of the linear production game” by valuing excess resources.
  - Their solution uses coalition-specific shadow prices and lies in “the core of the revised linear production game.”
- Comparison:
  - This is revised-characteristic-function core membership, not demonstrated coverage of all coalitions under the original profit characteristic function.
  - Nothing inspectable establishes monotonicity of a fixed owner’s payment under componentwise changes in that owner’s resource report.
  - Nothing inspectable states an original-worth subsidy construction or an owner-independent multiplicative subsidy bound.
- Unresolved obligation: inspect the full definitions, averaged-dual formula, core theorem, and axioms—especially whether any comparative-statics axiom varies an individual resource bundle while technology and owners remain fixed. Absence cannot be inferred from the abstract.

### 2. van Gellekom–Potters–Reijnierse–Engel–Tijs

J. R. G. van Gellekom, J. A. M. Potters, J. H. Reijnierse, M. C. Engel, and S. H. Tijs, “Characterization of the Owen Set of Linear Production Processes,” *Games and Economic Behavior* 32(1), 139–156 (2000). DOI: [10.1006/game.1999.0758](https://doi.org/10.1006/game.1999.0758).

- Access: legitimate Tilburg repository copy is indexed, but direct retrieval returned HTTP 403. Substantial original-text passages were inspectable through the repository’s indexed rendering.
- Original URL: [Tilburg PDF](https://pure.uvt.nl/ws/files/390653/27810_19820.pdf).
- Inspected statements/pages:
  - p.139: article identity, authors, DOI, and abstract.
  - p.141, Definitions 2.1–2.2: fixed agents, resources, products, production matrix \(A\), resource-endowment matrix \(B\), and prices \(c\).
  - p.150, Lemma 3.6: one-person efficiency, rescaling, shuffle, consistency, and deletion characterize the Owen set.
  - pp.151–153, Section 4 and Theorems 4.1–4.2: “shuffle” concerns redistribution, splitting, or merging of resource bundles through matrices \(X\); full nonnegative shuffling commutes with the core only for additive games, while zero-one merging/permutation is tied to convexity.
- Comparison:
  - The Owen set is an efficient subset of the core of the original linear-production game at a fixed process.
  - “Shuffle” is not own-report monotonicity: it compares transformed ownership structures/processes, rather than increasing one fixed owner’s report while holding all other owners and permitted report coordinates fixed.
  - Core allocations are exactly budget balanced; this is efficient original-core selection, not subsidized coalition coverage under a monotone reporting rule.
  - The inspected characterization contains no owner-independent subsidy bound.
- Concrete discrepancy: the paper’s Section 4 shows that even core correspondence under resource shuffling requires restrictive additivity/convexity conditions. It therefore cannot simply be cited as supplying the present all-profile own-report-monotone selection.
- Unresolved obligation: direct inspection of pp.142–150 is still needed to exclude a separate monotonicity property or comparative-static proposition elsewhere in the article. The indexed portions do not justify an article-wide absence claim.

### 3. Engelbrecht-Wiggans–Granot

Richard Engelbrecht-Wiggans and Daniel Granot, “On market prices in linear production games,” *Mathematical Programming* 32(3), 366–370 (1985). DOI: [10.1007/BF01580765](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01580765).

- Access: full original not obtained. Only bibliographic records and the article abstract were inspectable; no theorem page was recovered.
- Inspected statement: the abstract states that appropriately defined resource market prices coincide with the optimal dual solutions of one of the linear programs characterizing the production game.
- Comparison:
  - This supports a fixed-process equivalence between market prices and optimal dual solutions.
  - It does not, on the inspected evidence, concern monotonicity of individual-owner payments under own resource reports.
  - It does not establish a subsidized cover of original coalition values or a subsidy-factor bound.
- Unresolved obligation: inspect pp.366–370, including the exact definition of market prices and the principal theorem, for whether prices or induced owner payoffs are compared across changing endowments. No negative conclusion is warranted from the abstract alone.

### Bottom line

The strongest inspected predecessor result is van Gellekom et al.’s characterization of efficient original-core Owen allocations, including precise transformation properties of ownership bundles. Nishizaki et al. instead expressly work with a revised characteristic function. Engelbrecht-Wiggans–Granot identifies market prices with optimal dual solutions at a production process.

No inspected original theorem matches the requested three-part conjunction. Priority remains unresolved until the full Nishizaki and Engelbrecht-Wiggans–Granot theorem texts, and the inaccessible middle pages of van Gellekom et al., are directly checked. No files were changed.
