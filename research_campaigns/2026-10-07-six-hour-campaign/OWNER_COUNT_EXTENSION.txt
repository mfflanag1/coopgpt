OWNER-COUNT BOUNDS AND GENERAL CONCAVE HOMOGENEOUS PRODUCTION
7 October 2026. Conventional proof; independent cross-model audit completed
without a correctness finding. See review/owner_extension_adjudication.txt.

MAIN RESULTS AND SCOPE
(i) In the finite min-linear mechanism, the budget's union bound may use
min{K,2^n}, where n is the fixed owner count. The mechanism and its selection
rule are unchanged; only the budget analysis improves.
(ii) A direct version exists for EVERY finite-valued, coordinatewise-monotone,
concave, positively homogeneous f:R_+^d -> R, with d,n finite and positive.
It does not require continuity at the boundary. It uses a relative supporting
form on each positive-coordinate face, extended by +infinity outside that face,
and caps owner contributions at the finite grand value.
(iii) A compactness proof also works for boundary-continuous f and is recorded
as an independent fallback. It requires a subnet on the space of all profiles,
not an unjustified subsequence argument on an uncountable product.
(iv) Classical marginal/core bounds supply optional fairness floors at an
additional total cost at most T, with precise denominators given below.

For any integer j>=2 define

 alpha_j=log[j^3/(j^2+j-1)]>0,
 C_j(H)=j+(log H+2log j+1)/alpha_j.

The direct general mechanism has total payment at most

 min{n,C_j(2^n)} T.                                       (1)

All its payments are nonnegative and deterministic, it covers every coalition,
and each owner's payment is componentwise nondecreasing in its entire report
bundle. Owners, resources and technology remain fixed across reports.
For the report-independent choice

 L_n=n log 2,
 j_n=max{2,ceil[L_n/(log(2+L_n))^2]},                       (2)

the owner-count upper bound is

 (log 2+o(1)) n/log n * T                                  (3)

as n tends to infinity, uniformly in d, f, ownership and profiles.
This is an UPPER BOUND complementary to the sharper K-sensitive theorem.
There is no claim of necessity, optimality, or sharp leading constant as a
function of n. No measurability or efficient-evaluation conclusion is claimed.

1. FINITE MIN-LINEAR CASE: COUNT THE ACTUAL SELECTED RANGE
Fix a profile b=(b_1,...,b_n), b_i in R_+^d, and a finite family of K nonnegative
forms. The raw minimizer W(b,B) uses one report-independent priority, also
independent of q. Define its range over all owner masks by

 V_b={W(b,B):B in {0,1}^n}.

Then |V_b|<=min{K,2^n}. Once b is fixed, V_b is a fixed set independent of q
and of the subsequent random mask. Its dependence on b is irrelevant to a
pointwise-in-profile probability bound.

For each w in this range set

 a_i(w,b)=min{w.b_i,T},     d_i(w)=a_i(w,b)/T,
 rho_w=sum_i d_i(w),

when T>0. Every d_i is in [0,1]. The chosen form satisfies sum_i B_i d_i(W)<=1.
For lambda>0 and alpha=-log(q+(1-q)e^(-lambda)), the fixed-form bound is

 Pr(sum_i B_i d_i(w)<=1)<=exp(lambda-alpha rho_w).

Thus

 Pr(rho_W>=x)<=min{1, |V_b| exp(lambda-alpha x)}.

All remaining integration and budget steps of the raw-capped theorem apply
with H=min{K,2^n} replacing K. One can tune j from H, which is fixed by the
technology and owner set, or tune from 2^n alone for (1)-(3).
There is no profile-dependent retuning of j or deletion of zero-report owners.

2. GENERAL PRODUCTION AND RELATIVE SUPPORTING FORMS
Now let f be finite-valued, concave, coordinatewise nondecreasing and positively
homogeneous of degree one on R_+^d. Positive homogeneity and finiteness imply
f(0)=0; monotonicity then implies f>=0.

For x>=0 let J={e:x_e>0}. Its coordinate face is

 F_J={y>=0:y_e=0 for e outside J}.

If J is nonempty, x is in the relative interior of this face. The restriction
of f to its relative interior is a finite concave function on an open positive
orthant, so it has a finite relative supergradient u at x. Initially its
support inequality is on the relative interior:

 f(y)<=f(x)+u.(y_J-x_J).

Monotonicity makes every u_e nonnegative: apply this inequality to x+t e_e,
which has value at least f(x), for t>0. Homogeneity applied to y=lambda x,
first lambda>1 and then 0<lambda<1, gives Euler's identity

 u.x_J=f(x).                                             (4)

The supporting inequality extends to ALL of F_J even without boundary
continuity. For boundary y in F_J and epsilon in (0,1), put
y_epsilon=(1-epsilon)y+epsilon x, which is in the relative interior.
Concavity and the interior support inequality give

 (1-epsilon)f(y)+epsilon f(x)
   <=f(y_epsilon)
   <=u.y_epsilon=(1-epsilon)u.y+epsilon f(x).

Cancel and divide by 1-epsilon to obtain f(y)<=u.y.

There is a canonical choice of u if desired. The relative supergradient set
at x is nonempty, closed and convex. All its coordinates are nonnegative and
obey (4), so 0<=u_e<=f(x)/x_e for each e in J. It is compact. Its unique element
of minimum Euclidean norm defines a deterministic choice depending only on
f and x. This is a mathematical definition, not a promised finite-time oracle.

Define the extended nonnegative linear form

 ell_x(y) = u.y_J if supp(y) is contained in J,
            +infinity otherwise.                         (5)

For x=0 use ell_0(0)=0 and ell_0(y)=+infinity for every nonzero y.
Equivalently, the coefficients outside J are +infinity, with infinity*0=0.
This notation never requires subtracting infinities.

Every ell_x globally majorizes f and attains it at x:

 ell_x(y)>=f(y) for all y>=0,      ell_x(x)=f(x).           (6)

It is additive and coordinatewise monotone on the nonnegative cone in the
extended-value sense. On its finite face it is an ordinary finite linear form.

3. DIRECT MECHANISM FOR GENERAL f
There are n fixed owners with bundles b_i>=0. Write

 c=sum_i b_i,     T=f(c),     v_b(S)=f(sum_(i in S)b_i).

For q in (0,1) draw independent owner bits with Pr(B_i=0)=q, and let
x_B=sum_i B_i b_i. Select the form ell_(x_B) from (5), independently of q.
For a selected form ell and full profile b put

 a_i(ell,b)=min{ell(b_i),T}.                               (7)

All these numbers are finite, including when ell(b_i)=+infinity. Define

 z_i^q=E a_i(ell_(x_B),b),
 p_i^q=E[a_i(ell_(x_B),b)|B_i=0],
 m_i^q=E_(B_-i)[f(b_i+sum_(h!=i)B_h b_h)-f(sum_(h!=i)B_h b_h)],
 r_i^q=q p_i^q+(1-q)m_i^q,
 M_i=j integral_0^(1/j) r_i^q dq.                         (8)

These are finite deterministic payments. In fact 0<=a_i<=T and 0<=m_i^q<=T,
so 0<=r_i^q<=T and 0<=M_i<=T individually.

Incentives: conditional on B_i=0, x_B and its selected form are independent
of the entire own report b_i. For this fixed extended nonnegative form both
ell(b_i) and T are nondecreasing under componentwise increases of b_i. Their
minimum is nondecreasing, including a transition from finite to infinite
form value. Thus p_i^q is own-monotone. The off value in m_i^q is independent
of b_i and its on value is monotone. The mixtures in (8) preserve incentives.
At b_i=0 both contributions and the final payment are zero.

Coverage: for any form ell selected at any mask, and any coalition S, if some
ell(b_i)>=T for i in S, then that owner's capped contribution alone is T>=v_b(S).
Otherwise all coalition form values are finite and uncapped, and (6) gives

 sum_(i in S)a_i=ell(sum_(i in S)b_i)>=v_b(S).

Thus every capped vector covers all coalitions, and so does z^q.

For the endpoint inequality, fix B_-i and select ell at the ON aggregate
x=b_i+sum_(h!=i)B_h b_h. Every on owner's bundle and the off aggregate have
support contained in supp(x). Their form values are therefore FINITE and
add to ell(x)=f(x). By (6),

 f(x)-f(x-b_i)>=ell(x)-ell(x-b_i)=ell(b_i).                 (9)

Moreover ell(b_i)<=f(x)<=T. Hence this is exactly the capped on contribution.
Averaging proves r_i^q>=z_i^q; consequently M covers every coalition.
No expression in (9) contains an infinite term.

4. GENERAL BUDGET: FINITE PAYMENTS AND AT MOST 2^n FORMS
Fix b. The forms {ell_(x_B):B in {0,1}^n} constitute a set of at most 2^n
extended forms, independent of q. For each form, its normalized CAPPED
contributions d_i=a_i/T lie in [0,1] when T>0, regardless of any infinite
uncapped coefficients.

For the selected mask, every OPEN form contribution is finite and their
sum is ell_(x_B)(x_B)=f(x_B)<=T. Capping leaves them unchanged. Therefore

 sum_i B_i a_i(ell_(x_B),b)=f(x_B).                        (10)

Let g(q)=E f(x_B), Z(q)=sum_i z_i^q and R(q)=sum_i r_i^q. All coefficients
of these finite mask polynomials are finite, so their usual identities hold:

 q sum_i p_i^q=Z(q)-g(q),     sum_i m_i^q=-g'(q),
 R(q)=Z(q)-g(q)-(1-q)g'(q),
 integral_0^b0 R(q)dq
   =integral_0^b0 Z(q)dq+T-(1-b0)g(b0)-2integral_0^b0 g(q)dq
   <=integral_0^b0 Z(q)dq+T.                              (11)

The Chernoff union in Section 1 applies to the capped finite coefficients
of these at most 2^n forms. With b0=1/j and lambda=2log j it gives

 Z(q)/T<=[n log 2+2log j+1]/alpha_j  for 0<q<=1/j.

Equation (11) proves the C_j(2^n) bound. The individual bound M_i<=T proves
the minimum with n in (1). If T=0, all capped contributions are zero and
monotonicity forces all masked production values to be zero. Thus all payments
are zero and the theorem covers zero-grand-value profiles directly.

For (2), j_n~n log 2/(log n)^2, alpha_(j_n)~log n, and
j_n=o(n/log n). This proves (3). For n=1, the simpler rule M_1=f(b_1) is
efficient and own-monotone; the universal finite bound is not advertised as
optimal for small n.

5. CONTINUOUS-f COMPACTNESS FALLBACK
This provides a separate existence proof under the stronger assumption that
f is continuous on the entire closed nonnegative orthant.

Enumerate all positive rational points q_1,q_2,... in R_+^d. At each q_k use
a finite nonnegative supporting form w^k, with w^k.q_k=f(q_k). Its support
inequality holds globally by Section 2. Put

 F_m(x)=min_(1<=k<=m) w^k.x.

Then F_m>=f, F_m decreases, and F_m(x)->f(x) for EVERY x>=0. To verify the last
claim even at the boundary, choose positive rational q>=x approaching x.
The corresponding nonnegative form obeys

 f(x)<=w^q.x<=w^q.q=f(q)->f(x).

Each such q appears at a finite index in the enumeration, so the decreasing
prefix minima have the claimed limit. This uses boundary continuity; an
interior-only continuity assertion would not justify the last arrow.

Apply the finite min-linear rule to each F_m using the SAME j=j_n and the
same fixed owner set. Let M^(m) be its full all-profile payment function.
At every profile b,

 0<=M_i^(m)(b)<=F_m(sum_h b_h)<=F_1(sum_h b_h),
 sum_i M_i^(m)(b)<=C_j(2^n)F_m(sum_h b_h).

Consider the product over ALL pairs (i,b) of compact intervals

 X=product_(i,b) [0,F_1(sum_h b_h)].

By Tychonoff's theorem X is compact. The sequence of mechanisms, viewed as
a net, has a convergent subnet. Its index map is cofinal, so the original
indices tend to infinity along the subnet. Denote the simultaneous coordinatewise
limit by M. For each own-report comparison, its two coordinates converge on
this SAME subnet; every monotonicity inequality survives. Each coalition sum
and budget sum contains only finitely many owner coordinates, so coverage
and budgets also pass to the limit. The right-hand production values converge
to f at every relevant aggregate. Therefore

 M(S;b)>=v_b(S),       sum_i M_i(b)<=C_j(2^n)f(sum_i b_i).

When the limiting grand value is zero this last inequality forces all limiting
payments to zero, even if some approximating grand values were positive.

Choosing a different limit independently at each profile would not preserve
cross-profile incentives. Nor is sequential compactness of this uncountable
product available merely from Tychonoff; a countable rational-profile diagonal
argument alone does not prove the requested all-real-profile statement.
No joint measurability or algorithm follows from this compactness construction.
The direct construction in Sections 2-4 avoids the product limit and, unlike
this fallback, also covers downward discontinuities at boundary faces.

6. OPTIONAL ORIGINAL-CORE FLOORS VIA MARGINAL CONTRIBUTIONS
At profile b, put c=sum_i b_i and

 U_i(b)=f(c)-f(c-b_i)=T-f(sum_(h!=i)b_h).                   (12)

This is nonnegative, at most T, and componentwise own-monotone: its off term
is independent of the whole own report. The original core consists of efficient
vectors y with y(S)>=v_b(S) for every S. It is nonempty here: take the supporting
form ell_c from Section 2 and set y_i=ell_c(b_i). Every b_i lies in supp(c),
so these numbers are finite, nonnegative, sum to ell_c(c)=T, and cover each
coalition by (6). The core is compact, since y_i>=0 and sum_i y_i=T.

Let H_i(b) be the maximum of coordinate i over this ORIGINAL core. The coalition
constraint for I\{i} gives the classical marginal upper bound

 H_i(b)<=T-v_b(I\{i})=U_i(b).                             (13)

For a FINITE family of K ordinary nonnegative forms, choose an off minimizer
w_i for each owner. Group owners G by their chosen form w. Set A=w.c>=T.
For a nonempty group, every term is finite and

 sum_(i in G) U_i
   =|G|(T-A)+w.(sum_(i in G)b_i)
   <=|G|T-(|G|-1)A<=T.

There are at most min{K,n} nonempty groups, giving

 sum_i U_i<=min{K,n}T.                                   (14)

Do not apply this algebra with extended forms whose value at c is infinity;
it would introduce undefined infinity-minus-infinity expressions.

An independent DIMENSION bound works for every f in this note. If c has at
least one positive coordinate, define

 rho_i=max_(e:c_e>0) b_ie/c_e.

Then 0<=rho_i<=1 and c-b_i>=(1-rho_i)c. Monotonicity and homogeneity imply
U_i<=rho_i T. Also

 sum_i rho_i<=sum_(e:c_e>0)sum_i b_ie/c_e
            =|supp(c)|<=d.

If c=0 all U_i are zero. Thus in general sum_i U_i<=min{d,n}T, and for finite
K one has the combined bound min{K,d,n}T.

Consequently, in the finite-K setting adding U_i/K to the hybrid payment
protects H_i/K, retains every own-report incentive and coalition guarantee,
and adds total payment at most T. K is the fixed family/cut count, not a
report-dependent count. This preserves the prior sharp K-asymptotic leading
coefficient one. For the broader owner-count theorem, adding U_i/d analogously
protects H_i/d at cost at most T; d is fixed across reports. One can instead
use n, or any justified fixed smaller denominator with the corresponding sum
bound. No subsidized core is recomputed in any of these comparisons.

7. ESTABLISHED INGREDIENTS TO CITE; LIMITS OF THE RESULT
The following are standard ingredients, not new claims of this supplement:
 - Existence of a finite supergradient for a finite concave function at a
   relative-interior point of its domain (the subgradient existence theorem
   applied to -f; a convex-analysis text such as Rockafellar is appropriate).
 - Euler's identity for a supporting form of a degree-one homogeneous concave
   function; the elementary two-scaling proof is included above.
 - Tychonoff product compactness and the characterization of compactness by
   existence of convergent subnets; a standard general-topology reference is
   needed if the fallback is retained.
 - Core-coordinate upper bounds by grand-minus-complement coalition worth;
   this follows directly from the defining core inequality, as shown above.
 - The finite Bernoulli Chernoff and tail-integration argument from the main
   theorem; all uses here involve finite capped coefficients despite the
   extended supporting-form notation.

The relative-interior supporting theorem is documented, after identifying the
coordinate face with its own Euclidean space, in Boyd, Duchi, Pilanci and
Vandenberghe, Subgradients (Stanford EE364b notes), Section 2.1, printed page3:
 https://web.stanford.edu/class/ee364b/lectures/subgradients_notes.pdf
The coordinator directly inspected this free original. The proof above derives
the remaining support properties explicitly. A standard topology reference
is still needed if the optional compactness fallback is used in a manuscript. The note makes no claim of a new convex-analysis or topology
theorem, or of an optimal dependence on the number of owners.
The direct general mechanism is mathematically specified using relative
supporting forms. Computing those forms for an arbitrary production function,
or obtaining regularity of the whole selected payment rule, requires additional
assumptions and is not supplied by this proof.
