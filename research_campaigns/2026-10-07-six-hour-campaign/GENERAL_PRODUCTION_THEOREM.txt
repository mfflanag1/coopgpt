SHARP BUDGETS FOR FINITE NONNEGATIVE MIN-LINEAR PRODUCTION
7 October 2026. Standalone conventional proof with independent model review and exact checks.
No novelty or efficient-computation claim is made.

CORE THEOREM: OWNERS MAY REPORT ARBITRARY RESOURCE BUNDLES
Let I be a finite nonempty set of fixed owners and E a finite nonempty resource
set. Fix nonnegative finite vectors V={w^1,...,w^K} in R_+^E, K>=1. Define

 f(x)=min_(w in V) w.x  for every finite x>=0.

Each owner i reports an arbitrary finite vector b_i in R_+^E. Resource types
may be shared across owners. Supplies are pooled additively. At the full
profile b=(b_i) define

 T(b)=f(sum_i b_i),      v_b(S)=f(sum_(i in S) b_i).

Owners may simultaneously underreport any subset of their own coordinates:
the comparison is b'_i<=b_i componentwise, with other owners' reports fixed.
The owner set, resource set, permitted report coordinates, and V remain fixed.
For every integer j>=2 there is a nonnegative deterministic payment rule M
covering every coalition, satisfying

 M_i(b'_i,b_-i)<=M_i(b_i,b_-i) whenever 0<=b'_i<=b_i,

and with the same finite bound (1) and report-independent choice (2) below.
Thus the sharp leading upper coefficient remains one and depends only on K,
uniformly over owner counts, resource counts, resource bundles, and profiles.
It covers nonnegative production LPs with positive activity profits, provided
every activity consumes a positive amount of at least one resource.
It gives an individual incentive guarantee for each owner's entire vector;
it does not assert coordinated group-strategyproofness across owners.

The scalar resource-owner theorem proved in detail afterward is the special
case b_i=c_i times the ith resource unit vector. The following complete
owner-bundle proof uses no effective capacities or special ownership pattern.

0. PROOF OF THE OWNER-BUNDLE THEOREM
Fix a report-independent priority on V. For independent OWNER bits with
Pr(B_i=0)=q, select the first minimizer of the RAW score

 W(b,B)=first argmin_(w in V) sum_i B_i (w.b_i).

For every fixed form w put a_i(w,b)=min{w.b_i,T(b)}. Define

 z_i^q=E[a_i(W,b)],
 p_i^q=E[a_i(W,b)|B_i=0],
 m_i^q=E_(B_-i)[f(b_i+sum_(h!=i)B_h b_h)-f(sum_(h!=i)B_h b_h)],
 r_i^q=q p_i^q+(1-q)m_i^q,
 M_i=j integral_0^(1/j) r_i^q dq.                         (V1)

Selection is uncapped; only a_i is capped. Every expectation here is finite.

Incentives: with B_i=0 every candidate raw score omits b_i, so the entire
minimizing family and the chosen W are independent of the whole report b_i.
For fixed W, both W.b_i and T(b) are componentwise nondecreasing because all
forms are nonnegative. Hence p_i^q is componentwise nondecreasing. In m_i^q,
the subtracted off value is independent of b_i and the on value is monotone.
The nonnegative report-independent mixtures r_i^q and M_i inherit this.

Coverage: for any w and S, if some w.b_i>=T for i in S, then a_i=T>=v_b(S).
Otherwise every S contribution is uncapped and
sum_(i in S)a_i=w.(sum_(i in S)b_i)>=v_b(S).
Thus every a(w,b) covers every coalition, and so does z^q. For each fixed
B_-i, let w1 be the raw minimizer with B_i=1. Its on value is its selected
linear score, while the off value is at most the same form's off score.
Subtracting gives

 f(b_i+sum_(h!=i)B_h b_h)-f(sum_(h!=i)B_h b_h)>=w1.b_i.

Moreover w1.b_i<=the selected on value<=T, so this equals a_i(w1,b).
Averaging proves r_i^q>=z_i^q. Therefore r^q and M cover every coalition.

Budget: fix b. Write g(q)=E f(sum_i B_i b_i), Z(q)=sum_i z_i^q and R(q)=sum_i r_i^q.
All are finite polynomials in q, and g(0)=T. Every open raw owner contribution
is a nonnegative summand of the selected value, which is at most T. Hence

 sum_i B_i a_i(W,b)=f(sum_i B_i b_i),
 q sum_i p_i^q=Z(q)-g(q),     sum_i m_i^q=-g'(q).

These give R=Z-g-(1-q)g' and, for b0 in (0,1),

 integral_0^b0 R(q)dq
   =integral_0^b0 Z(q)dq+T-(1-b0)g(b0)-2integral_0^b0 g(q)dq
   <=integral_0^b0 Z(q)dq+T.                              (V2)

For T>0 and each FIXED form define d_i(w)=a_i(w,b)/T in [0,1] and
rho_w=sum_i d_i(w). The selected form has sum_i B_i d_i(W)<=1.
For lambda>0, alpha=-log(q+(1-q)e^(-lambda)), convexity of
a -> log(q+(1-q)e^(-lambda a)) on [0,1] gives

 Pr(sum_i d_i(w)B_i<=1)<=exp(lambda-alpha rho_w).

A union over K forms and tail integration yield

 Z(q)/T<= [log K+lambda+1]/alpha.                         (V3)

Taking b0=1/j and lambda=2log j gives uniformly for 0<q<=b0
alpha>=log[j^3/(j^2+j-1)]. Combine (V2) and (V3) to obtain (1).
The choice (2) and the asymptotic calculation in Section 7 are unchanged.
When T=0, every capped a_i and every masked production value is zero, so all
payments vanish. If K=1, no owner contribution is capped, every payment is
w.b_i, and their total is T.

The mixture has an exact finite representation: for each subset S of the
other owners, its contribution is q^(n-1-|S|)(1-q)^|S| times
[q a_i(W(b,(1_S,0)),b)+(1-q)(v_b(S union {i})-v_b(S))].
This is a polynomial of degree at most n=|I|. Integration to 1/j is exact
with rational coefficients. For rational technologies and bundles the payment
is rational. No complexity bound or numerical integration is presumed.
This completes the owner-bundle proof.

SCALAR COROLLARY AND DETAILED ESTIMATES
Let E be a finite nonempty owner set. Fix a finite nonempty indexed family
V={w^1,...,w^K} of finite nonnegative vectors in R_+^E. For every finite
nonnegative report vector c define

 F(c)=min_(w in V) c.w,       v_c(S)=F(c*1_S),       T=F(c).

For every fixed integer j>=2 there is a nonnegative deterministic payment
rule, defined on ALL such c, which is nondecreasing in each owner's own report,
covers every coalition, and has total payment at most

 T [ j + (log K + 2 log j + 1)/alpha_j ],                    (1)
 alpha_j=log(j^3/(j^2+j-1))>0.

With L=log K and the report-independent choice

 j(K)=max{2,ceil[L/(log(2+L))^2]},                          (2)

the bound is (1+o(1)) T log K/log log K as K tends to infinity.
The error depends only on K, uniformly over vectors, reports, and owner counts.
Every payment is zero when T=0. If K=1 the constructed rule actually has
total payment exactly T, although the displayed universal finite bound is looser.

The vectors need not be dual vertices or integral. K counts the chosen fixed
family; one may discard redundant forms ONCE to tighten this complexity.
One must not redefine K or the family from the current report profile.
F has no additive offsets: F(0)=0 and positive homogeneity follow from its
definition. The theorem does not silently cover fixed production constraints,
free-production offsets, or nonlinear production values outside this model.

1. RULE: RAW SELECTION, CAPPED PAYMENT CONTRIBUTIONS
Fix a total priority on V independent of reports and all random choices.
For q in (0,1), independently draw B_e with Pr(B_e=0)=q, Pr(B_e=1)=1-q.
Select the first RAW-objective minimizer

 w*(c,B)=first argmin_(w in V) sum_e c_e B_e w_e.           (3)

For each form define its payment contribution

 a_e(w,c)=min{c_e w_e,T(c)}.                               (4)

Only the contributions in (4) are capped. The selection in (3) is ALWAYS
by the uncapped raw objective. Selecting by the capped objective is a
different rule and is not justified by this proof. No effective-capacity
normalization, path-flow interpretation, or strict-support lemma is used.

Define deterministic payments by finite expectations:

 z_e^q(c)=E_q[a_e(w*(c,B),c)],
 p_e^q(c)=E_q[a_e(w*(c,B),c) | B_e=0],                    (5)
 m_e^q(c)=E_(B_-e)[F(c*(B_-e,1))-F(c*(B_-e,0))],          (6)
 r_e^q(c)=q p_e^q(c)+(1-q)m_e^q(c).                        (7)

Here (B_-e,b_e) denotes the full mask with the specified own bit. The raw
own report c_e is retained when b_e=1 and replaced by zero when b_e=0.
Finally fix an integer j>=2 from the technology alone and put

 M_e(c)=j integral_0^(1/j) r_e^q(c)dq.                     (8)

Thus the final payment is a deterministic expectation over a common q drawn
uniformly from (0,1/j), then over the masks used in the displayed formulas.
No realized-random coverage guarantee is claimed or needed.

2. NONNEGATIVITY AND ALL-PROFILE OWN-REPORT MONOTONICITY
Every vector w is nonnegative, so F is nonnegative and coordinatewise
nondecreasing. The contributions a and the endpoint differences m are
therefore nonnegative.

Fix e, c_-e, and a mask of the other owners. With B_e=0, EVERY raw score in
(3) is independent of c_e. Hence the entire minimizing family and the
fixed-priority selected w* are independent of c_e, including at c_e=0.
For this fixed selected vector, both c_e w*_e and T(c) are nondecreasing
in c_e. Their minimum is nondecreasing. Taking conditional expectation proves
that p_e^q is own-report nondecreasing on [0,infinity).

In (6), the off value is independent of c_e and the on value is nondecreasing
in c_e. Thus m_e^q is own-monotone. Equations (7) and (8) preserve the property
because all mixture weights are nonnegative and report-independent.
Under binding underreports and payment-only utility, this is the required
incentive guarantee. The owner-bundle theorem above establishes the corresponding
simultaneous componentwise guarantee for each owner's multiple coordinates;
neither statement asserts group-strategyproofness across owners.

3. CAPPED FORMS STILL COVER EVERY COALITION
Fix c, w in V, and S subset E. If some e in S has c_e w_e>=T, then its
capped contribution is T, and by monotonicity

 sum_(e in S) a_e(w,c) >= T >= F(c*1_S).

If no such e exists, none of S's contributions are capped and

 sum_(e in S) a_e(w,c)=sum_(e in S)c_e w_e >= F(c*1_S),

by the definition of F as a minimum of the forms. Thus EVERY capped vector
a(w,c) covers every coalition. In particular z^q(S)>=v_c(S).
This argument also covers T=0 and S empty, since F(0)=0.

4. ENDPOINT MARGINALS DOMINATE THE ON CONTRIBUTIONS
Fix c and B_-e, and let w1 be the vector selected when B_e=1. Its selected
raw objective is

 F(c*(B_-e,1))=sum_(h!=e)c_h B_h w1_h+c_e w1_e.

The same vector is an admissible competitor for the off value, so

 F(c*(B_-e,0))<=sum_(h!=e)c_h B_h w1_h.

Subtracting gives the exact endpoint inequality

 F(c*(B_-e,1))-F(c*(B_-e,0))>=c_e w1_e.                   (9)

In particular the right side is at least a_e(w1,c). In fact they are equal:
the selected masked value is at most F(c)=T by monotonicity, and all of its
summands are nonnegative, so c_e w1_e<=T whenever B_e=1.

Conditioning (5) on the own bit and averaging (9) therefore yields

 r_e^q(c)>=q E[a_e(w*,c)|B_e=0]
             +(1-q)E[a_e(w*,c)|B_e=1]
           =z_e^q(c).                                    (10)

By Section 3, r^q and then M cover every coalition. No derivative, generic
position, or special tie-breaking assumption is needed for (9).

5. EXACT AGGREGATE IDENTITY AND INTEGRATION
Fix c and define

 g(q)=E_q F(c*B),       Z(q)=sum_e z_e^q(c),
 R(q)=sum_e r_e^q(c).

All of these are finite polynomials in q. The conditional p expectation is
a finite sum over the other bits, so it also extends polynomially to q=0,1.
In particular g(0)=T, g(1)=0, and g(q)>=0.

For every selected mask, each OPEN raw contribution c_e B_e w*_e is between
zero and the selected total F(c*B)<=T. Capping leaves every open term
unchanged. Therefore

 sum_e B_e a_e(w*(c,B),c)=F(c*B).                          (11)

Taking expectation of the complementary, closed terms gives

 q sum_e p_e^q(c)=Z(q)-g(q).                              (12)

Differentiating the finite multiaffine mask expectation in the common zero
probability q gives one conditional off-minus-on value for each coordinate:

 g'(q)=-sum_e m_e^q(c).                                   (13)

This is a finite polynomial identity, not an interchange of an infinite
series or a limit with an expectation. Consequently

 R(q)=Z(q)-g(q)-(1-q)g'(q).                               (14)

For any b in (0,1), direct integration by parts gives

 integral_0^b R(q)dq
   = integral_0^b Z(q)dq+T-(1-b)g(b)-2 integral_0^b g(q)dq
   <= integral_0^b Z(q)dq+T.                             (15)

Both the minus sign and the factor 2 in this equality are essential.

6. CHERNOFF BOUND FOR THE CAPPED CONTRIBUTIONS
Suppose T>0. For each fixed w let

 d_e(w)=a_e(w,c)/T in [0,1],        rho_w=sum_e d_e(w).

Although w* was selected using RAW scores, equation (11) shows that its
CAPPED normalized masked score satisfies

 sum_e d_e(w*)B_e=F(c*B)/T<=1.                            (16)

For lambda>0 define alpha(q,lambda)=-log[q+(1-q)e^(-lambda)]>0.
Convexity of a -> log[q+(1-q)e^(-lambda a)] on [0,1] bounds it above
by its endpoint chord -alpha(q,lambda)*a. Hence for every fixed vector w,
independence and Markov's inequality imply

 Pr_q(sum_e d_e(w)B_e<=1)
   <=e^lambda product_e [q+(1-q)e^(-lambda d_e(w))]
   <=exp(lambda-alpha(q,lambda)rho_w).                   (17)

For x>=0, if rho_(w*)>=x, some form with rho_w>=x satisfies the event in
(17). A union over at most K forms and tail integration give

 Pr_q(rho_(w*)>=x)<=min{1,K exp(lambda-alpha(q,lambda)x)},
 Z(q)/T=E_q rho_(w*)<=(log K+lambda+1)/alpha(q,lambda).    (18)

The proof requires only that the selected form satisfies (16); it does NOT
require selection to minimize the capped score. The capped coefficients
are fixed after c is fixed, so independence of the bits in (17) is valid.

Set b=1/j and lambda=2log j. For 0<q<=b,

 q+(1-q)e^(-lambda)<=b+(1-b)b^2=(j^2+j-1)/j^3,

so alpha(q,lambda)>=alpha_j and (18) holds uniformly with denominator alpha_j.
Combining this with (8) and (15) proves the finite bound (1).

7. EDGE CASES, PARAMETER CHOICE, AND FINITE REPRESENTATION
If T=0, every a_e is zero. Every masked profile is at most c, so all masked
production values and all endpoint differences are zero. Thus all payments
are exactly zero, without dividing by T.

If K=1 with form w, F(c)=c.w. Every c_e w_e<=T, so no contribution is capped.
Each p_e^q and m_e^q equals c_e w_e, as do r_e^q and M_e. Total payment is T.

The choice (2) is defined for every K>=1 and never uses log log K at small K.
For L=log K tending to infinity,

 j~L/(log L)^2,
 log j=log L-2log log L+o(1),
 alpha_j=log j-log(1+1/j-1/j^2)~log L.

Thus j=o(L/log L), while the fraction in (1) is (1+o(1))L/log L. This proves
the stated asymptotic bound with leading coefficient one.

For a subset S of E\{e}, put

 u_e(S)=a_e(w*(c,(1_S,0)),c),
 delta_e(S)=F(c*(1_S,1))-F(c*(1_S,0)).

Then r_e^q is the finite polynomial

 sum_(S subset E\{e}) q^(n-1-|S|)(1-q)^|S|
                  [q u_e(S)+(1-q)delta_e(S)],             (19)

where n=|E|. Its degree is at most n. With j integer, integration in (8)
has exact rational coefficients; rational vectors and reports give rational
payments. For integer A,B>=0 and b=1/j,

 integral_0^b q^A(1-q)^B dq
   =sum_(h=0)^B (-1)^h binom(B,h)b^(A+h+1)/(A+h+1).

This establishes an exact finite representation, not computational efficiency.
The number of forms and masks may be exponential.

8. GENERAL NONNEGATIVE RESOURCE TECHNOLOGIES AND POSITIVE PROFITS
Let A be a fixed finite nonnegative n-by-m matrix, with every activity column
nonzero, and let pi_j>0 be fixed finite activity profits. At aggregate resource
capacity c define

 F(c)=max {pi.x : x>=0, A x<=c}.

For each activity j choose a resource e with A_ej>0; feasibility bounds
x_j<=c_e/A_ej. The feasible packing polytope is thus compact for every finite
c, so production is finite and attained. Its dual is

 Q={w>=0 : A^T w>=pi}.

Q is nonempty (a sufficiently large multiple of the all-ones vector works),
pointed, and polyhedral. A bounded nonnegative linear objective attains an
optimum at a vertex. By strong duality,

 F(c)=min_(w vertex of Q) c.w.

There are finitely many such vertices, all nonnegative. Let any finite set
of fixed owners report resource bundles b_i>=0 and take c=sum_i b_i; coalition
S controls exactly sum_(i in S)b_i. Apply the owner-bundle theorem with this
fixed vertex family, or any fixed irredundant subfamily representing the same
production function. Neither A's positive entries nor
the profits need obey a common ratio bound. No bound on an uncapped product
c_e w_e or D_e w_e is assumed. Zero resource rows are harmless. An empty
activity set gives the identically zero production function.

No owner, resource, or activity identities change when an owner changes its
report. The theorem permits arbitrary real nonnegative coefficients, positive
profits, shared resource types, and owners with multiple resource coordinates
under componentwise binding underreports. It does not cover positive-profit
zero-resource activities (which would be unbounded), fixed capacity-independent
production restrictions that introduce dual offsets, or report comparisons
that exchange one resource for another rather than decrease componentwise.

For directed single-commodity flow, the dual vertices are the characteristic
vectors of minimal structural edge cuts. In that special case K is the usual
structural cut count. For general production or multicommodity packing these
vertices are fractional resource prices, and K is NOT an ordinary graph-cut count.

9. WHY CAPPING IS NECESSARY FOR THE UNMODIFIED HYBRID
The following counterfamily diagnoses the earlier uncapped construction;
it does not obstruct the new capped rule or all possible payment mechanisms.

There are two fixed resource owners and two unit-profit activities with
resource-use columns (1,1) and (M,0), where M>1. The dual constraints are

 w1+w2>=1,   M w1>=1,   w>=0.

There are exactly TWO dual vertices

 a=(1,0),     b=(1/M,1-1/M).

At capacities c=(M,1), production is T=2-1/M and effective usages are D=c.
When B1=0 and B2=1, a is the UNIQUE minimizing form, so its uncapped
resource-1 contribution is M. This event among the conditional off masks
has probability 1-q. Therefore, for every fixed tie priority,

 p1_uncapped^q >= (1-q)M,
 r1_uncapped^q >= q(1-q)M.

The uniform-q mixed rule on (0,b0) consequently pays at least

 M (b0/2-b0^2/3).

The earlier K=2 choice j=2 has b0=1/2, giving total at least M/6 and a
payment/value ratio at least M/[6(2-1/M)], which diverges at fixed K=2 and
fixed owner count 2. No row-rescaling of units removes this example's
relative activity-intensity ratio M. The cap (4) replaces the problematic
closed contribution M by T.

This is strictly a mechanism obstruction. Indeed the same technology has

 F(c1,c2)=c1/M+(1-1/M)min(c1,c2).

The payments

 y1=c1/M+(1-1/M)min(c1,c2)/2,
 y2=(1-1/M)min(c1,c2)/2

are own-monotone, cover its two singleton coalitions and the grand coalition,
and total exactly F. Thus the example itself admits a truthful core rule.

10. BENCHMARK AND AUDIT SCOPE
The existing coefficient-one unrestricted serial-pool lower bound transfers
to this class with exactly K=k: k serial bundles of parallel owned edges have
one minimal structural cut per bundle and represent the same all-profile
coalition game. The imported project result is in
paper5/truthful_cooperation/manuscript.tex (relative to the repository root),
Lemma lem:weighted-hierarchy and Theorem thm:order. Combining that lower with
the upper proved here yields a worst-case leading coefficient one for finite
nonnegative min-linear production when complexity is K and arbitrary finite
ownership sizes are allowed. The lower theorem is not re-proved in this file.

SIMPLE_DAG_SHARP_LOWER.txt separately proves a matching simple-DAG lower
using shared fans and explicitly accounts for all auxiliary strategic owners.
Naive parallel-edge subdivision would be invalid: it changes the owner set
and can make truthful efficient allocation possible.
There is no asserted exact finite-K minimax formula or efficient evaluation.

The new proof uses raw selection throughout, so none of its incentives,
coverage, or budget steps depend on effective-usage preservation. It extends
the previous hybrid integration argument by capping each form's individual
payment contributions at the same grand-coalition value T(c). Independent
Fable proof audit and coordinator checks found no gap in this upper theorem;
see review/general_production_{review.md,adjudication.txt}. The dedicated
exact verifier passed under python3 -B -O, including overlapping owner bundles,
heterogeneous coefficients/profits, polynomial identities and negative controls;
see verification/general_production_result.json. These are conventional proof
and bounded computational evidence, not formal verification or novelty confirmation.
