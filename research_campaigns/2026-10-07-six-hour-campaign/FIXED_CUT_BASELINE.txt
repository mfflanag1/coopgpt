ELEMENTARY FIXED-CUT BASELINE AND THE SCOPE OF THE ENVELOPE OBSTRUCTION
7 October2026. Independently checked by coordinator and Codex reviewer.
This is an elementary baseline, not a novelty claim.

Fix any structural s-t cut C using only the public topology. For a report
profile c with grand maxflow T(c), pay
 p_e(c)=1_{e in C} min(c_e,T(c)).
Both arguments of the minimum are nondecreasing in the owner's own report,
so every p_e is own-report nondecreasing on all nonnegative real profiles.
Payments are nonnegative. For any coalition S, the fixed cut remains a cut
in its subgraph, so v_c(S)<=sum_{e in C intersect S}c_e and v_c(S)<=T(c).
For nonnegative numbers a_i and T,
 sum_i min(a_i,T)>=min(sum_i a_i,T): if some a_i>=T the left is >=T;
otherwise the left is sum_i a_i. Consequently
 sum_{e in S}p_e >= min(sum_{e in C intersect S}c_e,T(c)) >= v_c(S).
Total payment is at most |C|T(c). Choosing a minimum-cardinality structural
cut gives a truthful coalition-covering mechanism with factor kappa(G).
No minimum-capacity cut is chosen as reports change. That would be a
different rule and would invalidate this simple incentive proof.

The aggregate-envelope obstruction family has a fixed final cut with100000
owners; the simple-DAG version has a fixed cut with2000 owners. Thus these
graphs admit all-profile truthful coverage with factors100000 and2000,
respectively, while the particular envelope's normalized budget grows without
limit. The general fixed-noise-law extension has the same separation with
its law-dependent, graph-independent final cut size M.
The Fibonacci graph has a source cut of size2 and hence an all-profile
factor2 rule. A sharper analysis of the proposed envelope on special
Fibonacci profiles would therefore concern that rule, not improve the best
known general mechanism on that topology.

This observation does not give a K-only bound: kappa can grow independently
of the number K of structural minimal cuts (a single large parallel bundle
has K=1). It also does not justify selecting the best of this rule and a
second rule using reports; report-dependent switching needs a fresh incentive
argument. A fixed, topology-based choice among certified bounds is valid.
