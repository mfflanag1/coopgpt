FRESHLY INSPECTED

1. Arin, Kuipers, Vermeulen (2008), An axiomatic approach to egalitarianism
in TU-games, IJGT 37:565–580. Open-access publisher original:
https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1007/s00182-008-0133-6.pdf
Read Definition 1, printed p569, and the immediately following geometric
interpretation; continuity and IIC discussion on p571. LS is the minimum-Euclidean-
norm efficient core allocation. Since its sum is fixed, this also minimizes
distance to equal division. Adjudication: exact C=1,r=2 baseline is classical.
Neither that definition nor the inspected axioms supplies our cross-report
aggregate funding lower bound. Calling the selection itself novel is incorrect.

2. Dietzenbacher and Dogan (2024), Population monotonicity and egalitarianism,
RM/24/007, version dated 29 April 2024:
https://cris.maastrichtuniversity.nl/ws/files/194209657/RM24007.pdf
Read population-monotonicity model, Example 3 (printed pp7–8), Lemma 3.2 and
proof (p10), Theorem 3.1 (p11). Population monotonicity compares subgames as
players enter. Their examples already expose core/monotonicity incompatibility;
the uniqueness proof explicitly lower-bounds a payoff by maxima of subgame
payoffs. Our fixed owners, actual bundle changes, and quantitative total-funding
growth differ. Adjudication: close conceptual precedent; elementary selector
obstruction must not be framed as a new qualitative incompatibility. The
quantitative all-cap construction is not a statement of their Theorem 3.1.
Only this free working version was compared here, not the final 2025 journal text.

3. Gong and Dietzenbacher (2026), Core bound reduced games and consistency,
IJGT 55, article 13, published 2 March 2026:
https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00182-026-00986-2
Read full-text definitions of pre-imputations and reduced games, Definition 1,
Lemma 3, Theorem 1 and proof, Definitions 2–4. Their lower/upper individual bounds
are extrema within one efficient core. Reduced games change the population and
reallocate a fixed sum. Our floor is a selected allocation schedule evaluated
at different owner reports; own-envelope maxima range across those reports.
Adjudication: distinct operation and contract. Do not confuse their term 'core
cover' with the upward coalition-cover polyhedron used in this draft.

4. Gonzalez-Sanz, Nutz, Riveros Valdevenito (2025), Monotonicity in
Quadratically Regularized Linear Programs, author version 16 April 2025:
https://www.math.columbia.edu/~mnutz/docs/GNR24.pdf
Read formulation and introduction pp1–2, Theorem 3.2 p7. Their parameter is
quadratic regularization on a fixed polytope; monotonicity concerns the path's
face structure. Our coalition right-hand sides change when an owner underreports,
while objective exponent and cap are fixed. Adjudication: relevant projection
sensitivity context, not an identified theorem giving the envelope funding bound.
No broad claim that all parametric quadratic-program literature was exhausted.

5. Laplace Mermoud (2024), Projection onto the core: An optimal reallocation
to correct market failure, arXiv:2411.11810v1:
https://arxiv.org/pdf/2411.11810
Read Section 2's efficiency hyperplane X(v), Section 4's Euclidean reallocation
problem, and Theorem 8's affine-face projection. Their correction reallocates
within a fixed total; transfers need not be nonnegative. Our correction preserves
each baseline coordinate at every report and can require extra funding.
Adjudication: related static projection, different optimization problem.

6. Bochet, Ilkilic, Moulin (2013), Egalitarianism under earmark constraints,
JET 148:535–562, free author/institutional copy:
https://yoksis.bilkent.edu.tr/pdf/files/10.1016-j.jet.2012.09.016.pdf
Read model, Proposition 1 around p544, and resource monotonicity discussion
pp560–561. This is physical allocation on a supplier/consumer network with
single-peaked preferences; resource monotonicity changes supplier supply.
Strategyproof preference reports are a different contract from own bundle
withholding and funded payments. The resource-monotonicity proof there is
omitted and referred to companion work; only the statement was inspected.
Adjudication: useful positive precedent, not a direct contradiction or subsumption.

7. Guo and Shmaya, The Interval Structure of Optimal Disclosure, author PDF:
https://yingniguo.com/wp-content/uploads/2018/12/interval-5-4-paper.pdf
Read introduction and Lemma 6.2 proof, printed pp22–23. It uses a least monotone
majorant of an interval endpoint in a disclosure mechanism. Adjudication:
monotone-majorant technique is established; the endpoint, incentives and
objective here differ. No priority claim is made for the elementary envelope
lemma, so this distant application need not be treated as the source of our
specific model. Publication metadata was not independently resolved here.

CURRENT PRIORITY JUDGMENT
The novel-looking ingredients reduce to classical selections, projection facts,
order closure, and elementary report-realization devices. The proposed contribution
is quantitative: fixed owners, a common technology, all-real unilateral reports,
and lower funding growth for every fixed cap and power, plus the separate uncapped
quadratic sequence. None of the inspected theorem passages states those bounds.
This observation does not certify historical novelty or the difficulty/significance
of the theorem. The closest structural precedent is explicitly retained above.
