SOURCE AND PRIORITY REVIEW — 21 OCTOBER 2026
Status: scoped comparison, not novelty confirmation. Free sources only.

WHAT MUST BE COMPARED
The proposed contribution is a fixed-owner, all-real-report production example
whose coordinatewise own-report envelope of the nonnegative coalition-cover
minimum-norm point violates the squared diagonal estimate. The final proposed step is stronger: a q-ary family whose true envelope
has no universal constant-times-sqrt(N) funding bound, with a quantitative
subsequence lower bound sqrt(N)*(log N/log log N)^(1/3). The hypercube
coefficient1 and its class upper theorem are intermediate results. Projection, KKT, stable-set polytopes, minimum-of-
additive representations, and common-veto games are established ingredients.
Do not claim that this campaign invented any of those ingredients.

1. QUADRATIC PROJECTION MONOTONICITY
Alberto Gonzalez-Sanz, Marcel Nutz, Andres Riveros Valdevenito,
Monotonicity in Quadratically Regularized Linear Programs (16 April 2025).
Free author original: https://www.math.columbia.edu/~mnutz/docs/GNR24.pdf
Read: Theorem 3.2 and the Birkhoff-polytope example around Lemma 4.9.
Their parameter changes quadratic regularization on a fixed feasible polytope.
Our parameter changes owner-incident right-hand sides while leaving all other
coalitions fixed. Thus their face-monotonicity result does not directly imply
our diagonal assertion or its failure. Their star-swap face inspired a tested
family, but that family has normalized mass H=1 in our formulation. This is an
explicit comparison of operations, not an assertion of independence from all
their methods. Projection geometry and KKT certification are classical.

2. CONGESTION AND MATROID MONOTONICITY
Satoru Fujishige, Michel X. Goemans, Tobias Harks, Britta Peis, Rico Zenklusen,
Matroids are Immune to Braess Paradox (original inspected, 11 January 2017).
https://arxiv.org/pdf/1504.07545
Read: Lemma 3.2 and its resource-cost sensitivity setting.
That result concerns separable resource-cost changes. Selectively penalizing
entire routes/bases containing a particular owner is a different operation.
The normalized dual formulation can be expressed through a quadratic potential
and path penalties, but this alone does not transfer their theorem. An exact
graphic-matroid example in this campaign has H>1, refuting a tempting overly
broad normalized-mass extension. It does not refute their result or establish
failure of a raw-payment or diagonal bound on matroid bases.

3. STABLE-SET GEOMETRY AND MOBIUS LADDERS
Henning Bruhn and Elke Fuchs, t-Perfection in P5-free graphs.
Author original: https://www.uni-ulm.de/fileadmin/website_uni_ulm/mawi.inst.081/Elke/tppfive.pdf
Read: definitions and Section 3 on near-bipartite graphs and prime antiwebs.
This source identifies even Mobius ladders as a known graph family among
prime antiwebs and discusses established stable-set-polytope obstructions.
The graph itself and its basic independent-set geometry are therefore not
new. The campaign's four-pattern, equal-weight local marginal certificate is
used to certify a particular owner-incident quadratic projection. No claim
is made that its combinatorial identities lack antecedents. A specialist
comparison with stable-set and fractional-coloring literature remains open.

4. PROJECTION ONTO AN EFFICIENT CORE
Dylan Laplace Mermoud, Projection onto the core: An optimal reallocation to
correct market failure (arXiv:2411.11810v1, November 2024).
https://arxiv.org/pdf/2411.11810
Read: Introduction and Definitions in Section 2, including X(v) and C(v).
This paper projects a given preimputation onto the core inside the fixed
efficiency hyperplane x(N)=v(N). Our q minimizes norm over the nonnegative
cover polyhedron without that equality and may spend more than v(N). Our
envelope subsequently combines separate own-report extrema. These differences
prevent treating a generic core-projection theorem as the desired diagonal
bound. Further theorem-level comparison may still be useful; we have not
exhaustively checked all algorithms and sensitivity consequences in the paper.

7. MINIMUM-NORM WINNING-COALITION COVERS ARE ESTABLISHED
Frits Hof, Walter Kern, Sascha Kurz, Kanstantsin Pashkovich, Daniël Paulusma,
Simple Games versus Weighted Voting Games: Bounding the Critical Threshold
Value (2018 original preprint), https://arxiv.org/pdf/1810.08841 .
Read: the minimum-norm cover argument in Theorem3 and the equality discussion
in Theorem4. This is the same static quadratic projection of the nonnegative
winning-coalition cover polyhedron. The projection variational identity is
therefore a directly relevant established ingredient, not a new method from
this campaign. Their theorem controls a static critical-threshold quantity;
it does not state our cross-profile own-report envelope bound. The new step
requiring priority assessment is the simultaneous incident-report construction,
its aggregation, and exact transfer to a fixed-owner production function.
The theorem alone neither proves nor refutes those steps. This comparison is
scoped to the read statements, not a claim that all follow-up literature is clear.

8. GAUGE DUALITY AND IDEAL CLUTTERS — ORIGINAL NOW ACCESSED
Ahmad Abdi, Gerard Cornuejols, Dabeen Lee, Identically self-blocking clutters,
author version4February2019: https://www.ahmadabdi.com/papers/ISBC.pdf .
Read: Section2, Lemma7; Section3 including Theorem12; Section4, Theorem13.
Lemma7 explicitly supplies the full-unit KKT representation and identity
sum lambda=||q||^2 used here. Gauge duality and reciprocal squared norms
of blocking polyhedra are established prior tools. Theorem13 states that
an ideal clutter with no common element has n*rho>=4, with a structured
cuboid equality case. Our hypercube complements have no common element
and n*rho<4, so nonideality follows (an inference from their hypotheses).
This locates the example relative to known cover geometry; it is not an
own-report-envelope theorem. Their statements do not couple the different
owner-specific relaxations used in our diagonal. No claim of novelty is
made for normalized row-hull duality or the static mass identity.
The LSE mirror failed earlier; the author-hosted original was successfully
read. This source comparison is now complete only for the listed statements.

NOVELTY VERDICT
The selected diagonal conjecture and the stronger constant-times-sqrt(N)
funding route are refuted by the internally audited construction. This is
a precise architecture-specific mathematical advance, not an unrestricted
mechanism lower bound or a claimed resolution of a recognized open problem.
Neither search non-hits nor cross-model review establish publication novelty.
Open priority tasks: the special local-marginal identity; own-report projection
envelopes under withholding; simultaneous fixed-owner min-linear realization;
and any existing leading-constant examples for this exact architecture.

9. STATIC COST OF STABILITY — ORIGINAL PREPRINT INSPECTED
Yoram Bachrach, Edith Elkind, Reshef Meir, Dmitrii Pasechnik, Michael
Zuckerman, Jorg Rothe, Jeffrey S.Rosenschein, The Cost of Stability in
Coalitional Games (2009): https://arxiv.org/pdf/0907.4385v1 .
Read: Definitions3.1–3.2, Theorem2.4, Theorem3.5 and its LP-dual proof.
Theorem3.5 bounds the minimum STATIC total cover by sqrt(n) times grand
value in superadditive games. It does not select one own-report-monotone
rule across profiles. Our examples already have zero static subsidy
requirement, with a common veto and an efficient supporting allocation;
large payments arise from the specified quadratic-envelope architecture.
Thus the current result neither contradicts nor strengthens that static
minimum-funding theorem. Its veto-core characterization is an established
ingredient, not a new insight here. The2018 JAIR journal landing page was
also inspected, but the PDF viewer supplied no full text in this fetch;
this campaign's theorem-level comparison is to the2009 original.
Journal record: https://doi.org/10.1613/jair.1.11270 .
