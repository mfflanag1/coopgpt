1. WHAT THE SOURCE PROOF ACTUALLY USES

Write g(v) for its unadjusted claims, before the final efficient adjustment.
The source states for EVERY TU game that g covers every coalition, and for
each i there is a tight coalition T_i containing i with
 g(T_i)=v(T_i), and g_j>=g_i for every j in T_i.              (1)

Suppose only coalition S's worth increases: v'(S)>=v(S), with every other
worth unchanged. Run the claims-freezing procedure for v'. Until S becomes
admissible, inductively all frozen claims equal the corresponding g(v).
At the next stage, for any newly frozen player i use its old witness T_i.
After subtracting the already frozen old claims, the average of remaining
old claims in T_i is >=g_i; increasing its worth cannot lower this average.
Admissibility therefore makes the new claim of i at least g_i.

Every newly admissible coalition U other than S has its old worth. Its
new claims sum to v'(U)=v(U), while old coverage gives g(U)>=v(U). Since
each of its members' new claims is >= the old one, all these inequalities
must be equalities. Thus all newly frozen claims remain unchanged unless
S itself becomes admissible. If S does become admissible, its members'
claims are all >= their old claims and remain fixed thereafter. If S never
becomes admissible, all claims remain unchanged through termination.

This proves g_i(v')>=g_i(v) for every i in S. No efficiency, nonempty core,
egalitarian stability, monotonicity of v, or superadditivity of v entered
the argument. The final source step PES(v)=g(v) is where stability enters.

2. IDENTIFICATION WITH UNWEIGHTED COVER LEX

For a nonnegative game, let Q(v)={x>=0:x(S)>=v(S) for every S}. The claims
vector is the unique lexicographic minimizer of the descending coordinate
list on Q(v), without an efficiency equality. One way to identify it is
the source's certificate (1). Each decrease of a coordinate requires an
increase in a coordinate already at least as high, by its tight coalition.
Taking the first changed highest stratum rules out a lexicographic
improvement. Conversely, the successive maximum-residual-average cover
algorithm provides precisely these tight witnesses and this unique vector.

Equivalently, the source's local admissible-coalition freezes can be ordered
by decreasing freezing level to obtain the usual global cover-lex freezes.
This identification is about the UNADJUSTED claims, not PES or Lmax inside
the efficient core. On nonstable games they differ, as the source's own
Example3 demonstrates: claims(2,2,2), PES(1,1,2), efficient-core Lmax(0,0,4).

Increasing finitely many coalition demands all containing i can be done
one at a time. The extracted all-game result then makes coordinate i
nondecreasing throughout. This is the exact incident-RHS property needed
for a fixed-owner production report increase; intermediate games need not
themselves arise from production because the claim theorem holds on all
nonnegative games.

3. EXACT RATIONAL-WEIGHT CLONING

Fix positive rational weights w_i and choose a common positive scale a
so m_i=a w_i are positive integers. Replace i by a block I_i of m_i clones.
Define a game on the expanded player set by
 vhat(union_{i in S} I_i)=v(S),
 vhat(A)=0 for every A that is not a union of whole blocks.
The empty coalition also has value zero.

Every clone singleton has nonnegative worth, so the expanded cover is
nonnegative. Partial-block zero constraints add no restriction beyond
nonnegativity. Permutations within each block preserve the game and the
claims algorithm; hence expanded claims equal some y_i within I_i. Put
x_i=m_i y_i. Whole-block constraints say exactly x(S)>=v(S).

For a positive clone claim y_i, the tight witness (1) cannot be a zero-
valued partial-block coalition: it contains a strictly positive coordinate
and all coordinates are nonnegative. Therefore its witness is a whole-
block coalition, yielding
 x(S)=v(S), i in S, and x_j/m_j>=x_i/m_i for every j in S.    (2)
If y_i=0, nonnegativity already prevents decreasing that coordinate.
The weighted version of the tight-witness characterization shows that x
is the weighted cover-lex allocation, minimizing the descending normalized
levels x_i/w_i. The common multiplier a is immaterial.

Increasing v(S) changes exactly ONE expanded coalition, union_{i in S}I_i.
All clones in each affected block weakly gain by the extracted all-game
claims theorem. Summing them proves weighted cover-lex coalitional
monotonicity, hence incident-RHS own monotonicity.

Important scope point: vhat need not be a monotone TU game, because a
partial-block superset of a positive whole-block coalition has value zero.
This causes no problem here: the extracted claims proof holds on all TU
games. Applying only the paper's stated stable-game PES theorem to vhat
would be unjustified.

4. POSITIVE REAL WEIGHTS: CONTINUITY JUSTIFICATION

Approximate fixed w>0 by positive rational w^ell. Weighted cover-lex
allocations remain bounded: their first maximum normalized level is at
most max_S v(S)/sum_{i in S}w_i^ell, locally uniformly finite. Take a
convergent subsequence of allocations. For each coordinate with positive
limit choose a tight witness as in (2); finitely many coalitions permit
a further subsequence on which all these witnesses are fixed. Feasibility,
tightness, and the inequalities x_j/w_j>=x_i/w_i pass to the limit.
Zero coordinates remain protected by nonnegativity. The limit therefore
satisfies the weighted cover-lex certificate and is its unique allocation.
Every subsequential limit agrees, proving continuity in positive weights.

Apply this simultaneously to v and v'; the rational monotonicity
inequalities pass to the limit. No zero weights or infinite clone systems
are needed. This continuity argument is an elementary extension supplied
here, not claimed to be textually present in the source.
