A DIRECT EXACT-GAME INTERPRETATION
Root source-comparison note; elementary derivation, no novelty claim. Added after the frozen proof snapshot. Does not change the reviewed proof or its result.

For any finite exact TU game v with core C, define w(S)=v(N)-v(S)-v(N\S). Then
 diam_L1(C)=2 max_S w(S).
Proof: every core point has y(S) between v(S) and v(N)-v(N\S). For any y,z in C, let S={i:y_i>z_i}; equal total payoffs give ||y-z||_1=2(y(S)-z(S))<=2max_S w(S).
Conversely choose S maximizing w. Exactness for S supplies z in C with z(S)=v(S). Exactness for its complement supplies y in C with y(N\S)=v(N\S), hence y(S)=v(N)-v(N\S). Equal total payoffs give ||y-z||_1>=2(y(S)-z(S))=2w(S). Thus equality follows. The empty/full coalition cases have width0; nonnegativity of every width follows from a nonempty core.

Our norm savings game is directly exact: if a(S) is nonzero, take u=a(S)/||a(S)|| and y_i=b_i-u dot a_i. This core point has y(S)=b(S)-||a(S)||=v(S). If a(S)=0, b itself attains v(S). Therefore w(S)=2||a(S)|| and the formula gives diameter4max_S||a(S)|| immediately.

This supplies another reason to classify the diameter calculation as an elementary exact-game corollary. Csoka/Herings/Koczy2009 defines exactness and discusses it for risk games, but we do NOT claim that this diameter formula is stated there. Nor do we infer that the formula is new because it was not located in the bounded search. The direct exactness proof avoids any mismatch between their discrete-state risk model and our norm realization.
