ρ ≤ √3 ≈ 1.732, uniformly in the number of owners. The previous ceiling was 2. The new restriction on the material contribution of both factors is enough to obtain a fixed gap c = 2 − √3. A cap on individual owner size is unnecessary for this upper bound.

This answers the precise question in the preceding handoff. It follows from elementary geometry. It should be recorded as a useful corollary, without promoting it into a separate paper or a field-level breakthrough.

Owners have nonzero two-dimensional exposures aᵢ, their aggregate exposure is zero, bᵢ = ‖aᵢ‖ and T = Σbᵢ. Coalition savings are v(S) = b(S) − ‖a(S)‖. A stable allocation gives every coalition at least its stand-alone savings and distributes exactly T. Internal transfers are allowed.

G = Σᵢ aᵢaᵢᵀ/bᵢ, trace(G) = T λmin(G) ≥ T/4 ⇒ ‖y − z‖₁/T ≤ √3 for every pair y, z in the entire savings core.

In particular this applies to the full ascending leximin rule and the full descending lexicographic-minimum rule: raising the smallest savings first versus lowering the largest savings first. It does not require computing either rule.

A diameter formula, followed by one inequality

The key is an exact expression for the largest distance between any two stable allocations. Let M be the largest norm of a coalition's aggregate exposure:

D₁(core) = 4 maxₛ ‖a(S)‖ = 2 max[‖u‖=1] Σᵢ |u · aᵢ|.

To bound the distance, collect the owners who receive more under y than z into S. Equal total payments imply ‖y − z‖₁ = 2[y(S) − z(S)]. The coalition and its complement put y(S) between b(S) − ‖a(S)‖ and b(S) + ‖a(S)‖. Thus the distance is at most 4‖a(S)‖.

The bound is attained by linear allocations bᵢ ± u · aᵢ for a suitable direction u. Every coalition constraint follows directly from Cauchy–Schwarz. Zero total exposure ensures both allocations distribute T.

Weighted Cauchy–Schwarz then controls the largest absolute projection:

(Σᵢ |u · aᵢ|)² ≤ T · uᵀGu ≤ T · λmax(G). D₁(core)/T ≤ 2√[λmax(G)/T]. In two dimensions, λmin(G) ≥ δT implies D₁(core)/T ≤ 2√(1 − δ).

At δ = 1/4 this is √3. The result also extends to higher dimensions with λmax(G) ≤ [1 − (d−1)δ]T when every eigenvalue is at least δT.

Sharp for the whole core; unresolved for the two rules

Take m distinct owners in each of four exposure directions:

(p,q), (p,−q), (−p,q), (−p,−q), where p = √(1−δ), q = √δ, 0 < δ ≤ 1/2.

All owners have stand-alone risk 1. The spectrum is exactly T(1−δ, δ), and the whole-core diameter reaches 2√(1−δ). With m ≥ 3, every owner has at most 1/12 of total stand-alone risk, satisfying the original 1/10 cap. There are arbitrarily many actual owners.

Both fairness rules nevertheless select the all-ones allocation. It is feasible, and a vector averaging 1 cannot have minimum above 1 or maximum below 1. So the two rules agree, even though other stable allocations are far apart.

What the finance interpretation does—and does not—say

Material dispersion across exposure directions prevents the nearly total reassignment of hedging benefits permitted by the one-factor construction. Since ρ/2 measures the fraction of total savings that changes recipients, the new ceiling is √3/2 ≈ 86.6%. This remains a wide worst-case bound; it does not show that practical differences are small.

The model divides benefits using internal credits or transfers. For a perfectly hedged pool, requiring all capital charges to be nonnegative makes every charge zero. Comparing fairness in savings is also different from comparing fairness in charges, because each owner has a different baseline bᵢ.

Zero aggregate exposure is essential to this proof. Real books with residual risk require separate analysis. There is no implication here about actual bank positions, regulatory reserve levels, causal effects, forecasts or truthful withholding incentives. No new financial data were fitted during this gate.

Proof first; computation as corroboration

13,960 exact coalition checks across 11 rational books verified the linear-charge witnesses, squared-norm coalition constraints, spectral calculations and applicable uniform bounds. Cases include conical families, orthogonal groups, mixed antithetic books, a triangle and a one-factor calibration.

324 floating linear-program objectives independently reconstructed finite whole-core diameters for the selected books with at most eight owners. The largest discrepancy from the formula was 2.2e-16. Established ingredients and remaining priority uncertainty

Csóka, Herings and Kóczy (2009), Section 4, establishes a broad connection between zero-aggregate-uncertainty risk games and exact games. That equivalence does not impose our fixed Gaussian-factor restriction.

Althoff and Frehse (2016), Lemma 1, records the standard sum-of-absolute-projections support formula for a zonotope. The spectral step here is weighted Cauchy–Schwarz. These are established ingredients.

Hougaard and Smilgins (2014) supplies relevant charge-core and Lorenz-allocation context; its fairness objective differs from our savings comparison. The later journal version was not checked.

The Joós–Lángi zonotope paper, Section 2, equations (2)–(3), also explicitly gives the signed-sum and absolute-projection radius identities. A direct exact-game argument supplies the core-diameter formula from complementary coalition widths. These connections strengthen the case for treating this as an elementary corollary.
