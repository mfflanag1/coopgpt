SPECTRAL CONTROL OF THE ENTIRE SAVINGS CORE
Root conventional proof candidate, 24 October 2026 local / 25 October UTC.
Source/priority status: elementary derivation from norm-core inequalities and Cauchy-Schwarz. Do not claim a novel general theorem or a recognized open problem solved. The specific project gate asks only for some uniform improvement below2.

1. Contract and existence
Let n>=2, a_i in R^d\{0}, sum_i a_i=0. Set b_i=||a_i||_2, T=sum_i b_i>0. For S subset N write a(S)=sum_S a_i and b(S)=sum_S b_i. Savings game v(S)=b(S)-||a(S)||_2, so v(N)=T and v({i})=0. The entire savings core is
 C={y in R^n: y(N)=T, y(S)>=b(S)-||a(S)|| for all S}.
It is nonempty since b is feasible, and compact since singleton/complement inequalities imply 0<=y_i<=2b_i. No capital-charge floors or all-profile incentives are imposed. Negative internal capital charges are allowed. Owners are distinct and fixed within each instance.
Complement inequalities, zero aggregate exposure and efficiency also give y(S)<=b(S)+||a(S)||. Equivalently the charge core K=b-C consists of k(N)=0, |k(S)|<=||a(S)|| for every S. It is centrally symmetric.

2. Exact full-core diameter
Define M=max_{S subset N}||a(S)|| and R=max_{||u||_2=1}sum_i |u dot a_i|. Then
 D_1(C):=max_{y,z in C}||y-z||_1=4M=2R.
Proof of the upper bound: for y,z in C let S={i:y_i>z_i}. Equal totals imply ||y-z||_1=2(y(S)-z(S)). Coalition lower/upper bounds give y(S)-z(S)<=2||a(S)||<=2M.
Proof that R=2M: sum_i u dot a_i=0, so sum_i|u dot a_i|=2 max_S u dot a(S). Maximizing over the unit sphere and the finite set of coalitions yields R=2 max_S||a(S)||.
Proof of attainability: choose a unit vector u attaining R. Both y_i^+=b_i+u dot a_i and y_i^-=b_i-u dot a_i lie in C, by Cauchy-Schwarz on every coalition, and have total T. Their distance is2 sum_i|u dot a_i|=2R=4M. Thus the formula concerns the entire core, with no reliance on a selector algorithm or sampled coalitions.
Geometric interpretation: R is the Euclidean circumradius of the centered zonotope Z=sum_i[-a_i,a_i]; this is standard support-function geometry. We do not infer priority from this terminology.

3. Spectral consequence
Let G=sum_i a_i a_i^T/b_i. It is positive semidefinite, trace(G)=T. For every unit u, weighted Cauchy-Schwarz gives
 (sum_i|u dot a_i|)^2
 <= (sum_i b_i)(sum_i (u dot a_i)^2/b_i)
 =T u^T G u <=T lambda_max(G).
Consequently, for EVERY y,z in C,
 ||y-z||_1/T <= 2 sqrt(lambda_max(G)/T).
In dimension2, lambda_min(G)>=delta T, with 0<delta<=1/2, implies lambda_max(G)<= (1-delta)T. Hence
 rho(x,z)<=2 sqrt(1-delta)
for the full ascending leximin and full descending lexicographic minimum, as for any pair in C.
For the precise proposed gate delta=1/4, rho<=sqrt(3)=2-(2-sqrt(3)), where c=2-sqrt(3)>0 is independent of n. The max-owner-share restriction is unnecessary for this upper bound; the original class satisfies it automatically as an additional hypothesis. No owner fragmentation is used.
In dimension d a condition lambda_min(G)>=delta T gives lambda_max(G)<= [1-(d-1)delta]T (0<delta<=1/d), with the analogous bound. The two-dimensional statement alone settles the gate.

4. Sharpness for the whole core, NOT the fairness-selector pair
Fix delta in (0,1/2], p=sqrt(1-delta), q=sqrt(delta). Take m distinct owners of each of the four exposures (p,q),(p,-q),(-p,q),(-p,-q). Then n=4m, b_i=1, T=4m, sum a_i=0, and G=T diag(p^2,q^2). Thus lambda_min(G)=delta T and max_i b_i/T=1/(4m). Taking m>=3 satisfies the target 1/10 concentration cap.
For u=e1, sum_i|u dot a_i|=Tp, so D_1(C)/T>=2p, and the spectral upper bound makes this equality. The extremal allocations are b +/- first-coordinate charges.
However b=(1,...,1) is feasible and the total average is1. Its minimum coordinate1 is the largest possible minimum and its maximum coordinate1 is the smallest possible maximum. Either equality forces every coordinate to equal1. Hence BOTH full fairness selectors equal b and their rho is0 in this sharp diameter family.
Thus the spectral ceiling is sharp for the entire core even under dispersed ownership, but sharpness for the two selectors is NOT established. It would be incorrect to present the diameter witness as a sharp selector witness.
For exact rational numerical calibration choose p=4/5,q=3/5: delta=9/25>=1/4, m=3, n=12, exact normalized diameter8/5 and selector distance0. The exact delta=1/4 extremizer is the algebraic p=sqrt(3)/2,q=1/2 family above, justified by the written argument, not by rational enumeration.

5. Interpretation and disposition
Material dispersion of exposure directions prevents the nearly total redistribution between core allocations seen in the one-factor construction. For delta=1/4 the fraction of total savings changing recipients is rho/2<=sqrt(3)/2. This is a worst-case bound, not observed finance data or a strong assertion that actual allocation differences are economically small.
The target's existence-of-c question is settled by elementary norm geometry; it does not justify a multi-hour campaign or a separate paper by itself. The exact supremum for the specific fairness selectors under the restrictions remains unproved here. A future question needs an economic reason for those selectors and for permissible transfers before pursuing that constant. No claim about genuine bank holdings, regulation, causal effects, forecasts or truthful resource withholding follows.
