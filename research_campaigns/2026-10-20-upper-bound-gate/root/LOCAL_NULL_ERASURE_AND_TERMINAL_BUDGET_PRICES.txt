LOCAL NULL-INCIDENCE ERASURE AND PRICES FOR TERMINAL BUDGET SUPPORT
21 October 2026 UTC. Parent derivation; native and opposite-model review complete.
This is a finite-certificate normalization and sufficient price-selection
lemma. It gives no improved unrestricted funding bound or novelty clearance.

1. EXACT DOMAIN AND DUAL
Use the fixed-owner real-box finite packing model in CONTRACT.txt. The finite
profile set Q contains complete actual-owner report vectors, with b_i(p)>=0
coordinatewise for EACH owner. The real report boxes lie in the nonnegative
orthant. A legal distinct
comparison e=(l,h,C) restores only members of C coordinatewise; outsiders are
fixed, passive members are permitted, and |C|<=s. Comparisons have one shared
whole-coalition weight mu_e>=0. For full OUT, d_e=F(delta_e), where
delta_e=sum_i(b_i(h)-b_i(l)). Let lambda_(p,S),nu_p>=0 and
  g_(p,i)=lambdaInc_(p,i)+incoming_mu_(p,i)-outgoing_mu_(p,i)<=nu_p.
Put L=sum lambda F(b_S)+sum mu d_e and D=sum nu F(B).
Discard zero-mu rows and trivial self-rows before forming the comparison graph;
throughout, this graph contains only positive-mu comparisons. Every remaining
edge strictly increases the sum of all resource coordinates: monotone reports
and fixed outsiders imply that a distinct endpoint changes at least one
coordinate positively. Therefore the finite comparison graph is a DAG.

2. LOCAL NULL-INCIDENCE ERASURE
At each coverage row (p,S), remove every owner i whose WHOLE report b_i(p) is
zero. Its aggregate resource bundle and hence its right side are unchanged.
If the resulting coalition is empty, its zero-right-side row may be discarded.
At each comparison (l,h,C), remove every member i with b_i(l)=b_i(h)=0.
The row stays legal: these members were unchanged, every changed member remains,
and the coalition only shrinks. Its delta and full-OUT right side are unchanged.
An empty resulting comparison coalition would force identical endpoints and
therefore cannot occur among the distinct comparisons retained above.
Combine identical resulting rows by adding their nonnegative weights.

For an owner/profile with b_i(p) nonzero, NO incident coefficient for that
owner has been removed. Its g is unchanged. For an owner/profile with b_i(p)=0,
all of that owner's coverage incidence vanishes. Every incoming comparison has
b_i(low)=b_i(p)=0 by monotonicity, so its incoming incidence is erased too.
The only remaining incidence is negative outgoing incidence on comparisons
where that owner changes from zero to a positive report. Therefore its new
g is <=0<=nu_p. This proves dual feasibility, with EXACTLY the same L and D.

The operation is local to owner/profile pairs; it does not require an owner
to be zero at every profile. It does not assert that the mechanism pays zero
reporters zero. It changes the certificate's labels using valid contract rows.
Arity and owner count are irrelevant to this normalization.

3. REPLACE COMPARISONS WITH AN ENTIRELY ZERO LOW COALITION
After section2, suppose an edge has b_C(l)=0. Nonnegativity of resources means
every member's low report is zero. Every surviving member has a positive high
report, since unchanged zero members have already been erased. Replace this
edge's multiplier mu by an additional coverage multiplier of the SAME weight
on (h,C), removing the comparison.

At high owner/profile coordinates the positive coefficient is unchanged. At
the low coordinates the negative outgoing coefficient disappears, but section2
ensures that the remaining g at each such zero-report owner is still a sum
of negative outgoing terms, hence <=0<=nu. This remains true when all such
edges are replaced simultaneously. At other owner/profile pairs nothing changes.
Because outsiders were fixed and b_C(l)=0,
  delta_e=b_C(h),  F(delta_e)=F(b_C(h)).
Thus full-OUT L and D are exactly preserved. Every retained positive-mu edge
now has a NONZERO aggregate low coalition bundle b_C(l).

For the TCWM-only dual the same substitution can increase L, since it replaces
a zero comparison right side by nonnegative F(b_C(h)); it does not decrease L
and still preserves D. The equality claim above is specifically full OUT.

4. A GENERAL SUFFICIENT PRICE-SELECTION LEMMA
Assume the original dual has TERMINAL BUDGET SUPPORT: no profile with nu_p>0
is the tail of a positive-mu comparison. A budget profile may be a sink or
isolated. The normalizations above only erase comparisons, so preserve this
property. They do not add profiles or change nu or D; they may change lambda.

Then the normalized dual admits nonnegative feasible production prices p_p
such that
  B_p.p_p=F(B_p) at EVERY profile with nu_p>0,
  a_e=p_l.b_C(l)-p_h.b_C(h)+F(delta_e)>=0
       on EVERY retained positive-mu comparison.
There is no efficiency requirement at zero-budget profiles.

PROOF. A strictly positive feasible price vector w exists: for finite A,pi
with every positive-profit column consuming a resource, take w=t*(1,...,1)
where t>=1 and t>=pi_e/sum_a A_ae for each positive-profit column. Larger
scalar multiples remain feasible. At each positive-budget profile choose an
optimal nonnegative production dual price. Such profiles have no outgoing
comparisons, so this choice creates no outgoing restriction.

Process the other profiles in reverse topological order. At a nonbudget sink
choose w. At any remaining nonbudget profile l, all successor prices have
already been chosen. Section3 gives w.b_C(l)>0 for every outgoing comparison.
Choose the finite scalar
  t_l=max(1, max_(e:l->h) [p_h.b_C(h)-F(delta_e)]/[w.b_C(l)] )
and put p_l=t_l*w. It is feasible and satisfies every outgoing a_e>=0.
Every comparison is handled once and prices are shared across all edges at
each profile. No member-specific comparison coefficient is introduced. QED.

5. SIGNED ACCOUNTING AND WHY THIS DOES NOT PROVE A SMALL FUNDING FACTOR
For the full-OUT dual and arbitrary feasible prices define u_(p,i)=p_p.b_i(p), coverage slack
c_(p,S)=u_p(S)-F(b_S(p))>=0, and sigma=nu-g>=0. Direct summation gives
  L-D = E + sum_e mu_e a_e - sum_(p,S)lambda_(p,S)c_(p,S)
                         - sum_(p,i)sigma_(p,i)u_(p,i),
  E=sum_p nu_p[p_p.B_p-F(B_p)]>=0.
For the TCWM-only dual, with the same a_e definition, subtract the additional
nonnegative term sum_e mu_e F(delta_e) from the right-hand side. The displayed
identity without that subtraction is specifically full OUT.
The preceding lemma makes E=0 because efficiency is required wherever nu>0.
It shows that every-profile efficiency is stronger than needed for this
particular dual identity. It does NOT produce an efficient price-base payment
mechanism on all profiles, since nonbudget prices can be arbitrarily expensive.

Nor does a_e>=0 control sum mu_e a_e by a useful multiple of D. Backward price
scaling may enlarge that sum and the subtractive coverage/slack terms together.
No rank-bound on these inflated prices has been proved. Thus the surviving
problem is still a quantitative signed accounting inequality or a controlled
budget/coverage transformation, not mere feasibility of these prices.

The current five-profile obstruction retains its stated force for efficiency
at EVERY profile. This lemma concerns a normalized dual and a different,
budget-adapted price condition. It does not silently retract or generalize
that fixed-Q result. The separate budget_price_relaxation note supplies an
explicit escape for its stored support even without normalization.
