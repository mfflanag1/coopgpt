RESULTS
For F(c)=min_{a<=r} c_a, r>=2, the sharp universal extra funding coefficient
for a TCWM correction to an arbitrary valid full-OUT rule is exactly r,
independent of the actual owner count, mixed ownership, real report boxes,
and protected coalition size s>=1. Thus the coefficients are 2 for min2 and
3 for min3. The upper uses the EXISTING CEA calibration; the lower below is
a separate exact consequence for the correction problem.

The same pure-owner institution gives a SHARP ALL-REAL OPTIMIZED ARCHITECTURE
GAP: unrestricted full-OUT optimum 1 versus efficient-price-dominating optimum
r, already requiring only singleton TCWM for the restricted lower. For its
specified efficient input mechanism the weaker sum-only additive repair cost
is exactly r-1. These are separate conclusions from the TCWM-correction cost r.

Here the correction problem requires Z>=0, sum_i Z_i<=A F(B), TCWM of Z
itself for the protected coalitions, and an efficient nonnegative production
price p at every profile satisfying p.b_i<=Y_i+Z_i. The input Y is any
nonnegative coalition-covering full-OUT rule with a finite funding factor.
TCWM of Z preserves full OUT of Y+Z. This problem is STRONGER than requiring
only that Y+Z be full OUT. The sharp lower does not apply to that weaker form.

2. UPPER A=r FOR ARBITRARY ACTUAL MIXED OWNERS
Let E=min_a B_a. For each row a, allocate estate E by constrained equal awards
among the ACTUAL owners with claims b_ia:
  t_i^a=min(b_ia,lambda_a),  sum_i t_i^a=E.
Use the smallest nonnegative water level solving the equation, with lambda=0
at E=0. Define Z_i=sum_a t_i^a. Then sum_i Z_i=rE and Z>=0, including E=0.

For a protected coalition C's restoration, each row's C claims weakly rise,
outsider claims stay fixed, and E weakly rises. If the new water level is
at least the old one, each C award weakly rises. If it decreases, every
outsider award weakly falls, so the coalition's award, equal to the estate
minus outsider awards, weakly rises. Thus each row's C total, and therefore
Z_C, is TCWM. This finite argument handles passive members, ties and zeros.

Choose any bottleneck row h with B_h=E. Its estate is its entire claim sum,
so t_i^h=b_ih for every owner. The efficient price p=e_h then satisfies
  p.b_i=b_ih<=Z_i<=Y_i+Z_i.
This proves the correction theorem. It uses only nonnegativity of Y; its
validity as an input mechanism matters when concluding that Y+Z is full OUT.
The stronger full-OUT property has a direct endpoint proof. Choose a bottleneck
row h at the HIGH profile. Its high awards equal the high claims, while its
low awards are at most the low claims. Hence
  Z_C^h(high)-Z_C^h(low)>=sum_{i in C}delta_ih
                              >=min_a sum_{i in C}delta_ia=F(delta_C).
Every other row's C award increment is nonnegative by the TCWM proof above.
Summing proves full OUT, including simultaneous restorations, ties and zeros.
This endpoint argument also verifies the stronger conclusion in the OLD note.

3. MATCHING LOWER A>=r, EVEN WITH AN EFFICIENT INPUT MECHANISM
Use r fixed actual owners. Owner i owns only resource i and reports
  b_i=c_i e_i,   c_i in[0,1].
These are legal fixed coordinate boxes; pure ownership is a subclass of the
arbitrary mixed-ownership theorem. Let V(c)=min_i c_i. The unrestricted optimum
one also follows from the elementary veto-owner observation: paying V to ANY
fixed owner covers every productive coalition, since every productive coalition
contains that owner. A protected coalition excluding it restores no amount of
its unique resource, so its outside-production value is zero; one containing
it receives the full increment of V. Thus this fixed-recipient rule is full
OUT. No novelty is asserted for this elementary cooperative-game observation.
The maximum-selector below is specifically useful for the stronger correction-
itself-TCWM lower, because it makes Y_i(L_i(t))=0 for EVERY owner i.
Select an owner w(c)
with maximum c_i, resolving ties by a fixed ordering, and define
  Y_w(c)=V(c),   Y_i(c)=0 for i!=w(c).
This rule has factor ONE and covers all coalitions: every proper coalition
lacks a resource and has worth zero, while grand worth is V.

It is full OUT for every coalition. For a proper coalition C, restored
resources have a missing row, so F(delta_C)=0. If the old selected maximum
is in C, increasing C's reports cannot move the selected maximum outside C:
outsiders are unchanged, and the old winner remains at least as large as
every outsider with the same fixed tie priority. Thus Y_C stays equal to V
or increases from zero to V. Since V is nondecreasing, this is TCWM. For
the grand coalition, total payment is V and
  min_i(c_i+delta_i)-min_i c_i >= min_i delta_i,
which is full OUT. Zero output is handled by the same formulas.

Let H=(1,...,1). For each owner i and each real t in(0,1), let L_i(t) have
c_i=t and every other coordinate one. Its production dual p>=0,sum p>=1
has objective
  t sum_a p_a + (1-t) sum_{a!=i}p_a.
Its UNIQUE efficient price is e_i. Owner i is not a maximum, so
  Y_i(L_i(t))=0,   p.b_i=t.
Any admissible correction must therefore satisfy Z_i(L_i(t))>=t. The
comparison L_i(t)->H restores ONLY owner i's own resource. Singleton TCWM
of Z gives Z_i(H)>=t. Since this holds for every t<1, Z_i(H)>=1 without any
continuity assumption. Summing over all r actual owners gives
  sum_i Z_i(H)>=r,   F(B_H)=1.
Hence A>=r. The rational sequence t=1-1/m gives finite exact certificates
  sum_i Z_i(H)>=r(1-1/m),
approaching r. This lower needs only singleton TCWM of the correction and
therefore applies for every protected size s>=1. It does NOT split owners,
alter the roster between comparisons, or assume dummy payments vanish.

Combining sections 2 and 3 proves the exact value r. For r=1 the distinct
answer is zero: singleton coverage already dominates the additive technology's
efficient price, so Z=0 works.

4. WHY TCWM OF THE CORRECTION IS A REAL EXTRA RESTRICTION
For the pure-owner lower example, set Y'_i(c)=V(c) for every owner and
Z'_i=Y'_i-Y_i>=0. Then sum_i Z'_i=(r-1)V, Y' dominates every efficient price,
and Y' is full OUT. However Z' is not TCWM: when an owner increases its report
enough to become the selected maximum, its correction may drop from V to zero.
Thus even this example permits a cheaper repair when only the sum Y+Z must
be full OUT.

4A. SHARP ALL-REAL ARCHITECTURE GAP ON THIS SAME INSTITUTION
This consequence was identified by the parent task and independently checked
here. Let W be ANY price-dominating rule on the entire pure-owner report box
that satisfies singleton TCWM. At L_i(t), efficient-price domination forces
W_i(L_i(t))>=t regardless of the input Y. Restoring owner i to H therefore
forces W_i(H)>=t for every t<1, so W_i(H)>=1. Hence sum_i W_i(H)>=r, and its
funding factor is at least r. No continuity or particular price selector is
assumed. The rule W_i(c)=V(c) for every owner attains factor r, dominates every
efficient price (each nonnegative owner charge is at most total V), and is
full OUT: for every nonempty C its increment is |C|[V(h)-V(l)]>=F(delta_C).
The unrestricted maximum-selector rule above has factor one, which grand
coverage makes optimal. Both exact optima hold for every s>=1.

If W=Y+Z>=Y is required but Z need not be TCWM, the terminal lower implies
sum_i Z_i(H)>=r-1, since sum_i Y_i(H)=1. The explicit W_i=V repair in section4
has sum_i Z_i=(r-1)V at every profile, proving its sharp correction coefficient
r-1 for this specified input mechanism. The weaker arbitrary-mixed-owner
repair coefficient is not determined here.

5. A VALID GROWING-RESOURCE EXTENSION, AND ITS LIMIT
The same construction extends to resource-disjoint minimum blocks. Suppose
  F(B)=sum_e pi_e min_{a in S_e}(B_a/A_ae),
where the nonempty row sets S_e are pairwise disjoint, |S_e|<=r, and all
displayed A_ae,pi_e are positive. Actual owners may supply many rows and blocks.
In block e run CEA on normalized row claims b_ia/A_ae with estate
E_e=min_{a in S_e} B_a/A_ae, multiply awards by pi_e, and sum blocks. Every
block's coalition total is TCWM; total correction is
  sum_e |S_e| pi_e E_e <= rF(B).
Selecting one bottleneck per block gives a global efficient feasible price,
and the same bottleneck-award argument proves domination. This includes
arbitrarily many owners and resources, but EXCLUDES competition for a shared
resource between activities. The bound is sharp already on one block.

For a shared-resource packing technology the two direct estate choices fail:

(a) Standalone estates have an unbounded budget. With m activities K+L_j->1,
    capacities K=L_1=...=L_m=1 give F=1. Each standalone activity estate is one,
    so independently adding its two-row CEA payments costs 2m. Arity is two.

(b) Efficient primal estates need not be coalition monotone. Reuse the exact
    OLD/NEW retained fixture from inputs/PRICE_CHARGE_FRONTIER.txt: activities
    K+A+B->1 and K+C->1+epsilon; owner0 has A=B=1,C=t,K=0, owner1 has K=1.
    For 0<t<1 the unique optimal plan is (1-t,t). Applying per-activity CEA
    with those estates assigns each row's full estate to its sole supplier.
    Owner0's resulting award is
      2(1-t)+(1+epsilon)t=2-(1-epsilon)t,
    which strictly decreases as owner0 restores C when 0<epsilon<1.
    This is the existing efficient-plan switching obstruction, not a new one.

Thus the old fixed rank-surplus failure blocks the straightforward shared-
resource extension of the successful CEA correction. No new estate-selection
inequality controlling both its budget and coalition changes has been proved.

6. CONCRETE CONSEQUENCE FOR THE GENERAL UPPER GATE
The min_r correction above has the STRONGER property that Z alone dominates
an efficient production price. A universal arity-three extension with this
property, TCWM for all coalitions of size<=s, and constant total cost A F
would make Z itself a coalition-covering TCWM rule of factor A. The retained
general lower K_3(s)>=sqrt(s/6) rules this out for fixed A and growing s.
Consequently a constant-cost general additive repair, if it exists, MUST
use the input payments Y in its domination argument; it cannot be obtained
by adding an independent constant-cost price-dominating blanket of CEA awards.
More quantitatively, any universal blanket of that kind has A>=sqrt(s/6).

The weaker goal p.b_i<=Y_i+Z_i may still admit a small correction and is not
excluded by that lower. Such a repair would compare optimized architectures
(at most an additive A overhead); it would not itself improve the currently
known unrestricted 3s^2 upper. A general exponent improvement still requires
a new globally valid mechanism or estimate, beyond the min/disjoint-block
calibration solved here.
