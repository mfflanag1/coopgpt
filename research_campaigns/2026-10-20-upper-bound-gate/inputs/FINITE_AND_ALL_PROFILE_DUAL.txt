EXACT FEASIBILITY DUAL FOR THE ACTUAL MULTIROW OWNER CONTRACT
18 October2026. Bounded independent work by reserve_feasibility.
Classical LP duality and product compactness; no novelty or improved s-bound claim.

1. FIXED INSTITUTION AND THE FULL PROFILE INDEX
Fix a finite owner set N, fixed actual endowments/eligibility, and one finite
nonnegative packing technology F(c)=max{pi.x:x>=0,Ax<=c}. Every retained
positive-profit column consumes a positive amount of some resource, so F is
finite at every finite capacity vector. Let P be the domain of complete legal
owner report profiles p=(b_i(p):i in N). Every b_i(p) is a RESOURCE VECTOR.
Do not replace owner i by a separate strategic player for each resource row.
No price formula, dummy-zero rule, continuity or measurability is required.

For nonempty S subset N define v_(p,S)=F(b_S(p)), where b_S=sum_(i in S)b_i.
Let f_p=F(c_p), c_p=sum_i b_i(p).
An admissible protected comparison e=(l,h,C) has |C|<=s, identical outsider
reports, b_i(h)>=b_i(l) componentwise for i in C, and both endpoints in P.
It includes ALL members of C even if some retain identical reports. Let
  delta_e=sum_(i in C)[b_i(h)-b_i(l)]>=0.
For TCWM set d_e=0. For full OUT set d_e=F(delta_e).
The set of changed owners must be contained in C; it need not equal C.

2. FINITE PRIMAL
For a finite Q subset P use unrestricted scalar payment variables y_(p,i)>=0
for each p in Q and every ACTUAL owner i. Minimize K>=0 subject to
  sum_(i in S)y_(p,i)>=v_(p,S),                  all p in Q and nonempty S;
  sum_(i in C)[y_(h,i)-y_(l,i)]>=d_e,            all admissible e with l,h in Q;
  sum_i y_(p,i)<=K f_p,                         all p in Q.
This is a finite LP, with arbitrary real coefficients if the input reports
or technology are real. For rational inputs the relevant LP values are rational.
It is not a scalar-unit-agent surrogate: all original report coordinates enter
the comparisons and coalition values, and there is one payment per actual owner.

Finite feasibility is automatic at K=|N|: the all-profile rule y_i(p)=f_p
is nonnegative, covers every nonempty coalition, and has that funding factor.
Restoration gives f_h-f_l>=F(delta_e) by superadditivity, so even full OUT holds:
the protected coalition's increase is |C|(f_h-f_l)>=F(delta_e).
This crude bound is used only to settle finiteness/duality and compactness.

3. FINITE DUAL AND THE SIGN CONVENTION
Use nonnegative multipliers lambda_(p,S) for coverage, mu_e for comparisons,
and nu_p for budgets. Define the owner/profile balance
  g_(p,i)=sum_(S containing i)lambda_(p,S)
          +sum_(e:h=p,i in C_e)mu_e
          -sum_(e:l=p,i in C_e)mu_e.
Dual feasibility is exactly
  g_(p,i)<=nu_p  for every p,i,       sum_p nu_p f_p<=1.
Its objective is
  L=sum_(p,S)lambda_(p,S)v_(p,S)+sum_e mu_e d_e.
There is ONE multiplier mu_e for the ENTIRE coalition C_e. It appears with
the same coefficient for every member at BOTH endpoints. Giving each member
an unrelated flow weight is a different and generally false dual system.

With fixed K, define D=sum_p nu_p f_p. The exact Farkas criterion is:
  the finite payment system is feasible iff L<=K D for EVERY nonnegative
  lambda,mu,nu satisfying g<=nu.
Necessity follows by multiplying the original inequalities:
  L<=sum_(p,i)g_(p,i)y_(p,i)
    <=sum_p nu_p sum_i y_(p,i)<=K D.
The middle step uses y>=0. Sufficiency is finite-dimensional Farkas/LP duality.
Writing sigma_(p,i)=nu_p-g_(p,i)>=0 makes the same certificate a weighted
sum whose payment coefficients cancel exactly after adding sigma*y>=0 rows.

When Q has a positive-production profile, its optimum K_Q is at least1 and
equals max L under D<=1. A nonzero optimum can be normalized to D=1.
There is no positive-L, zero-D certificate, since the K=|N| rule exists.
When all f_p=0, every coverage/outside RHS is0 and K_Q=0; the D=1 normalization
is then unnecessary and may be unavailable. Weak/strong duality handle this
case without dividing by zero.

4. ALL REAL PROFILES: EXACT FINITE-SUBSYSTEM EQUIVALENCE
Fix finite K>=0. Consider the product of compact intervals
  X=product_(p in P, i in N) [0,K f_p].
Each coverage, comparison, and total-budget inequality defines a closed cylinder
subset of X: it involves only finitely many coordinates, at one or two profiles.
This uses the product topology on PAYMENT COORDINATES, not a topology on reports.
No continuity of the mechanism as a function of its reports is assumed.

If every finite Q admits the finite primal with factor K, any finite collection
of these closed constraints is satisfiable. Take the finite union Q of the
profiles appearing in that collection, use its feasible payments, and assign
arbitrary values inside the intervals to all other coordinates. Tychonoff
compactness and the finite-intersection property then give one payment function
satisfying EVERY constraint on P. The converse follows by restriction.

Therefore, for the one fixed institution,
  K_*=sup_(finite Q subset P) K_Q,
and the infimum K_* is attained because K_*<=|N| and every finite Q is feasible
at that value. Equivalently, failure at any proposed fixed factor K always has
a finite-profile Farkas witness with actual owners and actual comparisons.

This is nonconstructive. It supplies no finite grid, bound on witness size,
algorithm, measurability, report continuity, or implementable selection rule.
Checking many finite grids does not verify all finite Q. A desired regularity
axiom would require a separate argument; this compactness proof does not add it.

5. EXACT RESOURCE-CONSERVATION INVARIANTS
For any feasible finite dual set sigma=nu-g and define
  B=sum_p nu_p c_p,
  Delta=sum_e mu_e delta_e,
  R=B-Delta.
Multiplying every owner/profile balance by that owner's actual resource vector
and summing gives the VECTOR IDENTITY
  R = sum_(p,S)lambda_(p,S)b_S(p)
      +sum_(p,i)sigma_(p,i)b_i(p) >=0.                 (RC)
It holds coordinate by coordinate for mixed owners. Outsider reports are
unchanged, so the comparison's aggregate capacity change equals delta_e.
Also, summing balances without resources gives, for every actual owner i,
  sum_(p,S containing i)lambda_(p,S)<=sum_p nu_p.

By superadditivity, homogeneity and monotonicity,
  L_TCWM<=F(R),
  L_OUT<=F(R)+sum_e mu_e F(delta_e)<=F(B).             (RB)
The first full-OUT bound is the more informative one. These are upper bounds
on a GIVEN dual objective, not universal factors times D. They preserve the
restoration amount under subdivision of a comparison with the same coalition
and multiplier. Counting arcs or total mu alone has no such invariance.

6. BUDGET PROFILES ON A COMMON OPTIMAL PRICE FACE CANNOT OBSTRUCT K=1
Suppose one nonnegative resource price w satisfies A^T w>=pi and
w.c_p=f_p for every p with nu_p>0. Using (RC) or the fixed-price payment rule,
  L_TCWM<=w.B-sum_e mu_e w.delta_e<=D;
  L_OUT<=w.B-sum_e mu_e[w.delta_e-F(delta_e)]<=D.
Thus a certificate with ratio>1 requires budget profiles with no common optimal
production dual price. In particular, no collection of coverage/incentive rows
can force factor>1 using a budget at only ONE prescribed profile. The fixed
price w optimal there defines y_i(b)=w.b_i everywhere, covers all coalitions,+satisfies full OUT, and is efficient at that profile (though possibly expensive
elsewhere).

Symmetrized projective lower certificates are fully consistent with this fact:
their one budget variable represents the average over MANY transformed profiles.
Expanding the symmetry average gives budget multipliers on that finite orbit,
not one budget inequality for the original asymmetric mechanism.

7. A COARSE PRICE-COVER BOUND FOR A GIVEN CERTIFICATE
More generally, suppose ell dual-feasible prices w^1,...,w^ell collectively
cover the budget support: for every p with nu_p>0, at least one has
w^j.c_p=f_p. Let G(c)=min_j w^j.c. Then G>=F everywhere and G(c_p)=f_p on
the budget support. Transform each ACTUAL owner's bundle to z_ij=w^j.b_i;
this creates public linear resource coordinates, not new strategic owners.

For T=G(c)>0, Z_j=sum_i z_ij, pay
  Y_i=T sum_(j=1)^ell z_ij/Z_j;
when T=0 pay0. Total is ell*T, and a bottleneck coordinate proves coverage
of G and hence F. Along a coalition restoration, on a bottleneck-k cell,
the derivative of its payment is
  DeltaZ_k [1+sum_(j!=k) a_j/Z_j]
  +sum_(j!=k) (T/Z_j)(1-a_j/Z_j) DeltaZ_j
  >=DeltaZ_k>=min_j DeltaZ_j=G(delta)>=F(delta).
Here a_j is the coalition's current holding in transformed coordinate j.
These coalition-restoration derivatives are nonnegative; finitely many bottleneck changes and the bound
0<=sum_C Y_i<=ell*T handle seams and zero-output boundaries. Thus full OUT
holds. Applying weak duality to these payments yields L<=ell D.

This is an exact certificate-structure bound, not O(s): no bound on the number
of needed optimal-price faces in terms of s is proved. The one-price case is
the preceding sharper invariant. Existing sharper min-ell mechanisms can also
be substituted, but no such dependency is needed for this note.
