## Findings

**F1 (moderate, scope). RESULT.txt:25–26, 191–198.** The theorem quantifies over duals whose positive-μ defects are nonnegative **for prices efficient at every q∈Q** (`B_q.p_q=V_q`). Exact efficiency at the zero-budget low endpoints L,M is what forces `p_C=0` there, hence `u_M({1,3})=0` and the fatal `−1/3`. Evidence: FINITE_AND_ALL_PROFILE_DUAL.txt:120–121 requires a common optimal price only where `nu_p>0`; in the displayed optimal dual `nu(L)=nu(M)=0`. With a merely dual-feasible `p_M` having `p_C(M)≥1/6`, `a_{M→Q,{1,3}} = 2p_C(M) − 1/3 ≥ 0` and the "never permitted" row becomes permitted. Verification: re-run the four-region/phase-I test with the efficiency equality imposed only on `{p : nu_p>0}` (note `A^Tp≥pi`, `p≥0` alone also allows `p.c_M>f_M`, so the budget interacts — my quick arithmetic suggests `p.c_M ≥ 5/2+p_C ≥ 8/3 > (285/278)(5/2)`, but I did not verify this in generality and the document does not claim or address it). As written, "no transformation within this fixed finite profile system" (lines 9–11) is true only for globally efficient price selections.

**F2 (minor, presentation/interpretive).** The sign convention makes `a_e = u_low(C) − u_high(C) + d_e ≥ 0` the *desired* property (RESULT:25–26, 148), i.e. prices must increase by **at most** `d_e`. The CONTRACT:4 reserve inequality is slack exactly when that quantity is negative. Internally consistent (§5's "defect −1/3" matches §6's table and the phase-I `q_e ≥ −a_e`), but the architectural relevance depends entirely on which direction the parent gate needs; the opposite-sign normalization question is neither addressed nor excluded.

**F3 (minor, redundancy).** RESULT:170–172's `s=0` / `s>0` split is unnecessary: `W(M)=(0,0,0,5/2)` satisfies every row except `M→Q{1,3}` and `M→Q{1}`, which are negative for all `(t,s)`. So the theorem reduces to "any dual avoiding those two rows has ratio ≤1", and is robust even to edge-dependent price selection — stronger than claimed, not weaker.
