35: Let E=min_a B_a. For each row a, allocate estate E by constrained equal awards

42: outsider claims stay fixed, and E weakly rises. If the new water level is
43: at least the old one, each C award weakly rises. If it decreases, every
44: outsider award weakly falls, so the coalition's award, equal to the estate
45: minus outsider awards, weakly rises. Thus each row's C total, and therefore

58: 3. MATCHING LOWER A>=r, EVEN WITH AN EFFICIENT INPUT MECHANISM
59: Use r fixed actual owners. Owner i owns only resource i and reports
60:   b_i=c_i e_i,   c_i in[0,1].
61: These are legal fixed coordinate boxes; pure ownership is a subclass of the
62: arbitrary mixed-ownership theorem. Let V(c)=min_i c_i. Select an owner w(c)
63: with maximum c_i, resolving ties by a fixed ordering, and define
64:   Y_w(c)=V(c),   Y_i(c)=0 for i!=w(c).
65: This rule has factor ONE and covers all coalitions: every proper coalition
66: lacks a resource and has worth zero, while grand worth is V.
67: 
68: It is full OUT for every coalition. For a proper coalition C, restored
69: resources have a missing row, so F(delta_C)=0. If the old selected maximum
70: is in C, increasing C's reports cannot move the selected maximum outside C:
71: outsiders are unchanged, and the old winner remains at least as large as
72: every outsider with the same fixed tie priority. Thus Y_C stays equal to V
73: or increases from zero to V. Since V is nondecreasing, this is TCWM. For
74: the grand coalition, total payment is V and
75:   min_i(c_i+delta_i)-min_i c_i >= min_i delta_i,
76: which is full OUT. Zero output is handled by the same formulas.

78: Let H=(1,...,1). For each owner i and each real t in(0,1), let L_i(t) have
79: c_i=t and every other coordinate one. Its production dual p>=0,sum p>=1
80: has objective
81:   t sum_a p_a + (1-t) sum_{a!=i}p_a.

83:   Y_i(L_i(t))=0,   p.b_i=t.

85: comparison L_i(t)->H restores ONLY owner i's own resource. Singleton TCWM
86: of Z gives Z_i(H)>=t. Since this holds for every t<1, Z_i(H)>=1 without any

88:   sum_i Z_i(H)>=r,   F(B_H)=1.
89: Hence A>=r. The rational sequence t=1-1/m gives finite exact certificates
90:   sum_i Z_i(H)>=r(1-1/m),

93: alter the roster between comparisons, or assume dummy payments vanish.

138: 5. A VALID GROWING-RESOURCE EXTENSION, AND ITS LIMIT
139: The same construction extends to resource-disjoint minimum blocks. Suppose
140:   F(B)=sum_e pi_e min_{a in S_e}(B_a/A_ae),
141: where the nonempty row sets S_e are pairwise disjoint, |S_e|<=r, and all
142: displayed A_ae,pi_e are positive. Actual owners may supply many rows and blocks.
143: In block e run CEA on normalized row claims b_ia/A_ae with estate
144: E_e=min_{a in S_e} B_a/A_ae, multiply awards by pi_e, and sum blocks. Every
145: block's coalition total is TCWM; total correction is
146:   sum_e |S_e| pi_e E_e <= rF(B).
147: Selecting one bottleneck per block gives a global efficient feasible price,
148: and the same bottleneck-award argument proves domination. This includes
149: arbitrarily many owners and resources, but EXCLUDES competition for a shared
150: resource between activities. The bound is sharp already on one block.
