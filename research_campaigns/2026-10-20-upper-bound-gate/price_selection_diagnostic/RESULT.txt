1. FINITE LP AND FARKAS CRITERION
Fix any finite report system Q and a feasible dual with positive comparison
support E. For e=(l,h,C), put d_e=F(delta_e). The desired prices satisfy
  p_q>=0,  A^T p_q>=pi,  B_q.p_q=V_q  (q in Q),
  b_C(h).p_h-b_C(l).p_l<=d_e         (e in E).                 (P)
No multiplier may be split among members of its actual coalition C.
Efficiency is required at EVERY profile, including profiles with budget
multiplier nu_q=0. This restriction is essential to the obstruction below.
Variants allowing inefficient prices at zero-budget or low profiles are not
excluded by this result.

For activity multipliers alpha_(q,j)>=0, comparison multipliers beta_e>=0,
and arbitrary real efficiency multipliers tau_q, define
  g_(q,a)=-sum_j A_aj alpha_(q,j) +tau_q B_qa
          +sum_(e:high=q) beta_e b_Ca(q)
          -sum_(e:low=q) beta_e b_Ca(q).
Finite Farkas duality gives the exact criterion: (P) is feasible iff EVERY
such multiplier tuple with g_(q,a)>=0 satisfies
  -sum_(q,j)alpha_(q,j)pi_j +sum_e beta_e d_e +sum_q tau_q V_q >=0. (F)
Indeed these weights combine the rows -A^T p<=-pi, the comparison rows and
the efficiency equalities. A nonnegative resulting coefficient vector with
negative RHS contradicts p>=0; the converse is the finite LP alternative.

An always-feasible phase-I form introduces q_e>=0 and permits the comparison
LHS<=d_e+q_e, minimizing sum_e mu_e q_e. Here mu_e>0 are the ORIGINAL dual
weights. Its objective is the least weighted negative-defect mass over all
efficient price selections. Its dual maximizes
  sum alpha pi -sum beta d -sum tau V
subject to g>=0 and0<=beta_e<=mu_e. Any positive feasible dual objective
is an exact obstruction. Zero objective with an exact feasible price witness
establishes (P); floating output alone establishes neither conclusion.
