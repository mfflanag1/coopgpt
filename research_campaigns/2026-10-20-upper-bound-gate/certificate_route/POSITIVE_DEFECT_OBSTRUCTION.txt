THEOREM (SCOPED STOP, NOT A FUNDING LOWER BOUND)
For fixed arity r=3 and protection size s=2, there is no finite c such that
every actual-owner feasible finite dual, with efficient nonnegative production
prices u, satisfies

  sum_e mu_e [u_low(C_e)-u_high(C_e)+d_e]_+ <= c D,                 (1)

where D=sum_p nu_p F(B_p), and d_e is either F(delta_e) (full OUT) or zero
(TCWM). Failure occurs with unique optimal prices at every selected profile.
The lower bound persists under nonnegative comparison-flow reweightings that
preserve the signed comparison incidence at every actual owner/profile
coordinate. It therefore does not arise from counting subdivided arcs.

The signed defect sum is small and its negative terms must remain. The dual
objective ratio does not grow. This rejects clipping negative edge defects
before global charging; it does NOT reject an O(s) mechanism or the entire
price-plus-reserve architecture.

1. FIXED ACTUAL OWNERS AND EXACT ARITY THREE
Let m>=2 be even, eps=1/(4m), and V_t=2+t/(2m), 1<=t<=m.
The sole activity uses one unit each of resources A,B,K and has profit one:
  F(a,b,k)=min(a,b,k).
There are three distinguished actual owners R,X,Y and one distinct actual
passive owner P_ij for each 1<=i<j<=m. Every owner has the entire report box
[0,8]^3. At profile p_t, put
  (A_t,B_t)=(V_t,V_t+eps) if t is odd,
             (V_t+eps,V_t) if t is even;
  b_R(p_t)=(A_t-1,B_t-2,3V_t),
  b_X(p_t)=(1,0,0),
  b_Y(p_t)=(0,2,0),
  b_Pij(p_t)=0.
All reports lie in their fixed boxes. Owner R owns three rows; no strategic
owner is split. Both A_t and B_t strictly increase with t, since consecutive
V increments are 1/(2m)>eps. The K report also strictly increases.

Total capacity is (A_t,B_t,3V_t). Thus F(B_total)=V_t and the unique optimal
production price is
  w_t=(1,0,0) for odd t; w_t=(0,1,0) for even t.
Uniqueness follows from the strictly slack other two constraints at x=V_t.
In particular
  u_R(p_t)=V_t-1 for odd t, and V_t-2 for even t.
All passive price payments are zero.

For every i<j, take C_ij={R,P_ij} and shared multiplier mu_ij=1/(j-i).
Only R changes reports. These comparisons are legal for the fixed actual
owners and include the unchanged passive member. Restoration value is
  d_ij=F(delta_ij)=V_j-V_i-eps*1_{i,j of different parity}.         (2)
The increasing third row matters: delta_K=3(V_j-V_i), which is larger than
the other minimum. A fixed third row would incorrectly make d_ij zero.

Using the actual efficient prices and (2), the full-OUT edge defect is exactly
  a_ij=u_R(p_i)-u_R(p_j)+d_ij
       = 1-eps   if i odd and j even;
       =-1-eps   if i even and j odd;
       =0        if i,j have the same parity.                     (3)
For TCWM, the defect is simply u_R(p_i)-u_R(p_j). On every odd-to-even pair,
this is 1-(j-i)/(2m)>1/2. Thus the same positive-part obstruction applies.

2. EXACT FEASIBLE DUAL AND AN O(m) DENOMINATOR
The ambient finite dual uses nonnegative coverage weights lambda_(p,S), one
nonnegative shared comparison weight mu_e per ENTIRE protected coalition,
and nonnegative budget weights nu_p. Its signed owner/profile coefficient is
  g_(p,i)=sum_(S containing i) lambda_(p,S)
           +sum_(e:high=p, i in C_e) mu_e
           -sum_(e:low=p, i in C_e) mu_e.
Feasibility means g_(p,i)<=nu_p. Its objective and denominator are
  L=sum_(p,S)lambda_(p,S)F(b_S(p))+sum_e mu_e d_e,
  D=sum_p nu_p F(B_p).
Multiplying the original nonnegative-payment, coverage, comparison and
budget inequalities gives L<=K D for any feasible factor K. These are the
unnormalized Farkas coefficients. No D=1 normalization is needed; when D>0,
all coefficients can be divided by D without changing any ratio. The exact
finite primal/dual equivalence is retained in FINITE_AND_ALL_PROFILE_DUAL.txt.

Write H_k=sum_(d=1)^k 1/d, H_0=0, and
  b_t=H_(t-1)-H_(m-t), nu_t=max(1,b_t).
Give the coverage row for {R,X,Y} at p_1 multiplier one; all other coverage
multipliers are zero. Its right-hand side is V_1. The actual-owner balances are
  g_(t,R)=b_t+1_{t=1};
  g_(t,X)=g_(t,Y)=1_{t=1};
  g_(t,P_ij)=mu_ij(1_{t=j}-1_{t=i}).
All are <=nu_t. For R at t=1, g=1-H_(m-1)<=1; for every passive, mu_ij<=1.
Hence sigma=nu-g>=0 and the dual is feasible with ONE coefficient for the
entire protected coalition at both endpoints.

As in the harmonic construction, b_t is negative in the lower half and
positive in the upper half. Summing positive divergence equals the mu flow
across that cut. At distance d, at most d edges cross, each of weight 1/d.
Therefore sum_t (b_t)_+<=m-1 and
  sum_t nu_t<=2m-1,
  D=sum_t nu_t V_t <= (5/2)(2m-1)<5m.                            (4)

3. EXACT GROWING POSITIVE AND NEGATIVE PARTS
Set
  H_odd=sum_(1<=d<=m-1, d odd) 1/d = H_m-(1/2)H_(m/2).
For each odd distance d, the number of odd-to-even pairs is (m-d+1)/2,
and the number of even-to-odd pairs is (m-d-1)/2. Therefore
  W_oe=sum_(i odd<j even) mu_ij
      =[(m+1)H_odd-m/2]/2,
  G_plus=(1-eps) W_oe,
  G_minus=(1+eps)[(m-1)H_odd-m/2]/2.                             (5)
Here G_plus is exactly the left side of (1) for full OUT, and G_minus is
the magnitude of the negative defect part. Since H_odd grows as (1/2)log m,
G_plus and G_minus each grow as (m/4)log m. From (4)--(5),
  G_plus/D >= (1-eps)[(m+1)H_odd-m/2]/(10m) -> infinity.           (6)
For TCWM, G_plus_TCWM >= W_oe/2, so its ratio also diverges.

Nevertheless the SIGNED full-OUT defect is only
  G_plus-G_minus = (3/4)H_odd+1/8.                               (7)
The weighted outside-production term is
  sum mu_ij d_ij=(m-1)/4-eps[m H_odd-m/2],
and the true full-OUT objective is V_1 plus this quantity. It is O(m), not
O(m log m). All negative edges remain present in the feasible dual.

4. POSITIVE-PART FAILURE SURVIVES FLOW NORMALIZATION
Consider any nonnegative comparison multipliers on these selected profiles
with the same signed comparison divergence at every actual owner/profile
coordinate. Every nonzero comparison changes R, so it must contain R. A
comparison that also carries a passive owner P_ij can contain no other owner
at size two: its whole coalition is {R,P_ij}.

Fix an original odd-to-even pair (i,j). Its passive owner has prescribed net
comparison divergence -mu_ij at p_i and +mu_ij at p_j, zero elsewhere. For
all alternative edges carrying that actual passive owner, telescoping gives
  sum_e mu'_e [u_R(low)-u_R(high)]
       =mu_ij[u_R(p_i)-u_R(p_j)] > mu_ij/2.                     (8)
For TCWM the bracket is the actual defect. For full OUT, adding d_e>=0
can only increase it. In either case sum of positive parts is at least the
signed sum in (8). Since no size-two comparison can carry two different
passive owners along with R, summing (8) across odd-to-even pairs yields
  alternative positive-part charge >= W_oe/2.                 (9)
The original nu and D remain feasible when comparison incidence is fixed.
Equations (4) and (9) prove divergence even after exact incidence-preserving
rerouting, shortcutting, or subdivision.

The same endpoint telescoping works if profiles along these monotone
intervals are added with zero new divergence, keeping other owners fixed.
The endpoint prices are unique; prices at added intermediate profiles may
be selected arbitrarily. This proof uses the net flow demands of distinct
actual passive owners, not the number of comparison arcs or a fabricated
independent weight for each coalition member.

This uses a distinct zero-report actual passive owner for every original
pair. The protection contract explicitly includes such unchanged members,
and each edge still has one shared multiplier for its entire two-member
coalition. Deleting these zero-report owners from comparison labels changes
their incidence and lies outside this theorem. Such a deletion is in fact a
valid broader dual normalization here; signed_followup.txt records it. Thus
the obstruction is specific to preserving these original endpoint demands,
and does not preclude a normalization that removes these artificial labels.

This is a statement about preserving comparison incidence. It does not forbid
transformations that also modify coverage weights, budget support, nonnegative
payment slack, original demands, or introduce other kinds of profiles. Such
more substantial transformations remain potential positive proof routes.

5. WHY THIS IS NOT AN UNRESTRICTED OR PRICE-FLOOR LOWER BOUND
On the displayed profile set, pay R the entire production V_t and every
other owner zero. Every coalition containing R has value <=V_t; a coalition
without R has zero K and hence value zero. All selected comparisons contain
R, and V_j-V_i>=F(delta_ij), so this is an efficient full-OUT rule. In fact it
handles every admissible comparison between the selected profiles, regardless
of additional passive members. Thus the displayed primal rule proves L<=D for EVERY feasible dual on this
finite profile set. The exact checker independently checks L<=D only for the
particular harmonic dual exhibited here.

For a finite price-floor example, the payments
  y_R(p_t)=V_t, y_X(p_t)=1, y_Y(p_t)=2, y_Pij=0
are also full OUT, cover every coalition, and dominate the unique efficient
price allocations. Their factor is (V_t+3)/V_t<5/2. Hence this family cannot
be promoted to an unbounded price-domination cost claim either. These are
rules on the selected profiles only, not rules on the full legal report box.

6. DISPOSITION
The all-size obstruction closes a precise attempted global inequality:
positive edge defects cannot be charged separately to O(s) times the budget,
even with exact shared-coalition balances and flow normalization, at fixed
s=2. Gross endpoint rank-surplus charging fails as well (the companion note).
A successful O(s) proof must retain cancellation among signed edge defects or
use a substantive transformation that changes more than the incidence flow.
No improved general upper, no unrestricted lower, and no universal failure of
the price-plus-reserve architecture follows.
