SUMMARY
The harmonic obstruction is removable by legitimate changes of incidence:
zero-report passive labels can be erased without changing L or D; the resulting
singleton flow can be shortcut into one global source/sink layer. Thus the
obstruction is a literal unchanged-incidence charging failure, not a barrier
to broader dual normalization.

A second, more general global bound keeps nonzero constant owners AND their
coverage valuations. If exactly k>=1 owners vary across a finite profile set,
then every feasible dual satisfies
  L <= k D + (H_J-k f_J) T
     <= k D + (r-k) f_J T
     <= max(k,r) D,                                               (FB)
where J is the set of constant owners, f_J=F(sum_(j in J)b_j),
T=sum_p nu_p, and H_J<=r f_J is one fixed rank-charge total at that background.
It applies to full OUT, arbitrary protected coalition sizes, actual multirow
owners, and every coverage coalition. It does not guarantee price domination.
For the alternating harmonic family k=1 and f_J=0, so it gives L<=D directly.

1. EXACT FINITE DUAL AND RESOURCE BALANCES
Standing contract: one fixed finite roster of actual owners and one fixed
finite nonnegative packing technology, finite on every finite legal capacity.
A legal comparison l->h has identical reports for EVERY owner outside C;
every changed owner belongs to C; and each protected owner's report weakly
increases coordinatewise. Passive members of C remain included. The same
actual owners and their original report boxes are used at every profile.
Consequently B_high=B_low+delta_e with delta_e>=0, the identity needed for
superadditivity in section 4. No owner is split or changes strategic identity.

At finite Q let lambda_(p,S), mu_e, nu_p be nonnegative, with one shared mu_e
for the entire actual coalition C_e. Define
  g_(p,i)=sum_(S containing i)lambda_(p,S)
          +sum_(e:high=p,i in C_e)mu_e
          -sum_(e:low=p,i in C_e)mu_e <=nu_p,
  sigma_(p,i)=nu_p-g_(p,i)>=0,
  L=sum lambda F(b_S)+sum mu d_e, D=sum nu F(B_p).
For full OUT d_e=F(delta_e); for TCWM d_e=0.
These are unnormalized Farkas coefficients. Multiplying any admissible
payments gives L<=K D. The retained finite LP duality and compactness note
establishes the converse criterion; no new feasibility LP is introduced here.

The exact resource identity is
  sum_p nu_p B_p - sum_e mu_e delta_e
   =sum_(p,S)lambda_(p,S)b_S(p)+sum_(p,i)sigma_(p,i)b_i(p)>=0.
The original signed price identity is
  L-D=sum_e mu_e a_e -sum lambda [u(S)-F(b_S)]-sum sigma u,
  a_e=u_low(C_e)-u_high(C_e)+d_e.
Neither identity licenses discarding negative a_e or outgoing flow terms.
The following operations retain legitimate complete-coalition coefficients.

2. TWO FREE LABEL-ERASURE LEMMAS
(A) CONSTANT OWNERS WITH NO COVERAGE INCIDENCE.
Suppose owner j reports the same bundle at every p in Q, and every positive
coverage multiplier is on a coalition excluding j. Remove j from every
comparison coalition label. Each comparison is still legal: j's report was
unchanged, all changed owners remain protected, and coalition size decreases.
Its restored bundle and d_e are unchanged. All other owners' g coordinates
are unchanged. The new g_(p,j) is zero, hence <=nu_p. Thus L and D are exactly
preserved and the new dual is feasible. No condition on whether j's constant
bundle is zero or valuable is required. This applies to several such owners
simultaneously; coincident resulting rows can have their multipliers combined.

(B) ZERO-REPORT OWNERS, EVEN WITH COVERAGE INCIDENCE.
Suppose b_j(p)=0 for all p in Q. In addition to removing j from comparison
labels, remove j from every coverage label. The coverage RHS is unchanged
because its aggregate bundle is unchanged. A resulting empty coverage row
has RHS zero and may be discarded. Again every surviving owner's g is
unchanged, while g_(p,j) becomes zero. L and D are preserved.
More generally, the same proof works for constant owners Z satisfying
F(b_S(p))=F(b_(S\Z)(p)) for every coverage row used, with simultaneous
removal from those rows. Zero reports are an easy sufficient condition.

These operations do NOT assume that the original mechanism pays null owners
zero. They construct a different valid dual using other existing contract
rows. They deliberately change the erased owners' incidence and sigma values.
Consequently they lie outside the preservation hypothesis in the positive-part
obstruction. Applying (B) removes every artificial passive label of that
harmonic family. The actual nonzero owners X,Y remain in coverage.

3. SINGLETON FLOW CAN BE SHORTCUT GLOBALLY
Suppose every positive comparison in one weakly connected profile component
has exactly the same singleton coalition {R}. Such comparisons strictly
increase R's report and leave all other actual owners' reports fixed.
Their graph is acyclic. Put gamma_p=incoming_mu-outgoing_mu at owner R.
Standard finite nonnegative-flow decomposition expresses all comparison flow
as paths from vertices gamma<0 to vertices gamma>0. To see this without a
circulation assumption, add a source edge of weight -gamma at every negative
vertex and a sink edge of weight gamma at every positive vertex; conservation
holds internally. Repeatedly extract a positive source-sink path. No residual
positive directed cycle can remain because the graph is acyclic.

Replace each extracted path by its direct singleton-R comparison between
its endpoints, with the path weight. It is legal: R's coordinates increase
along the path and all outsiders are fixed. Its delta is the sum of the path
deltas, so superadditivity gives F(delta_direct)>=sum F(delta_path_edges).
The new comparison divergence equals the old one at every owner/profile.
Thus all lambda, nu and sigma remain feasible, D is unchanged, and L does
not decrease for full OUT (it is unchanged for TCWM).

All retained direct edges now run from the global disjoint sets {gamma<0}
to {gamma>0}. This is the actual one-global-layer structure, not separate
per-coalition forests. Applying the retained one-layer theorem gives L<=rD
for the original component. The construction can be performed on disjoint
components with different singleton owners; their vertex sets do not overlap.

After the erasure in section 2, this applies to the harmonic family. Its
nonzero comparison divergences have negative lower-half and positive
upper-half support. The exact checker verifies path decomposition, direct
OUT domination, unchanged divergence, and the resulting one-layer support.

With multiple singleton coalitions in ONE connected profile component,
independent owner-wise shortcutting need not produce one global layer: the
same profile can be a sink for one owner and a source for another. This
operation alone does not resolve the genuine owner-mixing problem.

4. RANK-PRICED ELIMINATION OF A NONZERO FROZEN BACKGROUND
Now allow arbitrary legal comparisons and coverage rows. Let V be the actual
owners whose reports are not constant across Q, with |V|=k>=1. Let J=N\V;
write a_j for each frozen owner's bundle, A=sum_(j in J)a_j, and f_J=F(A).
Choose one optimal production at A and its established rank charges h_j>=0:
  H_J=sum_(j in J)h_j<=r f_J,
  h(S)<=H_J,
  h(S)>=f_J-F(A-a_S) for every S subset J.                        (4)
These charges are sums of resource shares inside each actual owner's payment,
not separate strategic row owners. They are constant across all p in Q.
When f_J=0 take all h_j=0; (4) is then still valid.

Define the explicit testing/payment potential
  W_(p,i)=F(B_p)-f_J if i in V;
  W_(p,j)=h_j if j in J.                                         (5)
It is nonnegative. It satisfies every coverage inequality:
* If S contains no varying owner, superadditivity and (4) give
  F(a_S)<=f_J-F(A-a_S)<=h(S).
* If S contains at least one varying owner, disjoint bundles give
  F(B_p)>=F(b_S(p))+F(A-a_(S intersect J)).
  Therefore
  F(b_S(p))<=F(B_p)-f_J+h(S intersect J)
            <=sum_(i in S)W_(p,i).
This preserves the original coverage valuation, including nonzero static
owners that occur in positive lambda rows. It does not delete their value.

It also satisfies full OUT. A distinct-profile admissible comparison must
change a varying owner, so |C intersect V|>=1, and
  W_C(high)-W_C(low)
   =|C intersect V|[F(B_high)-F(B_low)]
   >=F(delta_e)=d_e.                                             (6)
The equality counts every protected varying owner, including members passive
on this particular edge. Frozen protected members have constant W and remain
in the coalition. Superadditivity proves the inequality. TCWM follows too.

The total potential is exactly
  sum_i W_(p,i)=k[F(B_p)-f_J]+H_J.                                (7)
Multiply coverage and comparison inequalities by their ORIGINAL shared dual
weights and use the exact balance g=nu-sigma:
  L<=sum_(p,i)g_(p,i)W_(p,i)
    =sum_p nu_p[k(F(B_p)-f_J)+H_J]-sum_(p,i)sigma_(p,i)W_(p,i).
Dropping only the displayed nonnegative slack proves (FB). Since
0<=f_J T<=D, its last bound follows for either sign of r-k.
This is an explicit global signed accounting inequality, not a restatement
of the unresolved primal feasibility system.

For exact bookkeeping one can retain three nonnegative loss terms:
  kD+(H_J-k f_J)T-L
   =sum sigma W
    +sum lambda[W_S-F(b_S)]
    +sum mu[|C intersect V|(F_high-F_low)-d_e]>=0.                 (8)
Every original actual coalition and every shared comparison multiplier is
retained. The large positive/negative harmonic price defects are never clipped.

5. COMPONENTWISE CONSEQUENCE AND THE REMAINING BOTTLENECK
Split the positive-mu profile graph into weak components, retaining isolated
profiles carrying lambda or nu. A frozen owner is constant along a connected
component exactly when its report never changes on its edges. Thus, for a
component H, let k_H count the actual owners changed somewhere on its edges.
Apply section 4 componentwise; original dual feasibility and D split by profile.
Isolated profiles admit efficient static price payments and cost factor one.
Consequently
  L <= sum_(nontrivial H) max(k_H,r) D_H
       +sum_(isolated H) D_H.                                   (9)
In particular at arity three, any dual ratio greater than three must have a
comparison component in which at least four actual owners vary. Merely giving
the harmonic passive labels nonzero frozen reports does not evade this bound.
If the frozen background has zero production and k_H=1, the sharp bound is one.

This supplies a concrete sufficient normalization target: decompose/transform
a general certificate into feasible pieces with at most O(s) varying owners
per piece, while preserving or increasing total objective and increasing the
sum of budget denominators by only a constant. Then (9) would give O(s).
No such decomposition is proved. Arbitrary edgewise splitting creates owner
imbalances at shared profiles; paying these imbalances is the outstanding
cost. The negative-edge clipping obstruction cannot bound that cost while
holding all original demands fixed, and label erasure only treats unvalued
or null frozen members. This is a specific structural bottleneck, not claimed
progress on the all-real O(s) quantifier.

A single comparison has <=s changed owners, but a connected component can
accumulate arbitrarily many different changed owners. Arity does not itself
bound that union. A separate per-coalition forest cannot substitute for a
bounded component union or a controlled way to cut the shared profiles.

6. PRICE-FLOOR AND FULL-DOMAIN LIMITATIONS
The potential (5) need not dominate an efficient production price. In the
alternating harmonic family, the frozen X,Y background has no K and f_J=0;
(5) pays all V_t to R and zero to X,Y. At an odd profile the unique price gives
X payment one. Thus this global signed dual bound concerns unrestricted
payments and cannot silently be promoted to a price-plus-reserve rule.

The bound applies to a selected finite Q or any report-domain restriction
on which the same J really is frozen. In a full product of nontrivial report
boxes, growing numbers of owners can vary across finite certificates, so
k_H is not uniformly controlled by s. Compactness does not remove this gap.
For k=0, the single common complete profile uses efficient static prices and
factor one; it need not use the rank charges in (5).
