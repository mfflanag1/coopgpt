1. EXACT ACCOUNTING IDENTITY
Let F(B)=max{pi.x: Ax<=B,x>=0} for a finite nonnegative packing program with
finite value. For profile q write B_q=sum_i b_qi, V_q=F(B_q). A feasible
certificate has coverage weights lambda_qC>=0, full-OUT weights mu_e>=0 on
actual coalition edges e=(l,h,C), budget weights nu_q>=0, and nonnegative
payment slacks sigma_qi satisfying the actual-owner balance

  g_qi := sum_(C contains i) lambda_qC
          +sum_(e:high=q,i in C) mu_e
          -sum_(e:low=q,i in C) mu_e = nu_q-sigma_qi.

Each mu remains a single shared coefficient for its WHOLE coalition.
Set d_e=F(sum_(i in C)(b_hi-b_li)),
  L=sum lambda_qC F(b_qC)+sum mu_e d_e,
  D=sum nu_q V_q.
Choose arbitrary nonnegative production-dual-feasible prices p_q:
  A^T p_q>=pi.
Define
  u_qi=p_q.b_qi,
  epsilon_q=p_q.B_q-V_q>=0,
  k_qC=u_q(C)-F(b_qC)>=0,
  a_e=u_l(C)-u_h(C)+d_e.
Then the EXACT signed identity is

  L-D = sum_e mu_e a_e -sum_qC lambda_qC k_qC
        -sum_qi sigma_qi u_qi +sum_q nu_q epsilon_q.          (1)

Proof: summing the owner balance against u gives
  sum lambda u(C)+sum mu[u_h(C)-u_l(C)]
    =sum nu(V+epsilon)-sum sigma u.
Substituting F(b_C)=u(C)-k and the definition of a proves (1).

The last term in (1) is NONNEGATIVE. Dropping it for arbitrary feasible prices
would produce an invalid upper inequality. It vanishes exactly when
nu_q epsilon_q=0 at every q; in particular it vanishes when prices are efficient
at every q with nu_q>0. Thus efficiency at zero-budget profiles is unnecessary
for this certificate accounting identity. Once this condition holds,

  L-D = sum mu a -sum lambda k -sum sigma u.                  (2)

This observation concerns certificate-dependent prices. It does not construct
an all-profile efficient-price-plus-reserve payment architecture. At zero-
budget profiles an inflated base may exceed the production value, and the
relevant budget support itself depends on the selected certificate.

2. FINITE FEASIBILITY CRITERION
Fix one feasible certificate on a finite profile set, its positive comparison
support E, and Bplus={q:nu_q>0}. The relaxed selection problem is

  p_q>=0, A^T p_q>=pi                         for every q,
  B_q.p_q=V_q                                for q in Bplus,
  b_C(h).p_h-b_C(l).p_l<=d_e                  for e in E.    (P_B)

Only profiles incident to positive certificate rows need be explicitly kept;
prices at all other profiles can be completed by any efficient production
prices. The comparisons imposed on prices are precisely positive-mu rows,
not every legal comparison in the underlying payment LP.

For alpha_qj>=0, beta_e>=0 and free tau_q supported on Bplus, let

  G_qa = -sum_j A_aj alpha_qj + 1_(q in Bplus) tau_q B_qa
         +sum_(e:high=q) beta_e b_Ca(q)
         -sum_(e:low=q) beta_e b_Ca(q).

Finite Farkas duality gives: (P_B) is feasible if and only if every such tuple
with G_qa>=0 satisfies

  -sum alpha_qj pi_j +sum beta_e d_e +sum_(q in Bplus)tau_q V_q >=0. (F_B)

Proof: combine the inequalities -A^T p<=-pi and the comparison inequalities,
plus the supported efficiency equalities. Nonnegative resulting coefficients
and negative RHS contradict p>=0. The finite Farkas alternative gives the
converse. In particular, there is NO free efficiency multiplier at nu_q=0.

An always-feasible phase-I LP replaces the comparison RHS by d_e+q_e, q_e>=0,
and minimizes sum_e mu_e q_e, using the original positive weights. A rational
feasible solution of objective zero certifies (P_B). Its dual maximizes
  sum alpha pi -sum beta d -sum tau V,
subject to G>=0 and 0<=beta_e<=mu_e. A strictly positive exact dual objective
would certify an obstruction to (P_B). The retained witnesses all have zero
primal objective, so no numerical lower bound or solver tolerance is needed.

5. PRECISE UNRESOLVED CLAIM AND LIMIT OF THE DIAGNOSTIC
Strong proposed statement: for every finite admissible report system and every
optimal unrestricted feasible dual, (P_B) is feasible.
A weaker normalization statement asks only whether some optimal dual on that
same profile system admits (P_B). Neither is proved or refuted here. The new
56-support result includes one exact optimum already known to obstruct every-
profile efficient prices, so that obstruction cannot refute either statement.

An exact remaining inequality is (F_B): derive its nonnegativity for every
alpha,beta,tau with G>=0 from optimality of the original whole-coalition dual,
or provide one optimal certificate together with a rational tuple violating
(F_B). Actual-owner dual balances alone give (1), not this Farkas inequality;
complementary slackness for an optimal payment vector constrains payments,
whereas (F_B) constrains production prices. No valid implication between them
has been established in this diagnostic.

Even a proof of the strong statement would only remove negative supported
price defects. It would not itself bound sum mu a -sum lambda k -sum sigma u
relative to D by a target depending on coalition size/arity. Such a uniform
accounting estimate remains necessary for a general upper-bound improvement.
The permitted zero-budget price inflation can increase both positive defects
and coverage slack, as the explicit seed-15 identity demonstrates.

6. TERMINAL-BUDGET SELECTION CAN REQUIRE UNBOUNDED OFF-BUDGET INFLATION
Attribution: root derivation, independently checked here. This is a scoped
quantitative limitation, related to the earlier scalar-lift family; no novelty
claim or new unrestricted mechanism lower bound is intended.

Let F(A,B,K)=min(A,B,K), implemented by one unit-profit activity using all three
resources. There are two actual owners with fixed boxes
  Rbar=(3,0,0), Cbar=(1,2,3).
For any real 0<t<1 retain two profiles with R=(3,0,0) fixed and
  C_low=t(1,2,3), C_high=(1,2,3).
The protected coalition is the singleton C, so r=3 and s=1. Give coverage
lambda_low,{C}=1, comparison mu_low->high,{C}=1, and budget nu_high=1.
All other multipliers vanish. The owner balances are
  g_low,R=g_low,C=g_high,R=0, g_high,C=1.
Thus g<=nu, with sigma_high,R=1 and all other payment slacks zero. The low
tail bundle is nonzero and the only positive-budget node is terminal. There
are no local zero-report labels to erase. Values are
  F_low=2t, F_high=2, F(C_low)=t, F(delta)=1-t,
so this feasible certificate has L=1 and D=2.

The high total is (4,2,3), with unique bottleneck B. For a feasible production
price p>=0, p_A+p_B+p_K>=1, its cost satisfies
  4p_A+2p_B+3p_K = 2(p_A+p_B+p_K)+2p_A+p_K >=2.
Equality forces p_A=p_K=0 and p_B=1. Hence budget-supported efficiency forces
p_high=e_B and u_C(high)=2. Nonnegative supported defect therefore requires
  u_C(low)>=2-F(delta)=1+t.
The low total is (3+t,2t,3t); nonnegative prices imply
  p_low.B_low = u_C(low)+3p_low,A >=1+t.
Consequently EVERY price selection meeting these requirements has
  p_low.B_low / F_low >= (1+t)/(2t),
which diverges as t tends to zero. Equality is achieved by the feasible price
  p_low=(0,(1+t)/(2t),0),
because (1+t)/(2t)>1, its low total cost is 1+t, and its supported defect is
zero. Thus the stated inflation ratio is the exact minimum, not just a bound.

This proves that a qualitative terminal-budget price-selection lemma cannot
also promise a uniform multiplicative bound on off-budget price cost, even at
fixed r=3,s=1. It does not refute price selection for optimal certificates:
this particular certificate has ratio 1/2 and is not asserted optimal. Nor
does it imply any unrestricted funding lower bound. In identity (1), its
coverage-price slack is exactly 1, its supported defect is zero, and its
budget excess is zero, giving L-D=-1 with no divergent accounting term.
