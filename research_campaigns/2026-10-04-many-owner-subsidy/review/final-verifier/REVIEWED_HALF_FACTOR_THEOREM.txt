SHARP SHAPLEY COALITION FACTOR FOR WEIGHTED GLOVE GAMES -- 2026-10-04
Checked research note. Historical novelty and human specialist review unresolved.
The model and Shapley truthfulness are established ingredients; see SOURCES.txt.

1. SETUP AND STATEMENT
Let N=L union R be a finite partition into nonempty pools. Each original owner i
has real capacity c_i>=0 and belongs to exactly one pool. For S subset N define
 v(S)=min(sum_{i in S intersect L} c_i, sum_{j in S intersect R} c_j), T=v(N).
The original efficient core is
 C(v)={x: sum_i x_i=T, sum_{i in S} x_i>=v(S) for every S subset N}.
Let H_i=max{x_i:x in C(v)} and M_i=T-v(N without i).
No owner is replaced by independently acting clones.

The core is nonempty: pay capacities to the pool with the smaller grand total,
and zero to the other pool. This vector is efficient and covers every coalition.
The core is compact because singleton constraints give x_i>=0 and efficiency
bounds every coordinate by T. For every core vector x,
 x_i=T-x(N without i)<=T-v(N without i)=M_i, so H_i<=M_i.

Let phi denote ordinary individual Shapley value, with n=|N|:
 phi_i=sum_{U subset N without i} |U|!(n-|U|-1)!/n! [v(U+i)-v(U)].
It is nonnegative and efficient. The following hold for all real nonnegative
capacity profiles and all finite owner counts:
 (A) phi_i >= M_i/2 >= H_i/2 for each owner;
 (B) phi(S) >= v(S)/2 for each coalition;
 (C) the universal coalition factor 1/2 is sharp as an infimum over finite games.
The individual H/2 factor is also sharp at the two-owner profile (1|1).

Consequently a single constant multiple lambda*phi covers every coalition for
all profiles and owner counts if and only if lambda>=2. The rule 2phi is truthful
under binding capacity underreports with no operating costs, covers coalitions,
protects H, and spends 2T. This is a 100% subsidy, worse than the existing 50%
aggregate-price rule. It is an obstruction to Shapley scaling, not a general
mechanism lower bound. Arbitrary-mechanism bounds remain [1/8,1/2].

2. COMPLEMENTARY PREDECESSOR PAIRING: INDIVIDUAL FLOOR
Fix i of capacity a. Let A be the total same-pool capacity of all other owners,
B the total opposite-pool capacity, and D=B-A. Define
 f_a(z)=min(a,max(z,0)).
For a predecessor subset U, write z=opposite-pool capacity in U minus same-pool
capacity in U. The marginal of i is f_a(z). The complementary predecessor subset
U'=(N without i) without U has marginal f_a(D-z).

For all real z,D and all a>=0,
 f_a(z)+f_a(D-z)>=f_a(D).
If D<=0 the right side is zero. If D>0 and either argument is at least a, the
left side is at least a. Otherwise neither positive part is truncated and their
sum is at least z+(D-z)=D, which is at least f_a(D).

The Shapley weight of U equals that of U': the two factorial factors swap.
Pairing, summing, and using that the weights sum to one proves
 2phi_i>=f_a(D)=min(A+a,B)-min(A,B)=M_i>=H_i.
This covers a=0 and every tie. At (1|1), H_i=1 and phi_i=1/2.

3. COALITION FLOOR: A SHORT CONSEQUENCE
Assume by pool symmetry that c(L)<=c(R). For every i in L, removing i reduces
T=c(L) by exactly c_i. Thus M_i=c_i and part (A) gives phi_i>=c_i/2.
For every S, nonnegativity and part (A) imply
 phi(S)>=phi(S intersect L)>=c(S intersect L)/2>=v(S)/2.
The last inequality uses v(S)<=c(S intersect L), regardless of which pool is
smaller INSIDE S. If T=0 all coalition values and Shapley payments vanish.
This proof was also obtained independently in the delegated derivation.

4. ALTERNATIVE PAIR-PRICE PROOF
This gives a useful independently checked local inequality. Let i in L and j in R
have positive capacities a,b. Represent Shapley by independent uniform arrival
times on [0,1]. Conditional on an owner's arrival t, all other predecessor
indicators are independent Bernoulli(t). Excluding i,j, let D be right predecessor
capacity minus left predecessor capacity. Conditional expected marginals are
 E[(1-t)f_a(D)+t f_a(D+b)] for i,
 E[(1-t)f_b(-D)+t f_b(a-D)] for j.
The OTHER-owner distribution D is the same in these two separate expectations.
For every D,
 f_a(D+b)/a+f_b(a-D)/b>=1:
if D>=a-b the first term is 1; if D<=a-b the second is 1.
Dropping nonnegative (1-t) terms and integrating gives
 phi_i/a+phi_j/b>=integral_0^1 t dt=1/2.
For S, choose nonnegative bilateral quantities q_ij among its positive-capacity
owners with row sums<=c_i, column sums<=c_j and total v(S). Such quantities exist
by exhausting the smaller pool. Multiplying the pair inequality by q_ij and
summing yields v(S)/2<=phi(S). These quantities are a proof device, not a change
in who owns or reports capacity. Section 3 is sufficient without this argument.

5. EXPLICIT ASYMPTOTIC SHARPNESS FAMILY
For integer q>=2 let m=q^4 and r=q^4-q^3. There are m left owners of capacity 1,
r right owners of capacity 1, and one additional right owner b of capacity m.
Take S=all left owners together with b. Then v(S)=T=m. Put
 delta=m-r=q^3 and D0=m-1-r=q^3-1>0.

Fix a left unit. If b precedes it, its marginal is 1: the right-minus-left
predecessor capacity is at least m-(m-1)=1. If b is later, its marginal is 1
exactly when Y>X, where at arrival t the independent predecessor counts satisfy
 X~Binomial(m-1,t), Y~Binomial(r,t).
Consequently
 phi_left=1/2+eta, eta=integral_0^1 (1-t) Pr(Y>X) dt.
The strict inequality follows from integer unit counts. The variable Y-X has
mean -D0*t and variance (m-1+r)t(1-t)<=m/2. For t>0 Chebyshev gives
 Pr(Y>X)<=min(1,m/(2D0^2 t^2)).
Let h=sqrt(m/2)/D0. When 0<h<1, the integral of min(1,h^2/t^2) on [0,1]
is h+h^2(1/h-1)=2h-h^2<=2h. When h>=1 it is at most 1<=2h. Therefore
 eta<=2h=sqrt(2m)/D0.

At b's arrival t, let X~Binomial(m,t),Y~Binomial(r,t) independently. Its marginal
is min(X,Y+m)-min(X,Y)=(X-Y)_+, since X<=m. Its mean is bounded by
 E[(X-Y)_+] <= delta*t+E[|(X-Y)-delta*t|]
              <= delta*t+sqrt(Var(X-Y)) <= delta*t+sqrt(m/2).
Here the first inequality uses delta*t>=0, the second is Cauchy-Schwarz, and
Var(X-Y)=(m+r)t(1-t)<=m/2. Integration gives phi_b<=delta/2+sqrt(m/2).

Combining the two estimates,
 1/2 <= phi(S)/m = phi_left+phi_b/m
     <= 1/2+sqrt(2)*q^2/(q^3-1)+1/(2q)+1/(sqrt(2)*q^2),
which converges to 1/2. This establishes the infimum, not finite attainment.
This constructs different finite ownership profiles; it does not identify the
Shapley payment of a large owner with payments to independently acting units.

For an explicit finite consequence, sqrt(2)<3/2 and 1/sqrt(2)<3/4 give
 phi(S)/m <= 1/2+3q^2/[2(q^3-1)]+1/(2q)+3/(4q^2).
At q=13 this rational bound is strictly below 2/3 (EXACT_CHECKS.json checks the
arithmetic). Thus even 3phi/2 fails coalition coverage on a specific finite game
with 28,561 left units, 26,364 right units and one right owner of capacity 28,561.
The 54,926-owner game is certified by the proof, not enumerated by the verifier.

6. FIVE-OWNER CALIBRATION AND SCALING CONSEQUENCES
At c=(1,1|1,1,2), exact Shapley payments are
 phi=(41/60,41/60|11/60,11/60,4/15).
The coalition formed by the two left owners and the right owner of capacity 2
has value 2 and Shapley payment 49/30: its ratio is 49/60<5/6.
Multiplying by 6/5 pays only 49/25<2. This disproves the arbitrary-owner extension
of the preceding 20% rule on five owners, with every capacity positive.
Independent delegated witness: (2|2,1,1,1), coalition of the two capacity-2 owners,
has phi(S)/v(S)=33/40<5/6. Both are checked by all 120 individual arrival orders.
The old 2+2 theorem remains valid: at (2,1|1,1), the small left owner and either
right owner have Shapley payment 5/6 and coalition value 1.

For every constant lambda<2, Section 5 supplies q large enough that
lambda*phi(S)<v(S); lambda=2 works by Section 3. Moreover, allowing a scalar
lambda(c) to depend on the profile does not lower the UNIFORM worst-case budget
below 2T: coverage requires lambda(c)>=v(S)/phi(S), and Shapley efficiency gives
sum_i lambda(c)phi_i=lambda(c)T. Along the family this necessary multiplier
approaches 2. This observation does not exclude smaller multipliers on particular
profiles, improvements for fixed owner counts, or a non-scalar payment mechanism.

Own-report truthfulness is an established ingredient and also follows directly:
for each predecessor subset U the own marginal f_a(z) is nondecreasing in a,
with z independent of the owner's report. Therefore phi_i, and any nonnegative
CONSTANT multiple, is nondecreasing in its own reported capacity. With binding
underreports and no costs, truthful reporting maximizes utility. No claim of
monotonicity is made for arbitrary profile-dependent multipliers.

checks are stress tests and calibration, not substitutes for these proofs.
