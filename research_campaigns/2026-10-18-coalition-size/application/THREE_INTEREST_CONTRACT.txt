THREE-INDEPENDENT-INTEREST RESERVE CONTRACT: CONDITIONAL DESIGN
18 October 2026. Application lane. No operating institution has signed or
demonstrated this arrangement. This is a precise candidate to test, not a claim
that Summit users or M100 nodes own these rights.

DECISION AND TERMINAL STATUS
The institution required for a direct application is unverified. The following
terms make the mathematical object economically interpretable, but their actual
existence cannot be established by public power telemetry. A later agreement
must identify actual parties and satisfy every hard gate in MISSING_EVIDENCE.json.
Failing a gate leaves a conditional model test, not a reserve-market finding.

PARTIES AND PROPERTY
There are exactly three fixed beneficially independent economic interests A,
B and C. Each has independent control over a named compute portfolio and over
a legally allocated, enforceable firm input-power entitlement C_i at the SAME
specified delivery point P for the SAME contract interval. An owner may own
hardware or hold a lease with the relevant powers; title alone is insufficient.
An appendix must disclose common control and who actually chooses withdrawals.
Affiliates under common control are a single strategic interest for this test.

The delivery-point operator O provides a prepaid, irrevocable-for-the-interval,
nondiscretionary interconnection and dispatch service. It cannot withhold
access after reports or demand a surplus share as a condition of service.
Its fixed cost and tariff are disclosed. The operator is not a fourth strategic
veto owner merely hidden outside the model. If it retains a unilateral veto,
the actual game must be reclassified; the three-interest application fails.

A sponsor S is distinct from A/B/C's payment accounts. It commits an escrowed
cash ceiling before reports and publishes a value schedule and covered costs.
The sponsor is a fixed funding commitment, not an unmodeled indispensable
resource owner who can renegotiate after seeing reports. If the sponsor owns
the sole necessary power entitlement, the fixed-veto-owner obstruction returns.

PERIOD AND RIGHTS TABLE (EVERY ROW NEEDS AN EXECUTED APPENDIX)
Party | Controlled unit | Owned entitlement | Partial withdrawal | Outside use
A | named meters/workload units A_j | C_Aj at P | any certified union of A_j | own or authorized joint reserve
B | named meters/workload units B_j | C_Bj at P | any certified union of B_j | own or authorized joint reserve
C | named meters/workload units C_j | C_Cj at P | any certified union of C_j | own or authorized joint reserve
O | interconnection/dispatcher | fixed service obligation | no strategic withdrawal in interval | fixed tariff only
S | escrowed money | stated benefit and funding cap | no report-contingent renegotiation | unused escrow returned

Entitlements obey sum_ij C_ij <= deliverable firm capacity at P after separately
accounting for common facility loads, losses, reserve margins, and duration.
Every subportfolio is measured at an identified input-power boundary. A physical
node ID is not the contract unit unless the owner actually controls that node
and its associated entitlement and obligations. No nominal subdivision adds an
economic owner. There is no assumption that GPU or CPU watts equal facility watts.

PUBLIC REPORT AND ADMISSIBLE WITHDRAWAL
For a common reliability level alpha and public z_alpha > 0, let a subportfolio
have public load model L_ij=m_ij+<x_ij,Z>, with common factors Z~N(0,I). Set
t_ij=(C_ij-m_ij)/z_alpha and b_ij=(t_ij,x_ij). All quantities, dimensions and
factor estimation are fixed before mechanism reports. They are not private
claims on which truthfulness is being asserted. Gaussianity is a conditional
approximation; actual nonnegative physical loads and empirical tails must be
tested separately.

The initial ownership appendix specifies indivisible certified units and any
physically/contractually divisible fractions. At a report deadline each owner
commits a union or authorized fraction of its OWN units. For each feasible
withdrawal D_i and retained b_i-D_i, both must lie in the cone t>=||x||_2.
Cone membership is necessary for this model, not sufficient for physical rights.
The unit ledger must preserve capacity, workload responsibilities, factor
exposures and any public costs under the split. A workload cannot be moved in
time or reassigned to another node merely to improve an ex post deviation.

Capacity-only withdrawal D_i=(d,0) is allowed only if the owner has a contractual
right to detach that amount of firm capacity while retaining its load and the
retained cone and delivery constraints remain satisfied. Otherwise it is not
in the declared deviation set. Full join/exit alone is a different, weaker
institution; do not invoke partial-withholding costs for it.

Underreports are binding commitments of the retained units, not cheap messages.
An independent meter/ledger reconciliation determines which units were committed
before dispatch. Scheduling, maintenance or load-shaping actions that change the
public load process are outside the fixed-factor withholding theorem unless
explicitly incorporated in the feasible contract and separately analyzed.

Coalitions can coordinate simultaneous withdrawals of their OWN units, retain
their individual identities in settlement, and combine the withdrawn units
at P under the same public operational constraints. There is no implicit right
to use the resources of an unchanged owner. The outside-production benchmark
F(sum D_i) is admitted only if such joint outside operation is actually feasible.
If coalition pooling is prohibited outside the contract, that benchmark is
economically wrong even though its mathematical inequality is well defined.

Withdrawal does not duplicate physical capacity: withdrawn entitlements are
removed from the pool ledger before outside operation. Retained and outside
obligations must fit simultaneously at P. Shared services have explicit fixed
cost allocation or enter the public net-surplus function; they are not assumed
free simply because the telemetry contains only compute nodes.

DELIVERY AND CURTAILMENT
The product must specify duration, response time, ramp, notice, restoration,
meter accuracy, common-shock treatment and the load/curtailment service promise.
Ten-second mean headroom alone is not a promise about instantaneous peaks.
An executable dispatch and curtailment priority rule is fixed before reports;
it must preserve each owner's stated reliability promise when aggregate demand
exceeds capacity. An aggregate chance constraint alone does not supply this.
No owner may claim both the same reserve sold outside and the same pool payment.

Failure liability, refunds and penalties must be fixed publicly before reports,
with an identified party bearing residual loss. They cannot be appended to a
proved payment rule without rechecking its inequalities: a report-dependent
penalty can destroy withholding protection. Either account for these obligations
in a valid common net-surplus model or prove the final net-utility guarantee.
Risk preferences and heterogeneous private service penalties are not identified
by the Gaussian headroom formula or by the present telemetry.

For the conditional one-interval Gaussian model, normalized available reserve
is F(B)=sum_i t_i-||sum_i x_i||_2; physical headroom is z_alpha F(B).
S_slack=sum_i[t_i-||x_i||_2] and G_div=sum_i||x_i||_2-||sum_i x_i||_2,
so F=S_slack+G_div. These quantities are not energy saved, delivered compute,
or sponsor value. The label S_slack avoids confusing slack with sponsor S.

THREE GUARANTEES ON THIS SAME INSTITUTION
All versions keep the same owners, entitlements, deviations, outside options,
load fit, physical dispatch, reliability target and public valuation units.
They also require nonnegative payments and ordinary coverage of EVERY coalition
at EVERY feasible report, not only the observed initial profile.

1. Ordinary coalition coverage:
   sum_{i in Q} y_i(b) >= F(sum_{i in Q} b_i), for every Q.
   A fixed tie rule for a norm subgradient p at X=sum x_i gives
   y_i=t_i-<p,x_i>, sum y_i=F(B). Thus no external funding is necessary for
   this abstract coverage promise. The allocation can still fall for a member
   that restores part of its own bundle. Its efficiency is a utility result,
   not proof that the physical reserve product can be delivered.
   Here the full production supergradient is (1,-p), not p alone.

2. Individual withholding protection adds, for each i and feasible D_i,
   y_i(b)-y_i(b-D_i) >= F(D_i).
   This protects unilateral outside production, not joint outside pooling.
   Mere individual monotonicity has zero rather than F(D_i) on the RHS.
   A proposed individual rule needs both this all-profile certificate AND the
   retained all-coalition coverage certificate. An efficient Shapley allocation
   may not be silently presumed to supply both in this general cone game.

3. Coalition withholding protection adds, for every protected Q and simultaneous
   feasible D_Q,
   sum_{i in Q}[y_i(b)-y_i(b-D_Q)] >= F(sum_{i in Q} D_i).
   Members in Q with D_i=0 still count in the payment sum. Individual protection
   alone is not this inequality. TCWM is the same formula with RHS=0.

Neither a hierarchy of promises nor a three-owner example proves a strict
positive minimum subsidy for this institution. For two actual owners the
accepted fixed-gain split already gives efficient full OUT. For three, report
the cost of each specified mechanism, a proved lower bound if available, and
the gap left open. Do not call a construction's expenditure a necessary cost.
Finite enumerations of certified unit withdrawals are implementation audits,
not all-real-report proofs unless the institution genuinely has that finite set.

SETTLEMENT AND SPONSOR VALUE
Let r be the documented public net value per unit of delivered reserve for the
contract interval, and convert normalized utility payments by r*z_alpha.
If the sponsor buys all reserve, its cash bill is r*z_alpha*sum y_i, plus fixed
service and settlement charges. This is NOT merely the extra funding premium.
If ordinary service revenue finances r*z_alpha*F(B), incremental external
funding is max(0,r*z_alpha*[sum y_i-F(B)]) plus any unfunded real charges.
State the actual cash-flow arrangement; do not use both descriptions at once.

If opportunity costs are modeled, use the same public net-value baseline on
retained and withheld assets; compensate actual used resources only once.
The net cash identity in the supplied TWO_PARTY_SETTLEMENT... record is the
accounting reference. Public watts cannot identify private opportunity costs.

Sponsor net benefit compares its actual documented benefit with its own cash
outlay and real operating costs under each guarantee. Social net benefit counts
real resource/opportunity/administrative costs and delivered service value;
pure transfers among included parties cancel. A benefit from fewer deviations
needs evidence of behavior or an explicitly labeled structural assumption.
Holding dispatch and realized reports fixed, a stronger payment guarantee by
itself supplies no extra physical capacity. Never report subsidy/G_div as
uniformly bounded by a theorem that bounds total payments/F(B).

REQUIRED EVIDENCE AND OUTCOME
No identities, capacity rights, unit withdrawal ledger, joint outside permit,
dispatch promise, liability allocation, public value schedule or sponsor escrow
is established here.
Therefore the direct economic application is INSTITUTION-UNVERIFIED. The public
datasets can only inform a separate physical model-compatibility lane.
