CAPPED GRAPH PRODUCTION: AN INCENTIVE-CORRECT QUADRATIC NEAR-MISS
2026-10-18. Constructive lane. Conventional finite proof; not a new general upper.

MODEL AND SCOPE
Let G be a nonnegative packing value with at most two graph-resource rows per
positive-profit activity. Add a separate cap row charging each activity its
profit. This realizes the arity-at-most-three technology
  F(P,c)=min(P,G(c)).
Zero-profit activities can be discarded. Each actual owner i reports (P_i,c_i),
with arbitrary mixed ownership, fixed eligibility and nonnegative real reports.
Put P=sum_i P_i and c=sum_i c_i. Fix integer s>=1 and p=1/s. For R obtained by
independently retaining every owner other than i with probability p, define
  u_i(P;c_1,...,c_n)
    =E_R[min(P,G(c_i+c_R))-min(P,G(c_R))].                (1)
The cap P in EVERY expectation is the reported aggregate cap, not a sampled cap.
Set
  h(P,c)=min(1,G(c)/P) if P>0, and h(0,c)=1;
  y_i=P_i h(P,c)+p^(-1) u_i(P;c_1,...,c_n).             (2)
This uses full partition information through (1). All final payments are >=0.

ALL-COALITION COVERAGE
If P<=G(c), the first summand is P_i. Thus for every C,
  y(C)>=P_C>=min(P_C,G(c_C))=F(P_C,c_C).
If P>G(c), the cap never binds on any sampled graph bundle. Hence u_i is the
ordinary binomial marginal of G. The standard rank-two sampled-core inequality
  sum_(i in C) u_i >=p G(c_C)
proves coverage, even when |C|>s. For completeness this inequality follows from
  p sum_(i in C)u_i
   =E_R sum_(i in C intersect R)[G(c_R)-G(c_(R\i))]
   >=E_R G(c_(C intersect R))>=p^2 G(c_C).
The first inequality uses optimal dual resource prices on R; the second keeps
an optimal production plan's pieces supported on at most two actual suppliers.
This is the previously established sparse-production argument, not a new lemma.

TCWM FOR EVERY |C|<=s, INCLUDING UNCHANGED PROTECTED OWNERS
Split a restoration into its cap and graph coordinates, with the SAME owners
and coalition C throughout. For cap restorations graph reports are fixed.
  cap payment to C=P_C min(1,G(c)/P)
is nondecreasing as only members of C increase cap reports: before saturation
its derivative is1; when P>G(c), its derivative for any changed cap coordinate
is G(c)(P-P_C)/P^2>=0. Boundary cases follow directly or by continuity.
Every u_i is also nondecreasing in P: for fixed a>=b>=0,
  min(P,a)-min(P,b)
is nondecreasing in P. Therefore all graph payments, including those of passive
protected owners, are nondecreasing under this cap restoration.

For graph restorations, P and all P_i are fixed. The cap payment to C is
nondecreasing because G(c) is nondecreasing. To prove monotonicity of sum_C u_i,
use the following universal finite identity. Fix any monotone value function
v(c_S), restore a single owner j in C, and let Delta(T+j)>=0 be the resulting
worth increment. For 0<p<1, the coefficient of Delta(T+j) in the increment of
sum_(i in C) E[v(c_i+c_R)-v(c_R)] is
  p^|T|(1-p)^(n-1-|T|)
    * [1-p|C|+|T intersect (C\{j})|]/(1-p).             (3)
It is nonnegative when p|C|<=1. This applies to v=min(P,G), despite its fixed
cap destroying homogeneity. Restore each owner's graph bundle in sequence.
Equation (3) includes unchanged protected members and requires no derivatives.
For s=1,p=1, u_i=v(c)-v(c_-i) is directly monotone in i's own graph report.
Thus (2) is TCWM for all coalitions of size at most s on all real profiles.
No full-OUT claim is made: the fixed-cap function used in (3) is not homogeneous.

FUNDING
For the fixed-cap game v_P(R)=min(P,G(c_R)), removing each actual owner from an
optimal capped graph production plan loses at most the value of pieces using
that owner. Each piece uses at most two graph suppliers; the cap is held fixed.
Consequently
  sum_(i in R)[v_P(R)-v_P(R\i)]<=2v_P(R).
Conditioning inclusion gives
  p sum_i u_i<=2 E_R v_P(R)<=2 min(P,pG(c)).              (4)
The final inequality uses concavity of min(P,G(.)); it can also be bounded
separately by P and E G(c_R)<=pG(c). Hence
  sum_i y_i<=F(P,c)+2p^(-2)min(P,pG(c))
           <=(1+2s^2)F(P,c).                           (5)
In the uncapped regime P>=G(c), (5) improves to (1+2s)F. The genuinely capped
regime is where the second sampling loss appears.

EXACT QUADRATIC OBSTRUCTION TO THIS CANDIDATE'S FUNDING
Take N disjoint unit graph edges and 2N distinct private endpoint owners, each
reporting one unit of its endpoint resource. One additional owner reports cap
P=1 and no graph resources. All private owners report zero cap. The public graph
and actual owner roster are fixed. For any endpoint owner i, its marginal is1
exactly when its partner is sampled and none of the other N-1 complete edges
is sampled. Therefore
  u_i=p(1-p^2)^(N-1),
  sum_i y_i=1+2N(1-p^2)^(N-1),   F=1.                  (6)
Take N=s^2 and p=1/s. For s>=2 the factor is
  1+2s^2(1-1/s^2)^(s^2-1) ~ (2/e)s^2.
Thus no sharper analysis of (2) can yield o(s^2). This is not a lower bound on
arbitrary mechanisms, or even on all owner-sensitive common-cap mechanisms.
It pinpoints a specific obstacle: converting a capped graph marginal into
uncapped graph coverage costs one 1/p, and many independent nearly active
branches produce another 1/p in total marginal mass.

SURVIVING REDUCED TASK
A genuine improvement through this decomposition needs a different owner-aware
extension of a graph mechanism under a shared endogenous cap. It must retain
all-coalition coverage when P>=G, pay for passive coalition members' losses as
new branches activate when P<G, and avoid the N=s^2 family in (6). A fixed
positive sampling distribution or a globally shared adaptive mixture is already
blocked elsewhere in this lane. No assertion is made that solving this special
common-cap task suffices for every arity-three packing technology.
