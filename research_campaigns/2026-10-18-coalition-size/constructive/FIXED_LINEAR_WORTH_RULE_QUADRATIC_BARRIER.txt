ALL FIXED LINEAR WORTH RULES HAVE THE QUADRATIC ARITY3 BARRIER
2026-10-18 22:21 UTC. Candidate structural extension; independent audit requested.

THEOREM AND SCOPE
Fix integers r>=3 and s>=2. For each n, take the SINGLE public technology
 F(x)=min(x1,...,xr), n fixed actual owners, every owner eligible for all rows,
and the SAME report box [0,1]^r for every owner. Let
 y_i^(n)(b)=sum_(S subset N) alpha_(n,i,S) F(b_S),           (1)
where b_S=sum_(i in S)b_i, and the coefficients are arbitrary finite REAL
numbers independent of reports. They may depend
on n, labels, r,s, the public technology, and this common endowment/report box.
Assume payments are nonnegative, all coalitions C are covered by
 sum_(i in C)y_i(b)>=F(b_C), every coalition 1<=|C|<=s (including singletons)
satisfies TCWM for all
restorations inside the box, and total payments are at most kappa_n F(b_N).
Then
               liminf_(n->infinity) kappa_n>=s^(r-1)/r.   (2)

No marginal representation, dummy-zero property, anonymity, or sign constraint
on coefficients is assumed. This is a fixed-linear-architecture barrier on
this specified institution, NOT an unrestricted lower bound. It makes no claim
that coefficients on arbitrary technologies must be nonnegative: additive
technologies, for example, admit redundant signed representations. Adaptive
coefficients and nonlinear functions of coalition values remain outside(1).
Only the arity3 case is needed for the campaign. The proof is on one fixed unit
box at each n; it never chooses a new endowment box after fixing coefficients.

1. OWN-CONTAINING COEFFICIENTS MUST BE NONNEGATIVE
The exact subset-isolator in SIGNED_FIXED_MARGINAL_POSITIVITY.txt applies to(1).
For any fixed i and T subset N\{i}, it gives one unit-box restoration of owner i
for which exactly F(b_(T union{i})) increases, by delta/M>0; every other coalition
worth stays fixed. Terms not containing i are unchanged automatically. Individual
TCWM therefore gives
                    alpha_(n,i,T union{i})>=0.            (3)
The same fixed unit box contains EVERY i,T isolator and all later test profiles.
For r>3, set owner i's extra-row reports to1 and all other owners' extra rows0;
the first three rows remain binding. This proves(3) on the SAME min_r institution.

2. LABEL AVERAGING AND A NONNEGATIVE DUMMY REMAINDER
Finite averaging over all owner permutations preserves the institution, box,
nonnegative payments, all coverage, TCWM<=s, funding, and form(1). Thus it is
sufficient to prove(2) for a label-symmetric rule. Write its coefficients as
 a_(n,k) on sets containing i with k other members, and
 c_(n,k) on sets not containing i with k members.
By(3), a_(n,k)>=0. For T subset N\{i}, define
 M_i^(n)(b)=sum_T a_(n,|T|)[F(b_T+b_i)-F(b_T)],
 q_i^(n)(b_-i)=sum_T [a_(n,|T|)+c_(n,|T|)]F(b_T).         (4)
Then exactly y_i=M_i+q_i. The first term is a nonnegative fixed marginal rule.
The second term is independent of b_i and need not have nonnegative individual
coefficients. But set b_i=0 in the original mechanism. Then M_i=0 and payment
nonnegativity gives
                          q_i(b_-i)>=0                  (5)
for every feasible outsiders' profile. No dummy-zero assumption is imposed.

3. DUMMY REMAINDERS VANISH ON EVERY FIXED PADDED PROFILE
Fix D designated owner slots and take n>D; D stays fixed as n grows. These
slots carry arbitrary box reports. Let the
other n-D owners report0. For any designated i, consider the auxiliary profile
obtained by setting b_i=0 as well. Its value is F(b_(N\{i})), and it has at least
n-D+1 zero-report owners. Label symmetry gives each of these owners the SAME
payment. Owner i's payment there is exactly q_i(b_-i), by(4). Nonnegativity and
funding therefore imply the quantitative bound
 0<=q_i(b_-i)<=kappa_n F(b_(N\{i}))/(n-D+1)
             <=kappa_n F(b_N)/(n-D+1).                   (6)
This auxiliary comparison is within the same fixed owner model and unit box.
It is used only to bound payments; no coalition incentive condition is asserted
for changing an outsider's report. Designated slots may themselves report0.

4. BOUNDED POSITIVE MARGINAL MASS
Put A_n=sum_(k=0)^(n-1) binom(n-1,k)a_(n,k). At the profile where one focal
owner reports(1,...,1) and every other owner reports0, its payment is exactly
A_n: each own-containing worth is1 and every other worth is0. Therefore
                         0<=A_n<=kappa_n.                 (7)
For n>=2 define the finite positive measure
 mu_n=sum_k binom(n-1,k)a_(n,k) delta_[k/(n-1)].             (8)
Its total mass is A_n. The remainder q does not affect(7).

5. COMMON LIMIT ON ALL FIXED FINITE REPORT TESTS
If liminf kappa_n is infinite, (2) is immediate. Otherwise set
L=liminf_(n->infinity)kappa_n<infinity and choose a subsequence n_l->infinity
attaining it: kappa_(n_l)->L. Then take a further subsequence such
that mu_(n_l) converges weakly to a finite positive measure mu on[0,1]. Equation
(7) gives mu([0,1])<=L.

For a fixed profile with D designated slots, M_i depends on which D-1 other
slots are selected by a uniform k-subset of n-1 owners. Their indicator vector
is sampling without replacement from k ones and n-1-k zeros. Compare it with
D-1 independent draws from those same binary labels, having Bernoulli probability
p=k/(n-1). Couple these draws; they differ only if an underlying population
label repeats. Hence total variation is at most
 e_(n,D)=min(1,(D-1)(D-2)/(2(n-1))).                       (9)
Monotonicity bounds every local marginal in[0,F(b_N)]. Let m_i(p;b) denote the
ordinary independent-Bernoulli whole-owner marginal on the D designated slots.
Combining(6),(7),(9) gives the explicit total error
 |y_i^(n)(b)-int m_i(p;b)dmu_n(p)|
 <=kappa_n F(b_N)[e_(n,D)+1/(n-D+1)].                     (10)
Here a subset-independent zero remainder is harmless even for D=1 or2.
The function m_i(p;b) is a polynomial, and the right side tends to0 along the
chosen subsequence. Therefore the SAME measure mu gives the limiting payments
for EVERY fixed finite report profile. Weak convergence supplies polynomial
convergence simultaneously; no diagonal extraction over uncountably many
profiles is needed. Any fixed coalition's coverage and any fixed restoration
TCWM inequality pass to these limiting payments, by applying(10) to finitely
many owners and the two relevant profiles.

6. EXACT THRESHOLD TESTS FORCE SMALL SUPPORT
For completeness, the accepted root64 support argument uses the following
explicit tests, which all lie in the same min_r unit box. Embed a center amount
x as(x,...,x,0) and a leaf amount l as(0,...,0,l). Then every coalition's worth
is min(total center,total leaf). Choose k center owners each with amount1/k,
q protected leaves with 2<=q<=s, q-1 unchanged leaves each of amount1, and a
focal leaf restored from t/k to(t+1)/k, 0<=t<=k-1. For ordinary Bernoulli
marginals, the protected coalition's exact increment is
 (1/k)(1-p)^(q-2)(1-qp) Pr[Bin(k,p)>=t+1].                 (11)
The focal gain requires all other leaves absent; each unchanged member's loss
requires the focal leaf sampled and all remaining protected leaves absent.
Those probabilities give(11) directly. Passing TCWM through(10) yields its
nonnegative integral against the same mu for every k,t,q.

Taking q=2,t=k-1 and then k->infinity gives mu({1})=0 by dominated convergence
of(1-2p)p^k. If mu((1/s,1))>0, choose a non-atom a in(1/s,1) with mu((a,1))>0.
Set t_k=floor(ka), which always lies in{0,...,k-1}. The binomial tail tends to0
for p<a and1 for p>a. Dominated convergence in(11), with q=s, gives
 int_(a,1)(1-p)^(s-2)(1-sp)dmu>=0,
but its integrand is strictly negative and the interval has positive measure.
This contradiction proves support(mu) subset[0,1/s].

7. COVERAGE MOMENT AND CONCLUSION
Take r designated owners with pure unit bundles e_1,...,e_r; their coalition
produces1. Its owner's Bernoulli marginal is p^(r-1). Coverage passes through
(10), yielding r int p^(r-1)dmu>=1. The support result and mass bound imply
 1/r<=int p^(r-1)dmu<=mu([0,1])/s^(r-1)<=L/s^(r-1),
which proves(2).
