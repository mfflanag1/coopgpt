FOUR-OWNER PAIR OBSTRUCTION TO QUADRATIC COVER WEIGHTS BASED ON (V,f_i,g_i)
2026-10-18 22:27 UTC. Exact architecture obstruction; no general lower claim.

TECHNOLOGY AND THE FIXED REPORT COMPARISON
There are five rows P,X1,Y1,X2,Y2 and two unit-profit activities, each consuming
P and its branch's two private rows. This is a nonnegative arity3 packing LP:
 F(P,X1,Y1,X2,Y2)=min(P,min(X1,Y1)+min(X2,Y2)).              (1)
Four actual owners have these reports (all omitted coordinates are0):
 owner P: P=1,
 owner A: X1=1, and X2=0 at low / X2=1 at high,
 owner B: Y1=1,
 owner D: Y2=1.
Only A restores. The protected coalition is C={A,B}; B is unchanged. Every
owner can be assigned the same fixed full eligibility and unit report box.
Both profiles have grand value V=1 and every standalone value f_i=F(b_i)=0.

Let B=sum_i b_i and g_i=max_(0<=a<=B-b_i)[F(a+b_i)-F(a)]-f_i,
where a ranges over EVERY real aggregate vector in this coordinatewise box,
not just aggregates of whole-owner subsets. The exact potentials are
 low:  (g_P,g_A,g_B,g_D)=(1,1,1,0),
 high: (g_P,g_A,g_B,g_D)=(1,1,1,1).                        (2)
Indeed no marginal exceeds V=1. Each listed unit potential is attained by
retaining the corresponding complementary branch reports in a; low D cannot
contribute because the total X2 report is0. Owner A's potential is ALREADY1
before restoration, and remains1 afterward.

REJECTED FAMILY
Select y>=f with all-coalition coverage by minimizing
 sum_i w_i(b)(y_i-f_i)^2+psi_b(sum_i y_i),                  (3)
possibly imposing a total cap sum_i y_i<=K_b V with K_b>=1. For g_i=0, either
require y_i=f_i or use a strictly positive quadratic weight. For g_i>0, weights
are positive and depend on owner i only through the three scalar statistics
(V,f_i,g_i):
                       w_i(b)=phi_b(V,f_i,g_i).           (4)
The function phi_b may itself vary arbitrarily with the entire profile; only
owners with identical triples must receive identical weights at that profile.
Likewise psi_b may be any nondecreasing function for which a minimizer exists,
with no across-profile consistency. These allowances include every fixed rule
w_i=min(V/g_i^2,s/g_i), w_i=1/g_i, any positive power of g_i, and all their
nondecreasing total penalties. They do not include arbitrary owner-label or
bundle-direction-specific weights that distinguish equal scalar statistics.

EXACT LOW SOLUTION
At low, a coalition has worth1 exactly when it contains P,A,B; every other
coalition has worth0. Thus coverage reduces to
                    y_P+y_A+y_B>=1.                     (5)
Owner D is optimally paid0. The three productive owners have the same positive
weight by(2),(4). Strict convexity makes their quadratic minimum
                  y_low=(1/3,1/3,1/3,0),                (6)
with minimum total1. Adding any nondecreasing total penalty or any allowed cap
preserves(6): it already minimizes both the quadratic part and total payment.

EXACT HIGH SOLUTION SET UNDER ALL SUCH PENALTIES/CAPS
At high, positive-worth coalitions are exactly those containing P,A and at
least one of B,D. Coverage is therefore equivalent to
                 y_P+y_A+y_B>=1,
                 y_P+y_A+y_D>=1.                         (7)
All four weights equal one positive number w=phi_high(1,0,1).
For a fixed y_P+y_A=h, strict convexity gives y_P=y_A=h/2. If0<=h<=1,
positive weights and a nondecreasing penalty give y_B=y_D=1-h. Write q=1-h,
so0<=q<=1 and
 y_P=y_A=(1-q)/2,  y_B=y_D=q,  total Y=1+q.                (8)
If h>=1, the best choice has B=D=0 and h=1, which is q=0 in(8). Thus every
global minimizer has form(8). Its quadratic term is
              Q(q)=w[(1-q)^2/2+2q^2].                   (9)
The unique unpenalized minimum is q=1/5. Since total1+q increases with q,
every nondecreasing penalty forces every minimizer to satisfy0<=q<=1/5.
A cap restricts q<=K_b-1 and therefore cannot increase this upper bound; q=0
always remains feasible. No differentiability of psi_b is used.

THE PROTECTED PAIR LOSES
At low, C={A,B} receives2/3. At high, by(8), it receives
                 (1-q)/2+q=(1+q)/2<=3/5.
Its payment thus falls by at least
                         2/3-3/5=1/15>0.                (10)
Without a binding cap or total penalty, the high vector is exactly
                   (2/5,2/5,1/5,1/5).
All payments are nonnegative, every coalition is covered, actual owners and
eligibility are fixed, and only a coalition member restores. Thus TCWM already
fails for a protected pair, at every s>=2. No full-OUT condition is involved.

WHAT THIS ADDS
A weight chosen only from total value, standalone value, and maximum owner
surplus cannot distinguish the persistent common owners P,A from the competing
branch leaves B,D at high. Restoring an additional use for A makes the quadratic
selector transfer payment toward P and away from B; A's gain is too small to
compensate B. The same scalar importance of A at both profiles removes the
possibility of repairing this fixture by changing the weight's response to g.
This supersedes the parameter-specific rejections as a family-wide obstruction,
while leaving direction-sensitive nonlinear selectors and all general mechanisms
open. The separate general funding lemma remains valid; it has no TCWM content.

APPENDIX A: EVERY SMOOTH STRICTLY CONVEX NONDECREASING LOCAL COST
Replace the productive quadratic costs by h_b(y), where owners with the same
(V,f,g) share the same C1 strictly convex nondecreasing function h_b. The function
may change arbitrarily across profiles. Keep the same conditions on zero-g
owners, total penalties, and caps. At low, the unique optimum is still(6), by
strict Jensen and minimum total1. At high, the same global reduction(8) holds,
because the cost is strictly increasing on positive arguments. The reduced cost
 H(q)=2h_high((1-q)/2)+2h_high(q)
has derivative H'(1/3)=h_high'(1/3)>0. Strict convexity makes its unique minimum
q_*<1/3. Adding a nondecreasing total penalty or cap only lowers the possible
q. Therefore the same protected pair receives strictly less than2/3 at high.
This proves failure without assuming the penalty differentiable or convex.
For h(y)=y^p, p>1,
 q_*=1/[1+2^(p/(p-1))],
 loss >=(1-3q_*)/6>0.
The quadratic gap1/15 is p=2; p=3/2 gives q_*=1/9 and gap1/9.

Differentiability is substantive for this ONE transition. For example,
 h(y)=y^2+M(y-1/3)_+, M>=2/3,
is strictly convex and nondecreasing but nondifferentiable at1/3. The high
reduced cost has one-sided derivatives2/3-M<=0 and2/3+2M>0 there, so q=1/3
can minimize it. This avoids the single-transition loss by paying every active
owner1/3. Appendix B shows why such a repair cannot have a uniform finite
budget across growing branch counts.

APPENDIX B: STRICTLY CONVEX NONDECREASING (V,f_i,g_i)-BASED SELECTORS
THAT SATISFY PAIR TCWM NEED FUNDING AT LEAST (M+2)/3
This statement includes nonsmooth costs. For an integer M>=1 use the same
common-cap arity3 technology with M branches:
 F(P,X,Y)=min(P,sum_(j=1)^M min(X_j,Y_j)).                  (B1)
There are M+2 FIXED actual owners: P owns P=1; A owns every X_j coordinate;
leaf owner B_j owns Y_j=1. At profile m=1,...,M, A reports X_j=1 for j<=m
and X_j=0 otherwise. Thus the ONLY restorer between consecutive profiles is
A, activating one additional branch. All identities, eligibility, and report
bounds remain fixed; every report belongs to the same unit box.

At every profile, V=1 and all f_i=0. P,A and the m active leaves have g_i=1;
the remaining leaves have g_i=0. Assume coverage selection minimizes a sum of
strictly convex nondecreasing owner costs that are identical whenever the three
statistics (V,f_i,g_i) are identical. These costs may depend arbitrarily on the
entire profile. Inactive owners are either forced to their floor0 or assigned
strictly convex nondecreasing costs. An arbitrary nondecreasing total penalty
and an arbitrary feasible total cap may also be included, with no cross-profile
consistency. Minima are assumed attained. This is the same scalar-feature
restriction as above, now applied to entire separable cost functions.

At profile m, all nonzero coalition values are exactly1, for coalitions that
contain P,A and at least one active leaf. Therefore coverage is equivalent to
                  y_P+y_A+y_(B_j)>=1, j<=m.              (B2)
Inactive-owner payments must be0 at a minimizer: their reductions leave every
necessary constraint(B2) intact and strictly lower cost if they were positive.
For fixed y_P+y_A, strict convexity and identical costs force y_P=y_A=t.
For each active leaf, its payment must equal the minimum allowed by(B2),
q=max(0,1-2t), since its own cost is strictly increasing on positive arguments
and the total penalty is nondecreasing. If t>1/2, reducing both common owners
to1/2 strictly lowers cost. Thus EVERY global minimizer, even if the total
penalty itself is nonconvex, has
             y_P=y_A=t_m, all active leaves=q_m,
             2t_m+q_m=1, 0<=q_m<=1,
             total Y_m=1+(m-1)q_m.                      (B3)
The cap cannot obstruct these reductions: each reduces or preserves total.

At m=1, the three productive owners share the same cost and the constraint is
just their total>=1. Strict Jensen plus monotonicity therefore gives
                         q_1=t_1=1/3.                   (B4)
For each m>=2 protect the SAME pair C={A,B_1}. Owner A alone restores; B_1 is
unchanged. Its total at profile m is
                      t_m+q_m=(1+q_m)/2.
Pair TCWM therefore forces q_m>=q_(m-1). Combining with(B4) gives q_M>=1/3,
and thus
                   total Y_M>=1+(M-1)/3=(M+2)/3.         (B5)
Since F=1, any funding factor on this fixed M+2-owner institution is at least
(M+2)/3 if the selector satisfies all the required pair comparisons. Consequently
NO such selector family can have any finite funding bound uniform over owner
and resource counts, already when only TCWM for coalitions of size<=2 is required.

This is a failure of the stated selection architecture, not a general mechanism
lower bound. Its public technologies have only arity3, but the report comparisons
exploit A's mixed ownership of multiple branch coordinates. Bundle-direction-
sensitive costs, nonseparable objectives, nonmonotone costs, or a different
selection principle remain outside. A nonsmooth max norm is also outside the
strictly convex separable class, and no claim about it follows from(B5).

APPENDIX C: FIXED OWNER-SPECIFIC SCALAR-IMPORTANCE QUADRATIC WEIGHTS
There is a different extension that removes the equal-cost restriction. Suppose
w_i=phi_i(V,f_i,g_i)>0 for g_i>0, with arbitrary owner-specific functions phi_i
held fixed across profiles. These may depend on labels, public technology,
endowments, eligibility, and s, but not on other report information. In the
four-owner fixture, the statistics of P,A,B do not change, so their weights
w_P,w_A,w_B stay fixed. Newly productive D may have any positive w_D.
Set
 e=w_P w_A/(w_P+w_A), alpha=w_P/(w_P+w_A), 0<alpha<1.
For a common-owner total h=P+A, quadratic minimization gives
 A=alpha h, P=(1-alpha)h, and common cost e h^2.
Low: B=q, h=1-q, total1, with unique minimum
                q_low=e/(e+w_B)>0.
High: B=D=q, h=1-q, total1+q, with unpenalized minimum
                q_high=e/(e+w_B+w_D)<q_low.
Every nondecreasing total penalty and every feasible total cap can only lower
the high q; low is unaffected because its total is already the minimum1.
The protected pair receives
                      A+B=alpha+(1-alpha)q.
Its loss is therefore at least
        (1-alpha)[e/(e+w_B)-e/(e+w_B+w_D)]>0.              (C1)
This rejects EVERY owner-specific positive quadratic weighting based solely on
(V,f_i,g_i), not just common functions. It does not cover arbitrary profile
adaptation that changes P,A,B's weights despite their unchanged scalar statistics.
That is a separate scope from Appendix B, where arbitrary common adaptation was
allowed but identical-feature owners had to share the same cost.

APPENDIX D: FIXED OWNER-SPECIFIC SMOOTH CONVEX COSTS WITH ZERO SLOPE AT0
The same argument works more generally for fixed owner-specific C1 strictly
convex nondecreasing costs h_i(y) with h_i'(0)=0, depending on unchanged scalar
statistics as in Appendix C. Costs are assumed defined on[0,infinity). Constants
h_i(0) can be subtracted without changing choices. For h>0 let
 H(h)=min_(P+A=h)[h_P(P)+h_A(A)].
Strict convexity gives a unique split. Both P,A are strictly positive because
their marginal costs at0 vanish while positive quantities have positive marginal
cost. Equality of their strictly increasing continuous derivatives gives both
P(h) and A(h) strictly increasing in h, with P(h)+A(h)=h.
The function H is C1 strictly convex, H'(0)=0, and H'(h)>0 for h>0.

Low minimizes L(q)=H(1-q)+h_B(q), q in[0,1]. Its derivative is negative at0
and positive at1, so the unique minimizer q_low lies in(0,1). High adds h_D(q),
whose derivative is strictly positive at q_low. Thus the high unpenalized
minimizer q_high is strictly smaller than q_low. A nondecreasing total penalty
or cap only reduces high q further. The protected pair receives
                  A(1-q)+q=1-P(1-q),
which is strictly increasing in q. Hence it loses. This includes arbitrary
owner-specific powers w_i y^(p_i), with p_i>1 and positive w_i. Unlike Appendix
B, this variant needs costs of P,A,B to remain fixed across the two profiles.
It does not claim all strictly convex costs have zero derivative at0.

APPENDIX E: OPTIONAL TYPED-OWNERSHIP SEPARATION
The same counterexamples can be placed on the fixed TYPED eligibility model:
P owns only P; A owns only X coordinates; each B_j owns only Y_j. On this
institution an entirely different rule pays P the full F(B), all others0.
It has factor1, all-coalition coverage, and full OUT on every nonnegative real
profile. If a coalition omits P, both its production and its restored production
are0. If it contains P, it receives all output, which covers its own value;
F(B+d)-F(B)>=F(d) follows from superadditivity of the packing value.
Thus the selection-family lower is not an inherent funding difficulty even on
this typed institution. This factor1 rule is NOT asserted on the fully eligible
variant, where a coalition omitting P's owner may supply the P row itself.
