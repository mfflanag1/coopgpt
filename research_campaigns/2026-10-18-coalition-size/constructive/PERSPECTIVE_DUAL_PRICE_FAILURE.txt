A PARTITION-SENSITIVE DUAL-PRICE BUDGET TRICK FAILS SINGLETON TCWM
2026-10-18. Architecture rejection, not an unrestricted lower bound.

Fix a nonnegative strictly convex nondecreasing function h on [0,infinity),
h(0)=0, finite h(1). Given actual owner bundles b_i and V=F(sum b_i)>0,
select nonnegative resource prices p with A^T p>=pi minimizing
  J_b(p)=sum_i V h((p.b_i)/V),
and pay y_i=V h((p.b_i)/V). Unlike aggregate-only prices, this objective uses
the whole partition of resource reports among owners. Take zero payments when V=0.

TEMPTING FUNDING/COVERAGE BOUND
If h(z)>=z, all-coalition coverage follows from dual feasibility. At an optimal
production dual p*, t_i=p*.b_i are nonnegative and sum_i t_i=V, so convexity and
h(0)=0 imply
  sum_i Vh(t_i/V)<=Vh(1).
The minimizing objective is therefore a funding factor h(1). For example,
h(z)=z+lambda z^2 gives factor1+lambda; lambda of order s would be linear.
This correct budget bound says nothing about the required coalition increments.

SINGLETON OBSTRUCTION, ALREADY ARITY TWO
Use F(A,B)=min(A,B) and two fixed fully eligible owners. Let
  b_0=(1,t), b_1=(1,0),  1<t<2,
and restore owner0's B coordinate from t to2. Prices can be restricted to
p=(x,1-x), 0<=x<=1: lowering an excessive feasible price sum to1 weakly lowers
every owner's cost and strictly lowers at least one positive cost.
At the high profile, V=2 and t_0=2-x, t_1=x, so t_0+t_1=2. Strict convexity
uniquely minimizes the objective at x=1, with t_0=t_1=1. Thus
  y_0(high)=2h(1/2).
At the low profile, V=t and every feasible boundary price has
  t_0=t+(1-t)x>=1.
Consequently, whatever minimizing boundary price is selected,
  y_0(low)>=t h(1/t)>2h(1/2)=y_0(high).
The strict inequality follows because h(z)/z is strictly increasing for z>0
when h is strictly convex and h(0)=0, and 1/t>1/2. This violates singleton TCWM.
It is independent of how large h(1) is and hence of the proposed funding factor.

EXACT QUADRATIC INSTANCE
For h(z)=z+2z^2 and t=3/2, the unique low minimizer is x=9/20.
Then t_0=51/40,t_1=9/20 and
  y(low)=(1377/400,18/25),   y(high)=(2,2).
Owner0 loses577/400. The low objective derivative is -3/2+(10/3)x, its second
derivative10/3>0, and the stationary point is inside[0,1]. These are exact KKT
certificates. The proposed factor3 budget holds, but incentives fail.

SCOPE
This rejects costs evaluated and paid through one common fixed convex perspective
function h, with the objective minimizing their sum over resource prices. It
does not reject arbitrary profile-dependent price selection, a different payment
formula, nonsmooth non-strict costs, owner-specific compensation, or all dual
routes. The example explains why an easy budget proof cannot replace restoration
inequalities, even after price selection uses the full owner partition.
