EFFICIENT-PRICE DOMINATION ON THE BINARY UNIT-WORTH IDEAL
19 October 2026. Scoped structural consequence; not an all-real upper bound.

1. EXACT DOMAIN AND ADDITIONAL ARCHITECTURE
Let H be any finite linear 3-uniform hypergraph, with unit activity profits
and input coefficients. Each resource row has one fixed pure owner, capacity1,
reporting0 or1. All owners remain actual coalition members. Restrict profiles
to the ENTIRE downward ideal L_H={S subset V:F(1_S)<=1}.
This is not the full real box and not arbitrary mixed ownership.

Require nonnegative payments, EVERY-coalition coverage, total funding K F(1_S),
and TCWM/full OUT for every S subset T in L_H, T\S subset C, |C|<=s.
Explicitly, coverage is sum_(v in C)y_v(S)>=F(1_(S intersect C)) for EVERY C;
the incentive inequality is sum_(v in C)[y_v(T)-y_v(S)]>=0 for TCWM and
>=F(1_(T\S)) for full OUT. Funding is sum_V y_v(S)<=K F(1_S).
Additionally require at each S SOME nonnegative efficient production-dual
price p(S): sum_(v in e)p_v>=1 for all e, p.1_S=F(1_S), and
                         y_v(S)>=p_v(S)1_(v in S).           (1)
Prices may depend arbitrarily on S; they are not prescribed in the lower.
Equation(1) is the extra price-plus-nonnegative-reserve architecture.

THEOREM. The worst optimal funding factor over all finite H on this restricted
domain is the SAME for TCWM and full OUT and equals
 A_1=1;
 A_s=1+2s q_s=1+[s/(s-1)](B_s-1), s>=2,                     (2)
where q_1=1/3, q_m=[a_m/(a_m+1)]q_(m-1),
a_m=min(s-1,2m-2), and B_s is the accepted unrestricted ideal frontier.
In particular A_s has the SAME square-root leading constant as B_s:
 A_s/sqrt(s) -> sqrt(2*pi)/(3*sqrt(e)).
Moreover A_s-B_s=2q_s=O(s^(-1/2)).

Consequently no growing certificate confined to this whole binary unit-worth
linear-triple ideal can yield fixed-s divergence or quadratic-order funding
even AFTER imposing efficient-price domination. This does not control a
certificate containing nonbinary, higher-worth, non-linear-hypergraph, or
mixed-ownership profiles. It does not construct a mechanism on the full box.

2. REUSED STAR FACT AND AN EFFICIENT-PRICE CONSEQUENCE
The accepted ideal theorem proves that every productive S has F=1 and all
supported activities share a row. If there are m>=2 activities, linearity
makes the common cap v unique, with2m distinct private rows. Other active
rows are isolated. For m=1 there is a single supported triple.

At a star with m>=2, EVERY efficient nonnegative dual must price its cap1.
Indeed write c=p_v. Its positive-capacity row prices sum to1. Summing the
m supported-edge inequalities gives
 m*c+sum_private p >=m,
whereas sum_private p<=1-c. Thus (m-1)c>=m-1. Since c<=1, c=1 and every
other positive-capacity row price is0. Zero-capacity prices are unrestricted
by efficiency. Therefore (1) forces the cap owner's payment to be at least1.
This inference does not assume dummy-zero payments or a tie-breaking price.

3. EXPLICIT UPPER ASSIGNMENT AND PRICE WITNESSES
At F=0 pay everyone0. If m=1 pay the triple's three owners1/3 each and all
others0. If m>=2 pay the cap1, each private owner q_m, all others0.

Efficient dual witnesses are explicit on the FULL public technology:
 - At F=0 price every absent row1 and every present row0.
 - At m=1 price the supported triple's rows1/3 each, other present rows0,
   and every absent row1.
 - At m>=2 price the cap1, other present rows0, and every absent row1.
Every unsupported activity contains an absent row. These prices are feasible,
efficient, and their owner allocations are dominated by the displayed payments.
All omitted owners CHOOSE payment0 in this upper; lower rules are unrestricted.

Every coalition is covered. At m>=2 a productive coalition must contain the
cap, whose payment1 alone covers its worth (at most1). At m=1 a productive
coalition contains all three paid rows. At F=0 every coalition has worth0.

4. ACTUAL-OWNER INCENTIVES, INCLUDING PASSIVE MEMBERS
Use the accepted single-restoration classification. For T=S union{i} inside
the ideal with F(S)=1, any newly supported activity contains the old/final cap
and the restoring row i; linearity permits at most one new activity. If none
appears, payments do not change. Otherwise m rises to m+1. The restorer and
one previously isolated, unchanged private owner each gain q_(m+1).

The cap's payment weakly rises: from1/3 to1 when m=1, and from1 to1 later.
The only negative increments are those of the2m old private owners, each
q_(m+1)-q_m. Every protected C contains i. Including up to s-1 unchanged
members therefore gives the lower bound
 q_(m+1)-min(s-1,2m)[q_m-q_(m+1)]=0.
This remains valid when C includes the unchanged cap, newly activated mate,
isolated owners, or inactive owners. At F(S)=0 all old payments are0, so a
single restoration also gives TCWM. For multiple restorers, use the SAME C
at every single-owner step; all intermediate profiles remain in the ideal.

For full OUT, if F(S)=0, high coverage gives y_C(T)>=F(T intersect C)>=F(T\S).
If F(S)=1, the final common cap belongs to an old activity and hence is not
restored. Every activity supported at T uses it, so F(T\S)=0. The finite
OUT comparison is then precisely the proved TCWM comparison. This is an
endpoint proof; no positive OUT right side is lost by subdividing a path.

5. EXACT FUNDING PEAK
The total is1 at m=1, and1+2m q_m at m>=2. For s=1 later q_m=0, hence A_1=1.
For s>=2 let h=ceil(s/2), alpha=(s-1)/s. The reused recurrence gives
 Q_h=product_(j=2)^h 2j/(2j-1)=4^h/[2 binom(2h,h)], Q_1=1,
 q_h=Q_h/(3h), q_m=q_h alpha^(m-h) for m>=h.
Before the tail the ratio of consecutive m q_m is2(m+1)/(2m+1)>1.
In the tail it is[(m+1)/m]alpha, which equals1 exactly at m=s-1.
Thus for s>=3 the actual funding maximum is at m=s-1,s. For s=2
the actual maximum is only m=2: the formal m=1 term in m*q_m is not
the rule's single-edge cost. In both cases m=s is admissible. By contrast the accepted old
quantity (m-1)q_m is maximized at m=s,s+1, not at m=s-1,s.
The upper is A_s=1+2s q_s. The accepted formula B_s=1+2(s-1)q_s gives(2)
and its asymptotic consequences. These concern the restricted ideal only.

6. MATCHING GROWING LOWER WITH FIXED ACTUAL OWNERS
For s>=2 use the accepted pure-owner full projective triple technology on
nonzero vectors of F2^(s+1), with basis v,e_1,...,e_s and one pure unit owner
per row. Its ideal is invariant under joint GL permutations of rows/owners.
The added architecture is convex and invariant: when averaging a payment rule
over this finite action, average its transformed efficient price witnesses
as well. At each fixed profile, dual feasibility, efficiency and(1) are linear.
Thus the same symmetric seed/private-orbit argument remains legitimate.

At the single-edge seed q_1>=1/3 by coverage. Restoring the next low owner
while protecting it and min(s-1,2m-2) old private owners forces the same q
recurrence as a lower bound. Nonnegative old restorer payments are retained,
not set to0. At m=s, Section2 forces cap payment>=1 for EVERY efficient
price witness. Total funding is therefore at least1+2s q_s=A_s. This matches
the full-OUT upper. For s=1 one productive triple forces factor>=1.

7. DISPOSITION AND DEPENDENCIES
This is a sharpened architecture calibration on an already studied growing
restricted domain. It rules out that domain as a source of a worse exponent
for the proposed price-base architecture. It is not an unrestricted K3 upper,
does not establish PL completeness, and does not show that price domination
is harmless elsewhere (the sealed12-profile fixture still costs5/4 versus3/2).
