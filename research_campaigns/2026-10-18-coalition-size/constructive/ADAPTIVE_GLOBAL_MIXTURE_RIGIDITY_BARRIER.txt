REPLICATION RIGIDIFIES A GLOBAL REPORT-ADAPTIVE POSITIVE BINOMIAL MIXTURE
Draft completed 2026-10-18 21:57 UTC.
CONSEQUENTIAL ARCHITECTURE RESULT — opposite-model review found no fatal gap;
clarifications incorporated 2026-10-18 22:08 UTC. Root also checked the argument.
No unrestricted lower or upper improvement and no novelty assertion.

THEOREM (GLOBAL MIXTURE CLASS ONLY)
Fix integers r,s>=2. On every finite arity-at-most-r packing technology let
 y_i(b)=int_[0,1] m_i(p;b) dmu_b(p),                       (1)
where, for the fixed actual-owner set N,
 m_i(p;b)=E_R[F(b_i+sum_(j in R)b_j)-F(sum_(j in R)b_j)],
R includes every other actual owner independently with probability p, and
mu_b is an arbitrary
finite NONNEGATIVE measure shared by ALL actual owners/components at that
profile. It may depend on the entire profile, public technology, labels, n,s.
Assume nonnegative real reports bounded by fixed actual endowments, and:
 - all-coalition coverage: sum_(i in C)y_i(b)>=F(sum_(i in C)b_i) for EVERY C;
 - TCWM: sum_(i in C)y_i(b')>=sum_(i in C)y_i(b) whenever |C|<=s, outsiders
   are fixed, and each member's report increases coordinatewise (some may stay
   unchanged);
 - funding sum_i y_i<=K F(sum_i b_i), with one FINITE K>=0 uniformly over
   these finite models and all their admissible nonnegative real reports.
Then
                         K>=s^(r-1)/r.                  (2)
In particular no report-adaptive global nonnegative binomial mixture improves
the quadratic size exponent at arity3. Owner-specific measures, measures local
to active components, signed formulas, and arbitrary mechanisms remain outside
this theorem. Adaptation itself is allowed; the GLOBAL shared measure is key.

OVERVIEW OF THE ARGUMENT
Huge unchanged sentinel components make decreases in each of finitely many
moments of the global measure expensive under paired TCWM. Repeat a fixed finite
list of adverse report comparisons in fresh components many times. Bounded
moments then force one cycle in which all relevant moments barely change. A
limit measure is fixed across that entire list of comparisons. Compactness over
all finite lists recovers the previously proved fixed-mixture support restriction
mu([0,1/s])=mu([0,1]). A fixed hard min_r component supplies the coverage moment.
The proof never compares different actual-owner sets within one incentive test.

1. LOCAL TESTS AND THEIR POLYNOMIALS
Take ANY finite list W of valid finite report comparisons on disjoint two-input
minimum technologies. In each comparison one owner restores some report, a
coalition of size<=s containing that owner is protected, and local production
at both endpoints is at most1. We will only need the following explicit tests:

For q in{2,s}, k>=1, and0<=t<=k-1, take k outside center owners each holding
P=1/k, and q protected leaf owners. One focal leaf restores t/k to(t+1)/k;
the other q-1 protected leaves hold1 and do not change. All owners are fixed.
The coalition is all q leaf owners. Its Bernoulli marginal-sum change is
 (1/k) h_q(p) T_(k,t)(p),
 h_q(p)=(1-p)^(q-2)(1-q p),
 T_(k,t)(p)=Pr[Bin(k,p)>=t+1].                            (3)
The local production is1 at both endpoints because q>=2 and one unchanged
leaf already has capacity1. Formula(3) follows by conditioning on whether any
unchanged leaf is sampled: focal gain is(1/k)(1-p)^(q-1)T, and each unchanged
member loses(1/k)p(1-p)^(q-2)T. Endpoints p=0,1 are evaluated directly.

Every finite local marginal is a polynomial in p of degree at most local-owner
count minus1. Choose integer D>=r-1 and at least the degree of EVERY low/high
individual marginal needed in W. The functions
                  1, f_0(p),...,f_(D-1)(p),
                  f_k(p)=p(1-p)^k,                       (4)
form a basis for polynomials of degree<=D: their distinct leading degrees are
0,1,...,D with nonzero leading coefficients. Let T=|W|>=1.

2. ONE FINITE FIXED-OWNER MODEL FOR EACH REPETITION COUNT N
For integer N>=1 set
 L=N^2 T,
 A=N(D L+N T+1).
Construct a disjoint sum of these components:
 (a) an additive anchor with value A at its fixed report;
 (b) for k=0,...,D-1, a sentinel star with center capacity L and k+1 distinct
     leaf owners of capacity L, all fixed at these reports;
 (c) one hard min_r component with r distinct owners, one unit row each;
 (d) N fresh copies of every test in W, initially at their LOW report.
All owner identities, actual endowments and eligibility are fixed in this model.
Only focal owners in the N*T test copies ever restore reports. Every test copy
has value at most1 throughout; sentinels have value L; the hard component has
value1. Thus throughout the sequence,
          A+D L+1 <= F_total <= A+D L+N T+1=A(1+1/N).   (5)
For the explicit threshold tests in Section1, every local value is1 and the
upper bound is attained at every profile. Constancy is unnecessary for general
lists W: the upper bound in(5) is all the mass argument uses.
All columns have arity<=r. Larger capacities here are allowed real reports;
alternatively keep capacities at most1 and multiply the public activity profits
of sentinel/anchor components by L and A, respectively.

Disjoint additivity implies each owner's marginal depends only on its own
component: all other component terms cancel pointwise before expectation.
In particular the anchor marginal is A for every p, and a designated leaf of
sentinel k has marginal L f_k(p). This remains exact despite all other owners
being sampled with the same p.

3. UNIFORM MASS AND APPROXIMATE MOMENT MONOTONICITY
Write beta(b)=mu_b([0,1]). The anchor payment is A beta(b). Nonnegativity and
funding with(5) imply at every sequence profile
                     beta(b)<=B_N:=K(1+1/N).             (6)
The packing LP is monotone in capacities and has nonnegative value. Hence a
local marginal difference satisfies0<=F(c+b_i)-F(c)<=F(c+b_i), bounded by
that component's full value. Each focal test component has full value at most1, so
each focal test owner's marginal is between0 and1, and its actual payment is
between0 and B_N. Every f_k lies in[0,1].

Execute the N cycles in order. In cycle v, take each fresh copy in W from low
to high by its ONE focal-owner restoration, leaving all other reports fixed.
There are R=N T elementary transitions. At one such transition x->x+, pair
the focal owner with the unchanged designated sentinel leaf k. This coalition
has size2<=s, so paired TCWM and the focal payment bound imply
 L int f_k d(mu_(x+)-mu_x)
 >=-[y_focal(x+)-y_focal(x)]>=-B_N.
Hence each sentinel moment M_k=int f_k dmu satisfies
                       Delta M_k>=-B_N/L.               (7)
Pairing with the unchanged anchor similarly gives
                       Delta beta>=-B_N/A>=-B_N/L,       (8)
since A>=L. Importantly, the sentinel coalition is different from the original
test's q-owner coalition; both are legitimate TCWM inequalities for the SAME
one-owner restoration and SAME mechanism.

4. A CYCLE WITH NEARLY FIXED MOMENTS
For any one of the D+1 moments in(4), its values lie in[0,B_N] and each negative
increment has magnitude at most B_N/L. Across all R transitions its total
negative variation is at most R B_N/L. Its total positive variation is at most
B_N+R B_N/L. Thus its total absolute variation is at most
                         B_N+2 R B_N/L.
Sum over the D+1 moments and partition into N cycles. At least one cycle has
TOTAL absolute variation of ALL these moments at most
 epsilon_N=(D+1)B_N(1/N+2T/L)
           =(D+1)B_N(1/N+2/N^2) ->0.                    (9)
Let nu_N be the measure at the start of that cycle. The integral of each basis
function at every profile in the selected cycle differs from its nu_N integral
by at most epsilon_N. Therefore the same is true for any fixed polynomial P
of degree<=D, up to the finite coefficient factor
 |int P d(mu_b-nu_N)|<=||P||_basis,1 epsilon_N.           (10)
The coefficients depend on W, not N.

5. ONE LIMIT MEASURE SATISFIES EVERY TEST IN A FIXED FINITE LIST
The masses of nu_N are at most K(1+1/N). Finite positive measures on compact
[0,1] with uniformly bounded mass have a weakly convergent subsequence, with
limit nu having mass<=K. For each test w in the chosen cycle, actual TCWM is
 int M_w^high dmu_high -int M_w^low dmu_low >=0,
where M_w denotes the protected coalition's local marginal-sum polynomial.
Use(10) at its two endpoints and let N tend to infinity along the subsequence.
Both polynomials have degree<=D, so
                    int(M_w^high-M_w^low)dnu>=0.         (11)
All tests in W hold simultaneously for the SAME nu.

The hard min_r component is fixed at its pure unit-row profile throughout.
Its r owners each have marginal p^(r-1). Their coalition is covered at every
profile (coverage has NO size restriction), hence
                       r int p^(r-1)dnu_N>=1.
Weak convergence gives the same inequality for nu:
                       int p^(r-1)dnu>=1/r.             (12)
Thus for EVERY finite list W of threshold tests, there exists a positive nu
with mass<=K satisfying all tests in W and the hard coverage moment(12).

6. COMPACTNESS OVER THE TEST LISTS
Let M be the compact set of finite positive measures on[0,1] with mass<=K and
moment(12). Each threshold test defines a closed subset of M because its
integrand is a continuous polynomial. Section5 proves the finite-intersection
property. Therefore there exists ONE positive measure nu in M satisfying ALL
finite threshold tests(3), for q=2 and q=s and every integer k,t.
This is a compactness argument over necessary inequalities, not an infinite
strategic owner population or an assumption that finite-model mechanisms agree.

7. SELF-CONTAINED SUPPORT RESTRICTION AND FUNDING CONSEQUENCE
For q=2,t=k-1, the tests give int(1-2p)p^k dnu>=0. Dominated convergence as
k grows yields -nu({1})>=0, so nu has no atom at1.

If nu((1/s,1))>0, put sigma=sup{x in(1/s,1):nu((x,1))>0}; then sigma>1/s.
A finite measure has at most countably many atoms, so choose a non-atom
a in(1/s,sigma). It satisfies nu((a,1))>0. Set t_k=floor(k a); since a<1,
0<=t_k<=k-1, so every chosen test is among the admissible finite tests. The binomial-tail polynomial T_(k,t_k)(p) tends to0 for p<a
and1 for p>a, by the law of large numbers (or Chebyshev's inequality). Its
value at a is irrelevant. Dominated convergence in the q=s tests gives
             int_(a,1) (1-p)^(s-2)(1-s p) dnu(p)>=0.
The integrand is strictly negative on(a,1), a contradiction. Together with the
endpoint argument this proves support(nu) subset[0,1/s]. Using(12),
 1/r<=int p^(r-1)dnu<=nu([0,1])/s^(r-1)<=K/s^(r-1),
which is exactly(2).

8. AUDIT NOTES AND SCOPE
- No independence or regularity of the report-to-measure map is assumed.
- All restoration comparisons occur in one finite fixed-owner model per N.
- Fixed reports of protected sentinel/anchor members are essential.
- Nonnegative measures give nonnegative payments and the focal bound B_N.
- The anchor gives an actual mass bound; it is not assumed as normalization.
- Other components cancel pointwise in each marginal, despite shared p.
- The only compactness objects are finite measures, not mechanism functions.
- No finite-n measure is asserted to have support<=1/s. Only the extracted
  limiting measure does; finite-model global measures can be fully adaptive.
- The original support lemma is accepted in input root64 and its detailed
  source SEMIVALUE_MIXTURE_OBSTRUCTION_GATE.txt. The new work is Sections2-6.
- Global report-dependent mixtures are ruled out at subquadratic order for
  r3; owner/component-specific adaptation remains open. Full OUT was not used.
- The harmonic barrier remains a useful elementary finite inequality, but its
  asymptotic architecture conclusion is subsumed if this rigidity proof passes.

9. CONNECTED TECHNOLOGY AND BOUNDED-REPORT VARIANTS
If the architecture allows a different global measure per PUBLIC connected
component, join every component to one new gate row by positive-profit arity2
activities, using one existing row from each component and the gate. A fixed
actual gate owner reports0 at every profile. All bridges are infeasible in
every sampled coalition and every comparison. The public incidence graph is
connected and all calculations remain exact; arity remains at most r>=2.
Thus splitting only by public connectivity does not evade the theorem.

If both per-coordinate reports<=1 and unit activity profits are desired, large
sentinel/anchor values can instead be implemented by disjoint unit copies with
SHARED actual owners: a designated sentinel owner holds one unit leaf coordinate
in each of L independent copies of the same star, and each companion owner
holds the corresponding center/leaf coordinate in all those copies. Its total
marginal is exactly L f_k while each report coordinate is at most1. An anchor
owner holds A independent unit additive resources. Integer L,A in the proof
make this exact; the resource count grows but the actual-owner count does not.
The theorem concerns general growing-resource arity-r technologies. Unlike the
old fixed-weight theorem, it does not claim that the adaptive-mixture barrier
already holds on the SINGLE fixed min_r technology.

10. OWNER-COUNT-DEPENDENT FUNDING FACTORS
The conclusion also has the stronger formulation
                  liminf_(n->infinity) kappa_n>=s^(r-1)/r,
where kappa_n bounds funding uniformly over arity-r technologies and profiles
with exactly n actual owners. Suppose instead that an unbounded sequence of n
has kappa_n<=K<s^(r-1)/r. For a fixed finite witness list W, the model with N
cycles has C_W N+C_D actual owners, with constants independent of N; large
resource values A,L do not require more owners. Choose N tending to infinity
while fitting within each n, and pad the remaining positions with fixed zero-
report owners. Local binomial marginal polynomials are unchanged exactly by
these padded owners. The mass bound becomes kappa_n(1+1/N)<=K(1+1/N), so the
same finite-list limit and compactness contradiction apply. The optional
bounded-coordinate/unit-profit implementation in Section9 also retains the
same owner count. For the cardinality extension, n only increases the sampling
accuracy. This does not impose consistency of mechanisms or parameter maps
across owner counts.
