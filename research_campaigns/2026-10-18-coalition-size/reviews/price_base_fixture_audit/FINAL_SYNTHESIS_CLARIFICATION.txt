HIGH-PROFILE EFFICIENT PRICES ARE UNIQUE

At an LLR high the aggregate report is B=(2,1,3). Feasibility and efficiency
give p>=0, p_1+p_2+p_3>=1, and 2p_1+p_2+3p_3=1. Subtracting the feasibility
sum from the equality yields p_1+2p_3<=0. Nonnegativity forces p_1=p_3=0,
then p_2=1. Thus p=(0,1,0) is the unique efficient nonnegative dual.
At an LRR high the symmetric argument gives the unique p=(1,0,0).
In both cases the minority owner's price allocation equals1. At low aggregate
B=(1,1,2), by contrast, all efficient prices have p_3=0 and p_1+p_2=1;
there is a genuine continuum of low-profile choices.

Consequently “free to choose among all efficient prices at each profile” is
correct, but high-profile price choice is vacuous in this fixture. The 3/2
cost uses high-price uniqueness together with the protected-incumbent TCWM
constraints. It survives all low-profile tie choices, but does not demonstrate
that a larger substitution technology with nonunique high-profile prices must
incur the same cost or an asymptotic loss. This limits extrapolation; it neither
proves nor disproves the proposed O(s) reserve route. A growing-family test
must again leave all efficient price choices free, rather than importing this
fixture's unique selector as an assumption.
