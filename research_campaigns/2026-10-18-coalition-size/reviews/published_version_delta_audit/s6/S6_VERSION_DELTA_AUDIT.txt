Working-version anchors and limits
---------------------------------
MODEL, printed/PDF p5:
For fixed (N,v), an allocation x is nonnegative and sum_i x_i <= v(N).
Efficiency is exact equality. A subgame (S,v_S) restricts the same v to S.
An allocation scheme X assigns each S subset N a nonnegative vector and
  sum_(i in S) X_i(S) = v(S).

DEFINITION 2.1, printed/PDF p6:
For every T subseteq K subseteq S subseteq N,
  sum_(i in K\T) X_i(S\T) + v(T) <= sum_(i in K) X_i(S).
The coalition is K; T leaves; K\T remains present. The left side includes
the remaining coalition members' allocation and the departed subgroup's
standalone surplus. Thus it explicitly compares coalition totals, including
members who remain in the allocation process. Transfers/redistribution are
part of the working paper's manipulation interpretation. It is not an
individual-only condition.

REPORT DOMAIN, same p5-6 anchors:
These objects vary participation by deleting agents from a fixed TU game.
Calling this a binary-participation restriction is an interpretation of
that domain, not the source's definition of a bundle-report mechanism.
The inspected anchors do not specify partial multirow resource reports,
an arbitrary bundle-report domain, or mechanisms eliciting such bundles.

REMARK 2.1, printed/PDF p6:
APAS is a core selection; setting K=T yields the core inequalities.
The remark says efficiency would follow even if not imposed on the scheme.
Read this within the stated allocation model, which already imposes the
feasibility upper bound. It does not establish efficiency for unrestricted
overpaying payoff vectors.

PROPOSITION 2.1, printed/PDF p6:
A population-monotonic allocation scheme is an absence-proof allocation
scheme. The displayed proof uses population monotonicity and efficiency.
