Frozen targets, calibration checks, and continuation gates

Mathematics: can the quadratic coalition-size dependence be beaten?

For a fixed public finite packing technology, write F(c) = max{π·x : x ≥ 0, Ax ≤ c}, with nonnegative coefficients and profits. Every positive-profit activity consumes at most three resource rows and none is resource-free. Each instance has a fixed finite set of actual owners and fixed resource eligibility; an owner may hold several rows. Both owner count and resource count may grow across instances. Only binding underreports are admissible, and all comparisons retain the same owners, technology and eligibility.

A single nonnegative payment rule must cover every coalition at every feasible real report profile: Σᵢ∈C yᵢ(b) ≥ F(Σᵢ∈C bᵢ). Incentive protection is required for every coalition of size at most s. If such a coalition restores a feasible bundle d, its total payment cannot decrease (TCWM). Full outside-production protection instead requires the payment increase to be at least F(Σᵢ∈C dᵢ). Protected members may restore zero. No extra payment floor or dummy-zero condition is imposed.

Σᵢ yᵢ(b) ≤ K(s) F(Σᵢ bᵢ),   Ω(s1/4) ≤ K₃(s) ≤ K₃OUT(s) ≤ 3s².

K₃(s) is the worst-instance optimal factor for TCWM under this model; K₃OUT(s) uses the stronger full-outside contract. These are the internally accepted campaign bounds, with historical novelty still unconfirmed. Funding is relative to total production, not to incremental cooperation gain. There is no efficiency requirement in this existence question; computational complexity must be reported separately.

The first route must handle the cost of changing the allocation or prices with the report. The second needs growing resource dimension: fixed three-input min already admits TCWM factor 2 for all coalition sizes.

Application: does sharing firm power headroom justify the stronger contract?

Candidate institution: a campus or colocation operator coordinates three independently controlled compute portfolios behind one shared delivery point. Owners contribute documented firm capacity entitlements and auditable load portfolios. The operator provides fixed infrastructure access and settlement; it must not silently become the sole strategic owner of an indispensable input, which can permit an efficient veto-owner allocation. A contribution or withdrawal is a registered rack block together with its associated capacity rights and load obligations, subject to legal and physical control. It is not an arbitrary fraction of a public sensor series. The withdrawn coalition may pool its removed blocks. The operator is the candidate sponsor, subject to an explicit revenue or avoided-cost case. This is a proposed institution, not an observed M100 ownership arrangement.

In the conditional Gaussian model, public fitted factors satisfy Lᵢ = μᵢ + ⟨xᵢ,Z⟩, with common Z ~ N(0,I) and a common reliability quantile zα > 0. Set tᵢ = (Cᵢ−μᵢ)/zα; admissible contributed bundles obey tᵢ ≥ ‖xᵢ‖₂. For coalition Q, modeled headroom is H_Q = zα[Σ_Q tᵢ − ‖Σ_Q xᵢ‖₂]. Both a withdrawn subportfolio and its retained remainder must satisfy the cone, as well as the actual operating contract. Private factor estimates, private opportunity costs, ramp constraints and network bottlenecks are not automatically covered.
