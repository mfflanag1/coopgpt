POSITIVE TRANSLATION-INVARIANT INTEGRATION AND ALL-RULE REGULARIZATION
Independent proof, 18 October 2026. Root and opposite-model review found no
invalidating gap; the transformed-rule hypotheses below were made explicit. This is a
set-theoretic existence argument, not an algorithm or a funding improvement.
It uses the same ordinary choice/compactness framework as the all-profile
Tychonoff argument. No assertion of countable additivity is made.

CLAIM TO CHECK
For a fixed finite actual-owner packing institution with bounded report boxes,
the infimum funding over arbitrary payment rules equals the infimum over Borel
rules, and equals the infimum over rules smooth on each positive coordinate
face. The constraints are nonnegative payments, every coalition's coverage,
and TCWM or full OUT for any fixed protected coalition family. Infimum equality
does not assert attainment or a measurable mechanism at the exact original K.

1. A TRANSLATION-INVARIANT POSITIVE EXTENSION OF LEBESGUE INTEGRATION
Fix the number m of eligible coordinates. Let E be the real vector space of
ALL bounded functions on R^m with support contained in some compact set; no
measurability is imposed. Define the upper integral
 p(f)=inf{ integral g : g is Lebesgue integrable and g>=f pointwise }.
This is finite on E: outside a compact support f=0, and a constant times the
indicator of that compact set supplies a finite majorant. It is sublinear and
agrees with the ordinary integral on integrable measurable members of E.
Hahn--Banach extends ordinary integration to a linear I0:E->R with I0(f)<=p(f).
For f>=0, p(-f)<=0, hence I0(f)>=0. Also
 |I0(f)| <= p(|f|) <= vol(K) ||f||_infinity                 (1)
whenever supp(f) subset K. Thus I0 is positive and extends ordinary integration.

Let X be all such positive linear extensions satisfying |I(f)|<=p(|f|).
It is a nonempty closed subset of the compact product
 product_(f in E) [-p(|f|),p(|f|)].
Linearity, positivity, agreement on measurable functions, and the bounds are
closed coordinate conditions. Translation sends X to itself, because p(|f|)
is translation invariant and ordinary Lebesgue integration is invariant.

For a finite list a1,...,ak of translations, average the translated I0 over
all sums j1*a1+...+jk*ak with 0<=jr<N. For each ar and each f, telescoping in
jr shows that the change under translation by ar has absolute value at most
 2 p(|f|)/N.
A product-compactness cluster point as N tends to infinity is fixed by every
translation in that finite list. Hence the closed fixed-point subsets of X
have the finite intersection property. Compactness supplies I in X invariant
under EVERY translation in R^m. In particular,
 I[f(.+a)]=I[f], I[phi]=integral phi for measurable phi in E,
and I satisfies (1). This explicit construction avoids needing an unproved
measurable selection, a countably additive extension, or a Fubini theorem.

2. DEFINE ONE INTEGRATED RULE, EVEN WHEN y IS NOT MEASURABLE
The fixed institution has finitely many owners/resources, finite coordinate
endowments, and finite packing value at total endowment. Its packing value F is
monotone, positively 1-homogeneous, and superadditive. These follow by scaling
and adding feasible production plans under A,pi>=0; the first two are used
explicitly in the transformed-rule argument. An arbitrary admissible
rule at finite factor K obeys 0<=y_i(b)<=K F(total endowment), uniformly on its
whole report box. Fix 0<alpha<1, and choose a nonnegative smooth kernel phi with
compact support inside (log alpha,0)^m and ordinary integral one. For t in R^m,
let T(t) multiply the eligible owner/resource coordinates by exp(t).
Define
 Y_i^alpha(b)=alpha^(-1) I_t[ phi(t) y_i(T(t)b) ].          (2)
Only values on the support of phi matter; define the product to be zero
outside that support. Every integrand belongs to E. The SAME fixed I and phi
are used at every report and for every owner. The integration is finitely
additive/linear, not an expectation under an alleged countably additive law.

For each t in supp(phi), alpha b <= T(t)b <= b coordinatewise. The rule
z_i^t(b)=alpha^(-1)y_i(T(t)b), including the displayed rescaling, obeys coverage
because F(sum_C T_i b_i)>=F(alpha sum_C b_i)=alpha F(sum_C b_i).
It obeys nonnegativity and the same TCWM conditions. Its total payment is at
most (K/alpha)F(sum_i T_i b_i)<=(K/alpha)F(sum_i b_i). For full OUT the SAME
T at both endpoints gives F(sum_C T_i d_i)>=alpha F(sum_C d_i), so the
rescaled increment has the required outside worth. The actual protected
coalition is fixed. Multiply these pointwise inequalities by phi and apply
the positive linear I, with I(phi)=1. Formula (2) preserves them all.
No owner is split or relabeled, and no zero-owner payment is assumed zero.

3. SMOOTHNESS WITHOUT DIFFERENTIATING THE ORIGINAL RULE
Fix a zero-coordinate pattern J (positive coordinates indexed by J) and write
the positive coordinates as exp(z). Define f(w) to be y_i(exp(w_J),0_else)
when those reports are in their boxes, and zero otherwise. It ignores the
other coordinates and is globally bounded by the endowment budget; it need
not be measurable. Put z_tilde=z on J and zero elsewhere. For z at the chosen
face and t in supp(phi), transformed reports remain admissible, and translation
invariance rewrites the expression in (2), before division by alpha, as
 I_t[phi(t) f(t+z_tilde)] = I_w[phi(w-z_tilde) f(w)].       (3)
For z in a small neighborhood, the translated kernels have a common compact
support K. Every difference quotient of the smooth compact kernel converges
uniformly on K to its derivative. Multiplying by bounded f and applying (1)
proves differentiation under I directly, with bound vol(K)||f||_infinity
times the uniform kernel error. Repeating proves C-infinity regularity in z.
No dominated convergence theorem for a finitely additive measure is used.

At an endowment upper boundary, the kernel's support is strictly below zero,
so the same formula remains admissible in a neighborhood extending beyond that
boundary. Zero coordinates are kept zero, and no continuity across zero faces
is asserted. There are finitely many zero-coordinate strata; each is Borel,
and the rule is continuous on each stratum. Therefore the resulting complete
rule is Borel on the report box.

4. INFIMUM CONSEQUENCE AND LIMITS
Every arbitrary finite-K feasible rule gives, for each alpha<1, a Borel
facewise-smooth feasible rule at K/alpha. Letting alpha approach one proves
the claimed equality of the three infima, since the reverse inclusions are
automatic. If F is identically zero, the zero rule handles the degenerate case.
This does not construct a computable rule, prove attainment at K, establish
continuity across faces, or improve any dependence on coalition size s.

It also need NOT preserve a restricted payment architecture. In particular,
an integrated rule need not retain a common scalar h(P/G), a specified price
selector, or a globally shared mixture representation. Consequently this
argument cannot simply remove regularity hypotheses from a lower bound proved
only inside such a restricted class. The equality concerns the unrestricted
contract classes specified above.

PRIMARY-SOURCE CHECK FOR THE CLASSICAL EXTENSION INGREDIENT
P. Bandyopadhyay and A. K. Roy, "Uniqueness of Invariant Hahn-Banach
Extensions," Extracta Mathematicae 22(2), 93--114 (2007), Sections 1--2,
printed pages 93--95:
 https://matematicas.unex.es/~extracta/Vol-22-2/22J2Band-congreso.pdf
Root supplied this source and the present reviewer opened the journal PDF on
18 October 2026. Page 93 states invariant dominated extension for an abelian
semigroup preserving a subspace, its functional, and a sublinear majorant;
pages 94--95 give a proof using a new sublinear majorant and Hahn--Banach.
Translations of R^m are an abelian group and preserve the upper integral in
Section 1. This source supports the classical extension step, not the claim
that this particular economic application has a priority claim. The explicit
averaging/compactness proof above is independently sufficient for the step.

REVIEW TARGETS
The load-bearing new ingredient relative to the Borel note is Section 1's
positive translation-invariant extension on bounded compact-support functions.
The two places needing particular scrutiny are the product-compactness fixed
point argument and the uniform-kernel derivative estimate in Section 3.
