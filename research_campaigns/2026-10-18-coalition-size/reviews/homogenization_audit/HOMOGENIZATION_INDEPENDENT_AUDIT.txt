AUDITED CLAIM AND DISPOSITION
The revised root/HOMOGENIZATION_AND_LOCAL_JET_LIMIT.txt is valid under the
campaign's finite packing / fixed actual-owner / binding-underreport contract.
The endowment-box invariance and exact-factor homogeneous replacement hold
for arbitrary rules, including nonmeasurable ones. Homogeneous facewise-smooth
rules have the same funding INFIMUM, not necessarily the same attainment.
The final pointwise claim is valid with its explicitly STATIC coverage/budget
tests. The revised text correctly distinguishes first-order budget conditions
at a kink and adds a valid local-germ statement for every factor greater than1.

No invalidating gap remains in the revised source. Two interpretation guards:
(a) "extension" preserves existence and admissibility, not agreement with the
    given input rule on its original box; an example appears below;
(b) "exact factor K" means no increase in the permitted funding bound. The
    replacement may happen to satisfy a strictly better bound.

1. ASSUMPTIONS USED
There are finitely many actual owners, resource rows and activities. The public
packing program is F(c)=max{pi*x: x>=0, A*x<=c}, with A,pi>=0 and no positive-
profit resource-free activity. Thus F is finite at finite capacities,
nonnegative, monotone, positively 1-homogeneous and superadditive. Finite LP
duality supplies a nonnegative optimal price p with A^T p>=pi and p*c=F(c).
The finite packing value is continuous on the nonnegative capacity orthant.
For example, its dual is a nonempty pointed polyhedron with finitely many
vertices and F is the minimum of the corresponding finitely many linear forms.
These arguments do not need the special arity-three restriction.

An eligible owner/resource coordinate has strictly positive finite endowment.
Every report between zero and that endowment is allowed. Ineligible coordinates
are fixed at zero. Every protected coalition consists of the SAME actual owners
at both endpoints; unchanged members are allowed. No dummy-zero axiom is used.
The conclusions apply to the unrestricted payment contract, not automatically
to a specified architecture, computability class or extra regularity class.

2. BOX INVARIANCE: EXACT QUANTIFIERS
For two boxes e,f with identical eligible coordinates and a nonempty eligible
set, take 0<c<=min_(eligible a) f_a/e_a. If y is admissible on [0,f], then
z_i(b)=y_i(c*b)/c is admissible on [0,e] with the same allowed funding bound.
For a legal comparison (b,b+d,C), both endpoints are multiplied by the SAME
constant c, outsiders remain identical, and c*d is supported on the same C.
Coverage and OUT right sides scale by c, and division by c restores them.
The reverse comparison gives equality of the optimal factors of the two boxes.
If the eligible set is empty, all reports and values are zero and c=1 suffices.

The common scalar is fixed before the report. A normalization depending on b
could change outsiders between endpoints and would not inherit this proof.
The auxiliary orthant does not authorize actual overreports; a rule constructed
there is restricted to the institution's original box for implementation.

3. NEGATIVE-INFINITY MEAN: COMPLETE CHECK
Use the established positive translation-invariant extension I of integration
on ALL bounded compact-support functions, with measurable indicators integrated
in the usual way. For bounded arbitrary f:R->R and T>0, set
 M_T(f)=T^(-1) I(f*1_[-T,0]).
This is defined without assuming f measurable: the product has compact support.
Positivity gives |M_T(f)|<=||f||_infinity; M_T(1)=1. Take a cluster point of
these functionals in the product over ALL bounded f, along a cofinal subnet
T->infinity. Positivity, linearity and normalization are closed finite-coordinate
conditions, so the cluster point M has them simultaneously.

Translation invariance of I rewrites M_T(f(.+a)) with interval [-T+a,a]. The
absolute difference is bounded by ||f|| times its symmetric-difference length,
divided by T, hence at most 2|a| ||f||/T. Every fixed translation identity
therefore holds for M. If f vanishes below L, then for sufficiently large T its
intersection with [-T,0] has length at most max(0,-L). Positivity gives M(f)=0.
No countable additivity, Fubini theorem, sequential compactness or common
convergent sequence is being presumed. Ordinary choice/compactness is used.

Useful precise consequence: if bounded h>=r on some negative halfline, then
M(h)>=r. The bounded function (h-r)_- vanishes on that halfline and is killed
by M; positivity applied to (h-r)_+ proves the claim. This is the exact reason
finite endpoint comparisons survive the zero extension below.

4. HOMOGENIZATION: ALL PROFILES AND ALL COMPARISONS
For finite eligible b, define
 g_i,b(t)=exp(-t)y_i(exp(t)b) if the WHOLE profile exp(t)b is in [0,e],
          0 otherwise.
Every eligible coordinate of e is positive, so each fixed b is in the original
box for every sufficiently negative t. Coordinates of b equal to zero impose
no bound on t; ineligible coordinates stay zero. For each fixed b,
 0<=g_i,b(t)<=K F(sum b)
for every t. The bound may depend on b; no uniform orthant bound is required.

Define Y_i(b)=M(g_i,b) with ONE M for every i and b. A coverage row or a legal
TCWM/OUT row involves only finitely many profiles. There is one negative
halfline on which all these scaled profiles belong to the box. On it, the
original row divided by exp(t) has exactly its original right-hand side:
exp(-t)F(exp(t)b_C)=F(b_C), or exp(-t)F(exp(t)d_C)=F(d_C).
The preceding positivity consequence passes the row. Funding and nonnegativity
hold for the g functions at all t and pass directly. This argument treats
every real profile and every allowed partial restoration, not merely a grid.
It keeps actual owner membership, outsider equality and coalition size fixed.

For lambda>0 the whole-profile in-box condition on the two sides is identical,
and for ALL t, including the zero-extension region,
 g_i,lambda*b(t)=lambda*g_i,b(t+log(lambda)).
Translation invariance yields Y_i(lambda*b)=lambda Y_i(b). For lambda=0,
funding gives Y_i(0)=0. If F(sum b)=0 at a nonzero b, funding likewise forces
all its payments to zero, as required. No zero-owner payment is imposed when
the whole profile has positive production.

This constructs a new homogeneous admissible rule on the eligible orthant.
It need not extend the ORIGINAL VALUES of y. For a concrete one-owner scalar
example on [0,1], let F(b)=b and y(b)=b+b^2. This satisfies coverage, full OUT
and factor2 funding. For each fixed b, g_b(t) tends to b as t->-infinity.
A positive normalized negative-tail mean agrees with any such limit, so its
homogenization is Y(b)=b, which differs from y on every b>0. Thus replacement
and existence extension, not interpolation of the input, is the correct scope.

If the finite-box optimum is attained, the same construction gives a homogeneous
rule at that allowed factor. Attainment is available from the separately proved
all-profile compactness argument; it is not supplied by measurable selection.
Y itself can remain nonmeasurable, and this step is not an algorithm.

5. SMOOTHING ON THE UNBOUNDED ORTHANT
The earlier Banach-kernel proof used a globally bounded input on a finite box.
For the present Y on an orthant, LOCAL boundedness is sufficient. On every
compact set of log coordinates and a fixed zero pattern, reports are bounded
above by some finite c*, so 0<=Y_i<=K F(sum c*) by monotonicity and funding.
All translated kernels in a sufficiently small log-neighborhood have a common
compact support K0. Multiplication by Y therefore keeps every integrand in the
space of bounded compact-support functions.

For a fixed smooth nonnegative kernel phi of integral1 supported inside
(log alpha,0)^m and one invariant positive I_m, use
 Y_i^alpha(b)=alpha^(-1) I_m[phi(t)Y_i(T(t)b)].
Each SAME diagonal contraction T is used at both endpoints. Since
alpha b<=T b<=b, positivity passes exact coverage and OUT, with factor K/alpha.
For each derivative order, the shifted-kernel difference quotient converges
uniformly on K0. The integration error is bounded by
vol(K0)*sup_(K0)|Y_i|*the uniform kernel error,
which tends to zero. This proves facewise C-infinity regularity directly and
does not invoke dominated convergence for a finitely additive functional.

Every T commutes with common scalar lambda. Homogeneity of Y and linearity of
I_m then preserve exact homogeneity of Y^alpha. Finitely many zero patterns
give a Borel rule by continuity on each relative stratum. At an all-zero
profile the zero rule handles the degenerate stratum. Euler's formula holds
on every positive-coordinate face; coordinates outside that face are absent.
No cross-face continuity or exact-factor smooth attainment is implied.

Letting alpha increase to1 proves equality of the unrestricted and homogeneous
facewise-smooth funding infima. In particular, the use of an orthant instead
of a box causes no unbounded-integrand gap. The construction can leave a
restricted mechanism architecture, and is not a computational smoothing method.

6. POINTWISE JETS: THE VALID CLAIM AND THE NECESSARY LIMIT
At a fixed b choose optimal p>=0, and assign
 u_i=p*b_i, H[i;j,a]=p_a if i=j, and0 otherwise.
Dual feasibility gives p*b_C>=F(b_C) for every C and sum u=F(sum b).
Euler holds, TCWM rates are nonnegative, and every full-OUT rate obeys
 sum_(i in C,j in C,a) H[i;j,a] d_ja=p*sum_C d_i>=F(sum_C d_i).
Thus the listed incentive cone + Euler + STATIC coverage/funding tests pass
at K1, for every s. Zero-coordinate reporting faces do not change this test.

These tests omit derivative consequences of nearby binding budgets. The
revised source now correctly includes the exact counterexample: one owner,
F(A,B)=min(A,B), b=(1,1), K1. An admissible C1 jet would have
 -H_A>=0, -H_B>=0                      [positive-direction budget tests],
  H_A+H_B=1                           [Euler].
Their sum gives 0>=1. This is an exact three-row contradiction, not a numerical
failure. A frozen optimal p=(theta,1-theta) passes the static tests but violates
at least one of those budget derivatives. The unrestricted rule y=F still has
factor1 and full OUT; the obstruction is exact smooth attainment, not a positive
funding gap. Consequently "integrability is the only missing condition" would
be too broad. The revised source no longer says that.

7. GENUINE LOCAL GERMS FOR EVERY FACTOR GREATER THAN1
The source's added local-germ statement is valid and strengthens its properly
scoped limitation. Fix b with F(B)>0, an optimal p, and kappa>1. The continuous
ratio p*B'/F(B') equals1 at B. On some relative neighborhood U of b it is
defined and less than kappa. The actual linear formula z_i(b')=p*b_i' is a
smooth homogeneous rule as a formula. Dual feasibility supplies coverage
globally, and for ANY legal comparison within U its coalition payment increase
is exactly p*sum_C d_i>=F(sum_C d_i). Funding is at most kappa F(B') on U.
Thus local germs at productive points alone cannot force a funding gap above1.

U need not be a downward report box, and these independently chosen germs
need not agree on overlaps. Nothing here constructs one rule satisfying all
global budget comparisons, admissions or zero-worth boundary conditions.
If a comparison endpoint is on a zero-coordinate face but remains in U, the
linear formula is still well-defined and the stated endpoint inequality still
holds; no general compatibility between separately chosen face germs follows.
