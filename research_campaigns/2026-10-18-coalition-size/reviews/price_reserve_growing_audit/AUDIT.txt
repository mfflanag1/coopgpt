1. BINARY UNIT-WORTH PRICE-DOMINATION FRONTIER

The domain remains the entire downward binary ideal F(1_S)<=1 of a finite
linear3-uniform unit-profit/unit-coefficient technology, with one fixed pure
unit owner per row. All coalitions are covered. Incentives protect every
actual C of size at mosts, including inactive/unchanged members. Efficient
price domination is an additional architecture. No all-real extension follows.

The accepted ideal characterization gives a single supported triple or a
common-cap star with disjoint private pairs, plus possible isolated active rows.
For m>=2, summing its m edge-price inequalities and subtracting the efficiency
identity gives (m-1)p_cap minus the isolated-positive-price sum >=m-1.
Nonnegativity then forces p_cap>=1; efficiency forces equality and all other
positive-capacity prices zero. Thus every admissible price-base rule pays its
cap owner at least1. Absent-row prices are not forced to zero and are not
silently charged in the efficiency identity.

The proposed upper selects globally feasible dual witnesses on the FULL
public technology: absent rows have price1, with price1/3 on the sole supported
triple or price1 on the multibranch cap. Unsupported activities contain an
absent row. At zero worth every activity is unsupported, so absent-row prices
also prove feasibility at cost0. The payments dominate these allocations.
Coverage holds for every coalition: a productive subcoalition needs the full
sole triple or the multibranch cap and has worth at most1. Paying other owners
zero is a choice in this upper, not an axiom in the lower.

The single-restoration classification remains valid. Since the final profile
lies in the ideal, its common cap lies in an old supported activity and cannot
be the restoring owner. Linearity permits at most one new edge. On a new edge,
the restorer and previously isolated mate each gain q_(m+1); the cap gains
(including the1/3-to1 jump when m=1); only2m old private owners can lose.
For any protected C the worst possible sum is bounded below by
 q_(m+1)-min(s-1,2m)(q_m-q_(m+1))=0.
The same C is used through simultaneous binary restorations. Intermediate
profiles stay in the ideal. For full OUT, a productive low profile already
contains the final common cap, hence the restored bundle alone has worth0.
For a zero-worth low profile, all old payments vanish and high coverage
directly gives the possibly positive outside-worth increment. This endpoint
argument does not incorrectly add zero OUT increments along a path.

Funding is1 at a sole edge, and1+2m q_m at m>=2. At s1 all later q vanish,
so A1=1. For s>=3 the actual maxima are m=s-1,s. At s2 the formal m1
expression is not the actual sole-edge payment: the upper peaks only at m2,
with A2=5/3. The note explicitly makes this exception. Algebra gives
 A_s=1+2s q_s=1+[s/(s-1)](B_s-1),
 A_s-B_s=2q_s=O(s^(-1/2)),
and the same square-root leading constant as B_s.

The matching lower retains one actual pure owner per projective resource and
the accepted basis v,e1,...,es. The entire ideal and full technology are
invariant under joint GL resource/owner permutations. At each profile choose
any efficient price witness and average its transformed coordinates together
with the transformed payments. Feasibility, efficiency and owner domination
are all linear, so this averaging is legitimate. The accepted stabilizers give
the three-owner seed q1>=1/3 and transitivity of the2m active private owners.
Every ladder comparison retains the restorer's old nonnegative payment and
at mosts-1 passive owners. At m=s>=2 cap-price forcing adds cap payment>=1;
budget then yields A_s. For s1 ordinary coverage gives the matching lower1.
Thus the worst restricted optima for TCWM and full OUT are both exactly A_s.
This does not combine pure ownership with the linear-owner-count realization.

2. GLOBALLY ONE-LAYER COMPARISON SYSTEMS

The accepted charging lemma applies to arbitrary finite nonnegative packing
technologies, arbitrary mixed actual owners, real reports and optimal primal/
dual choices. Complementary slackness gives h_i>=u_i>=0, sum u=V and
sum h<=rV. The deletion argument gives h(C)>=V-F(B-b_C) for EVERY coalition.
At zero-capacity rows b_ia=0 and no positive production can consume that row;
the declared0/0 convention is valid. Zero-profit activities may be discarded
when choosing the primal; they do not contribute to h or its arity bound.

Assign u to every source/isolated profile and h to every sink. The GLOBAL
source/sink partition makes this a well-defined assignment. Both choices
dominate the same efficient price allocation and cover every coalition.
For a comparison protecting the same actual C, outsider bundle O is fixed:
 u_l(C)=F(B_l)-p_l.O <=F(B_l)-F(O).
Since B_h=B_l+delta and F is superadditive,
 u_l(C)+F(delta)<=F(B_h)-F(O)<=h_h(C).
This is exactly full OUT for the assigned payments. All signs are correct;
simultaneous multiresource changes and unchanged members are retained in C.
At V=0 all u,h vanish, and the same inequality covers both zero endpoints
and zero-to-positive comparisons. Reflexive comparisons are automatic.

The factor-r existence claim is therefore valid on the selected finite
profile/comparison system. A per-coalition source/sink or forest normal form
is insufficient: one actual profile may receive one coalition label and emit
another. The proposed assignment would then require both h and u there;
outgoing reserves cannot be dropped. The note correctly retains this global
hypothesis and makes no rule on the full real box, general factor-r upper,
or unrestricted funding improvement claim.
