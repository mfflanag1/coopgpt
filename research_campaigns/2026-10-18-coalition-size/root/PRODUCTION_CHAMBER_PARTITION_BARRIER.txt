PRODUCTION-CHAMBER PARTITIONS ALONE FORCE OWNER-COUNT FUNDING
Root derivation, 18 October 2026. Candidate pending independent review.
Restricted representation theorem, not an unrestricted funding lower bound.

THEOREM
Let n>=1 actual owners each have a strictly positive report interval for every
one of r>=2 input resources. Reports are independent coordinate-box underreports.
Let T_a=sum_i b_(i,a) and F(T)=min_(1<=a<=r) T_a. Use exactly the r closed
production chambers P_a={b:T_a<=T_c for every c}, intersected with the report
box. Suppose a nonnegative payment rule y is continuous on the box, affine
on each P_a, and obeys total funding sum_i y_i<=K F(T) for some finite K.
Then each owner payment has the form y_i(b)=c_i F(T), with one constant c_i>=0.
If the rule also covers every coalition, then each c_i>=1 and K>=n.
Conversely y_i=F(T) satisfies coverage, all-coalition full OUT and factor n.
Thus the optimum within this supplied production-chamber representation is
exactly n. This is already true without imposing any incentive inequalities.

PROOF
Fix an owner i and a chamber a. Write its affine formula as
 y_i(b)=h_i^a+sum_(j,c) L_(i;j,c)^a b_(j,c).
All reports with every a-coordinate zero belong to P_a and have F=0. Finite
funding and nonnegativity force every y_i to vanish there. Those reports form
a full coordinate box in the remaining variables, with each side positive.
An affine function zero throughout that box has h_i^a=0 and all coefficients
L_(i;j,c)^a=0 for c!=a. Consequently on P_a,
                    y_i(b)=sum_j l_(i,j)^a b_(j,a).           (1)

Choose distinct resources a,c. At a sufficiently small positive value t, put
T_a=T_c=t and every other resource total strictly above t, still well inside
all endowment bounds. Independently distribute t over the n owners' a reports
and over their c reports, with each owner-coordinate strictly positive. Such
distributions exist because every endowment is positive. This profile lies
in P_a intersect P_c. The two formulas agree by continuity, hence
       sum_j l_(i,j)^a b_(j,a)=sum_j l_(i,j)^c b_(j,c).        (2)
Holding the c distribution fixed and varying the a distribution in the
relative interior of its simplex forces l_(i,j)^a to be constant in j.
The same holds for c; equation (2) equates the two constants. For n=1 this
last equality alone suffices. Running over all pairs of resources gives one
constant c_i common to every j and every chamber. Equation (1) is then c_i T_a
on P_a, which equals c_i F(T). Nonnegativity at a productive profile gives
c_i>=0. All arguments used one fixed box and the same actual owner roster.

For each actual owner i, choose a legal report with that owner alone supplying
the same sufficiently small epsilon>0 in every resource and all other reports
zero. Singleton coverage gives c_i epsilon>=epsilon, hence c_i>=1. At any
productive report total funding now requires K>=sum_i c_i>=n.

For the matching rule y_i=F(T), coalition coverage follows from monotonicity:
sum_(i in C)y_i=|C|F(T)>=F(sum_(i in C)b_i). For a nonempty fixed coalition C
restoring d>=0, its payment increment is |C|[F(T+sum_C d_i)-F(T)], which is
at least F(sum_C d_i) by superadditivity. This proves full OUT for every C,
with all real profiles and factor n. The empty coalition is trivial.

CONSEQUENCE AND LIMITS
Refining only as far as the production LP's optimal-price chambers cannot
discover an owner-independent upper mechanism, even for the two-input min
technology. The general finite piecewise-affine certificate theorem remains
correct; its partition must have additional structure to escape this example.
An ownership-sensitive refinement or a non-affine formula can escape the
stated class. No impossibility for all such alternatives is asserted.

This does not contradict existing bounded mechanisms for min production.
It does not prove a necessary number of cells for an arbitrary refinement,
nor address discontinuous or merely facewise-smooth selectors. The unrestricted
campaign lower/upper interval is unchanged. Historical priority is unconfirmed.
