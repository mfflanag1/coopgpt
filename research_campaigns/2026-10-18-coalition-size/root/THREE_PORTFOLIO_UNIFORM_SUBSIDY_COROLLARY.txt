THREE PORTFOLIOS: A CONDITIONAL 5/4 UNIFORM-FUNDING LOWER
18 October2026. Routine application of the project's existing min2 lower,
not a new general mathematical frontier or a measured application result.
Independent review pending. The sealed application access gate is unchanged.

WHY THIS COROLLARY IS USEFUL
The application witness proves a particular efficient allocation is not
monotone; that alone does not prove subsidy is necessary. A stronger, but
explicitly conditional, statement follows from an already recorded theorem.
It concerns a uniform contract over a fixed finite owned-unit domain, not
the minimum payment at the observed/full profile of a real facility.

FIXED THREE-OWNER INSTITUTION IN THE ALGEBRAIC MODEL
Use one Gaussian factor and normalized reserve F(t,x)=t-|x| on t>=|x|.
Each of three fixed owners owns precisely two publicly specified units:
  L=(1/2,1/2), R=(1/2,-1/2).
Each may retain any subset of its OWN two units, so its report is one of
0,L,R,L+R. Its withdrawn complement is owned and cone-feasible. No identities
or eligibility change. These four reports and all 64 joint profiles are fixed
before mechanism choice. Coalitions can coordinate allowed partial withdrawals.

For totals containing a copies of L and b copies of R,
  F((a+b)/2,(a-b)/2)=min(a,b).
Thus this is exactly the binary min2 report domain by a positive-on-cone public
coordinate transformation, without inventing strategic owners. At full reports
each owner alone produces1, and every pair produces2. There is no fixed owner
whose participation is indispensable throughout the domain. Some underreported
profiles do have a pivotal owner; that does not provide a fixed-veto allocation
valid over all profiles.

CLAIM
Every nonnegative payment rule covering every coalition at every feasible
profile and satisfying TCWM for every pair has worst-case funding factor
at least5/4 on this finite three-owner institution. Hence the same lower holds
for full OUT. It does not distinguish optimal individual-OUT from coalition-OUT
funding and does not give a sharp full-OUT constant.

PROOF (EXISTING MIN2 ARGUMENT, SPELLED OUT IN RESERVE UNITS)
The finite domain is invariant under owner permutations and simultaneous
exchange of L and R. Average a candidate rule over these symmetries, relabeling
payments back. Coverage, nonnegativity, pair TCWM and any funding factor kappa
are preserved by finite averaging. It suffices to bound this symmetric rule;
the original need not be anonymous.

At profile (L,R,0), payments are (a,a,z), z>=0. Grand coverage gives2a+z>=1,
so the fixed pair C={owner1,owner3} gets a+z>=1/2.
Restore owner3 from0 toL, leaving owner1 unchanged and owner2 atR. At(L,R,L),
symmetry gives payments(x,y,x). Pair TCWM yields2x>=a+z>=1/2, hence x>=1/4.
Owner2 together with either L-owner produces1, so x+y>=1. Total production
is1 and total payment2x+y>=1+x>=5/4. Therefore kappa>=5/4. QED.

This averaging proves an all-mechanism lower for the fixed finite domain.
For a nonsymmetric original mechanism the expensive profile may be in the orbit
under owner permutations AND global L/R exchange; no claim is made that this
named profile must be expensive for every rule.
Both key profiles have positive production, so no zero-production budget or
dummy-zero convention is being smuggled into the proof.

ECONOMIC INTERPRETATION AND LIMITS
The two units have opposite public factor exposures and zero standalone
headroom at the chosen reliability quantile. Owning them together creates
positive standalone reserve. The finite domain presupposes that an owner can
actually detach either matched capacity-and-load unit and fulfill its outside
obligations. Public telemetry does not establish these rights, perfect opposite
exposures, costs, deliverability, sponsor value, or Gaussian tail accuracy.
Those missing conditions remain exactly the application gate's limitations.
The lower is on TOTAL funding relative to production at some report, so it
requires at least a25% external premium there if production revenue funds the
unit baseline. It is not an empirical subsidy estimate, an expected subsidy,
or a gain-only premium guarantee, and not a claim of a deficit at full reports.
