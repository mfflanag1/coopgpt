AGGREGATE COMMON RESOURCE PRICES: SINGLETON INCENTIVES CAN FORCE ALL-SIZE COST
Root derivation18October2026. Pending independent audit. A corollary of the
projective lower, not a new unrestricted theorem or a historical novelty claim.

CLASS AND DOMAIN
Fix n>=2 actual owners, all with the SAME fully eligible real report box[0,1]^d.
Consider any rule
  y_i(b)=p(B).b_i,  B=sum_i b_i,                         (1)
where the same finite resource-price vector p(B) is used for every owner and
depends on the aggregate B, public technology and fixed institution only.
It may depend arbitrarily on n, the actual endowments, s and B. It may be
discontinuous, nonlinear and otherwise unrestricted. It may not depend on the
partition of B among actual owners. No price-optimality or efficient-core
selection condition is imposed. Assume the campaign's nonnegative payments,
all-coalition coverage, singleton TCWM and funding bound on ALL real profiles.

FIXED-ROSTER AGGREGATION LEMMA
For this class, singleton TCWM implies TCWM for coalitions of ANY size on
every comparison whose two aggregate endpoint vectors satisfy0<=B<=B'<=1
coordinatewise. Indeed let C be any original protected group, put
c=sum_C b_i, c'=sum_C b'_i, and w=sum_(i outside C)b_i. Then c<=c',
c,c',w belong to[0,1]^d, and B=w+c, B'=w+c'. Form two AUXILIARY profiles
on the SAME n-owner institution: actual owner1 reports c then c', actual
owner2 reports w unchanged, and every other actual owner reports0. These
reports are admissible because actual eligibility is fully shared and the
unit box includes every coordinatewise subvector of1. Only actual owner1
restores; no owner roster, endowment or incentive definition is changed.
Singleton TCWM at those auxiliary profiles gives
  p(B').c' >= p(B).c.
By(1) these are exactly the two coalition sums for C in the original profiles.
The analogous implication holds for full OUT if singleton full OUT is assumed,
with added right side F(c'-c), but TCWM suffices below.

CONSEQUENCE OF THE PROJECTIVE WITNESS
The binary-projective profiles and every profile in their finite symmetry orbit
have at most one unit on any resource row. Every restoring comparison used in
the square-root proof therefore satisfies the lemma's endpoint condition.
Consequently its entire arbitrary-coalition ladder applies to(1), EVEN IF the
advertised protected size is only s=1. With M>=1, n=2M+1 and
d=2^(M+1)-1, that proof forces
  K >= 1+[2(M-1)/(3M)] Q_M,
  Q_M=4^M/[2*binom(2M,M)].                              (2)
For M tending to infinity this is Omega(sqrt(n)), also Omega(sqrt(log(d+1))).
The common-price class is preserved by the resource automorphism average:
the averaged price at B is the corresponding transformed average of p(gB).
Owner-permutation averaging is redundant because(1) already treats owner
labels consistently. The original rule is costly somewhere in the finite
orbit, not necessarily at the named symmetric representative.

For fixed arity r>=3, put k=r-1 and use the accepted binary-circuit version:
n=1+kM, d=2^(1+(k-1)M)-1. The same argument yields
  K >= 1+[k(M-1)/((k+1)M)] Q_(k,M),
  Q_(k,M)=Gamma(M+1)Gamma(1+1/k)/Gamma(M+1/k),
hence Omega_r(n^((r-2)/(r-1))) along these institutions, already for singleton
incentives. This is a routine transfer of the parent lower, not an improved
unrestricted size exponent. Padding counts is unnecessary for this conclusion.

MECHANISM-DESIGN CONSEQUENCE AND SCOPE
No common resource price depending only on total supply can have a finite
owner-and-resource-independent factor even for individual TCWM in this broad
fully eligible arity3 class. In contrast the general campaign upper at s=1
is3, so such prices omit information that a bounded general rule must retain.
An upper construction using prices must use the actual partition into bundles,
owner-specific prices, nonlinear bundle payments, or another mechanism.

Do not extend this conclusion to prices depending on the FULL report profile,
to typed eligibility that prevents the auxiliary aggregate reports, or to a
fixed-resource institution. On a typed common-cap institution a fixed cap-owner
payment can have factor1; that does not contradict this construction. The
aggregation lemma is an algebraic consequence of(1), and resembles established
merging/partition-invariance ideas. Historical attribution needs its own source
comparison. The quantitative obstruction derives from the new parent theorem.
