MULTIPLICATIVE REGULARIZATION OF A MEASURABLE ADMISSIBLE RULE
Root derivation18October2026. Elementary tool, not a funding-bound improvement,
not a claim about arbitrary nonmeasurable mechanisms, and not a novelty claim.

Fix one finite actual-owner institution with coordinatewise report boxes
0<=b_i<=e_i, fixed public production F that is nonnegative, monotone and
degree-one homogeneous, and a BOREL MEASURABLE payment rule y. Assume nonnegative
payments, coverage of every coalition, TCWM (or full OUT) for the specified
fixed coalition family, and total funding at most K F(sum_i b_i), for finite K.
Fix0<alpha<1. For every eligible owner/resource coordinate choose independently
a random multiplier t_ij in(alpha,1), with a smooth density whose log-transform
has compact support inside(log alpha,0). Let T_i be the corresponding diagonal
matrix. Define
  y_i^alpha(b)=alpha^(-1) E_T y_i(T_1 b_1,...,T_n b_n).    (1)
Only reports are transformed. Actual owners, endowments, eligibility and the
public production technology remain fixed. This defines one deterministic
payment rule by integration, not a random realized-payment contract.

EXACT CONTRACT PRESERVATION
For each fixed T the transformed reports are admissible, and
  alpha sum_C b_i <= sum_C T_i b_i <= sum_C b_i.
Coverage for the original rule and monotone homogeneity imply
  sum_C y_i(Tb)>=F(sum_C T_i b_i)>=alpha F(sum_C b_i).
Taking expectations and dividing by alpha proves exact coverage of (1).

If b'=b+d is a restoration supported on a protected coalition C, the SAME T
at both endpoints leaves all transformed outsiders fixed, while transformed
members restore T_i d_i. Thus original TCWM gives the same coalition-sum
monotonicity after expectation. For full OUT it gives the stronger inequality
  sum_C[y_i(Tb')-y_i(Tb)]>=F(sum_C T_i d_i)
                                 >=alpha F(sum_C d_i).
Hence(1) satisfies the SAME full OUT inequality, without any approximation or
change to the restored actual owners. Unchanged protected members stay unchanged.

Finally, nonnegativity and the original budget give
  sum_i y_i^alpha(b)<=alpha^(-1) K E F(sum_i T_i b_i)
                    <=(K/alpha)F(sum_i b_i).
No concavity, exchange of an optimization with expectation, or assumption of
zero payments to a currently zero-report owner is used. The integral is finite
because every integrand lies between0 and K F(sum_i b_i).

REGULARITY CONSEQUENCE AND ITS PRECISE LIMIT
On any fixed coordinate face, keep its zero coordinates zero and write all
positive coordinates as exp(z). The payment in(1) is convolution of the locally
bounded Borel function z -> y(exp(z),zeros) with a compactly supported smooth
kernel in the log coordinates. It is therefore smooth on the relative positive
interior of that face. This last statement can also be checked directly by
writing the integral with kernel phi(w-z): differentiating the compact smooth
kernel is justified against the locally bounded integrand. At a report-box
upper boundary, support strictly below0 gives an open neighborhood on which
the same formula uses only admissible reports.

The resulting rule need NOT be continuous between different coordinate faces.
No such boundary continuity is asserted. At zero-production profiles it is
zero by its budget; at positive-production faces individual payments may have
different limits. Differential proofs still need their actual seam checks.

If the infimum funding factor is restricted to Borel measurable rules, allowing
only rules smooth on every relative positive coordinate face does not change
that infimum: the restricted class is a subclass, and(1) approaches any finite
admissible factor as alpha tends to1. Attainment is not claimed. The campaign's
unrestricted lower allows arbitrary rules without measurability; this lemma
does NOT identify its optimum with the Borel optimum. It is useful only as a
regularity reduction within a stated measurable design class.

The integration may be computationally intractable and gives no finite exact
algorithm. However, each FIXED multiplier array T in[alpha,1] separately gives
an admissible transformed/rescaled rule by the same pointwise argument above.
Consequently any fixed finite convex mixture of these rules also preserves
exact coverage, incentives and K/alpha funding. This includes samples drawn
once and then reused at EVERY report, or exact nonnegative convex quadrature.
Such a finite mixture need not be smooth. Fresh independent samples at each
report, signed quadrature, or inexact numerical payment evaluation need not
preserve the all-profile inequalities. This correction was identified by the
independent regularity audit and verified by root from the pointwise proof.
The observation is not an additional paper or a new research-success gate.
