EXACT TWO-PARTY SETTLEMENT AND THE ECONOMIC APPLICATION GATE
17 October 2026. Routine contract corollary and implementation scope.
Classical two-player core/Shapley/Nash ingredients; no static novelty claimed.
All-profile outside-production statement is proved below. Root review requested.

1. TWO ACTUAL OWNERS NEED NO SUBSIDY, EVEN UNDER FULL OUT
Let F be any nonnegative monotone concave degree-one homogeneous production
function, with F(0)=0. There are exactly TWO fixed actual owners, reporting
bundles b_1,b_2. Put f_i=F(b_i), T=F(b_1+b_2), G=T-f_1-f_2>=0.
Choose a fixed public bargaining weight lambda in[0,1], BEFORE reports, and pay

  y_1=f_1+lambda G,
  y_2=f_2+(1-lambda)G.                                  (1)

These payments are nonnegative, exactly efficient, and cover every coalition.
They have FULL outside-production protection for every nonempty coalition:

 sum_{i in Q}y_i(b)-sum_{i in Q}y_i(b-d_Q)>=F(sum_{i in Q}d_i).             (2)

Proof for singleton1. Write y_1=(1-lambda)F(b_1)
+lambda[F(b_1+b_2)-F(b_2)]. Both bracketed terms increase by at least F(d_1)
when owner1 restores d_1, by superadditivity. Their convex combination does
too. The proof for owner2 is identical. For Q={1,2}, total payment is exactly
F(C), so its increase is at least F(d_1+d_2), again by superadditivity. These
are all possible nonempty incentive coalitions. Ties, zero reports, boundary
discontinuities and partial withdrawals are included. QED.

Only nonnegativity and superadditivity are used in this proof; concavity and
homogeneity specify the program's common production class. No differentiability
or oracle approximation is needed. The owners remain fixed throughout.

At lambda=1/2, (1) is the classical two-player Shapley/equal-gain allocation.
It also equals the symmetric Nash bargain with standalone disagreement values.
Other fixed lambda give the corresponding fixed weighted split. The new
research claim should not be the static formula. The useful consequence is
that the program's strong withholding contract is efficient in this ownership
regime. A welfare improvement or stabilization subsidy cannot be manufactured
merely by labeling two hardware configurations as two independent owners.

2. FLOORS AND BARGAINING WEIGHTS ARE DIFFERENT CONTRACTS
For two owners, the full-owner grand marginal is M_i=f_i+G. The equal split
always gives y_i>=M_i/2. More generally both floors y_i>=theta M_i hold for
all profiles if theta<=min(lambda,1-lambda). Therefore theta<=1/2 can be met
efficiently by lambda=1/2. If theta>1/2, a complementary pure-owner profile
with f_1=f_2=0,T=1 demands payments at least theta from each owner, so no
efficient rule can meet both floors. This obstruction is from the added
bargaining floors, not from ordinary standalone participation or withholding.

If one weakens to individual-only OUT, then for theta>=1/2 the rule
y_i=(1-theta)f_i+theta M_i has total S+2theta G, covers coalitions and has
individual OUT. It generally fails grand-coalition monotonicity: at the pure
complementary profile total is2theta, whereas restoring one owner's missing
complement can leave production unchanged and make gains zero, forcing an efficient
total1. This is a contract comparison, not a recommended extra entitlement.

3. PUBLIC COSTS CAN BE SETTLED WITHOUT DOUBLE COUNTING THE OUTSIDE BASELINE
Suppose physical activities x have public gross service revenue pi dot x,
resource use Ax, and owners have a public linear opportunity value ell>=0
for resources retained outside the service. Let

  F_net(C)=max{(pi-ell A) dot x: x>=0, Ax<=C},

with negative-profit activities omitted. An optimal plan x* uses each owner's
resource quantities u_i, with sum_i u_i=Ax*, u_i<=b_i. Give owner i CASH

  cash_i=ell dot u_i+y_i,

where y is a mechanism for F_net. Its unused resources retain baseline value
ell dot(b_i-u_i), so its total utility is ell dot b_i+y_i. Costs and baseline
are not paid twice. Total cash less gross service revenue is exactly

  sum_i cash_i-pi dot x*=sum_i y_i-F_net(C).              (3)

Thus the two-owner rule (1) has zero external deficit. More generally a
gain-only rule sum y<=S_net+K G_net has external deficit at most
(K-1)G_net, where S_net=sum_i F_net(b_i). This denominator is incremental
service surplus beyond the owners' actually feasible standalone production.

Under withholding, the owner's linear baseline on ALL its resources is
unchanged: withheld resources retain their baseline outside. If a withdrawing
coalition can run the same public activities on its withheld bundle, it adds
net surplus F_net(d). The mechanism's OUT inequality therefore gives the
intended economic protection. This is not a private-cost or private-value
truthfulness claim. A physical plan and a resource-use attribution u_i are
needed to execute cash settlement; a min-linear value oracle alone supplies
utilities and budgets, not an allocation or metered energy record.

4. EXACT SETTLEMENT IS COMPUTABLE FOR A SMALL FIXED OWNER SET
For finite F(c)=min_{k<=K}w_k dot c with rational public rows/reports, (1)
needs only F(b_1),F(b_2),F(C) and rational arithmetic when lambda is rational.
It can be accompanied by exact standalone, coalition-coverage, and budget
certificates. Its all-profile incentive guarantee is the theorem, not a
finite list of simulated deviations.

For larger fixed n, the gain-only modified-mask rule can also be evaluated
exactly using a fixed rational a in(0,1]. Cache F(C_B) and the fixed-priority
minimizing row for every one of the2^n masks. The weight of one particular
mask B with k members is

  W_B(a)=sum_{l=0}^k (-1)^l binom(k,l) a^(n-k+l)/(n-k+l+1).

These rational weights are nonnegative and sum to one across all masks.
Use the payments in GAIN_ONLY_MASK_AND_DIMENSION_THEOREM.txt equation(6)
and their fixed weighted average. Evaluation requires2^n value/support
queries, plus the owner-row dot products; it is exponential in owner count.
No performance claim follows from testing this on a small Mac instance.
The parameter/rule choice must be fixed using public class information, not
chosen after seeing whichever realized payment vector is cheapest.

5. THE APPLICATION GATE
The mathematically informative AI-infrastructure case needs at least three
independently strategic ownership interests AND an ownership/access structure
that avoids an efficient fixed veto-owner allocation. Existing campaign
boundaries remain in force:

* One indispensable power owner can receive all service surplus efficiently
  under the floor-free contract; imposing arbitrary hardware marginal floors
  creates a different economic problem.
* Hardware providers that each control their own GPU/memory complements
  compress to one effective service capacity per provider. Counting technical
  slices as separate strategic owners would give the wrong model.
* With many bundled providers and independent public power entitlements,
  the two-pool or public source-price model may still be informative. The
  existing exponential construction gives individual OUT and at most
  [1/(2e)] times actual pooling gains in external subsidy; full coalition OUT
  is a materially stronger and generally more expensive commitment.
* Private bids, power dispatch rights, feasible standalone service, metered
  energy and request-level latency must be specified or measured separately.
  No such real-cluster evidence is created by this theorem or its checker.

The next useful project is to freeze an institution with genuine independent
withholding rights and an explicit public-value model, then compare the weaker
individual contract with the strong coalition contract on that SAME institution.
A two-owner pilot is a good settlement sanity check, but its subsidy problem
is mathematically solved by (1); it is not a fresh hard research target.
