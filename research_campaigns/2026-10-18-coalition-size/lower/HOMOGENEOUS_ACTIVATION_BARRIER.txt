HOMOGENEOUS ACTIVATION CERTIFICATE BARRIER
18 October 2026. Method-specific structural lemma, not a payment upper bound.

The binary-circuit lower has exponent (r-2)/(r-1). The following elementary
count shows why one cannot raise that exponent merely by enlarging each
symmetric private group while keeping the same zero-to-productive activation
and one-orbit TCWM telescoping argument.

FINITE HITTING-SET LEMMA
Let H be a nonempty hypergraph on g vertices. Suppose its automorphism group
acts transitively on vertices and each edge has at most k vertices. Every
vertex has the same positive degree d. If C meets every edge, then
  d |C| >= |E(H)| >= d g/k,
so |C|>=g/k. The first inequality counts incidences from C, and the second
uses sum_e |e|=dg<=k|E|. This applies equally to variable edge sizes and loops.

PRIVATE-PRODUCTION INTERPRETATION
Fix a currently available common cap and a group of g private owners. At the
fully activated profile suppose the group's private production incidence is
invariant under a transitive action on these owners. Every profitable activity
uses the common cap and at most k=r-1 additional resource rows. For every
activity supported by the group, choose minimal sets of owners whose reports
supply positive amounts of all its required private rows. Such a set has at
most k members: one owner per required row suffices for some positive activity
level. Let H contain every such minimal witness. It is nonempty, invariant
under the transitive action, and has edge size at most k.

Suppose the group becomes productive by restoring C owners from zero to their
final reports, with every other group owner already at its final report. If
the dormant group could not produce any activity using the fixed cap, C must
meet every edge of H. Otherwise an activity witness was already fully present.
The finite lemma therefore forces c=|C|>=g/k.

CERTIFICATE MULTIPLIER LIMIT
Suppose N old paid private owners and all g newly activated private owners
form one common payment orbit at the new profile. The usual protected
coalition consists of the N old owners plus the c actual restorers. If T_old
is the old paid total, TCWM and nonnegative zero-owner payments imply only
the homogeneous multiplier
  T_new >= [(N+g)/(N+c)] T_old.
The orbit-activation hypothesis gives
  (N+g)/(N+c) <= (N+g)/(N+g/k)
               <= ((N+g)/N)^((k-1)/k)       for N>0.
For the last inequality, set x=g/N and use concavity
  (1+x)^(1/k) <= 1+x/k.
Across arbitrary successive group sizes, these upper bounds telescope to
  product of forced multipliers <= (N_final/N_initial)^((k-1)/k).

Thus a constant seed followed only by this type of one-orbit activation
certificate cannot force an exponent exceeding (k-1)/k. The binary-circuit
construction has g=k and c=1 at each step and achieves this exponent in order.
For arity3 this ceiling is1/2, matching the new square-root lower.

PROTECTED-COALITION SIZE, INCLUDING SUBSET TAILS
Put alpha=(k-1)/k. If every step uses all N old owners, protection requires
N+c<=s. Since g<=kc, each new population obeys
  N+g <= N+kc <= k(N+c) <= ks.
Consequently the product is at most (ks/N_initial)^alpha; the exponent
claim is in the protected size s, not merely in an unrestricted population.

The same conclusion holds if a step instead selects N' old owners with
0<=N'<=min(N,s-c). Old payments are uniform within the old paid orbit, so
the corresponding total-payment multiplier is
  phi = [(N+g)/N] [N'/(N'+c)].
It is no larger than the full-old multiplier, because N'/(N'+c) increases
with N'. Moreover, if phi>1 then
  N < g N'/c <= kN' <= k(s-c),
and hence N+g<ks. Here c>=1 follows from a nonempty zero-to-productive
activation. Thus all population levels after ks admit only multipliers
at most1. Telescope the displayed population-power upper bound through
the LAST step with phi>1, retaining earlier factors even if below1, and
discard the later factors at most1. The result is at most
  max{1,(ks/N_initial)^alpha}.
This bounds this particular chained homogeneous certificate even with
protected subset tails. It does not bound branching or multi-orbit sums
of other inequalities, nor a mechanism's actual expenditure.

PRECISE LIMITATION
This does NOT bound the cost of any actual mechanism. It does NOT rule out a
larger lower from additional coverage constraints, productive dormant groups,
multiple payment orbits, nonuniform multiplier systems, interactions between
different caps, or a different production-support geometry. The conclusion
only bounds the multiplier supplied by the specified homogeneous TCWM ladder
under the explicit transitivity and zero-private-production hypotheses. A
stronger arity3 exponent needs to leave at least one of those hypotheses or
use genuinely additional inequalities; increasing group size alone is not an
escape.
