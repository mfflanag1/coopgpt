EXACT EXAMPLES FOR THE AFFINE GADGET'S INCENTIVE DISTINCTIONS
These examples concern nonnegativity, all-coalition coverage and the incentive
premises in Section4 of the general note. They have NO finite total-funding
factor on the full gadget box. They do not assert a stronger impossibility
for uniformly funded gadget mechanisms; the package's separate accepted-rule
examples establish actual budget-transfer failures.

Use r2, one original minimum F(x)=min(x1,x2), profit1, one actual owner with
endowment(1,1). The gadget has resources(o1,o2,l1,l2), all with endowment1,
and activities
 z1 uses(o1,l1), z2 uses(o2,l2), w uses(l1,l2), each profit1.
At equal links M its value is M+min(x1,x2), provided M>=min(x1,x2).

A. TCWM alone does not justify subtracting a changing linear baseline.
On the entire gadget box[0,1]^4 pay the sole owner constantly2. This covers
every coalition because the gadget grand value at the all-ones profile is2,
and the empty coalition has value0. Payments are nonnegative and TCWM holds
for every report change. The linear report map has l1=l2=(x1+x2)/2 and
baseline B=(x1+x2)/2. Subtracted payment is2-B. Raising the original report
from(0,0) to(1,1) lowers it from2 to1, violating even individual TCWM.
The constant gadget rule is deliberately unfunded at zero production.

B. The constant-baseline map does not preserve full OUT from coverage and
full OUT alone. Choose the globally feasible gadget dual price
                         p=(0,0,1,1).
Every stage receives cover price1 and the bridge cover price2. Pay the sole
actual owner z=p dot its reported bundle on the ENTIRE gadget box. This is
nonnegative, covers all coalitions, and satisfies full OUT: each increment
is p dot d>=G(d) by dual feasibility. Under the constant map l1=l2=1 and
B=1, the pulled-back original payment is z-B=1 at every original report.
The original restoration(0,0)->(1,1) has outside worth1, but payment gain0.
Again this gadget rule is not uniformly funded: one isolated positive link
row has zero production and positive payment. Thus the counterexample tests
the exact Section4 incentive assumptions and does not add an unproved funding
claim to the audit.
