1. EXACT RESTRICTED DOMAIN AND QUANTIFIERS
Let H be any finite linear3-uniform hypergraph on resources V: each activity
has exactly three distinct rows, unit profit, unit coefficients, and two
activities share at most one row. There is one fixed pure owner per row,
with actual capacity1 and binary reports0 or1. Write
 F(S)=max{sum_e x_e : x>=0, sum_(e containing v)x_e<=1_(v in S)}.
Define the downward-closed profile ideal
 L_H={S subset V:F(S)<=1}.
Only profiles in L_H are considered. The mechanism is not asserted to exist
outside this ideal, or on any intervening nonbinary report. Inactive owners
remain actual owners; their payments are variables in a lower-bound contract.
The explicit feasible assignment below CHOOSES their payments to be0.

At every S in L_H, require nonnegative payments y_v(S), all-coalition coverage
 sum_(v in C)y_v(S)>=F(S intersect C) for every C subset V,
and funding sum_V y_v(S)<=K F(S). For all endpoints S subset T in L_H,
D=T\S subset C and |C|<=s, full OUT is
 sum_(v in C)[y_v(T)-y_v(S)]>=F(D).
TCWM replaces the right side by0. These compare the same fixed actual owners.
The ideal L_H is a restriction of the campaign's domain, not a substitute
for its all-real fixed-box requirement.

For every integer s>=1, the worst optimal funding factor over all finite H
on this restricted ideal is EXACTLY B_s, for both TCWM and full OUT, where
 B_1=1,
 q_1=1/3,
 q_m=[a_m/(a_m+1)]q_(m-1), a_m=min(s-1,2m-2), m>=2,
 B_s=max_(m>=1){1+2(m-1)q_m}.                         (A)
For s>=2, put h=ceil(s/2), alpha=(s-1)/s and
 Q_h=product_(j=2)^h2j/(2j-1), with Q_1=1. Then
 B_s=1+[2(s-1)Q_h/(3h)] alpha^(s-h),                  (B)
and the maxima occur at m=s,s+1. As s grows,
 B_s-1 ~ [sqrt(2*pi)/(3sqrt(e))]sqrt(s).
Historical priority is unassessed. The restricted upper is a barrier for
lower-bound certificates, not a solution of the unrestricted mechanism task.

2. ALL PRODUCTIVE PROFILES IN THE IDEAL ARE STARS
A nonempty supported hypergraph always has F(S)>=1, since one activity runs
at intensity1. Thus a productive profile in L_H has F(S)=1. LP duality gives
a nonnegative row cover p on S with total cost1 and sum_(v in e)p_v>=1 for
every supported activity. Hence every supported edge contains every row
with p_v>0. There is a common row v. If there are m>=2 supported activities,
linearity makes this row unique and the other2m rows are pairwise distinct.
Thus the support consists of a cap star with m disjoint private pairs and
possibly isolated positive rows. If m1, all three active rows are symmetric
for the assignment below; any one may be called cap without changing it.

3. EXPLICIT PAYMENT RULE
At F(S)=0 pay all owners0. Otherwise let m be the number of supported edges.
Pay each of the2m private owners q_m, the cap1-2q_m, and every isolated or
inactive owner0. At m1 this simply pays the three activity owners1/3.
The q_m sequence is nonincreasing in[0,1/3], so all payments are nonnegative
and the cap payment is nondecreasing in m.

Every supported activity receives total payment1. For any coalition C,
prices given by y on S and1 on every row outside S form a globally feasible
packing dual; its cost at capacity1_(S intersect C) is precisely
sum_(v in C)y_v(S). Therefore every coalition is covered. The total payment
at a productive profile is1+2(m-1)q_m, establishing funding bound(A).
At unproductive profiles both total payment and worth are0.

4. ALL SINGLE-OWNER RESTORATIONS INSIDE THE IDEAL
Consider T=S union{i}, with i absent from S and both endpoints in L_H.
If F(S)=0, all old payments are0 and every new payment is nonnegative, so
TCWM holds for every coalition.
If F(S)=1, all old supported activities remain present. Since T belongs to
the ideal, Section 2 supplies a common cap v for the FINAL supported family.
It lies in an old activity, so v!=i. Every newly created activity contains
both i and v. By linearity, at most one such activity exists. If none exists,
the supported activity family and all payments are unchanged. If one exists,
the number of activities rises from m to m+1. The other new private endpoint
was an isolated positive row at S; otherwise two activities would share
both it and v. It gains payment q_(m+1), and the restoring owner i gains the
same amount. The cap gains; exactly the2m old private owners may lose,
each by q_m-q_(m+1). A protected coalition contains i and at mosts-1 other
owners. Its total change is therefore at least
 q_(m+1)-min(s-1,2m)[q_m-q_(m+1)]=0
by the recurrence. This includes s1, where later q_m vanish.

For simultaneous binary restoration D subset C, add the members of D one
at a time and use the SAME coalition C at every step. All intermediate
profiles remain in L_H by monotonicity of production. Summing the valid
single-restoration inequalities proves TCWM for every allowed endpoint
comparison, including arbitrary branching and incomparable intermediate
profiles in a dual certificate.

5. THE SAME RULE SATISFIES FULL OUT ON THIS IDEAL
For endpoints S subset T, if F(S)=0 then all old payments are0 and high-profile
coverage gives sum_C y(T)>=F(T intersect C)>=F(D).
If F(S)=1, the final supported hypergraph has a common row v belonging to an
old activity. Thus v is already in S and is absent from D. Every activity
supported on T uses v, so none can be supported solely on D: F(D)=0.
The full OUT inequality is then exactly the TCWM inequality just proved.
This is a direct endpoint argument; no claim that positive OUT right sides
can always be summed along single-owner steps is used.

6. EXACT PEAK AND MATCHING LOWER IN THE RESTRICTED CLASS
Through h=ceil(s/2), q_h=Q_h/(3h). Thereafter q_m=q_h alpha^(m-h).
For m>1, the ratio of the nonconstant funding term at m+1 to that at m is

The latter crosses1 exactly at m=s, giving the two maxima s,s+1 and(B).
The gamma formula Q_h=Gamma(h+1)Gamma(3/2)/Gamma(h+1/2) gives the asymptotic.
For s1, q_m=0 for m>=2, so B1=1 directly.

7. CONSEQUENCE FOR THE LIVE LOWER SEARCH
No nonnegative multiplier certificate using only binary unit profiles of
worth at most1 in linear3-uniform unit-profit technologies can force funding
larger than B_s. This remains true with ALL actual coalitions, inactive-owner
payment variables, and arbitrary branching among the selected profiles.
Every individual comparison still has comparable endpoints S subset T inside
the ideal; different vertices or branches of the certificate may be incomparable.
Therefore a projective certificate beating the square-root exponent must use
at least one profile of worth>1, nonbinary reports, or a departure from this
linear unit-profit technology. The theorem does not exclude those routes.
