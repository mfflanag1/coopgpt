THE EXACT FINITE STATEMENT
For integers M,R>=1, set
  n=1+2MR,  s0=R(2M-1),
  Q_M=product_(m=2)^M [2m/(2m-1)]  (Q_1=1).
There is one finite unit-profit, unit-consumption, arity3 packing technology,
with 2^(M+1)-1 resource rows and n fixed fully eligible actual owners, such that
every nonnegative coalition-covering rule satisfying TCWM for coalitions of
size at most s0 has worst-case total-funding factor

                  kappa >= Q_M R/(R+Q_M).                 (1)

Coverage is for EVERY coalition at EVERY feasible real report. All owner
domains are the same full unit cube. There is NO marginal floor, continuity,
linearity, price formula, or zero-payment requirement. Every comparison below
keeps actual owners, eligibility and technology fixed. The lower consequently
also applies to full OUT, which implies TCWM.

In particular, for s>=2, choose an integer t>=1 with 2t^3-t<=s, and put
M=t^2, R=t. Then

  K3(s) >= t Q_(t^2)/(t+Q_(t^2)) >= sqrt(3t^2+1)/4.         (2)

Taking t of order s^(1/3) gives K3(s)=Omega(s^(1/3)), improving the supplied
quarter-power lower if this proof survives audit. Row count is exponential in
M but finite; row counts are explicitly allowed to grow without a size bound.
The number of actual owners is n=2t^3+1=O(s).

1. PUBLIC TECHNOLOGY AND FIXED ACTUAL OWNER DOMAIN
Let V=F_2^(M+1), with basis v,e_1,...,e_M. There is one resource row for every
nonzero vector of V. For each unordered triple {a,b,a+b}, where a,b are
distinct nonzero vectors, include ONE unit-profit activity consuming one unit
of each of its three distinct rows. Duplicate descriptions of the same triple
are discarded. All positive-profit columns thus use exactly3 owned rows.
There are no resource-free positive-profit activities.

Each of the same n owners can report ANY vector in [0,1]^(|V|-1), with binding
coordinatewise underreports. Their actual endowments can all be the all-ones
vector, so every report used below and every restoration is feasible in one
fixed institution. The large all-ones endowment is never substituted into a
budget comparison: funding is evaluated at the actual report profile.

Every invertible linear map of V permutes the resource rows and the activities,
and preserves the owner report cube. Average a purported kappa-funded rule over
the finite product of the owner-permutation group and GL(V), relabeling payments
back to their actual owners. All coverage, nonnegativity, funding and TCWM
inequalities survive this finite convex combination. Thus one may assume the
rule invariant under these symmetries, without requiring symmetry of the
original mechanism or strategically replacing any owner.

Assign distinct fixed owner labels as follows:
 - one root owner p;
 - M groups H_j, each containing R owners;
 - M groups L_j, each containing R owners.
These labels describe the profiles; they do not restrict any owner's fixed
eligibility. A group label never changes across a report comparison.

2. ALL USED PROFILES HAVE GRAND PRODUCTION EXACTLY1
The only nonzero row reports in the construction lie among
       v,  e_j,  e_j+v  (1<=j<=M).
Any supported projective triple on this set has the form
       {v,e_j,e_j+v}.                                    (3)
Indeed the sum of two private rows with DIFFERENT indices has two nonzero
e-coordinates and is outside the displayed set. The sum of the two private
rows with the SAME index is v. Summing v with a private row gives its mate.
Consequently on all these profiles the exact production formula is
       min(c_v, sum_j min(c_(e_j),c_(e_j+v))).             (4)
Rows outside the displayed set remain at zero throughout every comparison.
The full projective technology is needed for cross-profile automorphisms, not
because unreported activities can produce.

At every state c_v=1 and at least one pair has both capacities>=1. Therefore
grand production is exactly1. A feasible primal uses one supported branch at
quantity1. A dual certificate prices only row v at1 on the supported technology;
equivalently the direct argument in (3)-(4) kills every unsupported activity.

3. THE SEED PROFILE AND THE CAP-EXCHANGE SYMMETRY
At D:
 - p reports one unit of row v;
 - every owner in every H_j reports one unit of row e_j (aggregate R per row);
 - one specified owner l_* in L_1 reports one unit of row e_1+v;
 - every other L_j owner reports zero.
Thus the only supported activity is {v,e_1,e_1+v}, and F(D)=1.

The invertible linear map
       g(v)=v+e_1,  g(e_j)=e_j for every j
exchanges v with v+e_1 while fixing ALL high rows e_j. Composing this resource
map with the permutation exchanging the actual labels p and l_* preserves the
entire profile D, including every zero-report owner. Consequently p and l_*
receive the same payment a. All R owners in H_1 receive a common payment b.

The coalition consisting of p, l_* and ONE owner in H_1 has value1. Coverage
and the total budget imply
       2a+b>=1,             R b<=kappa.
The second inequality drops only nonnegative payments. Hence
                    2a >= 1-kappa/R.                      (5)

This is the new bootstrap. A common-cap star by itself cannot exchange its
cap row with a private row as above. No lower bound on a marginal payment has
been assumed: (5) follows from arbitrary payment variables and symmetry.

4. RESTORE THE FIRST LOW GROUP WITHOUT CHANGING OWNERS
Restore the R-1 currently zero owners in L_1 to one unit each of row e_1+v.
Let C_1 denote this resulting profile. The protected coalition is the fixed
group L_1 itself, of size R. Its original payment is at least a, including
possibly positive payments to its zero-report members. TCWM gives
                    sum_(i in L_1)y_i(C_1)>=a.             (6)

At C_1 both rows e_1 and e_1+v have R identical pure unit owners. The invertible
linear map e_1 -> e_1+v, fixing v and all e_j (j!=1), swaps those rows and fixes
every other reported row. Combine it with a bijection of the fixed groups
H_1,L_1. The profile is preserved, so these groups have equal total payments.
Writing T_1 for their combined payment, (5)-(6) yield
                       T_1 >= 2a >= 1-kappa/R.             (7)

5. THE GROWING FIXED-OWNER TRANSITION LADDER
For m=1,...,M, profile C_m has all H_j owners reporting their same unit high
row, all L_j owners with j<=m reporting their unit low row, all L_j owners with
j>m reporting zero, and p still reporting unit v.

At C_m, all 2mR owners in the active pairs H_j union L_j (j<=m) have one common
payment q_m. The automorphisms needed are only permutations of the basis
indices among 1,...,m and independent linear flips e_j -> e_j+v on active
indices. They preserve v, all inactive high rows and their zero mates. Together
with owner-label permutations they act transitively on the active pure-unit
owners while preserving the entire profile. Therefore T_m=2mR q_m.

To pass C_(m-1) -> C_m, restore the SAME R owners of L_m from zero to e_m+v.
Use the actual coalition
   A_m = (union_(j<m)(H_j union L_j)) union L_m.
Its size is R(2m-1)<=R(2M-1)=s0. Its unchanged members are precisely the old
active owners. Old payment is at least T_(m-1); new payment is R(2m-1)q_m.
TCWM and nonnegativity give
       R(2m-1)q_m >= T_(m-1),
       T_m >= [2m/(2m-1)] T_(m-1).                         (8)
No step changes ownership or ignores the unchanged coalition members.

Iterating (8), then using (7) and the budget at C_M, gives
       kappa >= T_M >= Q_M(1-kappa/R).
Rearranging proves (1). This algebra remains valid if the displayed seed lower
is negative, so no unstated kappa<R case distinction is needed.

6. RATE AND ELEMENTARY EXACT BOUNDS
The identity and bounds are
  Q_M = 4^M/[2 binom(2M,M)],
  sqrt(3M+1)/2 <= Q_M <= sqrt(M).                           (9)
For the lower bound, the squared ratio 4Q_M^2/(3M+1) starts at1 and increases:
after cancelling positive factors, its consecutive-ratio comparison reduces
to M>=0. For the upper bound, Q_M^2/M starts at1 and decreases because
       (2M+2)^2*M <= (2M+1)^2*(M+1).
The exact checker verifies the cleared polynomial identities independently.

For M=t^2,R=t, (9) gives Q_M<=t, hence (1)>=Q_M/2>=sqrt(3t^2+1)/4, proving(2).
For every sufficiently large s, choosing t=floor((s/2)^(1/3)) is feasible since
R(2M-1)=2t^3-t<=s. Thus the lower is uniform in s, not merely a sparse sequence.

OPTIONAL SHARPENING OF THIS CERTIFICATE'S ASYMPTOTIC CONSTANT
Using the classical central-binomial asymptotic Q_M~(sqrt(pi)/2)sqrt(M), choose
R~sqrt(pi M). The resulting normalized lower along the feasible sequence tends
to (pi/2)^(1/3)/3 times s^(1/3). This constant is not needed for the exponent
claim and is not asserted as the optimal unrestricted constant.
