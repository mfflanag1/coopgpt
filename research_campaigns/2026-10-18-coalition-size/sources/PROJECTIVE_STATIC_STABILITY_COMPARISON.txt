ORIGINAL-SOURCE COMPARISON: PROJECTIVE GEOMETRY AND STATIC STABILITY
Read 18 October 2026; no claim of an exhaustive priority search.

Bachrach, Elkind, Meir, Pasechnik, Zuckerman, Rothe and Rosenschein,
The Cost of Stability in Coalitional Games, arXiv:0907.4385v1 (2009).
Free original: https://arxiv.org/pdf/0907.4385
Inspected Theorem3.5 and its proof, printed pp6–8 (PDF pp7–9).

The paper establishes an asymptotically sharp square-root player-count bound
for the static cost of stabilizing superadditive games. Its lower example uses
projective planes: players are points and a coalition wins if it contains a
line. The construction and subsidy concept are established ingredients.

Our candidate instead fixes binary projective triple production, with resources
as points and actual owners reporting resource bundles. At each report, LP
duality supplies an efficient core, so static additive cost of stability is0.
The proposed square-root cost is forced by compatibility of payments ACROSS
reports under TCWM. The projective dimension grows while activity arity stays3;
the cited example grows line size. Consequently its static theorem neither
proves nor contradicts this incentive lower. Similar geometry and square-root
scaling alone do not establish priority or novelty of the new argument.

Still required: specialist comparison with quantitative strong-group-monotone
sharing, endogenous core selection, and other cross-profile subsidy bounds.
