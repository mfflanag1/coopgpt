PRIMARY-SOURCE CHECK: THE SUM INCENTIVE HAS A CLASSICAL ANTECEDENT
Root directly read the free author-hosted 24May2004 version of Moulin and
Sprumont, On Demand Responsiveness in Additive Cost Sharing:
https://www.ruf.rice.edu/~econ/papers/2004papers/dmdrespr41.pdf
Archived PDF hash is recorded in moulin_sprumont_2004_source.json.

Printed page6 defines Strong Group Monotonicity using one changing member
inside an otherwise fixed coalition and requires that coalition's summed
share not fall. It expressly includes unchanged members. This is the scalar,
unrestricted-coalition antecedent of the campaign's TCWM inequality.
The paper assumes integer personalized demands, arbitrary monotone cost
functions, exact budget balance and additivity in the cost function.
Proposition2 on printed page12 makes Strong Group Monotonicity incompatible
with Additivity and Separability for at least three agents in that model.

These statements do not establish our sparse-production funding bound:
our reports are real multirow owner bundles, coalition coverage is required,
subsidy is allowed, and protected coalition size can be bounded. Nor does the
impossibility automatically transfer to this restricted production domain.
The incentive principle itself should not be advertised as new. Serial-path
weak group monotonicity is insufficient evidence for its stronger sum version.

This resolves the delegated comparison's failure to obtain the most relevant
original. It leaves priority of the quantitative sparse-production synthesis
open. No source absence or AI review is a novelty confirmation.
