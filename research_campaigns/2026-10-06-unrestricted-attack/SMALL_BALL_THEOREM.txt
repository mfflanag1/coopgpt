SHARP PAIRWISE SMALL-BALL BOUND AND A STRICT GENERAL UPPER REFINEMENT
6 October 2026. Minor quantitative tightening; historical novelty unconfirmed.
The rule remains the existing capped-Banzhaf construction. Main advance of this
campaign is HIERARCHY_THEOREM.txt, not this refinement.

Normalize the capped capacities by T>0: a_i in[0,1], pool totals rho_j,
minimum rho_s=1. Let X_j=sum_(i in N_j)a_i U_i with independent uniform[0,1]
variables. The existing payment rule has total factor E[rho_W], where W is
the uniquely smallest X_j (ties have probability zero).

For rho>=2 define n=floor rho, theta=rho-n, and
 q(n)=1/(n+1)!;
 q(n+theta)=[1-(1-theta)^(n+2)]/[theta*(n+2)!], 0<theta<1.
For every pool j with rho_j>=2, P(X_j<X_s)<=q(rho_j), with equality attainable
in this PAIRWISE comparison. Consequently its all-profile total factor is at most
 Q(k)=2+integral_2^infinity min{1,(k-1)q(t)} dt,
and Q(k)<U(k)=R_k+1/log R_k for every k>=2, R_k log(R_k/e)=log k.
Unequal owner counts and zero reports are allowed, as in the original upper.
This does not assert that Q(k) is an optimal mechanism bound or that its
additive gap from U(k) converges to any specified constant.

PROOF
Drop zero coefficients. A sum of independent uniform summands has a density
symmetric about rho/2 and nondecreasing to its left. For completeness, center
each summand. If f is even and nonincreasing on the positive half-line, its
convolution with uniform[-a/2,a/2] is even and has derivative
 [f(x+a/2)-f(x-a/2)]/a <=0 for almost every x>=0.
Thus convolution preserves symmetric unimodality; induction starts with one
uniform density. Its CDF F_j is convex on (-infinity,rho_j/2], hence on[0,1]
when rho_j>=2.

The coefficients b_i of the short pool sum to1. Jensen pointwise gives
 F_j(sum b_i U_i)<=sum b_i F_j(U_i).
Independence and continuity give P(X_j<X_s)=E F_j(X_s), so
 P(X_j<X_s)<=integral_0^1 F_j(u)du=E[(1-X_j)_+].
The latter is a convex symmetric function of the coefficients of X_j.
On the compact polytope {a in[0,1]^r:sum a=rho}, its maximum is achieved at
a vertex. Vertices have n unit coefficients, one theta if nonzero and zeros
otherwise (two fractional coordinates permit opposite perturbations).
This comparison changes only distributions in a bound, not strategic owners.

For S_n=sum_(i=1)^n U_i and 0<=t<=1, simplex integration gives
 E[(t-S_n)_+]=t^(n+1)/(n+1)!.
At theta=0 this is1/(n+1)!; conditioning on theta*U gives precisely q above.
This construction attains equality with a singleton short pool. Coupling shows
q decreases continuously as rho increases, including at integer endpoints.

At most k-1 pools have rho>t for t>=2, hence
 P(rho_W>t)<=min{1,(k-1)q(t)}.
Tail integration and the bound1 on lower tails yield E rho_W<=Q(k).
For t>=2 and n=floor t,
 q(t)<=1/(n+1)! < (e/(n+1))^(n+1) <=(e/t)^t.
The strict factorial inequality follows from the exponential series; the last
step uses monotonic decrease of (e/x)^x for x>1. Therefore
 Q(k)<=e+integral_e^infinity min{1,k(e/t)^t}dt
      =R_k+integral_(R_k)^infinity k(e/t)^t dt
      <=R_k+1/log R_k.
On (R_k,infinity) the first integrand comparison is strict, so Q(k)<U(k).
The last tail estimate is the same elementary tangent bound as in the existing
upper proof. The optional core floor costs at most one additional T.
