FULL BINARY HISTOGRAM SEARCH — exact lower certificate encoding
This is a new SEARCH, not a proved unrestricted improvement.

There are k pools of m fixed original owners each. Reports are 1 or H>=2.
A pool type h has h high owners and m-h low owners; a state s=(s_0,...,s_m)
contains s_h pools of each type, sum s_h=k. By default ALL states are included.
The optional --strip mode keeps s_0 in {0,1,2}, arbitrary s_1 and s_2 summing
to k-s_0, and s_h=0 for h>=3. Edges leaving this set are omitted. This only
drops valid necessary constraints, so any resulting accepted dual remains
a lower certificate. The number of type-1/type-2 pools can grow with k;
no upper claim or asymptotic theorem follows from this restricted search. This is a restricted
report alphabet used for necessary conditions on arbitrary all-report rules;
it cannot by itself prove an unrestricted upper theorem.

Any proposed all-report rule may be averaged over permutations of equal-size
pools and independently over owners within each pool. For each group element,
relabel inputs and outputs together. Nonnegativity, budgets and all coalition
inequalities are linear and relabel invariant. An increase of a fixed original
owner relabels to an increase of one owner, preserving own monotonicity. Hence
the average is a valid mechanism with the same bound. At each binary state,
payment depends only on h and whether the owner is low/high. This is a proof
device, not splitting or merging strategic owners.

Valid constraints for invariant payments a_(s,h), b_(s,h):
Budget: T_s B - sum_h s_h[(m-h)a_(s,h)+h b_(s,h)] >=0,
where T_s=min_(s_h>0)(m-h+Hh).
Coverage: choose u_h low and v_h high owners from EACH type-h pool. Their actual
coalition worth is min_h(u_h+Hv_h), and payment is
sum_h s_h(u_h a_(s,h)+v_h b_(s,h)). Thus each recorded coverage row is actual.
Monotonicity: raise one low owner in a type-h pool to H, changing state to
s'=s-e_h+e_(h+1). The owner's payment comparison is b_(s',h+1)-a_(s,h)>=0.
The exact checker also permits an empty selection in a present type, yielding
a valid zero-worth coalition; the discovery driver only needs positive-worth
coalitions. No other owner's payment is constrained by that edge. These are fixed-owner
report changes. A sequence of state types need not retain common pool prices.

Each exact certificate uses a finite nonnegative combination of these rows.
If the sum is alpha B + sum_v d_v y_v >= q with alpha>0 and all d_v<=0,
nonnegativity gives alpha B>=q. Equivalently add -d_v times each nonnegativity
row to cancel all payment coordinates. verify.py reconstructs all coefficients
from state/coalition/edge descriptors and uses Fraction only. It does not import
the driver or depend on its floating matrix. A positive uncancelled payment
coefficient rejects the certificate. Altered descriptors are negative controls.

The numerical driver rounds candidate nonnegative dual weights. Any positive
residual payment coefficients are canceled by adding valid budget rows at that
state; the necessary extra coefficient is max_v d_v/(s_h owner_count_v).
This can weaken the lower bound but cannot invalidate it. The separate checker
still reconstructs and verifies the resulting certificate without trusting this
repair recipe or solver status. It is a practical rational certificate extraction
step, not a claimed novel theorem advancing the research gate.

Cut separation: for each possible worth threshold t<=T_s, choose the cheapest
subset of each present pool type among all count pairs u+Hv>=t. Repeating that
choice across its s_h copies minimizes payment at that threshold. Its actual
worth is the minimum of the selected capacities. Every violated coalition is
captured at its own worth threshold, since independent minimizers cost no more.
This justifies numerical completeness when every state/threshold was examined
and there are no violated rows. Numerical primal feasibility remains approximate
and restricted-domain, never an all-profile result. Partial cutting planes still
produce valid lower certificates if the exact checker accepts their actual rows.
