HYPOTHETICAL INTERNAL TREASURY SERVICE CONTRACT —25 October2026
Research model, not an executed agreement, legal analysis, actual institutional mandate or verified regulatory-capital rule.

Decision: a single firm's risk committee assigns monthly internal risk-capacity charges and diversification credits among six separately accountable business units. One corporate owner holds the positions; the mathematical players are fixed units, NOT independent legal asset owners. Units retain their position P&L and responsibility for their preassigned exposure mandates. Treasury centrally accounts for offsets; it does not trade or optimize exposures in this experiment.

Fixed book (2-year,10-year signed DV01, in $1,000 per basis point):
1 short-maturity inventory: (100,0)
2 long-maturity inventory: (0,80)
3 curve position: (60,-30)
4 short-maturity hedge: (-80,0)
5 long-maturity hedge: (0,-70)
6 offsetting curve position: (-60,20)
Positive DV01 loses money when the associated yield rises. Total=(20,0), so residual risk is positive. These round exposures are hypothetical design inputs chosen before reading yield outcomes, not estimated actual positions or tuned separation witnesses. Fixed DV01 is a linear, constant-exposure rebalancing abstraction; ignore carry, convexity, roll-down, basis and transaction costs. Positions do not deliver a trading-return backtest.

A unit or group can challenge its charge using the model's stand-alone risk-capacity cost. That is an internal accounting/appeal benchmark. No claim of a legal right to withdraw assets or form an external bank is made. A real implementation would need approved mandates, enforceable booking rules, a sponsor budget and transfer permissions; none have been observed here.

For covariance Sigma set c(S)=kappa*sqrt(q(S)^T Sigma q(S)), using centered Gaussian one-day97.5% Expected Shortfall. b_i=c({i}), B=sum b_i, r=c(N), T=B-r. The monthly savings allocation y has y(N)=T and y(S)>=b(S)-c(S) for EVERY coalition. Internal charges k=b-y then sum to r and satisfy k(S)<=c(S). Negative k is an explicitly permitted internal rebate in the primary contract. The two proposed selectors maximize the sorted ascending savings vector or minimize the sorted descending savings vector lexicographically. They encode absolute-dollar egalitarian bargaining criteria, not RORAC-optimal incentives or established superiority over Euler allocation.

Baseline: Euler charges k_i=kappa*(q_i^T Sigma q(N))/sqrt(q(N)^T Sigma q(N)) at positive residual risk, with savings b_i-k_i. This is the established economically motivated benchmark. Proportional savings y_i=T*b_i/B is an additional simple comparator, whose coalition feasibility must be checked rather than assumed.

Transfer-cap diagnostics impose sum_i max(-k_i,0)<=L with L/T in {0,.05,.25}. Report infeasibility as infeasibility, not as a solver-selected allocation. All three use the same fixed owners and coalition eligibility.

Budget interpretation: quantities are modeled dollars of one-day risk capacity, not observed profits, cash released or regulatory capital. If a common shadow rental rate lambda per risk-capacity dollar applies, modeled credits scale as lambda*y and aggregate modeled cost as lambda*r. No numerical lambda or profit estimate is asserted without sponsor evidence. No welfare/utility, strategic-incentive or actual-funding claims follow from a static core calculation.

Source context: Tasche2008 arXivv3 supplies capital-allocation/Euler background. Fed SR16-3 illustrates why central management and natural offsets can matter for internal charges, but it concerns funding/contingent-liquidity pricing and does NOT validate this market-risk ES contract. The role and limits of each source must remain explicit.
