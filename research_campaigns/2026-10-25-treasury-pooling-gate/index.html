What is being allocated, and who owns it?

One firm holds the assets. Its six accountable business units receive internal risk-capacity charges and, where permitted, credits for offsets. The mathematical players are fixed units, not independent legal asset owners. A coalition’s stand-alone risk is an internal appeal benchmark; an actual legal right to withdraw is not assumed.

The positions are fixed signed exposures to changes in fitted two-year and ten-year Treasury yields. Positive DV01 loses when the corresponding yield rises. Entries below are $1,000 per basis point.

The two proposed rules raise the smallest savings first (full ascending leximin) or lower the largest savings first (full descending lexicographic minimum). They express different absolute-dollar bargaining priorities. Their standard comparator is Euler risk allocation, with charges κqᵢᵀΣq(N)/√[q(N)ᵀΣq(N)].

All amounts are modeled risk capacity. Turning them into funded economic value would require an observed sponsor budget or shadow rental rate, which this study does not have. Complete hypothetical contract.

The result survives a missing-quote check

The primary rule resets the previous observation after an invalid source row. It consequently omits the next available quote change as well. Ordinary weekends have no rows and are spanned, making this an asymmetric interval convention. The original exclusion list did not fully itemize the additional dropped changes.

After seeing the outcomes, we explicitly declared one post-outcome measurement diagnostic: difference consecutive valid quotes with gaps of at most seven calendar days, without interpolation. It restores 77 holdout changes and repeats the same book and pipeline. The primary remains preserved; the diagnostic is not a newly designated primary.

What still matters: the contract and the comparator

Credits are consequential

Forbidding all negative charges leaves only 9 of 84 primary monthly games feasible. A cap allowing credits up to 5% of modeled savings is feasible in all 84; so is 25%. The unrestricted fairness allocation uses credits between 1.97% and 4.06% of savings.

These are example-specific results. Selected credit demand is not the minimum required credit budget, and modeled credits are not measured sponsor expenditure.

Euler is materially different

The two fairness rules agree with each other but reassign a median 43.71% of modeled savings relative to Euler. Both alternatives satisfy the modeled coalition conditions.

That proves the core is not a singleton. It does not show that the fairness allocation creates more value, better incentives, or superior risk management.

The book has exact offset blocks: units {1,4} carry the entire residual exposure, and units {2,3,5,6} net to zero. Coalition conditions fix each block’s total charge, but they do not fix every unit’s charge. The distinct feasible Euler allocation rules out the review’s concern that numerical agreement merely reflects a singleton core. No general selector-coincidence theorem is asserted.

Checks, review findings and their limits

The independent verifier reads the raw CSV separately and imports neither experiment script. It reconstructs both cleaning conventions, the prior-window covariances, every daily standardized score, all unit tail ratios and the bootstrap intervals.

It checks 1,094 selector vectors against 69,306 coalition inequalities, repeats 704 cap-feasibility LPs, and certifies 320 selected full-lex stages in charge coordinates. The largest constraint violation is 5.94 × 10⁻¹⁶ of total stand-alone risk. Both implementations use SciPy/HiGHS: this is independent code and formulation, not a separate solver or an exact mathematical certificate.

Two Fable 5.1 snapshot reviews found useful disclosure issues. The first prompted pre-outcome clarification of covariance estimation and the duplicated aggregate series. The second prompted fuller missing-data accounting and a check of the book’s block structure. The suggested singleton concern was ruled out by the distinct feasible Euler witness. The suggestion that restoring missing changes must worsen calibration was also unsupported: the observed ratio decreases.

Established methods, limited institutional analogy

Tasche’s Euler-allocation treatment supplies the conventional benchmark and its economic motivation under stated hypotheses. It does not endorse these savings selectors. Federal Reserve transfer-pricing guidance supplies context for central management of funding and contingent-liquidity charges; it does not validate this market-risk ES model. Neither source makes the hypothetical contract operational.

Norm risk, coalition-core constraints and Euler allocation are established ingredients. This run makes no novelty or field-level breakthrough claim. Automated review and finite checks are not human specialist assessment. Source record and boundaries.

The next gate is an institution with a real decision

Keep the mathematical record, and park this illustrative book. The most valuable next application step is to identify one treasury or risk committee whose allocation decision can actually change a limit, hedge, funded opportunity or appeal.

  1. Fix the accountable units and obtain compatible exposure and realized-P&L records.
  2. Document the existing allocation rule, transfer permissions, sponsor budget and why an alternative would matter.
  3. If that contract is credible, freeze a comparison of risk measurement and credit constraints on an untouched or prospective sample, with the current rule and Euler as benchmarks.

If those inputs are absent, leave the finance lane conditional. Another synthetic portfolio search would not resolve the economic question. Already inspected 2019–2025 outcomes must be treated as exploratory for any redesign.

This gate does not resolve the older CoopGPT incentive frontiers, nor does it release the parked AI-credit transaction from its own operating and cash-access evidence requirements.
