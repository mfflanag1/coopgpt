1. Huang, Mei, Yang and Cao (2025), Mechanism Design in Max-Flows.
 https://arxiv.org/html/2502.08248v1
Read sections2.1-2.2, Proposition1, section4 and Definitions3-4/Theorem2.
Their edge-owned acyclic network model permits capacity underreports without
costs. Mechanisms must be efficient. They show core-selection truthfulness
can fail, even on a series-parallel example, and give truthful Shapley and
minimal-cut rules. The latter distributes revenue among structural cuts with
capacity-proportional shares. Their pairwise cut classifications and critical
capacity thresholds are especially relevant precedents.
Comparison: our subsidized rule must cover EVERY coalition and is not their
efficient rule. Our D_e is maximum feasible flow THROUGH e, not a marginal
saturation threshold for the grand flow. The cut-based viewpoint and truthful
flow-game question are already established; the claimed contribution must be
the specific normalization, incentive proof and quantitative subsidy guarantee.
No matching theorem was located in the inspected text. Priority remains open.

2. Sascha Kurz, Measuring voting power in convex policy spaces.
 https://arxiv.org/pdf/1312.5936
Read Definition6.11, printed page17. The absolute continuous Banzhaf functional
is the integral of the difference between an owner's two endpoint values.
This is precisely the established functional used AFTER our normalization.
The functional is not claimed new. Its name alone supplies neither our
all-coalition coverage nor truthfulness as normalized capacities change.

3. Elkind, L.A. Goldberg, P.W. Goldberg and Wooldridge,
 A class of marginal contribution nets.
 https://www.cs.ox.ac.uk/people/leslieann.goldberg/papers/mcn.pdf
Read Definition4.2 and section4.2. Their threshold flow games are Boolean:
winning means reaching a specified target flow. They discuss series-parallel
decomposition and pseudopolynomial Shapley/Banzhaf computation for integer
capacities. Our game has continuous flow WORTH and report-dependent effective
capacities. This source does not establish our incentive/subsidy theorem, and
its algorithms do not prove efficient exact integration for our mechanism.
