Laminar means that any two coverage coalitions are disjoint or one contains the other. The theorem lets every certificate coefficient change: coverage, incentives, budgets and slack. Even with that freedom, the best laminar certificate on this profile set loses strength.

This is a precise limit on a proof method. It does not improve the unrestricted √s-to-s² funding frontier, exclude a bounded-loss transformation, or establish novelty. The optimal payments below apply to the three specified profiles, not to the full real report box.

Every laminar certificate is capped at 1

Consider two cheaper payment arrays, both totaling 2 at each profile. U always gives everything to D. V does the same at a and p, but pays B = 1 and D = 1 at q. Both satisfy all incentive comparisons.

U fails only the coverage sets AB and ABC at q. V fails only AD and ACD at q. Every set in the first group crosses every set in the second: they share A, while B and D lie on opposite sides. Therefore a laminar coverage family cannot use both groups. One of U or V satisfies every positively weighted row of any such certificate, proving its ratio is at most 1. A grand-coalition row attains 1.

The result survives nonzero reports

Replace every formerly zero B or C report by 1/32 of that owner's endowment. All owners then report a nonzero bundle at every profile. The unrestricted certificate still gives a lower bound of 12/11, while every laminar certificate has ratio at most 34/33. This strict separation is verified exactly. No exact optimum is claimed for the perturbed example.
