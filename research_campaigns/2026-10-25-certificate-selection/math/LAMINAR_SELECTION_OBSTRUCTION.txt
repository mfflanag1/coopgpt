OPTIMAL CERTIFICATE SELECTION CAN REQUIRE CROSSING COVERAGE
25 October 2026. Conventional finite proof; focused independent AI review
complete with no invalidating findings. Root disposition: ../reviews/PROOF_DISPOSITION.txt.

1. CLAIM AND SCOPE
An optimal finite-profile funding dual need not admit laminar coverage
support at each profile, even when every comparison multiplier, budget
multiplier, coverage multiplier and slack may be changed. Here laminar means
any two positive-weight coverage coalitions are disjoint or one contains the
other. The counterexample has four fixed actual multirow owners, one
unit-profit activity of arity exactly three, and protection size s=2.

On the three specified profiles Q the full optimum is exactly 9/8, while the
largest ratio of ANY dual with laminar coverage at each profile is exactly 1.
All legal protected comparisons between members of Q are available, including
unchanged passive members. Payments to zero reporters are permitted throughout.

This disproves universal optimal-laminar-selection on a fixed finite Q.
It does NOT exclude adding other legal profiles, allowing crossing coverage,
or replacing the certificate at a bounded increased cost. It is not a growing
funding lower, a new unrestricted exponent, or a novelty claim. The full real
report box is retained as the underlying institution, but only Q is used in
the finite dual and the two finite payment witnesses below.

2. FIXED INSTITUTION
Resource rows are X,Y,Z. Production is F(c)=min(c_X,c_Y,c_Z).
There are four actual owners A,B,C,D with upper endowments
 A=(1,0,2), B=(0,2,0), C=(2,0,1), D=(1,2,2).
Each owner may report any vector in its original coordinatewise box [0,b_i].
Write a full endowment by its owner letter and a withheld bundle by 0.
The three complete profiles are
 a=(A,0,0,D), p=(A,0,C,D), q=(A,B,0,D).
Their aggregate capacities are (2,2,4), (4,2,5), (2,4,4), respectively;
all have production value 2.

The only distinct-profile comparisons in Q are a->p, changing C, and a->q,
changing B. For each, every protective coalition of size at most two that
contains the changed owner is legal. Every restored bundle has production
value zero. The two heads p and q are incomparable. Singleton and grand
coverage as well as all other nonempty coalitions are required at each profile.

3. EXACT LOWER CERTIFICATE
Give each of the following five coverage rows multiplier one:
 (a,{A,D}) value 2;
 (p,{A,D}) value 2; (p,{C,D}) value 2;
 (q,{A,B}) value 1; (q,{A,D}) value 2.
Give comparison (a,p,{A,C}) multiplier one and comparison (a,q,{B,D})
multiplier one. Both are valid whole-coalition comparisons; A and D are
passive protected members, respectively. Their full-OUT right sides are zero.
Give budget multiplier two at each of p and q and zero at a.

The full owner/profile coefficients g, in order A,B,C,D, are
 a: (0,-1,-1,0), p: (2,0,2,2), q: (2,2,0,2).
These obey g<=nu. The objective is L=9 and budget cost D=8, giving 9/8.
Equivalently divide all coefficients by eight to get D=1, L=9/8.

4. EXACT MATCHING FINITE PRIMAL
The following nonnegative payments, in order A,B,C,D, cover all coalitions
and satisfy all size-at-most-two full-OUT comparisons inside Q:
 a: (1/2,0,0,3/2);
 p: (1/4,0,1/4,7/4);
 q: (3/4,1/4,0,5/4).
Their totals are 2,9/4,9/4, so the maximum factor is 9/8.

To check coverage, at a only coalitions containing D can have positive value,
with singleton D worth 1 and AD worth 2. At p, singleton D is worth 1,
AD and CD are worth 2, and AC is worth zero; grand value is 2. At q,
singleton D is worth 1, AB and BD are worth 1, AD is worth 2, and grand
value is 2. Zero reporters may be added without changing these values.
The displayed payments meet each inequality, including all padded coalitions.

For a->p, the increases in payments to C, AC, BC, CD are respectively
1/4,0,1/4,1/2. For a->q, the increases to B, AB, BC, BD are respectively
1/4,1/2,1/4,0. They are all nonnegative, as required by zero OUT values.
These exhaust the distinct legal comparisons in Q. Thus weak duality and
the lower certificate establish the exact finite optimum 9/8.

5. EVERY LAMINAR-COVERAGE DUAL HAS RATIO AT MOST ONE
Consider two alternative finite payment arrays, each with total 2 everywhere:
 U: pay (0,0,0,2) at every profile.
 V: use U at a and p, and pay (0,1,0,1) at q.
Both are nonnegative and satisfy ALL legal protected comparisons in Q.
For V, the a->q increases to B,AB,BC,BD are 1,1,1,0; all a->p increases
are zero. For U every increase is zero.

U covers every coalition at every profile EXCEPT at q the two coalitions
 E_U={ {A,B}, {A,B,C} }.
V covers every coalition at every profile EXCEPT at q the two coalitions
 E_V={ {A,D}, {A,C,D} }.
Each S in E_U crosses every T in E_V: they share A, while B is in S\T and
D is in T\S. Adding the zero reporter C never removes that crossing.

Consequently a laminar coverage support at q cannot contain positive
multipliers from both exception classes. If it uses none from E_U, array U
satisfies every positive coverage row. Otherwise it uses none from E_V,
and array V satisfies every positive coverage row. The chosen array also
satisfies every nonnegativity and comparison row and costs exactly F(B)
at every profile. Applying the usual weighted-inequality argument to the
given dual gives L<=D. This argument allows arbitrary comparison and budget
coefficients and does not assume that zero reporters receive zero payments.

The upper ratio 1 is attained by grand-coalition coverage and a matching
budget at any one profile, with no comparisons. Thus the optimal laminar
ratio is exactly 1. The same L<=D bound holds for any finite SUM of feasible
laminar-coverage duals on Q. Replacing the 9/8 optimum by such a sum while
retaining its objective therefore requires at least 9/8 times its budget;
a scaled grand-coverage certificate attains that replacement cost on Q.

6. CONSEQUENCE FOR THE RESEARCH ROUTE
Coverage changes alone are not enough to justify the particular next step
"select a laminar optimum." Crossing coverage can be essential to the best
finite certificate. Any general transformation must either retain some
crossing structure, change the profile set, or explicitly account for a loss.
This is a fixed-factor obstruction, not a proof that a useful bounded-loss
laminar reduction is impossible. A growing obstruction or constructive
bounded-loss theorem remains a separate problem.

The more elementary idea of preserving the owner incidence of coverage and
uncrossing locally is even less viable. At one profile take a pure X-owner
with X=1 and m pure Y-owners with Y=1 each, F=min(X,Y), and incidence limits
q_X=m and q_Yj=1. The m coalitions {X,Y_j}, each of weight 1, give coverage
objective m. Every positive-value coalition contains X; a laminar positive
family must be a chain. Its smallest positive coalition contains some Y_j,
so all its weights sum to at most q_Yj=1, and every coalition is worth at most
1. Its laminar objective is at most 1. This is an unbounded LOCAL coverage-LP
loss, not an optimal full-dual counterexample or a new funding lower.

7. THE SEPARATION SURVIVES NONZERO REPORTS BY EVERY OWNER
For 0<epsilon<1/16 replace each zero B or C report in Q by epsilon times
that owner's full endowment. Keep A,D and every formerly full report fixed.
All four actual owners now report a nonzero bundle at every selected profile.
The two restorations and their passive protected members remain legal; their
OUT values remain zero. Every aggregate production value becomes 2+2epsilon.
The five coverage rows in section 3 have unchanged values: none contains
the owner whose new small report would affect that row's old aggregate.
Thus the same dual coefficients give ratio 9/[8(1+epsilon)].

Add a CONSTANT payment 2epsilon to each of B and C, at all three profiles,
in both arrays U and V. All comparison increases are unchanged. For any S,
its capacity increment is supported on newly small B and/or C reports and
has infinity norm at most 2epsilon times the number of B,C in S. The minimum
of three coordinates is 1-Lipschitz in that norm. Consequently every coverage
row previously satisfied by U or V is still satisfied after these payment
increments. The only possible exceptions remain E_U and E_V, respectively.
The same crossing argument therefore bounds every laminar dual ratio by
 (2+4epsilon)/(2+2epsilon)=(1+2epsilon)/(1+epsilon).
This is strictly below 9/[8(1+epsilon)] for epsilon<1/16.

For the exact choice epsilon=1/32, unrestricted finite funding is at least
12/11=36/33, while laminar certificates have ratio at most 34/33. No exact
optimum is asserted for this perturbed example. The statement rules out the
explanation that the separation is merely an artifact of whole zero reporters.
It remains a fixed-Q, fixed-factor obstruction.

8. EVIDENCE AND PRIORITY
The solver was used only to discover the four-owner instance. The proof above
and check_laminar_obstruction.py use exact rational arithmetic and exhaust
all coalitions and comparisons on the three declared profiles. They do not
search or certify the full real report box. The original all-profile funding
gap is unchanged. Classical ingredients are finite LP weak/strong duality
and the definition of laminar set families. Independent review and a primary
source comparison are recorded separately; no novelty confirmation is claimed.
