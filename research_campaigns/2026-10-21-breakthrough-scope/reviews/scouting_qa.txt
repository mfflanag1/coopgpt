**Verdict: revise.** The candidate correctly preserves the current frontier and identifies a potentially consequential direction, but its proposed lemma does not yet imply its advertised consequence, and its concrete geometry undermines the constant-production premise. It is not sufficiently supported as the strongest 150-minute target.

1. **Lemma \(A_\lambda\) omits essential hypotheses for the recurrence.**  
   Conditions (i)–(v) do not specify that the old active set is contained in the new active set, that the restoring owner belongs to the new active orbit, or that the seed coalition produces the common ladder value and shares payments with the designated active owners. Those facts are necessary to derive
   \[
   (A_{m-1}+1)q_m\ge A_{m-1}q_{m-1}.
   \]
   Stabilizer transitivity and active-set cardinalities alone do not establish them. The claimed lower bound is therefore **not an already-proved drop-in corollary** of the stated lemma. Terminal branch coverage also requires separate hypotheses if the manuscript’s stronger terminal certificate is used.

2. **The proposed projective-line extension introduces production without the cap.**  
   In \(\mathrm{PG}(m,3)\), a line contains four points. Including all collinear triples gives all four triples on that line, including the triple avoiding the designated root. At unit capacities, assigning \(1/3\) to each activity produces \(4/3\); summing row capacities proves optimality. With multiple lines through the root, each line’s three private points can independently produce one without the root. Thus the natural pencil realization loses constant production as branches accumulate. Pair multiplicity \(q-1\) does not establish useful activation; it creates competing production. This is a concrete obstruction to the offered route, although not a disproof of every possible \(A_\lambda\) construction.

3. **The claimed diagnosis “artifact of linearity, not arity” is unsupported.**  
   The manuscript establishes one construction with activation \(r-1\). It does not establish that changing the underlying geometry can increase activation while preserving production, seed symmetry and the required nested owner orbits. Calling this the remaining “finite geometry” problem substantially understates the missing work.

4. **The fallback obstruction lacks a precise contract and demonstrated consequence.**  
   The activation–production inequality leaves \(t_m\), admissible active sets and the architecture class undefined. Moreover, an inequality bounding one transition does not by itself prove a square-root ceiling for every relevant ladder: that requires explicit assumptions governing activation over all stages, seed payments and normalization by production. The vertex-transitive fractional-matching observation is valid for the stated uniform hypergraph setting, but does not supply that missing theorem.

5. **The primary-literature requirement was not met, and the cited bounds mix contracts.**  
   I could retrieve the [Immorlica–Mahdian–Mirrokni primary paper](https://immorlica.com/pubs/costshare.pdf). Its Theorems 3.2–3.3 concern set-cover and vertex-cover **cost recovery**; Definition 3.3 separately defines profit sharing, and Theorem 3.6’s flow proof uses individual incumbent cross-monotonicity. The candidate correctly warns against transferring that proof to coalition-sum TCWM, but supplies no exact new transferable leverage. The projective proposal has no inspected primary-source anchor. Listing sources without a verified theorem comparison does not satisfy the scout’s central sourcing requirement.

6. **The ranking overstates feasibility and understates the surviving upper route.**  
   “High” feasibility rests on treating the difficult implication as already established. Conversely, the previous upper gate did not exclude all promising upper work: it retained budget-supported price relaxation and identified the remaining quantitative accounting problem. A fair comparison should assess that specific opening against the proposed activation construction. The graph alternative likewise receives no concrete new lemma supporting its completion estimate.

The candidate **does pass** several criteria: it uses the correct \(\sqrt{s/6}\) versus \(3s^2\) frontier, detects stale records, distinguishes TCWM from OUT, avoids the fixed nonnegative marginal-weight barrier, and does not promote a finite witness as a general bound. Its limitations are candid.

A revised submission should state the complete nested-orbit lemma, rederive its normalized consequence, address the cap-free triples in the proposed geometry, and provide one inspected primary-source result that materially advances the route. Until then, retain activation amplification as an exploratory candidate, not the selected strongest attack.
