TOTAL FUNDING OF THE OWN-REPORT PROJECTION ENVELOPE
21 October 2026. Exact architecture-specific corollary.

CLAIM AND SCOPE
For every even integer s>=4 there is a finite unit-demand cover system with
N=s^2+1 fixed owners and minimum total feasible payment T=1 for which its
coordinatewise own-report projection envelope M satisfies

                 sum_i M_i >= (3/4)*sqrt(N) - 21/8.                 (1)

Consequently this architecture has examples with total funding at least
(3/4-o(1))*sqrt(N), along N=s^2+1. This is a lower bound on this particular
envelope architecture, not on arbitrary incentive-compatible mechanisms.
The common-veto systems also have the efficient allocation paying the veto
one and everyone else zero. No optimality of the coefficient3/4 is claimed.

The proof below concerns finite cover polyhedra and their incident-row
relaxations. Its transfer to genuine own reports of one production function
uses the separate balanced-closure and simultaneous Boolean realization
lemmas, exactly as in inputs/UNIFORM_VETO_AMPLIFICATION_REDUCTION.txt.
For every fixed s all owners remain fixed across the displayed witnesses.
The asymptotic sequence may use a different institution for each s.

BASE CERTIFICATE
Use proofs/activation_mass_simple_proof.txt, independently checked here by
parity counting and checks/mass_search_funding_corollary.py. Let the graph
have vertices Z/(2s), cycle edges and antipodal edges, with s even. Covering
rows are complements of all independent (s-3)-sets, so their size is s+3.
The graph's cyclic translation is transitive. With C the four-vertex closed
neighborhood of owner0, the explicit equal-weight mixture of 2(s-2) rows has
coordinates3/4 on C and s/[2(s-2)] elsewhere. Its supporting rows each meet
C in three vertices. Every off-owner row also meets C in three vertices:
its independent complement contains0 and excludes the three neighbors.

For clarity, the independent-set marginal count in that proof is exact:
write m=(s-2)/2. Each of the four local vertices occurs m times among4m
sets; each other vertex occurs2m-2 times. At even interior rung j=2r the
A/B contributions to the top layer are (m-r)+(r-1)=m-1, with the same
bottom total; C/D contribute m-1 on each layer. At odd interior rung
j=2r+1 the A/B top total is (r-1)+(m-r)=m-1 and the bottom total is
(m-r-1)+r=m-1, again with C/D contributing m-1. The two nonlocal endpoint
vertices occur2m-2 times as well. Thus the certificate holds for every even
s>=4, not merely the finitely many sizes checked by the executable.

Put D=2s^2+9s-18. The exact full and relaxed KKT quantities are

  n=2s,              rho=2s/(s+3)^2,
  ell=4(s-2)/D,      p=3(s-2)/D,       theta=p/ell=3/4.

Here ell denotes total relaxed dual mass, not a graph size. The relaxed
vector is3(s-2)/D on C and2s/D elsewhere. Each supported row gets multiplier
2/D (combine repeated rows if needed), and every supported and off-owner
row has payment1. Set demand1 on those rows and0 on other own-owner rows.
This gives primal feasibility, stationarity and complementary slackness.
All owners have translated copies. The full point1/(s+3) follows by cyclic
averaging and Cauchy-Schwarz, or by equally weighted translated-row KKT.

DIRECT COMMON-VETO CONSTRUCTION
Take B=s/2 disjoint base blocks and one common veto u; each generator is
u+x^b(S)>=1. Then N=Bn+1=s^2+1 and T=1: u=1 is feasible and any one row
forces total payment at least1.

The full quadratic projection has veto coordinate

  u_full=B*rho/(1+B*rho)=s^2/(2s^2+6s+9).

For any owner in any block use its base witness in that block and the full
base point in every other block. Scale these points and their multipliers
by a=1/[1+(B-1)rho+ell], and set the veto to1-a. Veto stationarity is the
identity a[(B-1)rho+ell]=1-a. Incident demands become1-a+a*b_S; off-owner
demands stay1. Feasibility and complementarity follow from the base KKT.
Thus each of the Bn nonveto owners has own envelope at least a*p, while the
veto owner has envelope at least its full-profile payment u_full. Therefore

  sum_i M_i >= F_s := u_full + s^2*p/[1+(s/2-1)rho+ell].              (2)

These are coordinatewise envelope lower bounds from different reports,
which is exactly how this architecture defines its funding vector.

EXACT FINITE BOUND AND ASYMPTOTIC
Write
  Q=4s^4+30s^3+34s^2-3s-234,
  f_s=F_s-u_full=3s^2(s-2)(s+3)^2/Q.
The denominator is positive because it is the original positive KKT
denominator multiplied by D(s+3)^2. Direct algebra gives

  (3/4)s-f_s=(42s^4+138s^3+207s^2-702s)/(4Q),
  21Q-2(42s^4+138s^3+207s^2-702s)
       =354s^3+300s^2+1341s-4914 >=0  for s>=4.

The last polynomial is positive at4 and increasing thereafter, so
f_s>=(3/4)s-21/8. Also

  u_full>=3/(8s),
  sqrt(s^2+1)-s<=1/(2s).

The first follows from8s^3-6s^2-18s-27>=0 for s>=4; the second follows by
squaring or rationalizing. Adding them proves (1). Degree comparison in
the displayed rational formulas shows F_s/sqrt(s^2+1)->3/4. This is the
limit of the explicit witness lower bound, not a claimed limit or upper
bound for the actual envelope sum. In fact
  f_s=(3/4)s-21/8+O(1/s),
  F_s=(3/4)s-17/8+O(1/s).

FIXED BASE PLUS UNION PRODUCT: SECONDARY COROLLARY
Suppose a fixed symmetric n-owner unit-demand base (every full RHS is1)
has full norm rho>0 and chosen KKT owner witnesses with the same positive
p,ell and theta=p/ell. Their mass always satisfies ell<=rho: if z is the
full projection and q=A^T lambda a relaxed projection, then
  ell=sum_R lambda_R <=sum_R lambda_R*z(R)=z dot q
      <=||z||*||q||<=||z||^2=rho.
The last step uses that z remains feasible for the relaxed system. This
does not assume ell=||q||^2, which can fail for fractional relaxed demands.
Let t be a positive integer and take its union-of-rows product, with nt owners. The
exact reduction gives

  rho_t=rho/t,
  p_t=rho*p/[rho+(t-1)ell],
  ell_t=rho*ell/[rho+(t-1)ell].

Now take B_t=ceil(t/rho) common-veto copies, N_t=B_t*n*t+1. The explicit
nonveto envelope sum is bounded below by

  W_t=B_t*n*t*p_t/[1+(B_t-1)rho/t+ell_t].

Let c=rho/ell-1>=0 and b_t=B_t*rho/t. Then
  1<=b_t<1+rho/t,
  W_t/t=n*theta*[t/(t+c)]*b_t/
                       [1+b_t-rho*c/(t(t+c))],
  N_t/t^2=n*b_t/rho+1/t^2.
Consequently

  W_t/sqrt(N_t) -> theta*sqrt(n*rho)/2 = sqrt(H)/2,
  H=n*rho*theta^2.

In particular the actual envelope sum has liminf after normalization at
least this number. An exact finite conservative bound is

  W_t/sqrt(N_t) >= [theta*sqrt(n*rho)/2]
       *[t/(t+c)]/[sqrt(1+rho/t+rho/(n*t^2))].                    (3)

The additional full veto coordinate is nonnegative and bounded by1, so it
does not affect this asymptotic lower-bound coefficient. More generally,
choosing B_t*rho/t->x>0 gives coefficient
theta*sqrt(n*rho)*sqrt(x)/(1+x), maximized at x=1. This optimization concerns
these displayed witnesses and this construction, not all possible covers.

For the independently certified172-owner base,
  rho=172/7921, p=63/3887, ell=84/3887, theta=3/4,
  sqrt(H)/2=129/178.
Thus even a fixed base followed by the two amplifications gives the
coefficient129/178. The varying-s direct construction improves this to3/4.

COMPARISON WITH THE EXISTING INTERVAL INGREDIENTS
The already available cyclic-interval base n=2k-1, row size k has
rho=(2k-1)/k^2 and normalized witness theta=2/3. Formula(3) therefore gives
coefficient(2k-1)/(3k), tending to2/3 as k grows. Even k=3 gives5/9>1/2.
Thus a universal leading upper coefficient1/2 for this raw own-report
projection envelope was already incompatible with those ingredients;
the present family improves the available architecture-specific lower
coefficient. This note makes no literature-priority or novelty claim.
