IDEALITY GATE: EXACT OBSTRUCTION TO THE EASY TRANSFER, AGGREGATE QUESTION OPEN
2026-10-21. Bounded analytic gate; no claim of a universal H counterexample.

Setup. For binary rows A, let Q={x>=0:Ax>=1}; let z be its minimum-norm point,
rho=||z||^2. For each coordinate i, selectively lower only rows containing i,
let q^i be the new minimum-norm cover, choose KKT row multipliers lambda^i,
and write L_i=sum lambda^i, theta_i=q^i_i/L_i. The questions are
H=rho sum_i theta_i^2<=4 and J=sqrt(rho/n) sum_i theta_i<=2.
Zero-mass witnesses are interpreted by continuity or omitted.

Primary source comparison. Abdi--Cornuejols--Lee, Identically self-blocking
clutters (2019), https://ahmadabdi.com/papers/ISBC.pdf, Lemma 7 supplies the
full unit-demand multiplier identity sum lambda=rho. Theorem 13 proves
n rho>=4 for an ideal clutter without a common element and characterizes
the equality case by ideal cuboid structure. Lemma 19 supplies a decomposition
of the full minimum-norm cover into integral covers, with complementary
intersection structure. These are static full-demand statements; none asserts
integrality after arbitrary incident fractional RHS changes.

A concrete obstruction to transferring ideality through an own relaxation.
Take six coordinates and four rows
    135, 146, 236, 245.                                      (Q6)
The full covering polyhedron is ideal. Its seven vertices are precisely the
three pair-cover vectors 12,34,56 and the four displayed row vectors. The
stdlib verifier enumerates all C(10,6)=210 possible active constraint bases
with exact Fraction elimination and finds exactly these seven vertices.
Its recession cone is the nonnegative orthant, so the vertex certificate
certifies integrality of this pointed rational polyhedron.

Its full minimum-norm cover is z=(1/3)1, rho=2/3, because all row sums are 3,
the equal row mixture is (1/2)1, and equal row multipliers 1/6 certify KKT.
The full normalized hull point is v=(1/2)1.

Delete only row 135. This is an allowed incident-row downward relaxation for
owner 1 (also owners 3 and 5). The remaining covering polyhedron has the
fractional vertex
    x=(0,1/2,0,1/2,0,1/2).
Indeed all three remaining rows are tight. On its positive coordinates the
active equations are x4+x6=1, x2+x6=1, x2+x4=1, with determinant of absolute
value 2; together with x1=x3=x5=0 they determine x uniquely. Thus even an
endpoint own deletion destroys ideality. This is not the deletion of a
vertex of the clutter, the minor operation that preserves ideality.

The earlier completion lemma also does not follow from ideality. At v=(1/2)1,
for the row a=135, (v-(1/2)a)/(1/2)=246, which is not in the row hull. A binary
point in a convex hull of binary vectors can only be represented using that
same vector, and 246 is absent. Thus uniformity plus ideality does not imply
that completion hypothesis.

This example is NOT an aggregate-mass obstruction. In fact theta_i<=1/2 for
every owner and every incident relaxation, so H<=1 and J<=1, sharply.
Here the row Gram matrix is 2I+11^T. Write any local normalized KKT witness
w=A^T alpha, alpha>=0, sum alpha=1, and threshold t=1/L. Then each row's
potential is 1+2 alpha_r. There are exactly two own and two off rows.
Feasibility and complementarity say every off row has potential >=t, and
every used off row has potential =t; every used own row has potential <=t.
Also t>=||w||^2=1+2 sum alpha_r^2>=3/2>1. Thus both off rows have positive
alpha and the same coefficient beta=(t-1)/2. Every own coefficient is at
most beta, including unused rows. Therefore theta<=2 beta and
1=theta+2 beta, giving theta<=1/2. Full equal alpha attains equality.

A useful positive edge of the source theorem. Whenever an ideal clutter
satisfies n rho=4, the universal binary cap 0<=theta_i<=1 already gives
H<=rho n=4 and J<=sqrt(rho n)=2. For n rho>4 it gives no desired bound.
The source inequality points in the opposite direction from the dense-base
condition n rho<=4 used in our common-veto upper theorem.

Exact remaining bottleneck. A general ideal-clutter theorem still requires
an aggregate coupling between the different normalized local mixtures.
The full ideal cover decomposition and blocker identity do not themselves
supply such a coupling. The off-owner row subsystem is a genuine vertex-
deletion minor and remains ideal, so that subsystem's integrality is usable;
the projection after adding selectively weakened own rows is the missing
step. The unresolved lemma is exactly whether the above local KKT systems,
with ideal full Q, force rho sum theta_i^2<=4 or sqrt(rho/n)sum theta_i<=2.
No proof or counterexample to either implication was obtained in this gate.
Reopening this route needs a new coupling lemma, not an assumption that
ideality survives the own relaxation.
