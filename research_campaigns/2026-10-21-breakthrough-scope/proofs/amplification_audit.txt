VERDICT
The two amplification constructions, balanced closure, and simultaneous
fixed-owner star realization are correct under their stated hypotheses.
There is no missing pure-ownership, uniqueness-of-dual, endpoint-relaxation,
or row-rank assumption. Exact finite amplification parameters are supplied
below. I also give an explicit closure and production formula specialized
to the common-veto output, eliminating the need to solve auxiliary core LPs.

The conclusion is a failure of the TRUE projection-envelope DIAGONAL bound.
It does not by itself refute the separate sqrt(n) bound on the envelope's
sum of payments, and it does not establish any lower bound for unrestricted
payment mechanisms. The resulting production instance admits an efficient
mechanism paying only the fixed veto owner, even under full OUT.

1. REQUIRED FINITE INPUT CERTIFICATE

Let A have R>=1 nonempty 0/1 rows on m coordinates. Its full RHS is exactly
one on every row. Supply its unique minimum-norm projection z and any
nonnegative full multipliers lambda satisfying

 z=A^T lambda,  Az>=1,  lambda_S (z(S)-1)=0.

Put rho=||z||^2>0. For every coordinate i supply a RHS b^i, projection q^i,
and chosen nonnegative multipliers lambda^i satisfying

 0<=b^i<=1; b^i_S=1 for every row S not containing i;
 q^i=A^T lambda^i; Aq^i>=b^i;
 lambda^i_S (q^i(S)-b^i_S)=0.

Write p_i=q^i_i, L_i=sum_S lambda^i_S. Require L_i>0. If a chosen witness has
L_i=0, q^i=0 because rows are nonempty and multipliers are nonnegative; replace
that zero witness by the full witness. This cannot remove a positive
contribution to the target. Unused coordinates may use the full witness,
with p_i=0 and L_i=rho.

The decisive input is the exact strict inequality

 H := rho sum_i (p_i/L_i)^2 > 2.

No such input has been found by this audit. Rational b^i, q^i, lambda^i and
z,lambda make every parameter and subsequent coefficient rational. The
projection and a valid multiplier certificate are rational for rational
finite input data; alternatively, supply the explicit rational certificate
rather than relying on this existence observation.

2. KKT AND DUAL MASS: ALL MULTIPLIERS ARE ALLOWED

Use the objective ||x||^2, with Lagrangian multipliers normalized so that
q=A^T lambda. The quadratic dual is

 max_{lambda>=0} 2 b.lambda - ||A^T lambda||^2.

The primal nonnegativity constraints do not contribute a hidden term.
Writing stationarity as q=A^T lambda+mu, with mu>=0 and mu_i q_i=0, gives
mu=0 because A^T lambda>=0. Equivalently, minimizing the Lagrangian over
x>=0 has minimizer A^T lambda, already nonnegative.

Complementarity gives ||q^i||^2=lambda^i.b^i<=L_i. The same lambda^i is feasible
for the full unit-RHS dual. Therefore

 0 <= L_i <= 2L_i-||q^i||^2 <= rho.                         (2.1)

For a full multiplier, sum lambda=rho. For relaxed systems the total mass
need not be unique. This is harmless: the constructions explicitly use the
selected lambda^i, including its selected L_i. Also 0<=p_i<=L_i because p_i
is the sum of its incident multipliers.

Concrete nonunique-mass check: use rows {1,2},{0,1},{0,2},{0}, with relaxed
RHS (1,7/10,7/10,1/5). The projection is q=(1/5,1/2,1/2). Both

 lambda=(2/5,1/10,1/10,0), L=3/5;
 lambda=(1/2,0,0,1/5),     L=7/10

satisfy exact KKT. Both were separately passed through the products by the
independent checker. Thus the nonuniqueness issue is tested in an example
where the TOTAL mass itself changes, not merely duplicate-row multipliers.

3. EXPLICIT FINITE UNION LENGTH FROM RATIONAL H>2

Set

 theta_i=p_i/L_i,
 h_i=rho theta_i^2,
 a_i=rho/L_i-1 >=0,
 D=sum_i h_i a_i,
 delta=H-2 >0.

A sufficient explicit integer union length is

 t=max{1, floor(2D/delta)+1}.                              (3.1)

There are t disjoint copies of the base coordinates. For every tuple of
base rows (S_1,...,S_t), impose the unit-demand union row consisting of
S_k in block k for every k. The product has tm coordinates and R^t rows
(before identifying any duplicate representations).

Its full projection is z/t in every block, with squared norm

 r := rho/t.

A full multiplier on tuple (S_1,...,S_t) is

 product_k lambda_(S_k) / [t rho^(t-1)].                    (3.2)

It has total mass r. Summing (3.2) on one coordinate gives z_i/t. A tuple
with positive multiplier consists entirely of tight full rows, so it is
tight. This verifies full feasibility, stationarity, and complementarity.

For a focal owner i in one of the t blocks, put

 alpha_i=rho/[rho+(t-1)L_i],
 beta_i=L_i/[rho+(t-1)L_i].

The relaxed tuple demand is

 alpha_i b^i_(S_focal)+(t-1)beta_i.                        (3.3)

It belongs to [0,1], since alpha_i+(t-1)beta_i=1. A tuple excluding the
focal owner has b^i_(S_focal)=1, and hence demand exactly one. Thus this is
an incident-row-only relaxation of the PRODUCT system, not a relaxation
of other owners' rows.

The proposed projection has alpha_i q^i in the focal block and beta_i z
in every other block. The tuple multipliers are

 [alpha_i/rho^(t-1)] lambda^i_(S_focal)
       product_(other blocks k) lambda_(S_k).             (3.4)

Focal stationarity gives alpha_i q^i. Another block gives
(alpha_i L_i/rho) z=beta_i z. On positive multipliers, all base rows are
tight at the appropriate demands, proving complementarity. Feasibility
holds term by term. Strict convexity makes this the unique projection.
Its total dual mass and focal coordinate are

 ell_i=alpha_i L_i=rho/(t+a_i),
 c_i=alpha_i p_i=rho theta_i/(t+a_i).                      (3.5)

There is one such witness for each of the tm owners. Consequently

 E_t=t sum_i c_i^2,
 R_t:=E_t/r = sum_i h_i /(1+a_i/t)^2.                      (3.6)

This is nondecreasing in t and tends to H. The finite choice (3.1) works
without square roots: (1+x)^(-2)>=1-2x for x>=0 implies

 R_t >= H-2D/t >2.                                       (3.7)

Compute the exact rational epsilon=R_t-2>0 from (3.6). No floating-point
limit comparison is needed in a final certificate.

4. EXPLICIT FINITE COMMON-VETO COPY COUNT

Use B disjoint copies of the union product plus one fixed veto owner u.
For every union-product row in each copy append u and retain demand one.
A sufficient exact integer choice is

 B=floor[t/(rho epsilon)]+1 = floor[1/(r epsilon)]+1.      (4.1)

The final owner count is n=Btm+1. There are B R^t generator rows. Every
row is nonempty and contains u. The minimum l1 covering value is EXACTLY
T=1: u=1, all other coordinates zero is feasible, and any one row forces
sum of all nonnegative coordinates to be at least one.

Write z_U for the union product's full projection. The final full
projection is

 u_full=Br/(1+Br),
 x^b=z_U/(1+Br), b=1,...,B.                              (4.2)

Full multipliers are the union-product full multipliers divided by 1+Br
in each copy. Coordinate and veto stationarity are immediate. The full
projection squared norm equals u_full, as an additional consistency check.

For any focal union owner with witness coordinate c_i, whole witness q_U,
and total mass ell_i, let

 gamma_i=1/[1+(B-1)r+ell_i],
 u_i=1-gamma_i.

In the focal copy change its row demand d to u_i+gamma_i d; other copies
retain demand one. Off-owner rows in the focal copy also retain demand
one. All changed demands lie in [0,1]. The exact projection is

 u=u_i,
 x^focal=gamma_i q_U,
 x^other=gamma_i z_U.

Multiply each corresponding base multiplier by gamma_i. Veto stationarity
is gamma_i[(B-1)r+ell_i]=u_i; all other stationarity equations and all
complementarity relations follow from the union-product systems.

Thus the TRUE own envelope, after the fixed-owner realization below,
obeys M_u>=u_full and M_(copy,block,i)>=gamma_i c_i. By (2.1), ell_i<=r,
so gamma_i>=1/(1+Br). Hence

 sum_j M_j^2
   >= [B^2 r^2+B E_t]/(1+Br)^2
    = 1+[B r epsilon-1]/(1+Br)^2
    > 1=T^2.                                            (4.3)

The strict margin in (4.3) is an explicit positive rational number. Both
finite parameters t and B have now been fixed, not left as asymptotic
limits. The owner and row counts may be enormous; this is an existence
construction with a symbolic finite certificate, not an efficiency claim.

5. BALANCED CLOSURE AUDIT

For arbitrary finite nonnegative demands a with a(empty)=0, let C(a)(S)
be the minimum nonnegative total cover of all rows contained in S. The
minimum exists by finite feasibility and compactness of bounded sublevels.
Then C(a)>=a and

 P_S(C(a))=P_S(a) for every S.                            (5.1)

To check the nontrivial inclusion, a cover x of a restricted to S restricts
on every U subset S to a feasible cover of a on U, so x(U)>=C(a)(U).
Every minimizing subgame cover is therefore a nonnegative efficient core
of C(a). Nonnegativity also gives coalition monotonicity by restriction.
Demand-array monotonicity follows from containment of feasible sets.

If an owner-i relaxation changes only rows containing i, closure on a
coalition S omitting i is IDENTICAL to full closure, because none of the
changed rows is contained in S. The relaxed closure is at most the full
closure everywhere, and (5.1) preserves every raw projection polyhedron.
Therefore simultaneous realization applies. There is no requirement that
the raw sparse demand array itself be balanced or coalition-monotone.

6. SPECIALIZED EXPLICIT CLOSURE AND PRODUCTION FUNCTION

The common-veto output makes the preceding step particularly simple. Let
R_g be its generator supports, all containing u, and let d^j_g be the
chosen final RHS for focal owner's witness j. Use j=0 for the full RHS
(all ones). For every coalition S define

 w^j(S)=max({d^j_g : R_g subset S} union {0}).              (6.1)

This is EXACTLY the balanced closure: pay only u the displayed maximum
when u is present; every included row gives the matching lower bound on
total payment. Coalitions without u have value zero. In particular v=w^0
has v(N)=1 and every w^j<=v. Off-j agreement follows because all included
rows of a coalition omitting j retain demand one.

For each nonempty A subset N define a nonnegative coefficient vector
p^(j,A) on n base resources by

 p_i=1 if i is outside A;
 p_u=w^j(A) if u is inside A;
 p_i=0 for all other i inside A.                          (6.2)

This is the standard subgame efficient core (paid entirely to u) extended
with coefficient one outside A. It majorizes w^j on every coalition and
attains w^j(A) at A. All coefficients are explicit rational maxima from
(6.1); no auxiliary optimization or unspecified core choice remains.

Introduce one control resource z_j for each actual owner j and define

 F(x,z)=min{
   p^(0,A).x                  for nonempty A,
   p^(j,A).x + z_j            for each witness j, nonempty A
 } .                                                       (6.3)

The veto's own witness can simply be its full-report choice, so a separate
relaxed veto game is unnecessary. Using 2n resources remains a valid count.
Every owner j has fixed endowment e_j+e_(n+j), full report equal to that
endowment, and its designated low report e_j. The report domain is the
entire coordinatewise interval below this fixed endowment.

At the all-high profile, an owner-j form adds one on a coalition containing
j, so is at least one and hence at least v(S). If j is absent, off-j agreement
makes it majorize v(S) already. Full-game forms attain v(S). When only owner
j drops its control coordinate, its own forms attain w^j(S); every other
form still majorizes that value. Thus the same actual owners and one F
realize ALL chosen projection witnesses at one full profile.

Equation (6.3) is a finite minimum of nonnegative linear forms, and hence
is nonnegative, finite-valued, monotone, concave, and degree-one homogeneous
on the entire orthant. Rationality is explicit. All partial and fractional
reports remain legal; they may enlarge the true envelope but cannot erase
the witnessed diagonal lower bound.

7. GENERAL BOOLEAN REALIZATION: ADDITIONAL AUDIT

The general supporting-form proof is sound. For an efficient nonnegative
core of each subgame A, assigning H outside A majorizes all coalitions,
because a coalition not contained in A has at least one outside coefficient
H and every game's total value is at most H. In the Boolean construction,
a form indexed by U either incurs an H control penalty or has U containing
the coalition's active report set, so monotonicity and locality give the
correct lower bound. Choosing the matching profile and A=S attains equality.

The converse's boundary argument is also valid without assuming continuity
on the closed orthant. At an aggregate c, restrict F to the relative interior
of the positive-coordinate face. A finite concave function has a finite
supergradient there. Monotonicity gives nonnegative coefficients; homogeneity
gives p.c=F(c). For a boundary sub-bundle z, use
F(z)<=F(z+epsilon c)<=p.(z+epsilon c) and then let epsilon decrease to zero.
This yields the efficient nonnegative subgame core needed for necessity.

The star corollary does not require independently chosen relaxed games to
be mutually compatible on profiles where several owners are low. Formula
(6.3), or its general-core counterpart, defines those other games itself.
The finite witness star is enough for the envelope lower bound.

8. SCOPE: DIAGONAL, ENVELOPE FUNDING, AND ARBITRARY MECHANISMS

At the full profile F(B)=v(N)=T=1. Equation (4.3) refutes
sum_j M_j(B)^2<=F(B)^2 for the true own-report minimum-norm envelope.

It does NOT imply sum_j M_j(B)>sqrt(n)F(B). Cauchy-Schwarz says the diagonal
bound would SUFFICE for that funding bound; the converse is false. A vector
can have squared norm greater than one and total mass less than sqrt(n).
A funding counterexample needs a separate certified l1 lower bound exceeding
sqrt(n), with the final owner count n=Btm+1. If the available witnessed
coordinate sum is W, the exact rational comparison is W^2>n because T=1.
H>2 alone supplies no such inequality.

It also gives NO unrestricted incentive lower bound. In the explicit F,
the full-game form indexed by A=N is simply x_u. Thus F(x,z)<=x_u globally,
and any coalition omitting the actual veto owner has production zero.
The mechanism paying y_u(B)=F(B), every other owner zero, is efficient and
coalition-covering on the full real report domain. For a withholding coalition
containing u, superadditivity gives F(B)-F(B-delta)>=F(delta); for a coalition
omitting u, payments and withheld production are both zero. Therefore this
efficient mechanism satisfies full OUT for every coalition. The reduction
is specifically an architecture counterexample, not a lower bound for
arbitrary mechanisms or a bounded-arity result.
