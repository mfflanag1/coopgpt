VERDICT
The construction is valid for every integer Q>=3, including composite Q,
and for every positive multiple of its initial dimension. The finite
rational distribution exists by the all-cut transport argument below.
It gives theta_i=1 for every owner and J>Q-1, H>(Q-1)^2. The direct common-
veto construction, with the previously audited explicit production function,
gives the quantified conclusion

  for every finite C>0 and integer N0, there exist N>=N0 and a finite
  nonnegative min-linear production function F on R_+^(2N), with fixed
  bundles e_i+e_(N+i), full output T=1, and true own-report envelopes M_i,
  such that sum_i M_i > C sqrt(N) T.

This is a counterexample to a universal constant-times-sqrt(N) funding
bound for this particular quadratic-projection envelope architecture.
It is not a lower bound for every possible mechanism, and it does not
assert the conclusion at every prescribed owner count N.

1. PARAMETERS AND LOCAL INDEPENDENCE
Let j>=1 be an integer, Q>=3, and set
 d=16jQ(Q-1)(Q+2), h=8jQ(Q-1), x=h/d=1/[2(Q+2)],
 n=Q^d, M=Q^(d-1), m=n-(Q-1)d-1,
 mu=(Q-2+x)/(Q-1), delta=1/[4Q(Q-1)], beta0=mu+delta,
 K=floor(beta0*m), beta=K/m, b0=1-beta0.
Vertices are elements of(Z/QZ)^d, colored by coordinate sum modulo Q.
Every axis line contains every color once even when Q is composite.

For excluded color0, take the nonzero colors and keep a uniform
((Q-2)d+h)-subset of their (Q-1)d neighbors of0. For excluded color c!=0,
take all colors except c, remove0, choose a uniform h-subset S of axes,
add c e_j for j in S, and for every(j,l),j in S,l!=j independently remove
c e_j+A e_l with A uniform among1,...,Q-1.

Two distinct added points c e_j,c e_l differ in two coordinates; a single
axis line cannot contain both. Hence a line newly completed by additions
contains exactly one added point. Through c e_j in direction j,0 is missing.
In direction l!=j the specified repair removes one of the other Q-1 points.
Each repair target is initially available: its color is c+A!=c. Targets
have weight2, so repairs do not change focal exclusion or neighbor counts.
Repeated deletions are harmless. Every subsequent construction step only
deletes vertices, so independence is preserved throughout.

Each available set excludes0 and has exactly (Q-2)d+h neighbors. A fixed
neighbor a e_j has inclusion probabilities mu in phase0, x in phasea, and
1 in the other Q-2 phases. The mean is(mu+x+Q-2)/Q=mu.

2. EXACT EXCEPTIONAL AVAILABILITY
Let E be the weight2 points, e=(Q-1)^2*binomial(d,2), and write
y=h(h-1)/(d(d-1)). A point a e_j+b e_l starts available in Q-1 phases.
Its only possible deletion phases are a and b, neither equal to its
excluded phase a+b, since a,b are nonzero modulo Q. This remains true
when a+b=0 or when nonzero elements have nontrivial additive order.

If a!=b, the phases are distinct and its exact availability is
  B=(Q-1)/Q-2x/[Q(Q-1)].
If a=b, the two deletion events occur in the same phase. Their overlap
probability is y/[Q(Q-1)^2], because the two ordered-pair coins are
independent but the two axis-selection events have joint probability y.
Thus its exact availability is
  B+y/[Q(Q-1)^2].
No other added point can target this weight2 point. This also explicitly
checks the potential overlap issue omitted by a mere union-bound argument.

Direct algebra gives B-mu=1/[2Q(Q-1)]=2delta. Every exceptional availability
is therefore at least beta0+delta>beta. Independently retain an available
point with probability beta divided by its exact availability. These are
finite rational probabilities in(0,1), and every exceptional marginal
becomes beta. If P is the resulting number retained, then0<=P<=e and
EP=e beta. Its dependence on phase and on all earlier choices is allowed.

3. FAR CLASS COUNTS AND THE COMPLETE TRANSPORT PROOF
The weight>=3 points are untouched by all local changes. Their class sizes
are
 f0=M-1-(Q-1)binomial(d,2),
 fc=M-d-(Q-2)binomial(d,2), c!=0.
For each coordinate pair there are Q-1 nonzero coefficient pairs summing
to0, and Q-2 summing to any prescribed nonzero color. This counting only
uses the additive group, not field division. Consequently sum f_c=m-e.

A state omega records phase, additions, repair coins, and exceptional
thinning. It has finite rational probability p_omega. Its available far
set is precisely all classes except its phase c. Retain T_omega=K-P_omega
far vertices. The total desired expectation is ET=K-beta e=beta sum f_c.

Here is the finite selection lemma, derived independently. The network
source->omega has capacity p_omega*T_omega; each available omega->vertex
arc has capacity p_omega; each vertex->sink arc has capacity beta. For
a fixed subset U of vertices on the sink side, minimizing each state's
side gives cut capacity
  beta(|F|-|U|)+sum p_omega min(T_omega,|U intersect A_omega|).
Thus all cuts have capacity at least beta|F| if and only if
  beta|U|<=sum p_omega min(T_omega,|U intersect A_omega|)         (H)
for every U. Total source and sink capacities both equal beta|F|, so
rational maxflow saturates all these arcs. States of zero probability
are discarded. Divide each state's edge flows by its positive probability;
this gives a conditional vector in[0,1] with integer sum T_omega.

Such a rational vector is a rational convex combination of indicators
of T_omega-subsets. If it has fractional entries, there are at least two
because its sum is integral. Move mass between two in both directions to
their first boundary hits, and express the original as the rational convex
combination of those endpoints. Every endpoint has fewer fractional entries.
Recursion terminates. This procedure preserves the original state
probabilities; it introduces no reweighting of phases or exceptional sets.

For our states let u_c=|U intersect F_c|. The deficit in(H) is concave on
the whole box0<=u_c<=f_c: each state's contribution is the minimum of a
constant and a linear function, followed by subtracting a linear demand.
Every point of the box is a convex combination of its2^Q corners, so it
suffices to prove nonnegativity at all unions of complete color classes.
The following three bounds suffice:
  K-e >=(Q-2)M;                                              (I)
  K <=sum_{c!=a} f_c for every a;                             (II)
  K-e >=(Q beta-Q+2)sum_{c!=a} f_c for every a.                (III)
They also ensure0<T_omega<=|A_omega| for every state.

For a corner with r<=Q-2 colors, its available count in any phase is at
most(Q-2)M<=T. Hence the expected minimum is its size times(Q-1)/Q,
at least beta times its size. For r=Q-1, let a be the omitted color and
f_R the selected size. In phases c in R, bound(I) makes the contribution
f_R-f_c. Their total expectation is (Q-2)f_R/Q. In phasea, bound(II)
makes the contribution T, and its expectation is at least(K-e)/Q.
Bound(III) proves the corner. This lower bound is pointwise in T and
requires no phase independence. For all Q classes, bound(II) makes the
contribution T in every state, giving equality ET=beta|F|. The empty cut
is also equality. These cover EVERY corner and hence EVERY subset cut.

7. EXACT TRUE-ENVELOPE LIMITS AND QUANTIFIERS
Fix Q and let the dimension multiple j tend to infinity. Polynomial terms
in d are negligible against n=Q^d, giving

symmetry of the complete production formula is required. Summation and

  (sum_i M_i)/sqrt(N) ->1/(2b0),

These strengthen, and agree with, the author's witness-only lower limits.

The full raw projection's total funding is exactly
  P_full=B rho/(1+B rho)+Bn/[k(1+B rho)].

  (sum_i M_i)/P_full ->1/b0>Q-1.
Here the raw limit1/2 is a normalized TOTAL-funding limit, not a claim that
each raw coordinate tends1/2. The raw squared norm separately tends1/2.

Given C>0 and N0, choose an integer Q>=3 with(Q-1)/2>C. The constant

projection and envelope by T. This is an explicit diagonal choice of Q
then dimension, with no exchange of two limits and no union-product step.
