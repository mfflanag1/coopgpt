UNIFORM STANDARD-FORM ZERO-ONE POLYTOPES HAVE NORMALIZED MASS H=1
2026-10-21. Addon to mass_geometry_completion_lemma.txt; parent proof file
is frozen for independent review. Conventional proof, not yet independently
reviewed. Uniform unit-demand profiles only.

Theorem.
Let A have nonempty zero-one rows and let C=conv(rows(A)). Assume there are a
real matrix B and vector b with the EXACT representation
                  C={x in R^n:x>=0, Bx=b}.
Assume the minimum-Euclidean-norm point of C is z=p*1 with 0<p<1.
Then every admissible normalized own-row KKT witness satisfies theta_i<=p.
Consequently H=rho*sum_i theta_i^2<=1, with equality at full demands.
Every actual downward incident-row relaxation satisfies q_i<=q_full_i.

Proof.
For any zero-one vertex a and any owner i in a, define
                  y=(z-p*a)/(1-p).
Coordinatewise y_j is either0 or p/(1-p), so y>=0. Moreover
B*y=(b-p*b)/(1-p)=b. Hence y belongs to C, and y_i=0. Every convex
representation of y by zero-one rows therefore uses only off-i rows.
The row-completion hypothesis holds for every i. Apply the completion lemma.
For completeness, that lemma uses z=p*a+(1-p)*y and y.v>=t to bound
||v||^2<=(theta_i/p)*z.v+(1-theta_i/p)*t. If theta_i>p, the inequality
t>=||v||^2 and Cauchy-Schwarz force v=z, a contradiction. QED.

Assumptions are substantive.
The exact standard-form equality is essential: merely having C contained in
{x>=0:Bx=b} does not imply that the completion y belongs to the row hull.
In particular, a nonintegral exact-cover relaxation cannot be substituted for
the true zero-one row hull. A uniform feasible point alone need not be the
minimum-norm point. A convenient sufficient condition is that every row has
the same cardinality k and (k/n)*1 belongs to C: then Cauchy-Schwarz proves
the minnorm claim. The p=1 singleton case is immediate; p=0 is excluded by
nonempty rows. For any nontrivial zero-one standard-form hull satisfying the
theorem, necessarily p<=1/2: if a has a zero coordinate then y at that
coordinate equals p/(1-p)<=1. Thus the theorem does not cover complete
k-subset systems with k>n/2; their separate exchange proof still gives H=1.

Example1: exact covers of equally sized partition blocks.
Partition the n=k*d owners into k disjoint blocks of size d>=2. Rows choose
exactly one owner from each block. C={x>=0:sum_{j in block}x_j=1 for every
block}, the product of simplices. Uniform z_j=1/d is feasible and minnorm,
so H=1. This is an exact-cover family for every k and d, not a finite test.

Example2: integral regular exact-cover systems.
Let B be a zero-one incidence matrix with every row sum d and every column
sum r>0. Suppose C={x>=0:Bx=1} is integral. Nonzero columns imply x_j<=1,
so all vertices are zero-one exact covers; these vertices are the cover rows.
Uniform x_j=1/d is feasible. Summing equalities gives r*sum_j x_j=m, where
m is the number of B-rows; regularity gives md=nr, hence sum_j x_j=n/d.
Cauchy-Schwarz proves z=(1/d)*1 is minnorm and the theorem gives H=1.
The integrality hypothesis must be checked or proved for the chosen B.
Partitions and regular bipartite perfect matching systems instantiate it.

Example3: balanced layered directed path systems.
Take an acyclic s-to-t network in which every s-to-t path has exactly L edges,
every edge lies on such a path, every internal vertex has indegree=outdegree,
and s has outdegree D=t's indegree. Owners are edges; rows are ALL s-to-t paths.
The convex hull is exactly the nonnegative unit-flow polytope Bx=b: repeatedly
extracting a positive s-to-t path decomposes any flow, and acyclicity excludes
circulations. The uniform edge flow1/D is feasible. Every row has L ones,
so this is the minimum-norm point (in fact there are D*L edges, from equal
total flow). Thus H=1 and own-row relaxed coordinates cannot exceed the full
unit-demand projection coordinate1/L. All paths, equal path length, balance,
and acyclicity are essential hypotheses of this example as stated.

Scope.
These are general safe classes for the normalized uniform-demand route. They
do not establish the actual own-report envelope diagonal at nonuniform or
all-real profiles and do not resolve the unrestricted campaign target.
