INDEPENDENT AUDIT: FUNDING VERSUS SQUARED-DIAGONAL AMPLIFICATION
21 October 2026. Audits FUNDING_VERSUS_DIAGONAL_REDUCTION.txt.

VERDICT
The union/veto formulas, both certificate limits, their optimizations, and
the strict positive counterexample criteria are correct. The funding gate
is J>2, equivalently rho*(sum_i theta_i)^2>4n; H>4 by itself does not
algebraically certify that funding gate. H controls the squared-diagonal
certificate instead.

One scope wording needs narrowing. The sentence "Proving J<=2 alone would
only block this amplification route" should say "would block this strict
asymptotic funding criterion." A limit at most1 does not by itself exclude
a finite t with selected funding above sqrt(N_t), a t-dependent choice of
the scalar c, or another product construction. No finite upper bound in
terms of J is proved in the audited note. This qualification does not affect
the valid implication from J>2 to a finite genuine counterexample.

1. BASE CONTRACT
All base rows must be nonempty and have full RHS1. With full projection z
and relaxed q=A^T lambda, normalized multipliers give
  L=sum lambda <=sum lambda*z(R)=z dot q
    <=||z||*||q||<=||z||^2=rho.
Thus0<L_i<=rho for the selected witnesses, and0<=p_i<=L_i because the focal
coordinate is a subset sum of its nonnegative multipliers. A zero-mass
witness can be replaced by the full witness, whose mass is rho>0; even an
unused owner then has a valid positive-mass witness with focal payment0.
No identity L_i=||q^i||^2 is required for fractional relaxed RHS values.

2. INDEPENDENT PRODUCT DERIVATION
For t copies, the full union projection z/t has norm rho/t. Its row-tuple
multiplier is the product of full multipliers divided by t*rho^(t-1).
Summing over other tuple entries gives z/t coordinatewise. A positive
tuple multiplier uses only tight base rows, so its union row is tight.

For owner type i define
  alpha_i=rho/[rho+(t-1)L_i], beta_i=L_i/[rho+(t-1)L_i].
Use alpha_i*q^i in its own base copy and beta_i*z in each other copy.
The tuple RHS is alpha_i*b^i+(t-1)beta_i, which is in[0,1] and equals1
off the focal owner. The tuple multiplier is alpha_i times the selected
focal multiplier times the other full multipliers, divided by rho^(t-1).
The focal marginal is alpha_i*q^i; another marginal is
alpha_i*L_i*z/rho=beta_i*z. Complementarity follows from the base KKT.
The total mass is alpha_i*L_i and focal payment alpha_i*p_i.

Put d_i=rho/L_i-1>=0. The exact quantities are therefore
  p_i(t)=rho*theta_i/(t+d_i), L_i(t)=rho/(t+d_i).
All d_i are fixed finite numbers because the base and witnesses are fixed.

Take B_t=max(1,floor(c*t/rho)), c>0. The full veto is
u_t=b_t/(1+b_t), b_t=B_t*rho/t. The selected private payment is
  a_i(t)=p_i(t)/[1+(B_t-1)rho/t+L_i(t)]
        =[rho*theta_i/(t+d_i)]/
             [1+b_t-rho*d_i/(t*(t+d_i))].                     (1)
This is precisely the common-veto KKT construction, not an optimization
over all private reports. There are B_t*t copies of each original owner
type and one veto; N_t=B_t*t*n+1. All full generator RHS values are1, so
the minimum total cover is1. The direct min-linear realization supplies
one production function on2N_t resources and the SAME fixed owners across
all selected unilateral reports. Fractional intermediate union RHS values
are allowed; they need not be deletion endpoints.

3. BOTH LIMITS, WITH THEIR NORMALIZATIONS
Since B_t/t->c/rho,
  N_t/t^2->cn/rho,
  t*a_i(t)->rho*theta_i/(1+c), u_t->c/(1+c).
There are only finitely many original owner types, so these limits may be
summed without any additional uniformity assumption.

Define the selected certificate values
  S_t=u_t+B_t*t*sum_i a_i(t),
  D_t=u_t^2+B_t*t*sum_i a_i(t)^2.
Then
  S_t/t -> c*sum_i theta_i/(1+c),
  S_t/sqrt(N_t) -> [sqrt(c)/(1+c)]*
                         sqrt(rho/n)*sum_i theta_i,
  D_t ->[c^2+c*rho*sum_i theta_i^2]/(1+c)^2.                  (2)
The veto contribution to the first normalized limit vanishes, but its
squared contribution in the second limit does not vanish. This distinction
is essential and is handled correctly in the audited proof.

Let J=sqrt(rho/n)*sum theta_i and H=rho*sum theta_i^2. Equation(2) becomes
  selected funding coefficient=sqrt(c)*J/(1+c),
  selected diagonal limit=(c^2+cH)/(1+c)^2.
For the TRUE envelopes, the justified statements are the corresponding
liminf LOWER bounds. They are not equalities for arbitrary bases. Equality
requires separate upper control, such as the audited special-class caps
used for the hypercube examples. Additional real reports can raise either
true-envelope aggregate.

4. OPTIMIZATION AND FINITE EXISTENCE
The first coefficient has maximum J/2 at c=1. Its squared gap from the
maximum is exactly
  J^2/4-c*J^2/(1+c)^2=J^2*(c-1)^2/[4(1+c)^2].
If J>2, choose c=1 and eta=(J/2-1)/2>0. By the limit definition there is
an integer t0 such that every integer t>=t0 has
  S_t/sqrt(N_t)>J/2-eta=(J/2+1)/2>1.
Fixing ANY such t gives one finite, genuine, fixed-owner production
instance whose true envelope total exceeds sqrt(N_t), with full output
and minimum total cover both1. No infinite institution or interchange of
growing-base limits is required. With rational certificate data, the
positive gate is checkable without square roots as
  rho*(sum_i theta_i)^2>4n,
and a finite candidate can be checked exactly via S_t^2>N_t.

A universal funding theorem for this architecture would therefore imply
J<=2 on every base/witness collection. The converse is NOT established.
In particular, even a proof of J<=2 would only exclude the strict limit
criterion above unless supplemented by a finite-product upper argument.

For H>2, the diagonal maximum is H^2/[4(H-1)] at c=H/(H-2). The exact gap is
  H^2/[4(H-1)]-(c^2+cH)/(1+c)^2
   =[H-(H-2)c]^2/[4(H-1)(1+c)^2].
Also the diagonal limit minus1 is [c(H-2)-1]/(1+c)^2. Therefore H>2 gives
a strict finite diagonal violation after choosing c>1/(H-2) and then
sufficiently large finite t. If H<=2, the displayed limits are below1 for
every finite c and have supremum1 as c tends to infinity. For H>4, the
simple fixed choice c=2 already gives limit(4+2H)/9>4/3; optimization is
optional. These statements concern selected asymptotic certificates, not
universal finite upper bounds.

5. HETEROGENEITY AND THE LOGICAL STATUS OF H>4
Cauchy gives J^2<=H, with equality exactly when all selected theta_i are
equal. More precisely, when H>0,
  J^2=H * (sum theta_i)^2/[n*sum theta_i^2].
The final factor measures how distributed the selected normalized masses
are across owners. Thus a large squared sum cannot be substituted for the
ordinary sum in the funding calculation. J>2 implies H>4 and also n*rho>4,
since theta_i<=1; the reverse implications are not supplied by this algebra.

No feasible H>4 example is established in either note. Therefore the
statement "H>4 alone is insufficient" should be understood as identifying
the correct certificate formula, not claiming an already constructed
admissible example with H>4 and J<=2.

There is also an exact structural padding observation under this base
contract. Append r unused coordinates (zero columns) to any base. Its full
projection extends by zeros and rho is unchanged. Retain existing witness
multipliers and use the full witness for every added owner, giving them
L=rho and theta=0. All original rows remain nonempty, all local relaxations
remain valid, and
  H_new=H_old, J_new=sqrt(n/(n+r))*J_old.
Thus H does not determine this construction's funding coefficient. If an
H>4 base were found, this padding would retain H>4 while making J arbitrarily
small. This is a CONDITIONAL observation, not a claim that H>4 has been
found. It does not assert that padding preserves a funding counterexample
or that every final owner count can be attained with the same lower ratio.

6. THE ONE-FOCAL POINTWISE FAMILY
For the audited family n=s^3,rho=1/s,p_full=1/s, use its inflated focal
theta and full witnesses for all other owners. Direct substitution gives
  J=1-1/s^3+theta/s^2 ->1,
  H=1-1/s^3+theta^2/s ->1.
Since theta<1 and s>=2, J<1+1/s^2<=5/4. These particular certificates
therefore do not approach the strict funding gate J>2, despite unbounded
pointwise theta/p_full. This is not an upper bound over all possible
witness collections for those bases. No all-owner symmetry is available.
