VERDICT AND PRECISE SCOPE
Both the13-owner certificate and the all-integer parameter family are
correct. They refute theta_i<=2p and every fixed-factor replacement
theta_i<=C*p under the assumptions that all rows have the same size and
the minimum-norm row-hull point is uniform p*1. They also refute a fixed

They do NOT establish H>4, a diagonal or funding counterexample, or a

The only changed witness used in the asserted aggregate is that one owner's
witness; every other owner uses its full witness. This aggregate tends to1.
No independent issue or missing off-owner constraint was found.

NOTATION AND AN INDEPENDENT FULL-COVER ARGUMENT
Keep the full covering coordinate z_i separate from the normalized row-hull
coordinate p. If all n-owner rows have size k and the displayed full KKT
point is z=(1/k)*1, then rho=n/k^2. Dividing its multipliers by rho produces
a row-incidence mixture v=z/rho=(k/n)*1, so p=k/n. Independently, every row
mixture has coordinate sum k; Cauchy-Schwarz gives squared norm at least
k^2/n, attained by this uniform v. Thus it truly is the minimum-norm
row-hull point. Equal row sizes alone would not establish its membership;
the explicit multiplier mixture supplies that missing condition here.

2. GENERAL PARAMETER COUNTS AND KKT
For any integer s>=2 put
  n=s^3, k=s^2, |A|=a=s^2-1, |B|=b=(s-1)s^2,
  r=s-1, h=s^2-s.
Use G={i} union A, all k-subsets of B, and all mixed rows
{i} union C union D with |C|=r from A and |D|=h from B.

All subset choices exist:1<=r<=a,1<=h<=b and k<=b. The sizes add to
1+r+h=k, and1+a+b=n. The three row classes are distinct. Write
  M=binomial(a,r)*binomial(b,h), O=binomial(b,k).
There are exactly1+M+O rows. Off-owner rows are precisely the O B-rows.
The mixed-row counts containing a fixed A or B coordinate are respectively
  M_A=binomial(a-1,r-1)*binomial(b,h),
  M_B=binomial(a,r)*binomial(b-1,h-1),
while a fixed B coordinate belongs to O_B=binomial(b-1,k-1) B-rows.
The exact inclusion ratios are
  M_A/M=r/a=1/(s+1), M_B/M=h/b=1/s,
  O_B/O=k/b=1/(s-1).

For the full system, place mass1/k on G and total mass b/k^2 uniformly
over all O B-rows. Every coordinate receives1/k, and every row is tight.
The full mass/norm is rho=n/k^2=1/s. By the row-hull argument above its
normalized uniform coordinate is p=k/n=1/s, whereas its raw covering
coordinate is z_i=1/s^2. These are different quantities.

Delete ONLY G. Place total multiplier mass
  T_mix=(s+1)/(2s^2), T_B=(s-1)^2/(2s^3)
uniformly within the mixed and B classes. Both classes and both masses are
nonempty and strictly positive even at s=2. The incidence ratios give
  q_i=T_mix=(s+1)/(2s^2),
  q_A=T_mix/(s+1)=1/(2s^2),
  q_B=T_mix/s+T_B/(s-1)=1/s^2.
Every mixed row has payment
  q_i+(s-1)q_A+(s^2-s)q_B=1,
and each B-row has payment k*q_B=1. The deleted row has payment
  q_i+(s^2-1)q_A=(s+1)/(2s),
which lies in(0,1) for s>=2. Consequently all original off-owner rows
remain satisfied at RHS1, all positive multipliers have tight RHS1, and
the deleted G has multiplier zero. This is complete exact KKT for every s.

Its mass is
  L=T_mix+T_B=(2s^2-s+1)/(2s^3).
Directly summing q_i^2+a*q_A^2+b*q_B^2 gives the same expression, independently
checking the normalization. Thus
  theta_i=s(s+1)/(2s^2-s+1),
  theta_i/p=s^2(s+1)/(2s^2-s+1)>(s+1)/2 -> infinity.            (1)
The strict inequality follows because2s^2-s+1<2s^2 for s>=2. In particular
the ratio diverges, while theta_i itself tends to1/2. The raw coordinate
ratio q_i/z_i=(s+1)/2 also diverges.

The factor2 normalized violation holds for every s>=3. Its numerator is
P(s)=s^3-3s^2+2s-2, with P(3)=4 and exact discrete difference
P(s+1)-P(s)=3s(s-1)>0. At s=3 one obtains n27,k9 and theta_i=3/4>2/3.
No parameter range, floor effect, or limiting probability is missing.

3. WHY THIS DOES NOT GIVE AN AGGREGATE COUNTEREXAMPLE
The focal incidence degree is1+M, while each A coordinate has degree
1+M/(s+1), strictly smaller. Hence the row family is not vertex transitive.
The compact example likewise has incidence degrees176,36,106 on i,A,B.
Uniformity of the minimum-norm full point is not a substitute for a
transitive row family. Transporting the large focal witness to all owners
would require changing the constraints and proving KKT again.

Using just the verified focal witness and the full witness for all others,
  H=rho*[theta_i^2+(n-1)p^2]
   =1-1/s^3+theta_i^2/s.
Since0<theta_i<1 for s>=2, this satisfies H<1+1/s<=3/2 and tends to1.
Thus the exhibited collection of owner witnesses cannot establish H>4.
This is a statement about that collection, not an upper bound on all
possible witnesses for the same family.

The common-veto block theorem's condition n*rho<=4 fails for this family's
s>=3 members, since n*rho=s^2. In the13-owner example n*rho=169/36>4.
There is therefore no conflict with that theorem. No total-funding claim
or general upper/lower theorem follows from this pointwise obstruction.
