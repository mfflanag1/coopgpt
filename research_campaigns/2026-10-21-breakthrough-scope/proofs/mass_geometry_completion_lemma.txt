ROW COMPLETION LEMMA FOR NORMALIZED OWN-ROW RELAXATIONS
2026-10-21. Conventional proof; independent audit still required.

This is a sufficient safe-class theorem at a UNIFORM unit-demand profile.
It does not prove the actual own-report envelope diagonal at arbitrary profiles.

Setup.
Let a_1,...,a_m be nonempty zero-one row vectors in R^n, C their convex
hull, and z the unique minimum-Euclidean-norm point of C. Put d=||z||^2>0.
The full unit-demand covering projection is p=z/d, with rho=||p||^2=1/d:
the minimum-hull condition gives a_r.z>=d for every r, hence p is feasible;
any convex representation z=sum mu_r a_r gives the projection certificate
p=A^T(mu/d), with positive mu supported on a_r.z=d.

An admissible normalized witness for owner i consists of alpha>=0, sum alpha=1,
v=A^T alpha, and t>0, satisfying
  a_r.v >= t                     for every off-i row (a_ri=0),
  a_r.v = t                      for positive-alpha off-i rows,
  a_r.v <= t                     for positive-alpha own-i rows.
Here theta=v_i=sum_{own}alpha_r. These conditions are exactly the uniform
incident-row downward-relaxation KKT conditions after lambda=alpha/t and
q=v/t. In particular ||v||^2=sum alpha_r(a_r.v)<=t.

Completion hypothesis for owner i with 0<r=z_i<1.
For every own-i row a, the vector
             b_a=(z-r*a)/(1-r)
belongs to the off-i face C_i^0=conv{a_s:a_si=0}.

Claim. Every admissible normalized witness satisfies theta<=z_i.

Proof.
Since b_a is a convex combination of off-i rows, b_a.v>=t. Thus every own
row a (whether or not used by alpha) satisfies
             a.v <= [z.v-(1-r)t]/r.
Average this inequality over the own part of alpha, and use exact value t on
its off part. Writing c=theta/r gives
             ||v||^2 <= c(z.v)+(1-c)t.                       (1)
Suppose theta>r, so c>1. Since t>=||v||^2, (1) implies
             ||v||^2 <= c(z.v)-(c-1)||v||^2,
and hence ||v||^2<=z.v. By Cauchy-Schwarz this implies ||v||<=||z||.
Because v belongs to C and z is its UNIQUE minimum-norm point, v=z. But
then theta=v_i=z_i=r, a contradiction. Therefore theta<=r. QED.

Raw-coordinate consequence.
For any actual downward incident-row relaxation, let q=A^T lambda be its
projection, L=sum lambda, and p the full projection. Its KKT conditions give
||q||^2<=L. Full feasibility gives L<=p.q<=sqrt(rho)||q||; hence L<=rho
when L>0. Therefore q_i=L theta<=rho*z_i=p_i. The full profile itself is an
allowed relaxation, so the own-row coordinate envelope at this unit profile
equals p_i whenever the completion hypothesis holds.

Edge cases.
If z_i=1, theta<=1=z_i is automatic. If z_i=0, every convex representation
of z uses only off-i rows. The full projection has a KKT certificate using
only those rows, and is therefore already the off-i covering projection.
Every incident-row relaxation has that same projection; q_i=theta=0.
Thus if completion holds for every owner with 0<z_i<1, then
             H=rho*sum_i theta_i^2 <= rho*||z||^2=1.
This bound is attained by using the full profile for every owner.

Regular bipartite perfect-matching row hypergraphs.
Let G be a d0-regular bipartite graph with N vertices on each side and d0*N
edges (owners). Rows are ALL its perfect matchings, each with N edges.
By degree counting Hall's condition holds. Removing a perfect matching leaves
a (d0-1)-regular bipartite graph; induction decomposes its edges into perfect
matchings. Consequently z_e=1/d0 is in C. Every point of C has coordinate sum
N, so Cauchy-Schwarz proves this uniform vector is the unique minimum-norm
point. For an arbitrary matching a, the vector (z-a/d0)/(1-1/d0) is the
uniform fractional perfect matching on G minus a, and is a convex combination
of its perfect matchings. It is therefore in the off-e face for every e in a.
The completion hypothesis holds for all owners. Thus H<=1, and every
incident-row relaxation satisfies q_e<=p_e=1/N. This includes the full
Birkhoff/permutation system (d0=N). For d0=1 the conclusion is direct.

Interpretation limits.
This proves coordinate monotonicity under arbitrary incident ROW relaxation
from unit demands for these row systems. It neither proves coordinate
monotonicity from arbitrary nonuniform demand profiles nor the global
all-real-profile own-report envelope diagonal. It is a general obstruction
to obtaining an H>2 witness from regular bipartite perfect-matching systems.

Irregular bipartite graphs.
Their matching polytope has equality constraints plus nonnegativity, but the
minimum-norm fractional matching z need not be uniform. The completion vector
for an owner i is feasible precisely when z_e>=z_i for every edge e in the
chosen own matching (the equality constraints are automatically preserved).
Thus this lemma applies to owner i if that inequality holds for EVERY matching
containing i. It does not generally extend merely because the rows are
perfect matchings: a matching can contain both low-z and high-z edges.
Any stronger irregular-graph theorem requires a different argument.

Conditional completion and a limited inflation-ratio principle.
The proof only needs completion of the particular conditional own mixture
u=sum_{own}alpha_r*a_r/theta, rather than of every individual own row.
If C has an exact representation {x>=0:B*x=b}, then z>=z_i*u coordinatewise
is sufficient: (z-z_i*u)/(1-z_i) satisfies the same equalities, is nonnegative,
and has coordinate i equal0. Consequently, if 0<z_i<1 and theta>z_i, some
coordinate j must satisfy v_j/z_j>theta/z_i (an edge with z_j=0<v_j counts as
infinite ratio). Otherwise u_j<=v_j/theta<=z_j/z_i for every j, and conditional
completion gives a contradiction. This DOES NOT prove a bound on all own
envelopes: v_j belongs to i's witness and is not automatically achievable as
j's own-report normalized coordinate.

Separate exchange calibration: all k-subset rows.
Let rows be all k-subsets of n owners, 1<=k<n. For a positive-alpha own row a
and any j outside a, replacing i by j gives an off row b. Hence
v_j-v_i=b.v-a.v>=t-a.v>=0. If some coordinate j had v_j<v_i, it would have to
belong to EVERY positive-alpha own row; then v_j>=sum_{own}alpha=v_i, again
a contradiction. Thus all coordinates satisfy v_j>=v_i, and sum v_j=k gives
theta=v_i<=k/n=z_i. This proves H<=1 for complete uniform hypergraphs even
when k>n/2, where the rowwise completion hypothesis is unavailable. It is a
calibration safe class, not an extension to arbitrary restricted hypergraphs.
