A limit of the minimum-norm envelope

A three-hour campaign found a general obstruction to one proposed payment rule. Making each owner's payment monotone can produce funding far beyond its raw projection benchmark.

The result

There is no fixed constant C for which this rule always spends at most C√N times output.

For every C >0 and N0, there is a finite instance with N ≥ N0 and
∑i Mi(b) > C√N · T.

Along an explicit sequence, the stronger bound is ∑iMi > (1/40)√N(log N/log log N)1/3T. The squared-payment total and the ratio to raw projection funding are also unbounded.

This is a result about the own-report envelope of the minimum-Euclidean-norm cover. It is not an impossibility theorem for all truthful mechanisms. Novelty and field-level significance remain unconfirmed.

What it means

The rule under study

First minimize the sum of squared payments while covering every coalition. Then, for each owner, take the largest payment it could obtain by underreporting its resources. This makes the payment nondecreasing in its own report and preserves coalition coverage.

Why the budget can grow

Each owner's maximum can come from a different report profile. Combining those separate coordinates need not preserve one common budget bound. The construction makes this mismatch grow across the whole owner population.

Owners and production stay fixed in every comparison. The example has two resources per owner, one finite min-linear technology with binary coefficients, and all real unilateral underreports remain allowed. The endpoint reports in the certificate only lower-bound that full supremum.

The existing manuscript already records a different mechanism with an O(√N) guarantee. Today's obstruction does not contradict it; it rules out this proposed substitute. The unrestricted mechanism gap and the coalition paper's arity-three gap are unchanged.

THE ECONOMIC AND MATHEMATICAL CONTRACT
There are N actual owners, each with a fixed two-coordinate resource bundle.
The production function is one finite minimum of nonnegative linear forms,
defined on all nonnegative real resource vectors. Its coefficients can all
be0 or1. The induced coalition value is production from that coalition's
reported resources. Full output in the examples is T=1.

The raw payment q minimizes sum_i x_i^2 over nonnegative vectors covering
every coalition. There is no efficiency equality. The proposed monotone
rule sets M_i to the supremum of q_i over all feasible underreports by i,
holding every other owner fixed. It covers coalitions and is own-report
monotone by construction. That incentive statement is payment-only and
individual. It is not coalition-proofness, full outside-option protection,
private-cost truthfulness, or a different ownership assignment.

The proof uses particular endpoint reports to lower-bound the TRUE supremum.
It does not replace the report domain by endpoints. Every other real
underreport remains admissible and can only increase the lower bound.

HOW THE FINAL CONSTRUCTION WORKS
1. Use the vertices of a q-ary cube and hyperedges consisting of complete
   axis-parallel lines. Cover rows are complements of independent sets
   of one fixed size. Translation gives a uniform full projection.
2. For each focal owner, construct a rational distribution of independent
   sets that avoids the focal owner while controlling neighbor and outside
   marginals. Local repairs break every newly completed line.
3. Thin the exceptional weight-two points. A finite rational transport
   argument selects the far points at an exact set size. Concavity reduces
   ALL transport cuts to unions of color classes; the proof checks every
   such case uniformly for every integer alphabet size Q>=3.
4. The resulting row mixture gives exact KKT multipliers for deleting only
   some focal-incident rows. Every off-owner row remains covered, including
   those outside the distribution's support. The focal coordinate equals
   the total multiplier mass. Symmetry supplies a witness for every owner.
5. Add a common veto to balanced disjoint copies. One explicit min-linear
   function with owner-specific control resources realizes all selected
   unilateral comparisons simultaneously, with fixed owners and bundles.
6. A finite inequality gives funding/sqrt(N)>(Q-1)/8. The owner-count
   growth converts this into the displayed logarithmic refinement.

UNBOUNDED FUNDING INFLATION OF THE OWN-REPORT MINIMUM-NORM ENVELOPE

Internal theorem draft; external correctness and priority review pending.

MAIN STATEMENT.
For every C>0 and N0, there exist N>=N0 fixed actual owners,2N resource
coordinates, one finite minimum F of nonnegative linear forms with binary
coefficients, and fixed full bundles b_i=e_i+e_(N+i), such that T=F(sum b_i)=1
and the TRUE own-report envelope satisfies

                 sum_i M_i^F(b) > C sqrt(N) T.

The same class has unbounded sum_i M_i^F(b)^2/T^2, and an unbounded ratio
between envelope funding and full-profile raw minimum-norm funding.
Along an explicit owner-count sequence the funding is at least

        (1/40) sqrt(N) (log N/log log N)^(1/3) T.

This is an architecture-specific obstruction. Every example admits the
simple efficient rule that pays all output to its common veto owner on
the fixed-bundle underreport domain. The theorem is therefore NOT a lower
bound for arbitrary mechanisms, a result for activity arity three, or a
solution of the unrestricted optimal-mechanism owner/resource gap.

CONTRACT.
For any real profile b define v_b(S)=F(sum_(i in S)b_i). Let q(v) minimize
sum_i x_i^2 subject to x>=0 and x(S)>=v(S) for every coalition. There is
NO efficiency equality. Define
 M_i^F(b)=sup{q_i(v_(a_i,b_-i)):0<=a_i<=b_i coordinatewise}.
This is the actual supremum over all real unilateral underreports, not
an endpoint surrogate. It covers all coalitions because M>=q(v_b).
It is nondecreasing in each owner's own report because the supremum's
domain expands, with other owners fixed. These are payment-only individual
incentives, not coalition-proofness, outside-option protection, or private-
cost truthfulness. At each profile an active supporting form of F gives
an efficient nonnegative cover. Thus each underreport's q coordinate is
at most T and every envelope is finite. The number of supporting forms
can be exponential; polynomial-time computation is not asserted.

PROOF ROADMAP.
Part I constructs a transitive family of binary cover rows. For each
owner, deleting only some of that owner's incident rows makes its local
minimum-norm coordinate equal the total multiplier mass. The normalized
aggregate grows without limit as the alphabet Q grows.
Part II adds a common veto and realizes ALL selected unilateral report
comparisons in ONE production function with the SAME owners and bundles.
Part III derives the finite and asymptotic funding consequences. Neither
numerical feasibility nor a finite search substitutes for the all-Q proof.

PART I. FINITE Q-ARY ROW CONSTRUCTION
The following construction follows the frozen reviewed source, with the
explicit full-cover KKT bridge requested by the reviewer added below.
Its original word 'candidate' records provenance; see the final audit
ledger for the current verification state. Symbols here are local: n is
base owner count, N later denotes amplified actual owner count.

1. PARAMETERS AND ROW UNIVERSE.
Fix integer Q>=3 (no primality assumption). Set
 d=16Q(Q-1)(Q+2), h=8Q(Q-1), x=h/d=1/[2(Q+2)],
 n=Q^d, M=Q^(d-1), m=n-(Q-1)d-1,
 mu=(Q-2+x)/(Q-1), delta=1/[4Q(Q-1)], beta0=mu+delta,
 K=floor(beta0*m), beta=K/m, s=(Q-2)d+h+K, k=n-s.
Vertices are (Z/QZ)^d. Hyperedges are complete axis-parallel Q-point lines.
Independent sets contain no entire line. The FULL row family consists of
complements of ALL independent s-sets. Translation acts transitively on
vertices and preserves this family. We construct one such set distribution,
proving in particular that the row family is nonempty.

At focal0 the neighbors are a e_j, 1<=a<=Q-1, 1<=j<=d. We construct
independent s-sets with focal absent, exactly (Q-2)d+h selected neighbors,
each neighbor marginal mu, exactly K selected outside vertices, and each
outside vertex marginal beta. Their complement mixture w has focal1,
neighbor entries a0=1-mu=(1-x)/(Q-1), outside b=1-beta.

2. AVAILABLE SETS AND LOCAL REPAIRS.
Color a vertex by its coordinate sum modulo Q; every axis line has all Q
colors. Choose excluded color c uniformly from0,...,Q-1.
If c=0, start with all nonzero colors and retain a uniformly selected
((Q-2)d+h)-subset of the (Q-1)d neighbors. Keep all other available vertices.
If c!=0, start with all colors except c, delete focal0, choose a uniform
h-subset S of coordinate indices, and add c e_j for j in S. For each added
c e_j and every l!=j independently choose A uniformly from{1,...,Q-1} and
delete c e_j+A e_l. Take the union of all these deletions.

Each resulting available set is independent. The original Q-1 colors
contain no full line. No axis line can contain two added vertices c e_j.
The axis line through c e_j in direction j loses0; in direction l!=j the
construction deletes one of its other Q-1 vertices. Thus every possible
newly completed line is broken. Each set excludes0 and contains exactly
(Q-2)d+h neighbors; no repair deletion is a neighbor.
For a fixed neighbor a e_j, conditional inclusion probabilities are mu
in phase0, x in phasea, and1 in each of the other Q-2 phases. Their mean
(mu+x+Q-2)/Q equals mu by the definition of mu.

3. EXCEPTIONAL MARGINALS.
Let E be all Hamming-weight2 vertices, e=(Q-1)^2*binomial(d,2).
For u=a e_j+b e_l (j!=l,a,b!=0), the original color-phase availability is
(Q-1)/Q. It can be deleted only through an added a e_j in phasea or an
added b e_l in phaseb. If a!=b, these are distinct phases, giving exact
availability
 B=(Q-1)/Q-2x/[Q(Q-1)].
If a=b, their union deletion probability is at most the sum of the two
probabilities, so availability is at least B (the duplicate correction
is positive). This argument also covers composite Q and a+b=0: neither
a nor b equals a+b, since both coefficients are nonzero.
A direct computation gives
 B-mu=1/[2Q(Q-1)]=2delta,
so every exceptional vertex has availability P_u>=B=beta0+delta>beta.
Each P_u is rational because the construction is finite rational.
Conditionally on availability, retain u with probability beta/P_u, using
independent thinning coins. Then every exceptional vertex has marginal
exactly beta. Independence and all neighbor counts are preserved. Let P
be the number retained, so0<=P<=e and E[P]=beta e.

4. FAR CLASSES AND FINITE TRANSPORT.
Far vertices have Hamming weight>=3. Let F_c be their color classes. Their
sizes are
 f0=M-1-(Q-1)*binomial(d,2),
 fc=M-d-(Q-2)*binomial(d,2) for c!=0.
Indeed there are Q-1 nonzero ordered pairs summing to0 and Q-2 summing to
a fixed nonzero color, for each chosen coordinate pair. Thus sum f_c=m-e.
In phase c every far vertex of color !=c is available and none of color c
is. A state omega records all local and exceptional-thinning choices;
its probability p_omega is rational. We need retain exactly
 T_omega=K-P_omega
far vertices, with each far vertex's unconditional marginal beta.
E T=K-beta e=beta*sum f_c.

We use the following elementary finite rational transport lemma. For
states with probabilities p_omega, available sets A_omega, integer target
sizes T_omega, and common desired marginal beta, such a selection exists
if expected target size=beta|F| and every U subset F satisfies
 beta|U| <= sum_omega p_omega min(T_omega,|U intersect A_omega|).
Proof: source->state capacities p_omega T_omega, state->available-vertex
capacities p_omega, vertex->sink capacities beta. Maxflow/mincut gives
precisely these inequalities (optimize each state's side of a fixed cut).
A rational flow saturates all source and sink arcs. Divide state-vertex
flows by p_omega to get conditional marginals in[0,1] of integer sum T.
Any such rational vector is a rational mixture of fixed-cardinality
indicators: shift between two fractional coordinates in both directions
until a coordinate hits0 or1, express the original as a convex mixture,
and recurse. Each endpoint has fewer fractional coordinates. This gives
a finite rational distribution of T-subsets of A_omega.

For our states the cut deficit depends only on u_c=|U intersect F_c|.
It is concave on the box0<=u_c<=f_c: every summand is the minimum of a
constant and a linear function, minus a linear desired demand. Therefore
it suffices to check box corners, i.e. unions of entire color classes.
We will prove below these three uniform bounds:
 (i) K-e >=(Q-2)M;
 (ii) K <= sum_{c!=a} f_c for every a;
 (iii) K-e >=(Q beta-Q+2)*sum_{c!=a} f_c for every a.
For a corner containing r<=Q-2 classes, (i) implies T is at least every
available count in that union, so its RHS is exactly (Q-1)/Q times its
size, >=beta times its size. For r=Q-1, let a be the omitted color and
write f_R for the union size. In each phase c in R the available portion
has at most(Q-2)M vertices, so (i) makes the minimum its available count.
In phasea, (ii) makes the minimum T. Thus the cut RHS is
 ((Q-2)/Q)*f_R + E[T*1_{phase=a}]
 >=((Q-2)*f_R+K-e)/Q >=beta*f_R by(iii).
For all Q classes, (ii) gives RHS=E T=beta sum f_c, equality. The empty
cut is trivial. All cuts hold, so the transport lemma completes the
fixed-size independent-set distribution.

5. UNIFORM SCALAR BOUNDS VALID FOR ALL Q>=3.
First d>=480. For every integer D>=20, 3^(D-5)>100D^2: check D20 and use
3(D/(D+1))^2>1 for induction. Hence
 M=Q^4*Q^(d-5)>=Q^4*3^(d-5)>100Q^4 d^2.
Every far-class size is positive and in[M-Qd^2,M]. Also
 e+Qd+2 <=Q^2d^2,
so flooring yields
 K-e >=beta0*Q*M-Q^2d^2,       K<=beta0*Q*M.
Put gamma=(Q-1)/Q-beta0. Since B=beta0+delta,
 gamma=delta+2x/[Q(Q-1)]>delta>=1/(4Q^2).
Moreover b0=1-beta0<1/(Q-1)<=3/(2Q), hence
 Q beta0-Q+2=2-Q b0>1/2.
Now(i) follows from
 K-e-(Q-2)M >=(Q beta0-Q+2)M-Q^2d^2>0.
For(ii), sum_{c!=a}f_c>=(Q-1)(M-Qd^2), so its excess over K is at least
 Q gamma M-Q(Q-1)d^2 > M/(4Q)-Q^2d^2>0.
For(iii), the coefficient Q beta-Q+2 is positive; beta>=beta0-1/m>mu
(as verified below), and Qmu-Q+2>0. Its product with a Q-1-class union is
at most (Q beta0-Q+2)(Q-1)M. Thus the excess of K-e is at least
 (Q-2)(Q-1-Q beta0)M-Q^2d^2
 =(Q-2)Q gamma M-Q^2d^2 >=M/12-Q^2d^2>0.
(The weak1/12 bound uses(Q-2)/(4Q)>=1/12.) These prove all cuts.

6. OFF-OWNER KKT AND FULL PROJECTION.
The enormous M bound gives m>h and m>1/delta. Therefore beta>=beta0-1/m
>mu, so a0>b. Every supported row contains focal0, exactly d-h neighbors,
and k-1-d+h=m-K outside vertices. Its potential is the common value
 t=1+(d-h)a0+(m-K)b
  =1+(Q-1)d*a0^2+m*b^2=||w||^2>0.
Every off-focal row complements an independent set containing0. Each of
the d axis lines through0 must omit at least one of its Q-1 neighbors, so
such a row has at least d neighbors. Its potential is at least
 d*a0+(k-d)*b.
This minus t is h(a0-b)-(1-b)=h(beta-mu)-beta. Because h delta=2,
 h(beta-mu)-beta >=2-h/m-beta0>0
using m>h and beta0<1. Thus ALL off rows are feasible at threshold t.

Set lambda=alpha/t and q_local=w/t, where alpha is the finite row-mixture
constructed above. Keep every off-row RHS1. Set own-row RHS1 on support
of alpha and0 on other own rows. Primal feasibility, stationarity
q_local=A^T lambda, and complementary slackness hold exactly. This is the
unique minimum-norm cover for an incident-only relaxation. Total dual
mass ell=1/t and focal coordinate q_local,0=1/t give theta0=1.

Since the full row universe is nonempty, all its rows have size k, and
translation acts transitively, the uniform point v=(k/n)1 lies in its
convex hull. Every point in that hull has coordinate sum k, so Cauchy-
Schwarz proves v is the unique minnorm point. For the full cover, the uniform vector z=(1/k)1 is feasible. Let alpha
be the translation-orbit distribution of any row. Its mean is(k/n)1,
so lambda_R=(n/k^2)alpha_R gives A^T lambda=z, and every row is tight
at z. These explicit KKT multipliers prove z is the full cover projection
and rho=n/k^2. Translate the focal witness to each owner.

PART II. COMMON VETO AND EXACT FIXED-OWNER REALIZATION

A. KKT amplification with binary deletions.
Let a base have n coordinates, full unit-demand projection z with norm
squared rho, and for each i a binary incident-row deletion witness q^i
with nonnegative KKT multipliers lambda^i. In our construction its focal
coordinate and total multiplier mass both equal ell=1/t. Complementarity
implies ||q^i||^2=ell: all positive multipliers are on retained unit rows.
Since deletion enlarges the feasible region, ell<=rho.
