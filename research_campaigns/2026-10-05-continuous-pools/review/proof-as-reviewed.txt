A CONSTANT BUDGET FOR REAL REPORTS IN AT MOST TWO ALTERED POOLS
2026-10-05 -- candidate conventional proof, pending independent audit

THEOREM
Let k>=2 pools have r>=2 fixed labeled owners each. Allow every real capacity
c_i in [0,1], and retain all profiles with at most m altered pools, where a pool
is altered if at least one report differs from 1. Define
 v(S)=min_j sum_{i in S intersect N_j} c_i,  T=v(N).
There is an explicit nonnegative payment rule covering every coalition and
monotone in each owner's own report on this whole domain, with total cost
 <=(2m+4)T, or <=(2m+5)T with the original-core floor H_i/k.
On the subdomain c_i in [1/r,1], the bounds improve to (2m+3)T and (2m+4)T.
In particular m=2 gives base <=7T and fair <=8T on the requested domain,
independent of k,r and every report grid. Allowing zero reports gives base <=8T
and fair <=9T. These constants are not claimed sharp.

EXPLICIT RULE
For i in pool j, write
 C_j=sum_{h in N_j}c_h, A_i=sum_{h in N_j, h!=i}c_h,
 E_i=sum_{h in N_j, h!=i}(1-c_h)=r-1-A_i.
For T>0, set
 b(c)=(1/k)*min(1, 2T/(r-1)),
 q_i(c)=[2E_i/(r-1)] * [T*c_i/C_j],
 y_i(c)=b(c)+q_i(c).
When T=0 set all these payments to zero. Because C_j>=T>0, no denominator
vanishes in the positive branch. The coefficient E_i depends only on OTHER
owners' reports. Every owner in an unaltered pool has E_i=0 and hence q_i=0.
The formula itself is defined on the full cube [0,1]^(kr); m only enters its
budget guarantee. It does not select, relabel, or drop an altered pool.

NONNEGATIVITY AND EVERY OWN-REPORT EDGE
Fix all reports except c_i=t. Then E_i and A_i are constant, E_i>=0, and
 T(t)=min(A_i+t, min_{l!=j}C_l)
is nondecreasing. The function t/(A_i+t) is nondecreasing where its denominator
is positive (derivative A_i/(A_i+t)^2>=0). Thus q_i is a nonnegative constant
times the product of two nonnegative nondecreasing functions, and is itself
nondecreasing. b is nondecreasing in T. At T=0 payment is zero, so a transition
into the positive branch cannot decrease it. This proves monotonicity along
all real increases, including disappearance of an altered pool. In particular
if i was its pool's last non-unit owner, E_i=0 throughout its own report edge.
The proof is on the full cube, so every retained edge in the at-most-m domain
is covered, in either direction when interpreted as no profitable underreport.

COVERAGE: TWO REGIMES EXHAUST EVERY PROFILE
If T=0 then every coalition has value zero.
If T>=(r-1)/2, then b=1/k. For any S put n_j=|S intersect N_j| and x_j=c(S
intersect N_j). Since c_i<=1, v(S)=min_j x_j<=min_j n_j. Hence
 sum_{i in S} b=(1/k)*sum_j n_j>=min_j n_j>=v(S).
Nonnegative q payments preserve coverage.

If 0<T<=(r-1)/2, choose any pool j with C_j=T. For each i in that pool,
 E_i=r-1-(T-c_i)>=r-1-T>=(r-1)/2.
Consequently q_i=[2E_i/(r-1)]*c_i>=c_i. Therefore
 y(S)>=sum_{i in S intersect N_j}q_i>=x_j>=min_l x_l=v(S).
The two regimes agree at equality; their overlapping proofs cover the boundary.
No condition on coalition size, owner labels, or discrete report levels is used.

UNIFORM BUDGET
Because 0<=E_i<=r-1,
 sum_{i in N_j}q_i <= 2T*sum_{i in N_j}c_i/C_j=2T.
Unaltered pools have q=0, so sum_i q_i<=2mT. The uniform part satisfies
 kr*b=r*min(1,2T/(r-1))<=2r*T/(r-1)<=4T.
This proves (2m+4)T on [0,1].

On [1/r,1], every C_j>=1, so T>=1. If r=2 the uniform part is at most
r=2<=2T. If r>=3, 2r/(r-1)<=3, so it is at most 3T. Thus in all cases
sum_i y_i<=(2m+3)T. No discretization or limiting theorem is needed.

ORIGINAL CORE AND FAIRNESS
The core is nonempty: pick a minimum-total pool j, pay c_i to its owners and
zero to others. Total is T and every coalition is covered. Every efficient
core vector x satisfies x_i=T-x(N without i)<=T-v(N without i)=M_i.
For an owner i of pool total C_j, let B_j=min_{l!=j} C_l. Then
 M_i=min(C_j,B_j)-min(C_j-c_i,B_j)
     =min(c_i, (B_j-A_i)_+)=(c_i-(C_j-T))_+.
Holding all other reports fixed, the middle expression shows M_i is
nondecreasing in c_i. Define Y_i=max(y_i,M_i/k). It remains nonnegative,
covering and own-monotone, and Y_i>=H_i/k.
For s=C_j-T>=0, sum_{i in N_j}(c_i-s)_+<=T: if no term is positive the
claim is immediate; otherwise with p>=1 positive terms their sum is at most
C_j-p*s<=C_j-s=T. Therefore sum_i M_i<=kT, and
sum_i Y_i<=sum_i y_i+(1/k)sum_i M_i<=sum_i y_i+T.
When T=0 these statements and the zero-payment convention still hold.

BOUNDARY REVISION, CONTINUITY AND SCOPE
The new rule need not equal the previous binary construction. For example,
k=2,r=2, pools (1/2,1/2),(1,1), T=1: the new base vector is
(1,1,1/2,1/2), total 3; the old base is (1/4,1/4,1/2,1/2), total 3/2.
The all-unit profile still pays 1/k to every owner. We have not proved that
preserving the old binary boundary is impossible or that factor 2 cannot hold.

The formula is continuous wherever T>0. At T=0, the full-cube bound with m=k
forces every nonnegative coordinate to tend to zero, so it is continuous there
as well. The original M_i and max operation are continuous too. Thus it gives
one explicit real-report rule whose restriction works on every grid; there is
no inference from finite feasibility, no compactness claim, and no unproved
exchange of limits.

The whole continuous cube with at most two altered pools is included, so lower
bounds diverging with k cannot be supported solely on this family. For general
m the proven bound grows linearly with m; this does not prove a constant bound
when arbitrarily many pools are altered. The original stronger binary factor2
result remains separate and valid. Unequal owners per pool, capacities above
the fixed reference 1, split/merge/group incentives, and historical novelty are
not asserted by this theorem. No field-level breakthrough is claimed.
