Conclusion: the particular deficit-weighted payment rule and bounded-altered-pool

1. Moretti and Norde, A note on weighted multi-glove games (2021).
Original freely available publisher PDF: https://d-nb.info/1240670206/34
Publisher/DOI: https://doi.org/10.1007/s00355-021-01337-8
Inspected Definition 3.1, Theorem 3.2 and its minimum-pool proof, and the
population-monotonicity definition in Section 2. Their integer-weight game is
the established minimum-of-pool-sums model; its proof of core nonemptiness
extends directly to nonnegative real weights. We credit that ingredient.
Their PMAS property compares allocations across subcoalitions; our condition
compares a fixed owner's payoff as its own capacity report changes. Their
classification is not the subsidized real-report theorem proved here.

2. Huang, Mei, Yang and Cao, Mechanism Design in Max-Flows (2025), arXiv v1.
Original: https://arxiv.org/html/2502.08248v1
Record: https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.08248
Inspected Section 2.1 (binding capacity underreports, no operating costs,
efficiency, NEIC/DSIC), Section 2.2 Example 1 (core-selection impossibility),
and Section 3 Proposition 1. These are established modeling and impossibility
ingredients. Their efficient mechanism allocates total flow; our rule may
exceed flow to cover every coalition and enforce the chosen core-coordinate
floor. The present bounded-altered-pool constant is a quantitative feasible
construction in that modified contract, not a new core-selection impossibility.

Their type monotonicity describes the effect of a player leaving a coalition:
remaining agents of that type and other types face opposite payoff directions.
This differs from monotonicity in one fixed owner's divisible capacity report.
They establish core-valued schemes in the unit-owner setting; that result
neither asserts nor refutes our subsidized fixed-owner real-report rule.
Institutional metadata also lists a 2007 journal version. This comparison is
to the inspected 2003 original; we do not claim a full 2007-version comparison.
The copyrighted PDF is not redistributed in this repository.

4. Gangam, Garg, Shahkar and Vazirani, Equitable Core Imputations for Max-Flow,
MST and b-Matching Games, arXiv:2403.06037v3.
Original: https://arxiv.org/html/2403.06037v3
Inspected introduction/results and Section 5.2 (Theorem 5.2, Owen-set/core
relationship). Their fairness analysis selects leximin/leximax efficient
imputations within the Owen set. Our H_i/k floor plus subsidy and own-report
condition is a different contract. The elementary core argument and the idea
of fair allocation are not claimed new here. This paper is relevant background
and a reminder not to identify our floor with leximin fairness.
