WHY THIS RULE DOES NOT SOLVE THE MANY-ALTERED-POOL PROBLEM
2026-10-05 -- exact direct substitution, no unrestricted lower bound

Take r=3, any k>=2, and c_i=1/3 for every owner in every pool. Then T=1,
all k pools are altered, E_i=4/3, and the rule in CONTINUOUS_THEOREM.txt pays
 b=1/k, q_i=4/9, y_i=1/k+4/9.
Hence its total factor is exactly 3+4k/3. Here M_i=1/3 and M_i/k=1/(3k)
is already below b, so the fairness top-up changes nothing.

Thus the ACTUAL cost of this particular rule grows linearly with the number
of altered pools; its general O(m) estimate is not merely a proof artifact.
For k>2 these profiles leave the two-altered-pool target, so they do not
contradict the successful constant-budget extension.

The witness establishes no lower bound on the best possible mechanism.
A new construction would need to share the additional payments across many
altered pools while maintaining own-report monotonicity. A divergent universal
lower bound would need linked profiles outside every uniformly bounded-m
family established feasible by this theorem (or another violated hypothesis).
Refining reports within the two-altered family cannot supply such a bound.
