One decision, one perimeter, fixed books

The proposed first scope is 3–8 existing books within one legal/accounting risk perimeter. That size permits complete coalition enumeration in an initial audit; it is not a theoretical restriction or a claim about any bank’s actual structure.

Primary question: Can the approved risk-capacity budget be assigned to fixed books while satisfying approved stand-alone appeal benchmarks and the institution’s actual internal-credit restrictions?

The sponsor must identify what could change: a specific limit review, hedge choice, funded activity or charge appeal. “A different allocation” alone is not an economic outcome. With fixed positions and fixed scenarios, reallocating charges does not reduce aggregate portfolio risk.

The existing internal rule is the main comparator. It must be evaluated on the same risk measure, scenarios, model version, horizon and valuation basis as any candidate. Euler is a benchmark only when its derivative or subgradient convention is defined. The two savings fairness selectors remain secondary until the sponsor explains their decision relevance.

A coalition appeal is an institutional assumption requiring approval. Without that right, the constraints are a normative diagnostic, not evidence of actual coalition stability or truthful incentives. Full proposed contract and staged design →

Why public risk totals cannot fill the gap

There is a precise identification problem even under favorable Gaussian assumptions. Three books can have identical individual loss distributions and the same total loss distribution, yet different coalition risk and different fairness allocations.

ΣA = [1 0 0] ΣB = [ 1 1/2 −1/2] [0 1 0] [1/2 1 0 ] [0 0 1] [−1/2 0 1 ] Both: stand-alone risks = (1,1,1), total risk = √3, total savings T = 3 − √3, after scaling out a common ES multiplier. Pair {1,3}: risk √2 in A, but 1 in B.

The equal savings vector (T/3,T/3,T/3) is feasible and uniquely selected by both fairness rules in A. It violates the pair {1,3} constraint in B. There, both rules select (1/2, 2−√3, 1/2). Thus the summaries cannot identify either coalition-constrained selector.

This does not mean every rule is unidentified: an unconstrained equal split is computable from a total. Nor does it demonstrate disagreement between the two selectors inside either model. It is an elementary explanation of why the study needs joint data, with no novelty claim.

Exact rational checks verify positive definiteness, matching marginal/total variances, all 14 coalition inequalities at the stated vectors, and the cross-model violation. The written proof establishes the lexicographic uniqueness. Complete argument · Standard-library verifier · Exact certificates.

What would justify proceeding

  1. Contract fit: a real decision owner ratifies the fixed perimeter, current comparator, appeal benchmark, permitted credits and decision to be affected.
  2. Data fit: joint losses, current charges and transfers reconcile. If coalition valuation is nonadditive because of netting, collateral or other interactions, supply a validated coalition valuation rule.
  3. Diagnostic pilot: evaluate constraints, budget feasibility and stability on the common specification. Keep allocation differences separate from benefits.
  4. Independent evaluation: only after a decision mechanism is credible, freeze the threshold, horizon, sample-size rationale and untouched/prospective outcomes.

Stop if the existing rule cannot be compared on the same specification, the intended rights do not exist, credit restrictions conflict with the desired guarantees, or the result only moves accounting numbers without affecting a decision. Report infeasibility or missing information directly.
