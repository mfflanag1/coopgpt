Subject: Small research pilot on internal risk allocation and hedging credits

We are scoping a research pilot on how a fixed set of internal books shares a defined risk-capacity budget when positions offset each other. A preliminary hypothetical Treasury exercise did not establish an economic benefit. Before pursuing it, we want to understand whether the allocation question affects a real internal decision.

The proposed first step is a short discussion with a treasury or market-risk owner and an independent model/risk reviewer. We would establish the existing charge rule, what risks have already been transferred or priced internally, whether hedging credits or coalition-style appeals are meaningful, and which limit, hedge or charge-review decision could change.

If there is a fit, the smallest analytical input is a de-identified, fixed-book scenario-loss matrix with common scenarios, weights, valuation date, horizon and currency, plus the comparable existing charges and credit rules. Raw trades and client identities need not be the starting point. Scenario losses can still be sensitive; an institution could retain them and run an agreed analysis internally, returning approved summaries instead. No transfer is requested before scope and permissions are agreed.

The initial deliverable would be an allocation/constraint audit against the existing method and, where appropriate, Euler allocation. It would not claim regulatory-capital savings, forecasting success, truthful incentives or actual pooling gains. A later empirical test would require a separately frozen design and independent evaluation period.

Is there a documented allocation or hedging-credit decision where this would be useful, and a suitable owner for a scoped discussion?
