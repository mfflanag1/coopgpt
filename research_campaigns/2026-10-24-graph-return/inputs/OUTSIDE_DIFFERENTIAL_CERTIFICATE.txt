FINITE DIFFERENTIAL CERTIFICATES FOR FULL COALITION OUTSIDE PRODUCTION
2026-10-17. Candidate structural lemma; independent review pending.

DOMAIN. Fix a finite public nonnegative activity matrix A with no zero column
of positive profit, and public nonnegative profits w. Let

 F(c)=max{w.x : Ax<=c, x>=0},

assumed finite and continuous on the nonnegative resource cone. Actual owners
hold resource bundles, and payments have aggregate-price form y_i=b_i.p(c).
Let p be locally Lipschitz on the strictly positive orthant. Require the
PAYMENT map H(o,a)=a.p(o+a) to extend continuously to every nonnegative pair
(outside bundle o, coalition bundle a), with H(o,0)=0. This boundary assumption
is substantive; interior differential conditions alone do not choose boundary
payments. Homogeneity of p is not needed for this certificate.

THEOREM. Full coalition OUT on all profiles is equivalent to the following
inequalities holding simultaneously at almost every positive aggregate c:

       g(c,a):=p(c)+Dp(c)^T a
       is a nonnegative production dual cover for every 0<=a<=c.             (1)

Equivalently, at almost every c,

 p_v+sum_j c_j min(partial_v p_j,0)>=0                 for every resource v;
 A_e.p+sum_j c_j min(sum_v A_ve partial_v p_j,0)>=w_e  for every activity e.  (2)

For a unit-profit resource graph, each A_e has two entries equal to1, so the
second line reduces to

 p_u+p_v+sum_j c_j min(partial_u p_j+partial_v p_j,0)>=1  for every edge uv.  (3)

The quantities in(2) are concave piecewise-linear functions of p and Dp, so
their superlevel constraints are convex. Together with p>=0 and a funding
inequality c.p<=K F(c), they give a convex differential feasibility problem.
No numerical discretization is claimed to certify all profiles.

1. NECESSITY AND THE HOLDING-FRACTION CUBE

Fix a positive differentiability point c and any coalition aggregate a<=c.
Hold the outsiders o=c-a fixed and add epsilon d to that coalition. Full OUT
on the new versus old profile gives

 H(o,a+epsilon d)-H(o,a)>=F(epsilon d)=epsilon F(d).

Differentiating at epsilon=0 yields g(c,a).d>=F(d) for every d>=0. Taking d to
be a coordinate unit vector gives g>=0. Taking d=A_e gives g.A_e>=w_e, since
the activity e alone is feasible at resource bundle A_e. Thus g is dual feasible.
These conclusions hold for every a at every differentiability point; there is
no a-dependent exceptional set requiring an uncountable intersection.

Conversely a nonnegative dual cover satisfies g.d>=F(d) for all d>=0 by weak
duality. Each coordinate or activity inequality for g is affine in a. Its
minimum over 0<=a<=c is obtained by setting a_j=c_j exactly when the corresponding
derivative coefficient is negative, and zero otherwise. This gives(2), replacing
exponentially many holding-fraction corners by one explicit negative-part sum.

2. SUFFICIENCY ON POSITIVE PROFILES: HANDLING EXCEPTIONAL LINES

Fix positive outsiders o and consider G(a)=H(o,a) on a>0. It is locally
Lipschitz. At almost every a its weak gradient equals g(o+a,a), because the
exceptional set for p is merely translated by the fixed o. By(1),

                    grad G(a).d >=F(d)

for any fixed nonnegative direction d. This almost-everywhere assertion must
not be integrated along an arbitrary exceptional line without justification.
Instead, on a compact interior neighborhood of any segment, mollify G. Its
smooth gradient is an average of weak gradients and therefore has the same
directional lower bound. Integrate the smooth inequality along the segment,
then let the mollification radius decrease to zero. Local uniform convergence
gives

                    H(o,a+d)-H(o,a)>=F(d).

Approximate a boundary coalition bundle by positive bundles and use continuity.
Finally replace any nonnegative outsider o by o+epsilon*1 and take epsilon to
zero using the assumed global payment continuity. This proves all-profile OUT,
including changes of support and trajectories lying in exceptional faces.

For a piecewise-C1 candidate with finitely many regions along segments, the same
argument reduces to direct integration on those regions and continuity at their
boundaries. The weak-gradient proof shows why local Lipschitz regularity is also
sufficient without any generic-position assumption.

3. CONSEQUENCES AND LIMITS

Coalition coverage follows from OUT by starting the coalition at zero and adding
its entire bundle: H(o,a)>=H(o,0)+F(a)=F(a). Thus a boundary-continuous candidate
with(2) need not verify a separate family of coalition-cover inequalities.

On graphs, the edge condition(3) couples every endpoint derivative through ALL
resource prices. The component-local factor-two obstruction is precisely what
arises when these cross-component derivative terms are artificially set to zero.
A mechanism below two must satisfy these coupled conditions by another route.

The aggregate-price existence equivalence separately justifies searching such
prices for universal owner-count bounds under the stated homogeneous technology.
It gives prices continuous within each support face, but not automatically the
global payment continuity assumed here. Therefore this differential theorem is
an equivalence for the specified regular class and a sufficient certificate for
constructed rules, not an unqualified replacement of the full boundary conditions
in the unrestricted mechanism theorem.

This lemma supplies a finite local certificate with a demonstrated obstruction
consequence. It does not itself improve the graph funding interval or settle
the original individual-only incentive problem. Historical priority is open.
