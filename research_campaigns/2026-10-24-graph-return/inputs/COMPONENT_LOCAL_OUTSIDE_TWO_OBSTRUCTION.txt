WHY INDEPENDENT BIPARTITE-COMPONENT PRICES CANNOT BEAT FULL-OUT FACTOR TWO
2026-10-17. Candidate method-boundary theorem; independent review pending.

This excludes a class of component-local constructions, not all mechanisms
on graphs. In particular it does not establish a floor-free unrestricted graph
lower bound of two. A better graph rule would have to couple distinct currently
tight components, or use a different decomposition altogether.

CLASS. On a graph's strict low/high Huber tight components, assign the same
homogeneous pair-price family: if the component has total long capacity A and
short capacity B, ratio t=B/A<1, every long vertex gets price p(t) and every
short vertex gets q(t). Prices depend only on that component's totals. They
are nonnegative, locally absolutely continuous on (0,1], continuous at t=1,
and symmetric at balance p(1)=q(1). Payments are a fixed actual owner's bundle
dot these prices. Assume coalition coverage, full coalition OUT, and a uniform
funding factor K on every finite unit-profit graph and positive profile.

THEOREM. Every rule in this class has K>=2. The utilization rule p(t)=t,q(t)=1
attains two on arbitrary graphs, by root's independently audited graph theorem.

1. NECESSARY SINGLE-COMPONENT CONDITIONS

On an isolated edge with capacities (A,B), nonnegative payments and budget give
 p(t)/t+q(t)<=K.
Coverage gives p(t)+q(t)>=1. A coalition owning the whole long side has payment
A p(B/A); monotonicity in its own resource gives
 u(t):=p(t)-t p'(t)>=0 almost everywhere.
A coalition owning the whole short side gives
 v(t):=q(t)+t q'(t)>=0 almost everywhere.
Thus k(t)=p(t)/t is nonincreasing in t, its limit C at zero exists and is at most
K, and p(t)<=Ct. In particular p(t)->0. Also q(t)<=K is bounded.

2. A FOUR-VERTEX CROSS-COMPONENT CONSTRUCTION

Choose 0<t2<t1<1. Use vertices L1,H1,L2,H2, capacities
 (1/t1,1,1/t2,1), and unit-profit edges
 L1-H1, L2-H2, L1-H2.

The Huber prices are t1/2,1-t1/2,t2/2,1-t2/2. The two disjoint original edges
are tight and their matching flows are one each. The cross-edge has cover sum
1+(t1-t2)/2>1 and zero flow. These satisfy the Huber KKT equations, so the two
strict components are exactly the two original edges. No report-dependent
choice of a decomposition is being guessed here.

Let an actual coalition own ALL of L1 and ALL of H2, while outsiders own H1
and L2. Withhold epsilon of L1 and epsilon of H2. The first component ratio
increases and the second decreases, so the cross-edge remains strict and the
same two-component description remains valid for sufficiently small epsilon.
The withheld bundle produces epsilon on edge L1-H2. Its payment loss divided
by epsilon tends to

                         u(t1)+v(t2).

Full OUT therefore requires u(t1)+v(t2)>=1 for almost every such pair t1>t2.
At common Lebesgue points of u,v, average this inequality over arbitrarily
small rectangles t1 in(t,t+h), t2 in(t-h,t) and let h decrease to zero. Hence

                         u(t)+v(t)>=1                  (1)

for almost every t in(0,1). This step does not assume continuous derivatives.

3. INTEGRATION FORCES FUNDING TWO

Integrating (1) from epsilon to1 and using p(1)=q(1),

 1-epsilon <= integral_epsilon^1 [p-tp'+q+tq'] dt
            =2 integral_epsilon^1 p(t)dt-epsilon[q(epsilon)-p(epsilon)].

The endpoint term vanishes because p,q are bounded. Letting epsilon tend to
zero gives 1<=2 integral_0^1 p(t)dt<=C, since p(t)<=Ct. Therefore C>=1.
Finally coverage q>=1-p and budget imply

 K >= liminf_(t->0)[p(t)/t+q(t)] >= C+1 >=2.

The conclusion applies even if the proposed pair family has a better guarantee
on isolated edges. Tight components alone do not contain every feasible
outside production plan, and the cross-edge is what creates the stronger
necessary inequality (1).

4. EXACT ZERO-LOSS FAILURE OF THE LOGARITHMIC PAIR RULE

For the sharp two-input rule p(t)=ct and upper-branch q(t)=c/t, choose
 t1=19/20, t2=9/10, epsilon=1/100.
After the coalition above withholds epsilon in both coordinates, the ratios are
 t1'=1900/1981 and t2'=891/1000. Both before and after, all four ratios lie
strictly above the branch threshold t0=1/c-1 and below1. Indeed c>8/15 implies
t0<7/8, while all four rational ratios exceed7/8. To verify c>8/15, use
exp(15/8)<exp(2)<8, so (8/15)[2-exp(-15/8)]<1, and the root function is strictly
increasing. The elementary bound e<11/4 proves exp(2)<8.

The coalition's payment from L1 is c B1=c, and from H2 it is c A2=10c/9.
Both are exactly constant under this report change, whereas the withheld
cross-edge produces1/100. The OUT inequality fails by exactly1/100.
Multiplying all component-local logarithmic payments by any fixed finite
factor leaves the payment loss zero, so fixed scaling cannot repair this rule.
