MODEL AND CLAIM
Use the finite unit-profit fractional resource graph and Huber optimizer p(c)
from composition/HUBER_PRICE_THREE_HALVES_v2.txt. Actual owners are fixed and
can hold arbitrary mixed nonnegative bundles. Define
 q_v(c)=min(2p_v(c),1),        Y_i(b)=sum_v b_iv q_v(c), c=sum_i b_i.
Then total payments equal 2F(c), every coalition is covered, and for every
coalition S withholding aggregate d from its reported aggregate a,
 a.q(c)-(a-d).q(c-d) >= F(d).                         (OUT)
Moreover Y_i >= F(c)-F(c-b_i), the full-owner deletion marginal.
Thus this is a factor2 rule with full coalition outside production AND every
full-owner marginal floor, on all profiles and arbitrary graph/owner counts.

The same claims transfer through the established antidiagonal resource-copy
lift to arbitrary rational public edge profits. A common all-profile compact
limit of aggregate price fields gives existence for real public profits.
Factor2 is sharp for this combined contract already on a single edge with
pure separated owners: the previously proved one-protected-owner floor lower
is 2-1/m for m energy owners and one hardware owner. No claim of optimality
for the floor-free OUT graph problem is made; its current interval is
[1.5430633898...,2].

1. FUNDING AND COVERAGE
For positive aggregate capacities the Huber KKT matching lambda satisfies
 sum_{e incident v}lambda_e = c_v min(2p_v,1)=c_v q_v.
The audited rounded-price certificate proves lambda is a MAXIMUM fractional
matching, so sum_v c_v q_v=2 sum_e lambda_e=2F(c).
Because q_v>=p_v, q is a feasible fractional vertex cover, proving coalition
coverage. Zero-capacity coordinates contribute zero, and the same identities
hold on the positive induced graph.

2. COALITION OUTSIDE-PRODUCTION DERIVATIVE
Fix a coalition's retained bundle a0>=0, its withheld bundle d>=0, and outside
aggregate o>=0. Trace the increasing segment a(t)=a0+t d and c(t)=o+a(t),
0<=t<=1. First suppose all aggregate coordinates are positive. The Huber
optimizer is continuous and piecewise rational along this segment, with
finitely many tight-component patterns as proved in the source theorem.
The same is true of q. It is enough to prove the payment derivative at least
F(d) on each fixed-pattern interval.

On a strict-low/high tight component let A and B be total low/high capacity,
with A>B>0. The Huber formula gives q_low=B/A and q_high=1. Let a and b be
the coalition's current low/high holdings; let l and h be the low/high sums
of d. Its component payment is a B/A+b, hence its segment derivative is
 l B/A + a(h A-B l)/A²+h
   = (B/A)(1-a/A) l +(1+a/A)h >= h,                 (1)
since 0<=a<=A. On a component with Huber prices1/2, q is identically1, so
the derivative is the sum of d on that component. A graph-isolated vertex
has q=0 and derivative0.

Define z_v=0 at strict-low vertices, z_v=1 at strict-high vertices, and
z_v=1/2 at half-price vertices (isolates receive0). This is a feasible unit
vertex cover: any neighbor of a strict-low vertex is strict-high; other
edges have a high endpoint or two half-price endpoints. Summing(1) and
the half-price contributions proves
 d/dt [a(t).q(c(t))] >= sum_high d_v+sum_half d_v
                       >= z.d >= F(d).
The final inequality is weak duality applied to the WITHHELD capacity d;
it does not require withheld production to use only currently tight edges.
This last point handles edges joining different tight components.
Integrating and using continuity across the finitely many pattern changes
proves(OUT).

3. BOUNDARY PROFILES
For epsilon>0 evaluate q at c(t)+epsilon*1 but weight payments by the actual
coalition holdings a(t). Every calculation above remains valid: actual low
holdings a are at most the regularized low total A; the directional totals
l,h are unchanged; the rounded cover still covers the full original graph.
Thus each regularized payment difference is at least F(d). As epsilon tends
to zero, uniqueness of the Huber minimizer on positive-capacity coordinates
implies convergence of those prices to the specified induced-graph rule.
All zero-capacity coordinates have zero actual holdings, and q is bounded
between0 and1. Both endpoint payments therefore converge to the actual rule,
proving(OUT) on every face. This is a proof device, not an added resource or
owner in the mechanism.

4. FULL-OWNER MARGINAL FLOORS
Let lambda be the maximum fractional matching from the Huber KKT certificate.
For a given owner's bundle b<=c, put t_v=b_v/c_v when c_v>0, and0 otherwise.
Define a feasible residual plan
 lambda'_uv=lambda_uv * [1-max(t_u,t_v)].
At vertex v its incident residual use is at most
 (1-t_v)sum_{e incident v}lambda_e <=(1-t_v)c_v=c_v-b_v.
Thus F(c-b)>=sum_e lambda'_e. The lost production is bounded by
 F(c)-sum_e lambda'_e
   =sum_uv lambda_uv max(t_u,t_v)
   <=sum_uv lambda_uv(t_u+t_v)
   =sum_v t_v c_v q_v = b.q(c)=Y_i.
Hence Y_i>=F(c)-F(c-b). This also gives the floor for any specified subset
of the owner's resources, by production monotonicity.

5. WEIGHTED TRANSFER
For rational edge weights use F_w(x)=F_H(Bx)/Q for EVERY capacity x, with
public B>=0 and the SAME actual owners receiving all their own resource
copies. Under a coalition withholding d, copied outside worth is exactly
F_H(Bd)/Q=F_w(d), so dividing the lifted OUT inequality preserves its full
outside-worth term, not merely payment monotonicity. Deleting an entire
owner likewise commutes with B, so all marginal floors transfer exactly.
Total payments are exactly2F_w(c).

For nonnegative real weights approximate by fixed rational public weights.
The lifted aggregate effective prices are uniformly bounded by the maximum
public weight plus1, since every copy q lies in[0,1]. Use ONE convergent
subnet of whole price fields in the compact product over all aggregate
profiles, as in UNIVERSAL_WEIGHTED_THREE_HALVES_v2.txt. Each coverage, budget,
OUT and marginal-floor inequality involves finitely many field coordinates
and a production value continuous in the weights. All pass simultaneously
to the limit. For OUT, instantiate each aggregate field comparison with two
actual owners holding a and c-a, and let the first withhold d<=a. This gives
every required finite-coordinate inequality before taking the common limit.
This gives existence, not an exact finite irrational algorithm.

ECONOMIC SCOPE
The arbitrary access graph for hardware and energy, with public net profits
(value-cost)_+, is a bipartite instance. Thus no complete-access assumption
is needed for this factor2 contract. Divisibility, public net profits and
binding capacity underreports remain essential model assumptions. Private
costs, latency feasibility, actual outside market access, and empirical gains
require separate modeling/data. No measurement claim is made here.
