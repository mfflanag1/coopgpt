BIPARTITE COMPLETION LOCALIZES FINITE GRAPH LOWER CERTIFICATES
24 October 2026. Analytic route closure; no improvement of the numerical frontier.

Scope. Fixed actual owners, arbitrary mixed nonnegative real resource reports,
all-coalition coverage, full pooled outside-production protection, and
nonnegative payments. Only finite certificates with nonnegative multipliers
are considered below. No component-locality assumption is made. This note
does not assert historical novelty or a new unrestricted lower bound.

1. MODEL AND CERTIFICATE CONVENTION

Let G=(V,E) be a finite bipartite resource graph with fixed sides L,R. Write
F(x) for its maximum fractional edge packing at vertex capacities x>=0.
The finite set of actual owners I is fixed. At a report profile p owner i
reports b_i(p)>=0; put B(p)=sum_i b_i(p). A legal transition e=(l,h,S) has
b_i(l)<=b_i(h) for i in S and equality for i outside S. Members of S may
remain unchanged. Put d(e)=B(h)-B(l)>=0.

The finite payment system consists of any chosen finite collections of
coverage rows, legal full-OUT rows, and budget rows:
  sum_{i in S} y_i(p) >= F(sum_{i in S} b_i(p)),
  sum_{i in S} [y_i(h)-y_i(l)] >= F(d(e)),
  sum_i y_i(p) <= K F(B(p)),
with every y_i(p)>=0. Include every profile mentioned by any row in a finite
profile set P. A row family need not contain all possible comparisons for
the conclusions below; every included comparison must be semantically legal.

A nonnegative finite lower certificate assigns multipliers
  lambda_(p,S)>=0 to coverage rows,
  mu_e>=0 to full-OUT rows,
  nu_p>=0 to budget rows (put nu_p=0 if no budget row is used).
The resulting coefficient of y_i(p) in the first two families is
  g_(i,p) = sum_{S containing i} lambda_(p,S)
          +sum_{e=(l,p,S): i in S} mu_e
          -sum_{e=(p,h,S): i in S} mu_e.
The certificate balance condition is
  g_(i,p) <= nu_p  for every i,p.                         (1)
Equivalently, the nonnegativity-row multiplier is
  eta_(i,p)=nu_p-g_(i,p)>=0.
Thus no payment coefficient, including a zero reporter's payment, is silently
discarded. Define the certificate's numerator and denominator by
  Ldual = sum_(p,S) lambda_(p,S) F(sum_{i in S} b_i(p))
          +sum_e mu_e F(d(e)),
  Ddual = sum_p nu_p F(B(p)).
For Ddual>0 the certificate proves K>=Ldual/Ddual. Indeed the weighted
coverage/OUT inequalities give Ldual<=sum g y, and (1), nonnegativity, and
the weighted budgets give sum g y<=sum_p nu_p sum_i y_i(p)<=K Ddual.

2. A GLOBAL COMPARISON PAYMENT FIELD

Define
  A(x)=sum_{v in L} x_v,   B(x)=sum_{v in R} x_v,
  H(x)=min(A(x),B(x)).
Every edge uses one unit on each side, so F(x)<=H(x) for every x>=0.
This is the complete-bipartite production function on the same two sides.

Use the established two-input logarithmic full-OUT rule, whose constant is
  kappa0=1+c,  c(2-exp(-1/c))=1,  1/2<c<1.
For reference, put t0=1-exp(-1/c), p(t)=ct, and
  q(t)=1-c-c(1-t)log(1-t)/t  when 0<t<=t0,
  q(t)=c/t                    when t0<=t<=1.
When A>=B>0, an owner with side totals (a_i,b_i) receives
  Y_i=p(B/A)a_i+q(B/A)b_i.
Exchange sides when B>=A>0, and put all payments zero when min(A,B)=0.
At the equality boundary p(1)=q(1)=c, so the rule is unambiguous.

The inherited theorem establishes at every real mixed-owner profile:
  Y_i>=0;
  sum_{i in S}Y_i >= H(sum_{i in S}b_i);
  sum_{i in S}[Y_i(h)-Y_i(l)] >= H(d(e));
  sum_i Y_i <= kappa0 H(B(p)).                            (2)
The first three claims imply graph coverage and graph full OUT globally,
including at profiles where F<H. The final claim is a comparison budget;
it need not give a kappa0 budget relative to graph output at those profiles.
This distinction is essential.

The inherited proof uses p'=c>0, q'<=0, q+tq'>=0, and
  p+q+t(1-t)q'>=1.
For coalition side shares alpha,beta in [0,1], its payment derivatives obey
  g_A=p-alpha*t*p'-beta*t^2*q'>=0,
  g_B=q+alpha*p'+beta*t*q'>=0,
  g_A+g_B>=p+q+t(1-t)q'>=1.
Integration along a report segment gives the full-OUT assertion, including
passive members, mixed owners, side-switches, and zero-output boundaries.

3. BUDGET-SUPPORT LOCALIZATION LEMMA

For every certificate satisfying (1), applying its coverage/OUT rows to Y
and then using Y_i>=0 gives
  Ldual <= sum_(i,p) g_(i,p)Y_i(p)
        <= sum_p nu_p sum_i Y_i(p)
        <= kappa0 sum_p nu_p H(B(p)).                    (3)
Consequently, with Delta(x)=H(x)-F(x)>=0,
  Ldual <= kappa0 [Ddual+sum_p nu_p Delta(B(p))].        (4)

In particular, if every profile with nu_p>0 satisfies F(B(p))=H(B(p)),
then Ldual<=kappa0 Ddual. Comparison profiles carrying zero budget weight
may be anywhere in the report space: (3) uses global cover/OUT feasibility,
not a confinement assumption on the transitions or coverage rows.

If Ddual>0 and Ldual/Ddual>=kappa0+epsilon with epsilon>0, then necessarily
  sum_p nu_p Delta(B(p)) >= epsilon*Ddual/kappa0.        (5)
Profiles with nu_p>0 but F(B(p))=0 must not be omitted from this condition.
Their budget inequality is still a genuine row and their completion gap
may be positive.

One may retain the sharper exact comparison total instead of (2): with
t_p=min(A(B(p)),B(B(p)))/max(A(B(p)),B(B(p))),
  Ldual <= sum_p nu_p H(B(p))[c+q(t_p)],                (6)
where the summand is defined as zero when H(B(p))=0. No claim is made that
the necessary conditions (4)--(6) are sufficient for any lower certificate.

4. WHY ORDERED COMPONENTS AND SCALE SEPARATION DO NOT SUFFICE

Suppose G contains diagonal edges L_j R_j, and has arbitrary additional
edges between its two sides. This includes the ordered graph with edges
L_i R_j for i<=j. At capacities a_j on L_j and b_j on R_j satisfying
a_j>=b_j for every j, packing b_j along each diagonal edge gives
  F(a,b)=sum_j b_j=H(a,b).                              (7)
There may be arbitrarily many components, arbitrarily separated positive
scales, and arbitrary mixed actual owners summing to these capacities.
Positive scaling of each pair preserves (7), as do global scaling and
relabelings that preserve the graph and the two sides.

Thus no finite certificate whose budget support stays in this cone can
prove more than kappa0. This remains true even when its transitions leave
the cone and return. In particular, the ordered chain a_j=1/t_j,b_j=1
from the component-local price obstruction lies wholly in this cone.

The explicit escape from the component-local calculation is that the
comparison rule uses the GLOBAL ratio t=(sum_j b_j)/(sum_j a_j). Changing
one component therefore changes prices paid on every component. For a
balanced coalition restoration its derivative is
  p+q+alpha(1-t)p'+beta*t(1-t)q'
    >=p+q+t(1-t)q'>=1.
The terms involving alpha and beta are not individual component prices.
Removing those global responses reinstates the component-local assumption
that this route was meant to avoid. Symmetrization over graph automorphisms
does not remove these responses from the exhibited valid payment field.

5. EXACT P4 HALL-DEFICIT COROLLARY

For the path 0--1--2--3, its two sides are {0,2} and {1,3}. Put
  H(x)=min(x0+x2,x1+x3),  C(x)=x1+x2.
The fractional matching value is
  F(x)=min(H(x),C(x)).
An exact rearrangement gives
  Delta(x)=H(x)-F(x)
          =min((x0-x1)_+,(x3-x2)_+),                   (8)
where z_+=max(z,0). Therefore any certificate strictly above kappa0 has
positive budget weight on at least one profile with BOTH x0>x1 and x3>x2.
More quantitatively, the weighted value of (8) must satisfy (5). These are
outer-rich profiles with a genuine Hall deficit, not merely component ties.

There is a second global comparison field: pay owner i
  Z_i=b_(i,1)+b_(i,2).
The middle vertices form a vertex cover, so C(x)>=F(x). Additivity implies
coverage and full OUT for Z at every profile. Applying the same certificate
therefore also gives
  Ldual<=sum_p nu_p C(B(p)),
  Ldual-Ddual <= sum_p nu_p [C(B(p))-F(B(p))]
    =sum_p nu_p max(0,B_1(p)-B_0(p),B_2(p)-B_3(p)).    (9)
If Ldual/Ddual>1+c and Ddual>0, the right side of (9) must exceed c Ddual.
Thus some positively weighted budget profile must ALSO have an inner
capacity exceeding its adjacent outer capacity. Such profiles are disjoint
from the strict Hall-deficit profiles in (8). A stronger certificate needs
budget support in both regimes, with the respective quantitative gap mass.
This is a necessary condition, not a stronger lower bound.

6. NEXT DIAGRAM, NOT LAUNCHED; STOP DECISION

The necessary two-regime support suggests a report diamond with budget
profiles near (M,1,1,M), M>1, and budget profiles with an inner surplus.
It would need legal fixed-owner transitions with productive pooled withheld
bundles and shared passive-member payments linking the branches. Restoring
only outer coordinates has zero P4 outside worth, so drawing such a diamond
alone supplies no useful positive OUT right side. No certificate is asserted.

Stop: the component-chain and scale-separation extension cannot beat kappa0
while its budget support stays in F=H. For P4, a stronger certificate must
use both the Hall-deficit regime and the other regime identified in (9).
No additional grid, profile search, or general mechanism claim is part of
this note. The frontier remains 1+c<=K<=2.
