1. THE CONTRACT THAT THE THEOREM ACTUALLY SATISFIES
A resource graph has unit-profit activities on edges. One unit on an edge uses
one unit at both endpoints. F(c) is the maximum fractional edge production
subject to resource capacities c. Owners retain their actual identities and
may each hold resources at many vertices. A report may reduce any of its own
coordinates. Every coalition T, including unchanged members, is eligible.
The rule has nonnegative payments and, with outsiders fixed, satisfies
 sum_T y_i(high)-sum_T y_i(low) >= F(total resources withheld by T).
Thus coalition coverage follows by restoring the coalition from zero reports.
The budget compares TOTAL payments to current output F(c); the factor1.7071
corresponds to additional funding of at most0.7071 times output. No extra
whole-owner marginal-allocation floor is imposed. No private costs, false
capacity overreports, ownership transfers, splitting or merging are covered.

2. HOW THE CONSTRUCTION WORKS
Write the path B--A--D--C. Make a directed bipartite double cover with original
capacities on left copies and alpha=1/sqrt(2) times capacities on right copies.
Any such flow pays at most(1+alpha)F after collapsing the two orientations.
The difficulty is choosing a maximum flow that respects coalition incentives.
The successful selector protects the leaf uses near beta=alpha^2=1/2 of their
capacities, clamped to each orientation's feasible maximum-flow interval. This
couples prices across the shared edge rather than treating pairs separately.

The complete proof differentiates a coalition's total payment along a restoration
segment. Every resulting coefficient is nonnegative; coefficients at endpoints
of every graph edge sum to at least1. These conditions bound the derivative by
the withheld bundle's production. Explicit integration handles ties and zero
capacities. Aggregating complete vertex groups preserves exact production,
owner payments and all deviations, so complete blow-ups and disjoint unions
inherit the bound. Incomplete group connections or nonunit profits are not proved.

3. LOWER SEARCH AND STRUCTURAL LESSONS
Four fixed cyclic mixed owners, four complete finite report diagrams R=1,2,4,16,
and every legal coalition/underreport constraint gave exact finite optimum4/3.
The independent rational checker verifies all28561 rows per instance, including
passive coalition members. This finite feasible payment table is not an all-real
mechanism. Its lower certificate falls below the existing general lower bound,
so the branch stopped rather than enlarging the same grid without a reason.

The new localization lemma gives a better search restriction. On a bipartite
graph let H be complete-bipartite production on the same sides, with H>=F.
A finite nonnegative dual certificate with budget weights nu satisfies
 L <= kappa0 [D + sum_p nu_p(H-F)(capacity at p)],
where kappa0=1+c_* and c_*(2-exp(-1/c_*))=1. Therefore certificates supported
only where F=H cannot improve kappa0, even when their transitions leave that
region. On P4, a stronger certificate must budget-weight both outer-rich Hall
deficits and a distinct inner-surplus regime. These are necessary conditions,
not an improved lower bound or a sufficient recipe.
