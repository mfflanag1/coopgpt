ASYMMETRIC DOUBLE COVER: FUNDING IDENTITY AND BALANCED-SELECTOR FAILURE
24 October 2026; bounded graph OUT gate. This is a scoped architecture diagnosis,
not a lower bound for arbitrary mechanisms or for every double-cover selector.

Let 0<alpha<=1. Replace each original vertex v by a left copy of capacity c_v
and a right copy of capacity alpha*c_v, and each original edge uv by both
u_L v_R and v_L u_R. Select a maximum bipartite flow, let w_v be its left use,
and propose aggregate prices p_v=w_v/c_v. At zero c_v use the payment limit
rather than an arbitrary quotient. No general OUT claim is established here.

UNIVERSAL FUNDING IDENTITY.
Every feasible directed flow, after collapsing the two orientations of each
edge and dividing by 1+alpha, is a feasible original fractional edge packing:
its incident use at v is at most c_v+alpha*c_v. Therefore
  sum_v w_v <= (1+alpha) F_G(c).
This holds without a selection rule and does not establish coalition coverage.

SINGLE EDGE.
For A>=B>0 the two directed edges force
 w_A=alpha B, w_B=min(B,alpha A).
The prices are p_A=alpha t, p_B=min(1,alpha/t), t=B/A.
For t<=alpha, a coalition with fractions theta_A,theta_B has restoration
coefficients
 g_A=alpha t(1-theta_A), g_B=1+alpha theta_A.
Thus g_A,g_B>=0 and g_A+g_B>=1. For alpha<=t<=1,
 g_A=alpha t(1-theta_A)+alpha theta_B,
 g_B=(alpha/t)(1-theta_B)+alpha theta_A.
The edge-sum minimum over the holding cube is alpha(1+t), attained at
(theta_A,theta_B)=(0,1). Thus this rule is full-OUT on a single edge exactly
when alpha(1+alpha)>=1. The price functions and payments are continuous
across t=alpha and t=1; bounded prices give payment continuity at zero.
Consequently alpha=(sqrt(5)-1)/2 gives single-edge funding <= golden ratio.
This observation alone does not improve the known sharp scalar OUT rule.

BALANCED-SELECTOR COUNTEREXAMPLE ON P4.
Vertices are L1,H1,L2,H2; edges L1-H1,L2-H2,L1-H2.
Capacities are A1=1/t1,B1=1,A2=1/t2,B2=1, where
 alpha<t2<t1<1.
Select among maximum double-cover flows the left-use vector minimizing
 sum_v w_v^2/c_v (equivalently any strictly convex balanced utilization cost
 with the same derivative as a strictly increasing function of w_v/c_v).
All right capacities can be saturated. Every maximum flow can be written
 w_L1=alpha B1+s, w_L2=alpha B2-s,
 w_H1=alpha A1-t, w_H2=alpha A2+t,
with s,t>=0 subject to the left capacities and the other flow bounds.
At s=0 the left utilization at L1 is alpha*t1, strictly greater than that
at L2, alpha*t2. Increasing s therefore strictly increases the convex
objective. At t=0 the utilization at H2 is alpha/t2, strictly greater than
that at H1, alpha/t1; increasing t likewise increases the objective.
All four left capacities are slack since t1,t2>alpha. Thus the unique selected
left-use vector has s=t=0, giving p_Lj=alpha Bj/Aj and p_Hj=alpha Aj/Bj.

Use three fixed owners: owner S holds all L1 and all H2, one outsider holds
all H1, and another outsider holds all L2. Let S withhold epsilon>0 from both
its resources. For sufficiently small epsilon the strict inequalities and
left-capacity slack persist, and the selected vector remains s=t=0. Its
payment before and after is identically alpha B1+alpha A2. Yet its withheld
bundle can operate the cross edge L1-H2 at level epsilon, so F(d)=epsilon.
Thus full OUT fails. This also fails singleton OUT because S is one mixed
owner; there is no ownership reassignment in the comparison.

The failure applies to all alpha<1 (choose alpha<t2<t1<1), and the chamber
has positive capacities and a unique smooth left-use vector. It is not a
boundary irregularity. For alpha=1 the indicated chamber is empty, so no
contradiction with the accepted utilization OUT2 rule occurs.

REMAINING SELECTOR QUESTION.
For arbitrary maximum-flow selection in the same chamber, OUT at the holding
corner containing all L1 and all H2 requires
  (partial_A1+partial_B2)(s+t)>=1.
The balanced choice s=t=0 violates it. A genuinely coupled selector could
satisfy it, so this calculation does NOT exclude the entire double-cover
architecture. Such a selector still needs all holding-cube inequalities,
coalition coverage, and compatible continuous boundary transitions.
