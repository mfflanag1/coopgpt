EXACT LIMITATION OF THE PRESENT PROOF RELAXATION
6 October 2026. This is NOT an obstruction to the envelope's sharp bound.

1. THE LOSS FROM FORGETTING WHICH CUT WINS CAN BE ARBITRARILY LARGE
Take a directed path of K edges, all reports equal to 1, one fixed original
owner per edge. Its structural minimal cuts are the K singleton edges, T=1,
and all effective capacities equal 1. Almost surely exactly one of the K iid
uniforms is smallest; by symmetry z_e=1/K for every e.

If only e reports t in [0,1], every effective capacity becomes t. For t>0 the
same symmetry gives z_e(t,c_-e)=t/K; at t=0 it is zero. Hence y_e=1/K and
sum_e y_e=1. Truthfulness already holds for all reports on this path.

In contrast, the common-profile majorant in THEOREM (7), BEFORE applying its
scalar bound, equals
 R(c)=sum_C sum_(A in partition(C)) D(A) Pr(sum_(h in A)D_h U_h<=T)=K,
because every A is a singleton of weight 1 and Pr(U<=1)=1.
Thus R(c)/sum_e y_e=K exactly, with growing graph/owner/cut counts.

It is impossible to prove R(c)<=C*U(K)T with an absolute C, since
K/U(K) tends to infinity. Therefore obtaining C*U(K) for y requires a different
intermediate estimate that retains competition among winning cuts or controls
the own-report maximizers jointly. Optimizing only the scalar constant in
the present majorant cannot meet that target.

This example is NOT a lower bound of order K for y: its actual envelope total
is exactly T. It is not a lower bound for all truthful mechanisms either.

2. THE UNIFORM-SUM CONSTANT 2 CANNOT BE UNIFORMLY IMPROVED
The standalone lemma in THEOREM says a Pr(sum_i a_i U_i<=b)<=2b.
For b=1 and nine weights a_i=1/5, exact inclusion-exclusion integration gives
 a Pr(sum_i a_i U_i<=1)=51907/40320>1.
This already rejects replacing 2 by 1 in that lemma.

For an all-parameter sharpness argument, take integer m>=2, n=m^3,
a=2-1/m and each a_i=a/n. The sum X has mean a/2=1-1/(2m) and variance
a^2/(12m^3). Chebyshev gives
 Pr(X>1)<=a^2/(3m).
Therefore a Pr(X<=1)>=a*(1-a^2/(3m)), which tends to 2 as m tends to infinity.
All weights remain <=1. In conjunction with the proved upper bound 2, this
establishes that 2 is the optimal universal constant for the scalar lemma.

The obstruction concerns an established elementary probability inequality,
not novelty or sharpness of the network envelope theorem. Its purpose is to
locate the avoidable loss: discarded cut competition, rather than this scalar
constant. The K^2 network bound remains potentially very loose.
