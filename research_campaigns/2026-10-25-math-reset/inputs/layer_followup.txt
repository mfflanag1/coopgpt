LAYER FOLLOW-UP: A BALANCED TWO-TERMINAL NETWORK CONTRACTS EXACTLY
21 October 2026 UTC. Conventional proof; parent reports completed opposite-model audit.
Main result is an exact structural normalization, not a general O(s) bound.
No original owner is split, and every comparison retains one shared weight.
A global one-layer support means that its sets of comparison tails and heads
are disjoint. No profile may both receive and emit a positive comparison.

1. STANDING CONTRACT AND COMPARISON DIVERGENCE
Fix a finite nonnegative packing technology F, finite on legal capacities,
and a fixed roster of actual multirow owners with their original report boxes.
Each nonzero distinct-profile comparison e=(l,h,C) has all outsider reports
unchanged, every changed owner in C, weakly increasing reports of members of
C, and |C|<=s. Let delta_e=B_h-B_l>=0. For full OUT, d_e=F(delta_e).
All comparison weights mu_e are nonnegative and shared by the whole C_e.
Write the COMPARISON-ONLY incidence
  gamma_(p,i)=sum_(e:h=p,i in C_e)mu_e
              -sum_(e:l=p,i in C_e)mu_e.
This differs from the full Farkas coefficient g by coverage incidence.

Only edges with mu_e>0 are in the active comparison graph; their OUT worth
may be zero and such edges must still be retained. Other legal comparisons
with zero multiplier need not have this graph structure. A resulting r-bound
bounds this particular dual certificate, not all other dual supports on Q.
Coverage rows and budgets at every profile, including profiles isolated in
the positive-mu graph, remain present and unchanged.

The finite positive-mu graph is acyclic: a distinct legal restoration strictly
increases the sum of all reported resource coordinates. Trivial self-rows have
zero incidence and RHS and can first be removed. Under the frozen complete
report-profile convention, a restoration with delta=0 has identical reports
for every owner at its endpoints and is just such a self-row. By contrast,
F(delta)=0 with delta nonzero is NOT a self-row. Constant-report owners may
be protected passive members and are never silently deleted here.

2. TWO-TERMINAL CONTRACTION LEMMA
Suppose a weak component of this comparison graph has one source profile a,
one sink profile z, and
  gamma_(p,i)=0 for EVERY actual owner i at every p other than a,z.   (BT)
There may be arbitrarily many branches, mergers, intermediate profiles,
coalition labels, actual owners, and resource rows. Intermediate coverage,
nonnegativity slacks and budgets need not vanish. Only comparison divergence
must vanish there.

Then replace this ENTIRE component by direct comparisons from a to z so that:
  * every new comparison is legal with |C|<=s;
  * every new weight remains shared by its entire actual coalition;
  * comparison divergence at every owner/profile is exactly preserved;
  * the full-OUT comparison objective does not decrease;
  * lambda, nu, sigma, and D remain unchanged.
Thus the replacement is a genuine global source/sink layer. Applied to a
feasible finite dual, the retained rank-r one-layer theorem gives L<=r D for
this component. The same applies componentwise to disjoint components of
this form; isolated profiles use efficient static prices.

3. PROOF BY AN OWNER-COORDINATE CUT
Every vertex lies on an a-to-z path, because the finite acyclic component has
unique source and sink. Every complete report profile on such a path lies
coordinatewise between a and z. For each owner, sum_p gamma_(p,i)=0.
The source has gamma_(a,i)<=0, the sink has gamma_(z,i)>=0, and (BT)
annuls every other term. Thus there is one common nonnegative owner vector
w with gamma_(a,i)=-w_i and gamma_(z,i)=w_i.

Let T be the actual owners whose reports differ between a and z. The component
is nontrivial, so an a-to-z path contains a strict non-self restoration.
Coordinatewise monotonicity makes its endpoint change strict; thus T is nonempty.
A cut in any such changed coordinate crosses a positive-weight path edge,
so its total weight is positive. This will establish M>0 below.
Fix i in T and choose one of i's resource coordinates that strictly increases.
Choose a threshold theta strictly between its endpoint values, unequal to any
vertex value in this coordinate. Partition profiles into those below and above
theta. This separates a from z. No comparison crosses backwards, since all
reports increase. Every crossing comparison must include i, since that owner's
coordinate changes across it. Thus
  sum_(e crossing cut)mu_e = sum_(e crossing cut, i in C_e)mu_e=w_i. (1)
For every other owner j, conservation of comparison incidence on the lower
side gives
  sum_(e crossing cut, j in C_e)mu_e=w_j.                          (2)
Hence w_j<=w_i for all j. Repeating the argument for every i in T shows
  w_i=M:=max_j w_j>0 for every i in T.                            (3)

Use the cut just chosen for one i in T. Its total weight is M. For EACH j in T,
its incidence also equals M. Nonnegative weights therefore imply that every
positive crossing edge contains every member of T. In particular
  T subset C_e for every positive crossing edge, and |T|<=s.       (4)
This is the load-bearing conclusion: zero internal comparison divergence and
two terminals force all endpoint changes to fit in a protected coalition.
It is not inferred merely from each individual edge having size at most s.

For every crossing edge, place its original whole coalition C_e, with its
original shared multiplier mu_e, on a direct a-to-z comparison. This is legal
by (4): owners outside T are unchanged between a and z, and all changed owners
are protected. Equations (1)--(2) show that the direct family has exactly owner
incidence -w at a, +w at z, and zero elsewhere, matching the original network.
No independent owner flows or per-member edge coefficients are introduced.

For the full-OUT objective, exact resource telescoping gives
  sum_(all original e)mu_e delta_e
   =sum_i w_i[b_i(z)-b_i(a)]
   =M[B_z-B_a].                                                   (5)
The first equality multiplies comparison incidence by actual owner resource
vectors and uses (BT). The second uses (3), since owners outside T have zero
endpoint change. Positive homogeneity and superadditivity of F now imply
  sum_(original e)mu_e F(delta_e)
   <=F(sum_e mu_e delta_e)=M F(B_z-B_a).                           (6)
The direct cut family has total weight M, and every direct row has restored
bundle B_z-B_a, so its objective is exactly the right side of (6). TCWM has
zero comparison objective before and after. Coverage weights and all full
owner/profile balances are unchanged. This proves the lemma.

4. UNBRANCHED CHAINS AND COALITION RECOMBINATION
The lemma includes chains of profile transitions with arbitrary parallel
coalition labels. A short direct proof there is instructive: interior balance
makes the transition owner-incidence vector v constant. A transition that
changes owner i has total weight M=v_i, while every v_j<=M. Hence every
transition has M=max_j v_j, every owner changed anywhere has v_i=M, and every
positive coalition at every transition contains all such owners. One can reuse
the first transition's coalitions directly between the endpoints.

This permits changing WHICH endpoint pair carries a coalition label. It is
strictly stronger than making separate forests for each fixed coalition.
The following growing exact family shows why such recombination is necessary.

5. A GROWING OBSTRUCTION TO PER-COALITION-DIVERGENCE NORMALIZATION
Let n=2q>=4 and m=n-1. Take n passive actual owners P_1,...,P_n, each reporting
(1/n,1/n,0), and one varying owner R reporting (0,0,1+k/m) at p_k, 0<=k<=m.
All have the fixed real box [0,2]^3. The sole activity consumes one unit of
A,B,K and has profit one. Thus arity is three and F(B_(p_k))=1 everywhere.
Every passive owner is nonzero and valuable: deleting one from grand-coalition
coverage at p_0 reduces value from one to 1-1/n. R is also valuable.

Use a 1-factorization M_0,...,M_(m-1) of K_n. An explicit formula labels the
passives infinity and Z_(n-1); matching M_t contains {infinity,t} and
{t+a,t-a} modulo n-1 for 1<=a<=q-1. Each matching covers every passive once,
and every passive pair appears in exactly one matching.
For every pair {i,j} in M_k, add comparison p_k->p_(k+1) with the WHOLE
coalition {R,P_i,P_j} and multiplier one. Its OUT value is zero because only
K is restored. Protection size is fixed at s=3.

Give grand coverage at p_0 weight one, and give only p_m budget weight q.
Exact balances are
  g_(p_0,R)=1-q, g_(p_0,P_i)=0;
  g_(p_k,all owners)=0 for 0<k<m;
  g_(p_m,R)=q, g_(p_m,P_i)=1.
They are <=nu. This is a feasible actual-owner dual with L=1 and D=q.
Its internal comparison divergence is zero, so section 2 applies.

Consider the sharply restricted normalization class that preserves comparison
divergence SEPARATELY for every fixed coalition label at every profile, while
allowing arbitrary nonnegative rerouting, subdivision, same-coalition path
compression, splitting across pieces, and added profiles along this same
monotone chain. The pieces must each be globally one-layer feasible duals.
Each of the qm distinct coalition labels has one unit of prescribed net flow
from its original low to high profile. It therefore requires at least one unit
of total comparison weight in any such normalization. Every nonzero comparison
contains R. At a sink profile of any one-layer piece there is no outgoing
comparison, so its budget multiplier is at least its incoming R weight;
nonnegative coverage weights only increase this requirement. Every permitted
profile has F=1. Summing budgets across pieces therefore gives
  sum_piece D_piece >=qm=(n-1)D.                                 (7)
No bound depending only on the fixed s=3 is possible for this class.
This bound is not a count of subdivided arcs: it uses the preserved unit flow
demand of each distinct coalition label. It already allows all same-coalition
path normalizations. No zero-report or zero-coverage-incidence erasure applies
to these passive owners.

Nevertheless copy ANY ONE matching directly from p_0 to p_m with weights one.
This preserves aggregate ACTUAL-OWNER comparison divergence, lambda, nu,
sigma, L and D exactly, and is one global layer. It changes divergence for
individual coalition labels, which (7) forbids. Thus the family does NOT
obstruct general normalization; it demonstrates the precise additional
whole-coalition recombination that a proof must permit. Its unrestricted
funding factor on these selected profiles is one, by the frozen-background
lemma. It is not a funding or price-floor lower bound.

6. WHY BALANCE ALONE DOES NOT SOLVE THE MULTI-TERMINAL CASE
Use four actual owners. At middle profile p each reports one in its own row.
There are two predecessors: l_12 decreases owners 1,2 to zero; l_34 decreases
owners 3,4 to zero. There are two successors: h_13 increases owners 1,3 to two;
h_24 increases owners 2,4 to two. Protect precisely the changed pair on each
of the four edges, with multiplier one. Thus the middle incoming coalition
incidence {1,2}+{3,4} equals outgoing incidence {1,3}+{2,4}.

However every predecessor-to-successor comparison changes THREE actual owners.
At protection size s=2, no direct endpoint comparison is legal. One cannot
couple the owner incidences independently and declare the resulting shortcut
an admissible whole-coalition row. For an explicit production technology take
F(c)=sum_i c_i (arity one, hence within the arity-three class). With budget
weight one at each successor and no coverage, the four-edge full-OUT dual is
feasible, with L=8 and D=12. This is an exact local legality obstruction, not
a growing lower or a failure of bounded-cost layer splitting.

The two-terminal lemma avoids this example because it has two source and two
sink profiles. Its coordinate-cut identity then has multiple distinct terminal
reports, and w_i is no longer one common source-to-sink owner flow vector.

7. PRECISE REMAINING TARGET AND DISPOSITION
Balanced two-terminal comparison regions can now be contracted without any
budget inflation, preserving shared actual-coalition weights and increasing
OUT objective. Per-coalition forests are insufficient, but whole-coalition
recombination resolves those regions, including arbitrary branching within them.

What remains is to decompose a general certificate into such regions or
one-layer pieces when comparison divergence can have many source/sink profiles
and mixed owner signs at the same profile. The cost of splitting those terminal
incidences must be controlled by production and original budget weights. Local
owner-incidence conservation does not guarantee legal terminal shortcuts, as
section 6 demonstrates. No O(s) bound on that terminal-splitting cost has been
proved here. Claiming it would simply hide the original unresolved step.

Therefore this route establishes a useful structural normalization and a
sharply scoped no-go for per-coalition preservation, but does NOT pass the
requested general O(s) upper-bound gate.
