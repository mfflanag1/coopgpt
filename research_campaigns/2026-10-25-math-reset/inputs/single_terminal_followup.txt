SINGLE-TERMINAL FOLLOW-UP: EXACT LIMITS OF ZERO-COST LAYER CONTRACTION
21 October 2026 UTC. Bounded extension of layer_followup.txt.
This note gives finite exact obstructions to two precisely stated normalization
classes. It does NOT disprove bounded-cost normalization or an O(s) upper bound.
The strongest fixture was proposed by the parent and independently checked here.

1. CONTRACT AND THE TWO PRESERVATION CLASSES
Fix a finite nonnegative packing technology, finite on the legal capacities,
one fixed roster of actual owners, and their original real report boxes. In a
legal comparison e=(l,h,C), every outsider report is unchanged, every changed
owner lies in C, every report increases coordinatewise, and |C|<=s. A single
nonnegative multiplier mu_e is shared by the entire coalition C. For OUT,
d_e=F(B_h-B_l). No owner is split and no protected passive member is omitted.

For each owner i and profile p put
 gamma_(p,i)=incoming comparison incidence-outgoing comparison incidence,
 lambdaInc_(p,i)=sum_(S containing i)lambda_(p,S),
 g_(p,i)=lambdaInc_(p,i)+gamma_(p,i),
 sigma_(p,i)=nu_p-g_(p,i)>=0.
The objective and budget are
 L=sum lambda_(p,S)F(b_S(p))+sum mu_e F(delta_e),
 D=sum nu_p F(B_p).
Comparison-only divergence gamma, rather than g, is zero at the internal
profiles considered below. A global one-layer family means that the set of
ALL comparison tails is disjoint from the set of ALL comparison heads: no
profile is both a tail and a head. Every edge directly joins the source set
to the sink set. Merely having disjoint graph-theoretic sources and sinks in
a longer directed graph does not meet this definition. Nor does a separate
forest for every C.

A decomposition means a sum of feasible one-layer duals. In the first class,
aggregate lambda, nu AND sigma must equal their original arrays, so aggregate
gamma is also fixed. In the second class only aggregate lambda and nu are
fixed; sigma may be reassigned freely, subject to nonnegativity. New profiles
may be added with zero aggregate lambda and nu (and zero sigma in the first
class). All multipliers remain nonnegative. The examples rule out even sums
of layers in their stated class, hence also a single one-layer replacement.

2. UNIQUE SINK: PRESERVING LAMBDA AND NU ALREADY FAILS
Take three actual owners, each owning its corresponding resource row. A profile
(x,y,k) means reports (x,0,0), (0,y,0), (0,0,k). One may use the fixed
single-row boxes [0,2]e_i for owner i, a subclass of the arbitrary actual-owner
contract. The same selected-profile obstruction also embeds in full [0,2]^3
boxes, without changing any listed owner or comparison.
Let epsilon>0 and
 F(A,B,K)=min(A,B)+epsilon K.
This is exactly the packing value of two activities: one consumes A and B
and yields one; the other consumes K and yields epsilon. Maximum column arity
is two, within the arity-three contract. Every owner is productive: removing
owner 1 or 2 at a reduces value by one, and removing owner 3 reduces it by
epsilon. Thus the third row is not an unused coordinate encoding device.

Use four distinct profiles:
 a=(1,1,1), b=(2,2,0), p=(2,2,1), z=(2,2,2).
At s=2 the supported comparisons, with whole-coalition shared weights, are
 a -> p, C={1,2}, mu=1;
 b -> p, C={3},   mu=2;
 p -> z, C={1,3}, mu=1;
 p -> z, C={2,3}, mu=1.
In the last two comparisons owner 3 changes while owner 1 or 2 is protected
passively. Each comparison is legal. The graph has two sources a,b and a
unique sink z. Its sole internal profile p is balanced for every actual owner:
 gamma_a=(-1,-1,0), gamma_b=(0,0,-2),
 gamma_p=(0,0,0),   gamma_z=(1,1,2).

Put lambda_(a,{1,2})=1, nu_z=2, and all other lambda and nu equal to zero.
Then g_a=g_p=0, g_b=(0,0,-2), g_z=(1,1,2), and
 sigma_a=sigma_p=0, sigma_b=(0,0,2), sigma_z=(1,1,0).
All finite Farkas inequalities hold. The exact values are
 L_OUT=2+4epsilon, L_TCWM=1, D=4+4epsilon.
Indeed the coverage value is one and the comparison values, counting weights,
are 1, 2epsilon, epsilon, epsilon. The resource check is
 sum nu B=(4,4,4), sum mu delta=(1,1,4),
 sum nu B-sum mu delta=(3,3,0)
   =lambda b_{1,2}(a)+sum sigma b.
No normalization of D is needed; all coefficients can be scaled together.

CLAIM. There is no decomposition into global one-layer feasible duals retaining
aggregate lambda and nu, even if sigma changes and new zero-budget profiles
are allowed. In particular no such replacement can preserve the objective.

PROOF. In any feasible one-layer piece, a sink receiving positive comparison
weight must have positive nu: at a protected owner it has positive incoming
incidence, no outgoing incidence, and nonnegative coverage incidence. Since
aggregate nu is supported only at z and all piece budgets are nonnegative,
every supported sink of every piece must therefore be z. This also excludes
all added zero-budget profiles as sinks.
Some piece has positive coverage weight on (a,{1,2}). Because nu_a=0, its
balance for owner 1 (and owner 2) forces positive outgoing comparison incidence
at a. Thus that piece contains a supported comparison from a to its only
possible sink z. But a->z changes ALL THREE actual owners, so every protecting
coalition must have size at least three, contradicting s=2. This proof does not
fix sigma, comparison incidence, individual coalition labels, or their weights.
It uses only fixed nonnegative coverage and budget coefficients. QED.

The mechanism is different from the earlier harmonic charge obstruction:
changing or removing labels cannot create an admissible a->z comparison.
It also shows exactly where the two-terminal cut proof stops: multiple sources
allow owner's incidence to originate at different report vectors. They need
not admit a common coalition transport to the single sink.

3. A BOUNDED BUDGET ESCAPE FOR THIS SAME FIXTURE
The obstruction is to PRESERVING THE BUDGET ARRAY, not to every bounded-cost
normalization. Keep lambda_(a,{1,2})=1 and nu_z=2, add nu_p=1, and use only
 a -> p, C={1,2}, mu=1;
 b -> z, C={3},   mu=2.
The source set {a,b} and sink set {p,z} are globally disjoint. All comparisons
are legal and all finite owner balances hold. The comparison objective remains
1+4epsilon because b->z restores two units of K with multiplier two. Hence
 L'_OUT=L_OUT=2+4epsilon,
 D'=D+F(B_p)=6+5epsilon,
 D'/D=(6+5epsilon)/(4+4epsilon)<=3/2.
This proves a cheap escape for THIS example, not a general 3/2 theorem.
Reallocating or increasing budgets is a necessary freedom for the proposed
general layer route; this example gives no lower bound diverging with s or
network size. The original certificate itself has L/D<1 and one positive
budget profile, a case already bounded by the retained older dual analysis.
Neither fact is claimed as new.

4. TWO-OWNER MIRROR FIXTURES WHEN ALL SLACKS MUST ALSO BE PRESERVED
These smaller fixtures isolate why preserving sigma is an additional restriction.
They are weaker than section 2 but cover both unique-sink and unique-source
orientations and use a genuinely multirow owner.

Let F(A,B,K)=min(A,B,K), s=2. Write a profile as (x,y), meaning owner 1 reports
(x,0,x) and owner 2 reports (0,y,0); both original boxes are [0,3]^3. Thus total
capacity is (x,y,x) and F=min(x,y). Grand coverage means the two actual owners.
All listed comparison weights are one.

UNIQUE SINK. Profiles a=(1,2), b=(2,1), p=(2,2), z=(3,3). Comparisons
 a->p with C={1}; b->p with C={2}; p->z with C={1,2}.
Use grand coverage at z with weight one and nu_z=2 only. Then
 gamma_a=(-1,0), gamma_b=(0,-1), gamma_p=0, gamma_z=(1,1),
 sigma_a=(1,0), sigma_b=(0,1), sigma_p=sigma_z=0,
 L_OUT=4, L_TCWM=3, D=6.
The resource residual is (4,4,4), exactly coverage (3,3,3) plus source slack
resources (1,1,1).

UNIQUE SOURCE. Profiles a=(1,1), p=(2,2), z1=(3,2), z2=(2,3). Comparisons
 a->p with C={1,2}; p->z1 with C={1}; p->z2 with C={2}.
Use grand coverage at a with weight one and nu_z1=nu_z2=1 only. Then
 gamma_a=(-1,-1), gamma_p=0, gamma_z1=(1,0), gamma_z2=(0,1),
 sigma_a=sigma_p=0, sigma_z1=(0,1), sigma_z2=(1,0),
 L_OUT=2, L_TCWM=1, D=4.
The resource residual is (3,3,3), exactly coverage (1,1,1) plus sink slack
resources (2,2,2).

In either example the middle p has lambda=nu=sigma=0. If all three coefficient
arrays are preserved in aggregate, each nonnegative layer piece has zero such
arrays at p, hence gamma_p=0. In a global one-layer piece a vertex with zero
gamma for every owner cannot have any supported comparison incident to it.
The same holds at every added profile with all three arrays zero.

After deleting these inactive vertices, every possible source-to-sink comparison
in either fixture changes BOTH owners and must protect {1,2}. In the unique-sink
case this would give negative owner-2 incidence at a or negative owner-1
incidence at b, although those original coordinates are zero. No incoming
comparison can cancel it: a,b are incomparable minimal terminal profiles.
In the unique-source case a direct comparison gives unwanted owner-2 incidence
at z1 or owner-1 incidence at z2, again without possible cancellation because
the sinks are incomparable maximal terminal profiles. Thus no one-layer
decomposition preserving aggregate lambda, nu AND sigma exists.

If sigma is allowed to change while lambda and nu remain fixed, these two
particular obstructions disappear at no cost. In the unique-sink example use
only a->z with C={1,2}, mu=1. The new source slacks are sigma_a=(1,1), sigma_b=0;
L=4 and D=6 are unchanged. In the unique-source example use only a->z1 with
C={1,2}, mu=1; the new sink slacks are sigma_z1=0, sigma_z2=(1,1), again with
L=2 and D=4 unchanged. This is why section 2's sigma-free obstruction is stronger.

5. CONSEQUENCE AND EVIDENCE BOUNDARY
The exact two-terminal contraction lemma does not extend verbatim even to a
unique sink with several sources and zero internal comparison divergence.
Preserving all lambda, nu, sigma fails in both one-terminal orientations;
allowing sigma to change still does not suffice in the unique-sink orientation
if lambda and nu stay fixed. The strongest example uses three productive actual
owners and s=2, with no zero-report dummy roster expansion.

A proof of a general O(s) layer decomposition must therefore use a freedom
absent from section 2, such as changing coverage coefficients or moving/adding
budget charges. The explicit escape shows why these finite examples do not
settle whether that freedom can be controlled at O(s). The sigma-free unique-
source orientation is not resolved here. No unrestricted funding lower bound,
price-plus-reserve impossibility, or general subquadratic upper bound follows.

single_terminal_followup.py uses Fraction arithmetic. It checks all full owner
balances, legal whole-coalition comparisons, objective values, and exact resource
identities. It enumerates terminal rows in the two-owner examples, verifies both
slack-reassignment repairs, checks the stronger fixture at epsilon=1/100,1/2,1,10,
and verifies its one-layer budget escape and cost. The impossibility with added
profiles and arbitrary layer decompositions is the symbolic argument above,
not an inference from finite enumeration. Results are saved alongside this note
in single_terminal_followup.json. No recursive model delegation was launched.
