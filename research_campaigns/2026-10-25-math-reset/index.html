A useful stop: coverage weights must be allowed to move

At every report, payments must cover every coalition’s production. A coalition of at most s actual owners may withhold resources, including alongside protected members whose reports stay unchanged. Full outside-value protection requires the coalition’s payment gain upon restoration to cover what the restored resources could produce alone. Total payments are compared with production at that report.

y_C(h) − y_C(l) ≥ F(B_h − B_l) Σ_i y_i(b) ≤ K F(B_b) Recorded unrestricted arity-three frontier: √(s/6) ≤ K₃(s) ≤ K₃ᴼᵁᵀ(s) ≤ 3s².

The proposed proof route combines weighted coverage and incentive inequalities into a finite certificate. Its objective is L and its budget is D. A global one-layer certificate has no profile that both receives and sends a positive comparison. The existing rank argument gives L ≤ 3D for each such piece.

First guard: a renamed problem is not progress

If a replacement may change all coefficients and only has to preserve the objective, its minimum budget J satisfies L/3 ≤ J ≤ L. The upper bound is obtained by a single scaled grand-coalition coverage row. Thus a general small replacement-cost bound is equivalent, up to a factor of three, to the original funding bound. It is a possible proof framework, but not an advance without a quantitative construction.

Growing obstruction to the more specific approach

Retaining the original coverage coefficients looks like a way to make the transformation concrete. The new family shows this restriction is too strong. There are 3m fixed owners, each owning three private input rows for one unit-profit activity. Every activity has arity exactly three, and protection size is fixed at s = 2.

Original optimal certificate: L = D = 6m; all slacks are zero. Any sum of one-layer certificates preserving its coverage array: D′/D ≥ m/2 − 1/4 → ∞. Yet the actual all-profile funding optimum is K = 1.

The certificate can even use only singleton coverage rows. The proof uses m source profiles whose sets of possible one-step destinations are disjoint. Each source differs from the common top report in three actual owners, so a protected pair cannot jump directly there. Retaining each source’s coverage forces a separate budget charge. Allowing new legal profiles, new budgets, free slack, and different coalition labels does not remove those disjoint destinations.

This is an obstruction to a proof architecture. It does not improve the unrestricted lower bound, show that the funding problem is impossible, or establish historical novelty. It concerns repairing a given optimal certificate while retaining its marked coverage rows; selecting a different optimal certificate remains an escape. Exact arithmetic checks supplement the symbolic argument; they do not replace it.

Both the base proof and the optimal-certificate strengthening passed cross-model review and root symbolic checks. Exact arithmetic replay passes at six sizes. This is internal scrutiny; human specialist and priority review remain outstanding.

AI electricity demand: actual data, but a missing predictor panel

The proposed question is whether information available when a forecast was issued—especially customer commitments—helps predict realized large loads and improve capacity timing. That needs compatible historical predictors and outcomes. General electricity demand is not automatically data-center demand, and data-center demand is not automatically AI demand.

The PJM source page provides forecast vintages and an accuracy report. We downloaded and parsed the original 23-sheet accuracy workbook. Its fields are zone, year, metric, and load MW. It contains annual July series, with no project identifiers, commitment categories/dates, or energization fields.

AEP, COMED, DOM and PS have usable historical labels. The RTO and DOM subzone sheets overlap with them and must not be treated as independent areas. The seven positive-horizon pairs are availability counts, not seven independent test observations or a statistical power calculation. Original issue dates, vintage fidelity, population consistency and retrospective revisions still need reconciliation.

AEP’s public methodology summary shows that commitments already enter official forecasts, but supplies aggregate totals rather than a linkable project history. The PJM Board letter discusses NDA information-sharing proposals. It does not prove all useful data are permanently non-public.

Contribution also needs work. ESIG already recommends realization, energization and ramp metrics. A September 2026 SSRN abstract directly overlaps generic long-horizon forecast-bias work; the full paper remains unverified. A generic forecast-error exercise is not the intended new project.

Hold, with a specific reopening condition: establish historical commitment information linked to compatible later outcomes. Then define a defensible held-out evaluation. Capacity-timing value additionally requires explicit early/late costs and lead times. No automatic recheck has been scheduled.

The next mathematical attempt must use more freedom

  1. Allow coverage charges to relocate or be recombined, or select a different optimal certificate. Requiring repair of every supplied certificate while retaining its marked coverage is ruled out by the new family, even for optimal zero-slack inputs. Preserve actual owners, legal reports and the objective; do not silently split strategic owners.
  2. Supply a constructive cost invariant. Merely asserting the existence of cheap one-layer replacements restates the unknown bound. The cost must survive whole-coalition comparisons, mixed ownership and growing owner/resource counts.
  3. Calibrate against both kinds of examples. Resolve the new factor-one obstruction without losing the existing Ω(√s) certificates. A constant-cost reduction to one layer is already incompatible with those lower certificates.
  4. Keep specialist review separate. The consolidated coalition paper remains a review candidate. This reset does not certify priority or send the packet.

No new general mechanism or improved exponent has been proved in this reset. A further long campaign is not automatically started.
