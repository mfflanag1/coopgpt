The concrete candidate

Efficient production prices give each owner a resource charge ui. These charges already cover every coalition's stand-alone value, but they can fall when an owner reveals more resources. The candidate adds a reserve ρi to compensate those losses.

The attempted formula takes the largest accumulated own-charge loss along any increasing report path ending at the current profile. It retains compensation even when somebody else's report changes. If finite, this construction covers coalitions and protects individual incentives. It fails the finite-budget test before the harder coalition-incentive test.

Payment: yᵢ = uᵢ + ρᵢ, with ρᵢ ≥ 0. Extra restriction tested: each owner's reserve never decreases when any owner restores resources. This restriction is a design choice. The original problem does not require it.

Small restorations repeatedly switch the prices

When A is scarcer, its owner receives the entire efficient charge F. When B is scarcer, the charge belongs to B's owner. Alternating small restorations repeatedly switch which resource is scarce. At every step, the restoring owner's efficient charge falls to zero, so its reserve must increase to keep its payment from falling.

The finite-chain lower bound is attained within the restricted class, so it is exact there. The matching finite-chain payments do not extend to a bounded irreversible rule on the whole box.

The escape is real, but not yet general

For y = (F, F), the reserve is the difference between F and the owner's current efficient charge. When prices switch, one reserve rises and the other falls. Total reserve stays at F. The payments are continuous even though the decomposition into prices and reserves switches.

More generally, any finite-budget price-floor rule in this example must release at least max(0, WL − (K−1)FL) cumulative reserve along the chain. That amount grows without limit while output stays below 2 and total resource movement stays below 2. Bounding accumulated positive deficits or reserve variation cannot establish the desired budget theorem.
