Root derivation. Not a novelty claim. Earlier frozen scope is preserved; this records the discovery that its main frontier is already a corollary of an existing project theorem.

1. The exact contract
For a common centered Gaussian factor and fixed ES tail probability, risk equals kappa times the absolute exposure, with common kappa>0. Work in normalized risk units kappa=1; scaling all game values scales both full selectors and leaves rho invariant. Savings v(S)=sum_i in S |a_i|-|sum_i in S a_i|. A full ascending leximin and full descending lexicographic minimum are applied to savings y, not raw capital charges k_i=|a_i|-y_i. There are no capital floors or across-report incentive constraints.

2. Direct realization of the project's extremal family
Let U,V>=0 have equal totals T>0 and disjoint supports coordinate by coordinate. Define a_i=(U_i-V_i)/2. Then sum_i a_i=0 and |a_i|=(U_i+V_i)/2. For every coalition S,
 sum_S |a_i|-|sum_S a_i|
 = (U(S)+V(S)-|U(S)-V(S)|)/2
 = min(U(S),V(S)).
Thus the entire game, core, full selectors, and normalized distance are IDENTICAL to the lower-envelope game, with the same actual owners. No owner splitting is needed in this translation.

The project's paper5/asymptotic_constant_two/sharp_asymptotic_separation.tex gives such disjoint-support U,V with entire core [U,V]. Its Fibonacci family has m>=2,r>=m+1, F=F_(m+1), H=r-m,
 U=(H,a_1..a_m,b_1..b_m;0^r), V=(0^(2m+1);1^r),
 a_i=F_(m-i+1)/F, b_i=1-a_i.
Total T=r and n=r+2m+1. Existing proof gives
 rho=2(F/(F+1)-1/(r-m+1)).
With r=m^2, rho tends to2; the existing sharper choice gives asymptotic deficit2/n+O(log(n)/n^2).
Conclusion: the unrestricted one-factor subclass ALREADY has supremum2. A two-factor law allowing collinear loadings cannot produce a larger frontier. This is an application corollary of project mathematics, not a newly solved open problem. Source theorem still has the project's external correctness/priority limitations.

3. Requiring loadings to span two dimensions is insufficient
Start with any zero-aggregate one-factor book with savings total T and core K. Retain each old loading as (a_i,0) and append two separate owners with (0,e),(0,-e), e>0. Their individual risks are e and their joint risk is0. Total savings T'=T+2e.
For every y in K, (y,e,e) lies in the new core: old capital charges k_i=|a_i|-y_i satisfy k(S)<=|a(S)|. The added charges are0, and for any mixed coalition this is at most sqrt(a(S)^2+e^2(j_+-j_-)^2). Efficiency holds.
Conversely every new core point gives the old owners total T and the added pair total2e, because the old group and the added pair both have zero aggregate exposure, their coalition values sum to T', and efficiency forces equality. Restricting to old coalition inequalities puts the old coordinates in K.
Swapping the added owners reflects the second factor and preserves every coalition value. Uniqueness of each full selector on a compact convex core implies its two added payoffs equal e. Within that symmetric slice, the feasible set is exactly K x {(e,e)}. Inserting the same two constants into two sorted vectors preserves their lexicographic ordering. Hence x'=(x,e,e),z'=(z,e,e),
 rho'=rho*T/(T+2e).
For any finite base book, let e/T tend to0. Full-rank two-factor books also have supremum2. Taking e=T/n^2 preserves the existing leading asymptotic deficit at N=n+2.
Limit boundary: this does NOT satisfy a uniform lower bound on the material contribution of the second factor. Isotropy of Z does not ensure diversified portfolio loadings. The added owners are fixed within each new book; these are different instances, not a falsely ownership-preserving perturbation of one institution.

4. Why the accounting objective matters
For any zero-aggregate norm book b_i=||a_i||, charge allocations satisfy k(N)=0, and savings allocations y=b-k. If we require k_i>=0 for all i, only k=0 is possible. It is feasible because every coalition norm is nonnegative. Therefore y=b is the unique feasible point under that floor and all selectors coincide.
More generally, if negative charges (credits) total at most L for EACH admitted charge allocation, sum_i(-k_i)_+<=L, then sum_i k_i=0 implies ||k||_1<=2L. Two allocations k,h have ||k-h||_1<=4L. Thus their savings disagreement obeys rho<=min(2,4L/T). This is a simple triangle-inequality bound, not a sharpness or novelty claim. Conversely any two unrestricted allocations with disagreement rho require at least one to have total negative charges >=rho*T/4.
Economically, a riskless pooled portfolio with positive/negative internal charges redistributes credits; it is not a need for additional aggregate regulatory capital. Hougaard/Smilgins2014 working paper Proposition6 selects zero charges under its Zero Aggregate Risk axiom. That operates on charges; it does not identify egalitarian savings selectors with capital-charge selectors.
