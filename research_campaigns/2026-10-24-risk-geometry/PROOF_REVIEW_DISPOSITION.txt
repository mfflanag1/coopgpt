ADDED UNIQUENESS LEMMA AND PROOF
Each full ascending lexicographic maximum and full descending lexicographic minimum is unique on a nonempty compact convex K. Existence follows by successively optimizing the finitely many continuous order-statistic functions on nested nonempty compact optimal sets.
For uniqueness, suppose distinct x,y share the optimal sorted vector; otherwise their sorted vectors are strictly ordered and both cannot be optimal. For ascending optimization, choose the smallest coordinate value r for which the sets of indices attaining r in x and y differ. At every smaller level the index sets coincide. In (x+y)/2 these smaller levels stay fixed. The entries equal to r are exactly the intersection of the two r-level sets, strictly fewer than before: a coordinate at r in one vector but not the other is averaged with a strictly greater value. Entries above r remain above r. Thus the ascending sorted midpoint is lexicographically greater, contradiction. For descending minimization use the largest differing coordinate level; its midpoint has strictly fewer entries at that level, while larger levels remain fixed, yielding a strictly smaller descending sorted vector. Convexity supplies the midpoint in K.
This also justifies symmetry of either full selector: a symmetry of K preserves the sorted objective, so uniqueness forces its fixed point. Inserting a common multiset of fixed constants preserves lexicographic comparisons: before the first unequal order statistic, common constants produce identical insertions; at the first unequal threshold the original ordering persists.
