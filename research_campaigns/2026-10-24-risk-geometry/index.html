How the existing theorem becomes a hedging game

Take a common centered Gaussian factor and one Expected Shortfall level. Normalize away its positive scale constant. Owner i has signed exposure ai. A coalition's savings from pooling are:

v(S) = Σ[i∈S] |aᵢ| − |Σ[i∈S] aᵢ|

The existing paper constructs games v(S) = min(U(S), V(S)), where U and V are nonnegative, have equal totals and have disjoint supports. Set:

aᵢ = (Uᵢ − Vᵢ)/2 v(S) = [U(S) + V(S) − |U(S) − V(S)|]/2 = min(U(S), V(S))

This equality holds for every coalition. It preserves the same owners, entire core and both full savings selectors: raising the smallest savings first, or lowering the largest savings first. Their normalized distance is therefore unchanged. Large core diameter alone would not have established this.

For the existing Fibonacci family, with m ≥ 2, r ≥ m + 1, F = Fm+1, n = r + 2m + 1 and total savings T = r:

ρ = ‖x − z‖₁/T = 2[F/(F+1) − 1/(r−m+1)]

Letting r = m² makes ρ approach 2. The earlier paper's sharper parameter choice gives deficit 2/n + O(log n/n²). Because ρ/2 is the fraction of total savings that changes recipients, nearly all benefits can change recipients between these rules. This is a worst-case mathematical statement, not a measurement of actual institutions.

Full rank alone does not repair the target

Place the old book on the first factor. Add two distinct owners holding (0, ε) and (0, −ε), with ε > 0. For every original core point y, (y, ε, ε) is feasible in the new core. Conversely the original group and added pair must each receive their own fixed total.

Symmetry and uniqueness of each full selector force the added payoffs to equal ε. Inserting two common constants preserves sorted lexicographic comparisons. Consequently:

x′ = (x, ε, ε), z′ = (z, ε, ε) ρ′ = ρ T/(T + 2ε)

The loadings now span two dimensions, yet ρ′ approaches the same ceiling when ε/T becomes small. The missing restriction is a material contribution from both factors, not merely a rank condition. The construction uses different instances; it does not split an existing institution while pretending to preserve its incentives.

Sharing benefits and assigning capital are different objectives

Write bi = ‖ai‖ and capital charges ki = bi − yi. This converts a savings-core allocation into a stable charge allocation. It does not preserve a fairness ranking after an owner-specific change of baseline.

In a perfectly hedged pool, total capital charge is zero. If every charge must be nonnegative, all charges must be zero: the only savings allocation is y = b. The extreme separation therefore relies on allowing positive and negative internal charges, or an equivalent transfer/rebate arrangement.

If Σᵢ max(−kᵢ,0) ≤ L for every permitted allocation, then ρ ≤ min(2, 4L/T).

This follows from ‖k‖₁ ≤ 2L and the triangle inequality. It is an elementary bound, without a sharpness claim. It makes the contract explicit: a small transfer budget rules out near-total redistribution.

The broad connection between risk allocation and exact games is established in Csóka, Herings and Kóczy (2009). Hougaard and Smilgins' 2014 working paper, Proposition 6, addresses zero charges under its Zero Aggregate Risk axiom. Its charge-based Lorenz rule is a different objective. The 2016 journal version was not compared.

A finance interpretation could concern sharing a common linear capital carrying-cost saving with permitted transfers. It is not automatically a rule for regulatory reserves, a truthful withholding mechanism or a yield forecast.

A narrower question that may need new mathematics

Can material diversification and dispersed ownership prevent extreme allocation disagreement? Fix zero-aggregate two-factor exposures and define:

bᵢ = ‖aᵢ‖, T = Σᵢ bᵢ G = Σᵢ aᵢaᵢᵀ/bᵢ, trace(G) = T Initial restrictions: λmin(G) ≥ T/4 and maxᵢ bᵢ ≤ T/10.

The first restriction gives both factor directions material weight. The second excludes a dominant owner. Allow the number of actual owners to grow. Ask whether ρ ≤ 2 − c for some fixed c > 0, or whether a valid family can still approach 2.

These restrictions exclude the two constructions used above. That does not prove an improved bound. A symmetric four-direction book gives a feasible calibration with ρ = 0, not a general result. The restrictions are proposed concentration controls, not established regulatory requirements.

Recommended next gate: audit this restricted question against prior work and test whether owner fragmentation makes it routine. Continue only for a proved bound, an unbounded admissible construction or a structural lemma with a demonstrated consequence. Another small separated example is insufficient.

The public-data illustration is feasible

The source documentation describes a revisable staff research product fitted to off-the-run coupon securities. It excludes Treasury bills, floating-rate notes, and on-the-run/first-off-the-run coupon issues. The retrieved history is not a real-time vintage.

It can calibrate hypothetical duration/slope books after the portfolio and fitting protocol are fixed. It cannot reveal banks' actual holdings or establish causality, reserve adequacy or investment returns. No factor estimation or market-outcome test was run.
