OPERATOR-CHOICE BOUNDARY: A CLASSICAL CALIBRATION, NOT A NOVEL THEOREM
24 October 2026. This is an abstract model, not a CoreWeave recovery estimate.

Question tested
Does giving a noncreditor the right to select a replacement operator, by itself,
produce a new coalition-financing theorem?

Frozen one-state model
There are m>=1 fixed actual owners and a finite nonempty set A of permitted
operators. For each a, net distributable cash V_a>=0 and finite public nonnegative
coalition requirements b_a(S) are fixed. Operating costs are already deducted.
The contract can specify an arbitrary separate nonnegative owner-payment vector
x(a) for every a. There are no cross-action or cross-state constraints, private
reports, investment choices, or renegotiation. The creditor receives q(a)>=0,
with x(N,a)+q(a)=V_a and q(a)<=D for fixed face D>=0. All declared owner coalitions
must satisfy x(S,a)>=b_a(S) for EVERY nonempty S subset N. The creditor is not
a member of these coalitions.
The question is a guaranteed cash floor Q for EVERY permitted operator selection,
not an equilibrium prediction about what the customer will choose.

Define tau_a by the owner-participation cover LP in MODEL_CALIBRATION.txt, L2,
and C_a=V_a-tau_a. Assume initially C_a>=0 for every a.

Boundary result
The largest robust creditor floor is
  Q_robust = min(D, min_{a in A} C_a).
If instead the creditor chooses the operator to maximize its receipt, the largest
receipt is
  Q_control = min(D, max_{a in A} C_a).
Thus control matters already in an elementary model, but computing the distinction
adds only minimization or maximization around the familiar participation LP.

Proof
For each a, every feasible x has x(N,a)>=tau_a, so q(a)<=C_a and q(a)<=D.
A floor valid for all a is bounded by their minimum. Conversely choose an optimal
owner vector for each a and set q(a)=Q_robust. Distribute the nonnegative excess
C_a-Q_robust to any one owner. This preserves every lower participation constraint
and exhausts the budget. The same argument at a maximizing action proves the
creditor-control formula. Finiteness ensures a maximum/minimum and the LP optima
exist. If any C_a<0, there is NO feasible allocation for every permitted action,
even at Q=0; clamping the negative minimum to zero would be wrong. A control regime
may avoid such an action only if the actual contract allows that choice.

Financing scope
For finitely many public states with fixed probabilities, explicitly require
C_a(w)>=0 for EVERY permitted a in EVERY state w; otherwise the all-state contract
is infeasible unless a separate default outcome is modeled. With these assumptions,
a controller allowed to select any action independently in each state gives
worst-case expected receipts E[Q_robust(state)]. A risk-neutral lender with required
gross return 1+r>0 can support an advance up to that quantity/(1+r).
This is a worst-case guarantee for a fixed face, not equilibrium debt capacity or
an estimate of customers' actual behavior. Richer contracts and recourse are absent.

Calibration and falsification
One owner with b({1})=2 for each of two operators, V=(12,8), D=8:
C=(10,6), hence Q_robust=6 and Q_control=8. Receipt6 is achieved at both actions
with owner payments(6,2). If the second V is instead1, all-action feasibility fails.
The example is not new economics, not an unrestricted mechanism lower bound and
not a falsification of any completed CoopGPT theorem.

Where genuinely additional mathematics would have to enter
At least one coupling removed by this model must be explicit: operator selection
responding to transfers or investment; one payment menu shared across private
reports; coalition deviations across actions; nonverifiable continuation quality;
consent/renegotiation decisions with an enforceable game form; or dynamic liquidity.
Naming such a coupling is not itself novelty. Compare its exact contract set,
information and equilibrium to the closest literature before a long proof attempt.

Decision
The pure public-information operator-menu route is closed as an elementary
classical reduction. This does not close contingent-control research generally.
