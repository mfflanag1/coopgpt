TRANSITION LIQUIDITY: EXACT BOUNDARIES, NOT A NOVELTY CLAIM
24 October 2026. Bounded follow-up to operating-rights-pilot.

DECISION
Timing can invalidate a terminal-value-only calculation. The following elementary
results make that distinction precise. They are useful diagnostics, not proposed
new theorems or a solution to CoopGPT's unrestricted incentive problems.
No issuer inputs are calibrated. No conclusion about an actual default/recovery follows.

1. FIXED OPERATING PLAN AND ACCESSIBLE CASH
Fix one operator, asset set, and already-compatible continuation rights for a known
sequence of payment events t=1,...,m. A payment and a receipt at nominally the same
date must be ordered correctly; if payment comes first they are separate events.
a_t >= 0 is mandatory operating/cure cash due at event t.
b_t >= 0 is cash actually available to this operator by that event. It excludes
trapped receipts and receipts belonging to another owner. Initial accessible cash
is s >= 0. Cash can be stored, never diverted, and does not earn interest.

A legally and economically executable facility supplies a single date-0 draw
q in [0,B]; it is not callable during the bridge. B is an INPUT, not inferred from
collateral value, a standstill, an unused face commitment, or a sponsor's wealth.
Every event here precedes the facility's repayment date. Repayment and all fees
for every permitted draw must already be covered by separately pledged later
proceeds, which are NOT counted in b_t. Thus q is not a grant or free surplus.
The proposition is conditional on that executable facility, and does not
derive its financial feasibility. The sequences a_t,b_t do not depend on q.
g >= 0 is an external, nonrepayable cash contribution at date 0. We are minimizing
this contribution, not the firm's total financing cost.

Let H = max(0, max_{1<=t<=m} sum_{j<=t}(a_j-b_j)).
Then the least contribution making all dates feasible is
    g* = max(0, H-s-B).
Proof: at each t cash balance is s+g+q-sum_{j<=t}(a_j-b_j) >= 0.
Since q<=B, feasibility implies g>=H-s-B and g>=0. Conversely draw q=B
and set g=g*. Every balance is nonnegative. Feasibility with a smaller draw is
also possible whenever s+g already exceeds H. This proves existence, not
optimal borrowing terms. If the draw itself changes costs or rights, this fixed
plan proposition does not apply without including those effects.

Timing counterexample, no borrowing: s=B=0.
Plan A events (a,b)=(6,0),(0,12) requires g*=6.
Plan B events (a,b)=(0,12),(6,0) requires g*=0.
Both have the same aggregate operating surplus 6 and identical rights.
Thus a terminal objective substitution misses a cash deadline. This does not
constitute a new financial mechanism.

No finite reserve bound follows from terminal net surplus and number of parties
alone: for every M>0, events (M,0),(0,M+1), s=B=0, have surplus 1 but g*=M.
This is an exact obstruction to those insufficient inputs, not an impossibility
for financing with pledgeable receipts or committed external funds.

2. A SEPARATE TWO-DATE SENIORITY MODEL
Do not conflate the committed facility above with new uncommitted lending here.
At date 0 mandatory cost K>0 must be paid. There is no initial cash, grant,
existing credit line, or other source. Continuation yields deterministic total
date-1 value R, of which exactly P in [0,R] is verifiable, assignable cash for
financial claims. The remainder cannot satisfy any financial claim, old or new.
A single existing creditor has a senior face claim D and a liquidation
payoff L, with 0<=L<=D. Already-granted operating rights are fixed.
New financiers are competitive, risk neutral, and require gross repayment equal
to K. No interest, uncertainty, transaction cost, private information, effort,
or hidden diversion. All parties weakly accept their reservation payoffs.
Without a consensual deal, the only alternatives are junior financing under
the unchanged old claim if feasible, and otherwise liquidation. No other
operating, financing, or side-transfer alternative is available.

If old debt cannot be altered and new money must be junior, funding is possible
iff K <= max(0,P-D).
Necessity: no junior lender can collect more than the residual. Sufficiency:
promise K from that residual.

If the old creditor may voluntarily consent to any replacement split of P,
funding WITH THAT CONSENT is possible iff P >= K+L.
Necessity: new financiers need K and the old creditor needs at least L.
Sufficiency: if junior financing is feasible, retain the old claim and allocate
(K,D,P-K-D) to new money, the old creditor, and the residual claimant; here
P>=K+D and the old creditor's unchanged-contract outside payoff is D.
If junior financing is infeasible, the specified outside option is liquidation;
allocate (K,L,P-K-L) instead. Both splits are nonnegative and meet outside options.
This is an existence result under transferable payoffs and unrestricted
consensual contracting, not an equilibrium bargaining or legal theorem.
It does not say actual lenders consent, courts approve priority, or actual
lease/PPA/customer rights can be transferred.

Example: R=P=12, K=6, D=10, L=4.
Junior capacity 2<6 blocks financing. A consented split (6,4,2) works.
If instead P=8 (same R,K,D,L), even consent cannot finance because 8<10,
despite real surplus R-K-L=2. This is a familiar pledgeability/debt-overhang
distinction, not a new theorem. The lost ability to pledge R-P is an assumption,
not derived from AI-specific hardware or contract law.

3. WHY COOPGPT DOES NOT AUTOMATICALLY APPLY
The coalition manuscript fixes actual owners, feasible resource-withholding
reports, and an all-profile payment map. Its TCWM condition concerns coalition
payment changes; OUT additionally protects production from withheld resources.
It does not model arbitrary private costs, renegotiation, financial seniority,
or dates at which receipts become spendable. Its total-payment bound kappa F(B)
is not a bound on gross borrowing, peak cash, or a reversible credit commitment.
Our first result fixes the plan and has no report domain or incentive theorem.
Our second fixes rights and assumes consent can be purchased; it has no
all-profile withholding mechanism. Merely retaining owner names does not transfer
the incentive results.

A valid future bridge must specify, for every allowed report/deviation:
who owns which cash account and asset, what each coalition can actually withdraw,
the induced service cash flows, all payment dates, repayment of advances,
and which surplus/borrowing/subsidy budget is bounded. No such verified institution
has been supplied by the public clauses inspected here.

4. PRIORITY AND SOURCE STATUS
Holmstrom–Tirole (NBER WP5817, November 1996; published JPE 1998) already
models required interim operating expenditures and limited pledgeability.
Original scanned working-paper pp.5–11 were read via OCR; equation (3)/(4)
on p.8 and implementation discussion p.11 were visually checked.
At p.8 rho_0=p_H(R-B/delta_p) is pledgeable continuation return; p.11 shows
why funding above that threshold needs ex ante commitment, including an
irrevocable credit line. This covers the fundamental economic mechanism.
Our public deterministic budget identity is not asserted to be their exact model
or theorem. Their effort incentives, initial investment choice, and uncertainty
are richer than our diagnostic.
Original: https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w5817/w5817.pdf

The independent scout was corrected after opposite-model QA and original checks.
