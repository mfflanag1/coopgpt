The decision

The broad framing has not earned the long mathematical phase. Complementary assets and fragmented creditors are established research subjects. Our simplest customer-control extension reduces to a familiar participation program. The public financing documents do not yet establish the complete chain needed to estimate operating recovery.

The campaign stopped after its initial gates. The original six-to-eight-hour proposal allowed stopping when the model reduced to classical ingredients or essential contract data were unavailable. Those gates have been reached for the versions tested here. The broader question remains open for investigation; no claim has been made that every financing/control model is already solved.

The useful output: a clause-level historical case map, five mathematical calibrations with explicit assumptions, exact arithmetic checks, a corrected literature comparison, and a much closer current research benchmark than the initial brainstorm had identified.

The connection to CoopGPT remains the allocation of joint surplus among actual owners. The difficult bridge is the contract: a lender, a customer, a power supplier and an equipment owner do not automatically have the same withdrawal rights or budget benchmark as the owners in our existing production mechanisms.

A concrete financing: what the documents establish

The case is CoreWeave's July 28, 2025 DDTL3.0 financing, with its December 31 amendment tracked separately. It is a historical case, not a representation of current loan terms. The original 8-K describes a $2.6 billion facility and distinguishes the parent guarantee of CCAC VII obligations from CCAC V obligations. Multiple borrower entities under one parent are not automatically independent resource owners.

The mathematical boundary we checked

1. Extra options cannot hurt the same optimizing lender

Hold the payoff function, costs, liabilities, debt face and selection authority fixed. Expanding the lender's feasible actions can only increase its optimized receipt. A claim that bundling is harmful must therefore identify an actual change in liability, available actions, incentives, decision-maker or other model primitives. Other parties can lose even when this lender gains.

2. Owner participation consumes cash before debt service

In a public, static benchmark, let V be net distributable cash and b(S) the minimum payment required by each nonempty coalition of fixed owners. The least total owner payment is:

τ(b) = min Σᵢ xᵢ subject to xᵢ ≥ 0 and Σᵢ∈S xᵢ ≥ b(S) for every nonempty S. Maximum creditor receipt = min{D, V − τ(b)}, provided V ≥ τ(b).

This is a classical cover program, not a new financing theorem. If V < τ, the stipulated allocation is infeasible, even with zero debt payment. These constraints omit creditor-containing coalitions and do not constitute a full bargaining or incentive model. Financing an initial investment additionally requires sufficient expected creditor receipts.

A checked three-owner example has pair requirements 1 and grand requirement 1. Its minimum total payment is 3/2, certified by matching primal and dual solutions. This illustrates why simply subtracting the grand coalition's requirement can be wrong; it is not asserted to describe an actual project.

3. Unrestricted operator choice adds a minimum around that program

For each permitted operator a, let Cₐ = Vₐ − τₐ. Suppose all Cₐ are nonnegative, all information is public, and payments can be specified separately for each operator. For fixed face D:

Cash floor under every permitted operator choice = min{D, minₐ Cₐ}. Receipt under creditor selection = min{D, maxₐ Cₐ}.

The proof constructs a feasible payment vector for every action and gives surplus to an owner. It works for any finite owner count but introduces no new cross-profile incentive mathematics. The complete model and proof explicitly distinguish a worst-case guarantee from an equilibrium prediction about customer behavior.

For one owner requiring 2 and operators producing cash 12 or 8, face debt 8 gives a robust floor of 6 versus a creditor-controlled receipt of 8. If the lower cash becomes 1, the all-action contract is infeasible. These are synthetic checks, not estimates or unrestricted lower bounds.

The other calibrations show how different controllers can choose different outcomes, and why a deliberately incomplete abstract consent disclosure fails to identify recovery. No pair of hypothetical completions was asserted to be legally admissible under the actual CoreWeave contracts.

The newest close comparison is Wagner's Leverage Beyond Debt, posted October 19, 2026. The author reports a contract database covering 37 listed firms and 155 arrangements, and a financing model linking supplier support with asset rights. We inspected the primary abstract and the versioned public repository. Its codebook already has assumption, purchase, sublease and sale fields. These are author-reported coverage figures, not totals independently reproduced here. The full paper download was blocked, so its theory has not been audited.

Hepp's 2026 working paper and Van Nieuwerburgh's Financing the AI Buildout also cover the broad financing architecture. We checked primary abstracts and dates, not complete proofs or empirical results. We did not combine numerical claims across different draft vintages.

The distinction between customer/authority step-in and lender step-in also has established infrastructure precedents in World Bank project-finance guidance. A private AI customer is not a public authority, but the distinction itself is not novel.

Novelty remains unconfirmed. No result here establishes that a recognized open problem has been solved. The review is useful precisely because it prevents us from turning familiar mechanisms or an existing dataset into an overstated contribution.

The next gate worth funding

Can one named, qualified replacement operator simultaneously obtain the equipment, premises, power and customer rights needed to generate the cash pledged to creditors? If so, who selects it, who pays cure costs, and which party can block the transition?

This is a proposed refinement, not an established open problem. First obtain the complete free Wagner paper and compare its exact model and coding. Use its public database with attribution as a starting point. Extend it only for a clearly distinct, observable question.

  1. Freeze a small comparison. Use the audited DDTL3.0 case, one independently matched data-center lease financing and one supplier guarantee with an executed public form. These are candidate categories, not already verified cases.
  2. Trace rights across documents. Record the controller, activation and cure dates, assignment/physical access, power and cooling continuity, customer consent, operator qualification, cash priority and execution evidence. Preserve unknowns. Do not invent a single numerical “rights score.”
  3. Require a consequence. Continue for a complete operating chain plus a distinct incentive or control implication. If the documents still do not support one, retain a disclosure-boundary case study.

For a long mathematical attempt, first specify the nonseparable friction missing from the static calibration: private information, investment incentives, cross-action coalition deviations, nonverifiable quality or dynamic liquidity. Then compare the resulting feasible contracts and equilibrium with the closest originals. Merely naming one of these frictions does not establish novelty.

My research judgment is to keep AI/power credit as the grounded application. Optional collateral pooling remains the closer direct extension of the coalition mechanism work, as described in the earlier macro/fixed-income scout. It has not been silently launched as a second campaign.

Independent review and reproducibility

Fable supplied a six-source literature scout; Codex supplied opposite-model QA. Root source checks rejected the scout as a ready research design because several proposed stopping rules were broader than their evidence. Useful references were retained. Two separate Fable snapshot reviews checked the mathematical calibrations; root verified and incorporated their scoped corrections. AI review is not specialist validation.

The final operator note explicitly requires feasibility for every action in every modeled state, the same full coalition family as its LP, and a positive gross required return. No default payoff is silently substituted for an infeasible allocation.
