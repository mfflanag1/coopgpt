S1 — Filing/transaction identity
https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1769628/000176962825000033/crwv-20250728.htm
Item1.01: the $2.6bn facility finances customer-contract capital expenditure.
CCAC V and CCAC VII are borrowers under common CoreWeave ownership. MUFG is
administrative agent, U.S.Bank Trust collateral agent, and U.S.Bank depositary.
The filing specifically distinguishes parent-guaranteed VII obligations from
V obligations. Entity count must not be treated as independent-owner count.

S2 — Credit agreement: checked clause map
https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1769628/000176962825000033/coreweave30-ex101xtermlo.htm
Section1.01: V holds the assigned Phoenix1 customer contract; VII holds broader
pledged assets. Certain V collateral excludes Dell servers. Some nonassignable
contracts are excluded, subject to legal qualifications.
Sections2.20(g),8.15,9.27: interparty terms govern specified receipts and remedies;
conflicts favor that agreement. Section4.02(q) identifies customer, DFS and agents.
Sections5.19–5.20: collateral-access delivery duties differ for new and existing
leases and allow extensions; the text does not prove completed delivery.
Section7.02: ordinary default remedies distinguish default by V from VII.
Section1.01 fallaway definition: release of parent recourse has multiple conditions,
including customer creditworthiness and replacement-servicer arrangements.
The posted document ends at signatures. Referenced interparty ExhibitF and
project-contract Schedule3.05 were not available in this copy.

S3 — Parent guarantee
https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1769628/000176962825000033/coreweave30-ex102xparent.htm
Sections2.1 and ArticleIII: payment/performance guarantee, statutory liability
limitations, and the Holdco pledge. This is additional recourse; it does not itself
establish that a creditor can operate a site. Underlying guarantee definitions and
credit-agreement fallaway conditions qualify broad descriptions of recourse.

S4 — Customer agreement
https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1769628/000119312525052207/d899798dex1024.htm
March7,2025 CoreWeave/OpenAI MSA. Section10(c) provides a customer-selected
substitute-operator option following the specified termination route, to be
implemented through separate definitive agreements. Section13(e) gives order
forms precedence over the MSA body. Several periods and economic terms are
redacted. The clause establishes a written contractual arrangement, not that the
separate agreements were executed or that every insolvency trigger is enforceable.

S5 — Amendment control
https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1769628/000176962826000104/ex10311.htm
December31,2025 amendment: sections2(a) and3 change, among other things, the
contract-realization calculation, MSA definition, liquidity schedule and parent
leverage reference. The MSA amendment date/counterparty details include redactions.
Do not combine July formulas and December definitions as if one unchanged version.

S6 — Legal scope boundary, not an issuer conclusion
https://uscode.house.gov/us/usc/119-120/t11/s365/
11USC365(e) restricts some insolvency-triggered terminations, with exceptions;
365(b),(c),(f) address cure, assumption and assignment. Current official text
through7October2026 inspected. No classification of these specific contracts made.
https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/UCC/9-408
Current NY text, revision5June2026, subsection(c) distinguishes certain security
interests from rights to use, assign or compel a counterparty's performance.
This is a reason to verify operating consent, not a legal ruling on this financing.
