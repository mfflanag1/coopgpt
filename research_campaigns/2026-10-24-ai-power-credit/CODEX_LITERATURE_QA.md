**Verdict: revise.** The candidate is a useful literature map, but its strongest reduction gates exceed the evidence. It should not yet support a priority judgment or a decision to stop the proposed model.

1. **Gate A mistakes one shared assumption for model equivalence.** Linear liquidation alone does not reduce a model to S4. S4 also specifies non-verifiable cash and continuation value, bilateral renegotiation, and distinct individual versus collective foreclosure claims. A reduction must map these features, feasible contracts, payoffs and equilibrium behavior. Nor must a surviving distinction reverse a sign: different thresholds or implementability constraints could matter. See S4, §2, printed pp. 8–11. [Original working paper](https://eml.berkeley.edu/~groland/pubs/bankruptaugust09.pdf)

2. **The closest unresolved prior-art check is S3 itself.** The candidate admits it has not verified Bolton–Scharfstein’s treatment of asset complementarity, yet proposes complementarity × fragmented foreclosure as D1. That makes D1 a pending literature check, not yet a well-isolated research distinction. I located an accessible 26-page published scan at HBS; its image-based pages still require examination before resolving this issue. The candidate’s reported access failures may be accurate for its session, but do not establish source unavailability. [Published original](https://www.hbs.edu/ris/Publication%20Files/Optimal%20Debt%20Structure_52b5eed9-b565-461c-9266-2726e5974779.pdf)

3. **Gate C drops a consequential hypothesis.** Hart–Moore Proposition 9 requires stochastic control to be available. On p. 1137, the authors explicitly explain how an outside party can improve incentives when control must be deterministic. The earlier source summary includes the qualification; Gate C omits it. Restore it and distinguish residual ownership from contingent enforcement rights. [Hart–Moore, Proposition 9 and following discussion](https://dash.harvard.edu/server/api/core/bitstreams/7312037c-4b76-6bd4-e053-0100007fdf3b/content)

4. **The “more owners adds no mathematics” conclusion overgeneralizes an example.** Equation (9), p. 1149, concerns a particular pipeline model with bilateral synergies, dispensable agents and no pipeline capacity constraint. It supports using owner count cautiously; it does not establish a general conservation theorem for financing models. The defensible statement is that additional owners alone do not demonstrate a distinct mechanism. [Hart–Moore, §IV.D, pp. 1148–49](https://dash.harvard.edu/server/api/core/bitstreams/7312037c-4b76-6bd4-e053-0100007fdf3b/content)

5. **D2 contains unsupported conclusions about acceleration.** Cross-default without pooling does not itself imply costly liquidation: maturity, available liquidity, enforcement, waiver and renegotiation determine the outcome. “Pure contamination” therefore needs a specified game. Calling strategic-default deterrence the “only candidate channel in the literature” is also unsupported by this bounded search. Retain it as one mechanism to test, and remove the exclusivity claim.

6. **The S5 comparison is substantially supported, with qualifications.** Propositions 1–3 establish the stated binary-project financing and contamination results. Proposition 2 requires both regimes to be feasible, and the quoted gains/losses are **per project**. Gate B becomes decisive only after matching the paper’s cash-flow, debt, recovery and default assumptions—not merely identifying pooled recourse. An accessible May 2013 original is available elsewhere. [Working paper, §3.1–3.3, pp. 12–15](https://bse.eu/sites/default/files/working_paper_pdfs/484.pdf)

The candidate does well on explicit access/version disclosures, separating several conjectures from source results, limiting proposed distinctions to two, and avoiding actual CoreWeave-rights claims. Acceptance requires checking S3’s original treatment of complementarity, correcting Gates A/C, and presenting D1/D2 as conditional tests rather than established gaps. A valid decisive gate would demonstrate equivalence of the proposed model’s feasible contracts, payoffs and equilibrium outcomes to a classical benchmark.
