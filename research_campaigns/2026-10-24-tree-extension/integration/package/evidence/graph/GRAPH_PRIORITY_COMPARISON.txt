Source inspected: Han Xiao and Qizhi Fang, Population Monotonicity in Matching
Games, arXiv:2105.00621v1 (2021), free original:

Sections 2 and 3, especially Lemma 3 and Theorem 11. No exhaustive priority
search or independent verification of their full characterization was performed.

Their players are graph vertices; coalition output is an induced-subgraph
maximum-weight matching. A population-monotonic allocation scheme efficiently
splits each population's output and makes each incumbent's payment nondecreasing
as population grows. Lemma 3 requires a P4's middle edge weight to dominate
the sum of its outer weights; a unit-profit P4 fails this condition. Theorem 11
characterizes admitting graphs by dominant-center double-star components.

Our proposed theorem has fixed owners who may hold mixed divisible resources,
coordinatewise reports, and outside-production protection for a deviating
coalition's total payment. It allows funding above output. It does not demand
that each passive incumbent individually gain when another owner adds resources.
These are materially different contracts. Our path construction neither solves
nor contradicts that paper's PMAS problem. This comparison does not establish
novelty, and a related PMAS obstruction alone does not subsume the quantitative
claim. Specialist priority review and broader original-source comparison remain
necessary before calling the construction new in the literature.
