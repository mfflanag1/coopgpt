2. What the tree attempt established

The tested architecture chooses a maximum flow in a scaled bipartite double cover,
then processes original leaves before newly exposed vertices, clamping each use
toward a fixed reserve fraction beta. For every 0<alpha<1 and 0<=beta<1 choose

        0<q<min(1-beta, alpha^(-2)-1, 1),
        c(q)=(1, alpha^(-1), q, q, alpha^(-1), 1)

on the six-resource path. One owner holds resources 0,2,3,5; two outsiders hold
resources 1 and 4. Saturation and the original-leaf clamps force the use vector

        (1-q, alpha(1+q), q, q, alpha(1+q), 1-q).

The mixed owner is paid 2 at both q and q/2, yet its withheld middle-resource
bundle produces q/2. Singleton outside-production protection fails. This is an
analytic parameter-family obstruction, checked additionally in exact arithmetic
at the canonical irrational case and nine rational cases.

The scope correction matters: arbitrary sequential leaf peeling could select a
newly exposed vertex before an original leaf. The initial research wording was
too broad. The authoritative final note and manuscript explicitly cover layerwise
orders; they do not rule out every sequential order or every tree mechanism.
They also do not cover beta=1 or report-dependent reserves.

Independent composition work found another exact failure. Split shared capacity D
between the star B-A-D and edge D-C to maximize total production, then sum valid
component payments. At B=C=1, A=D=0.9, and after reducing A=D to 0.8, the unique
maximizing split assigns zero D to the star. The owner of A,D receives 2/sqrt(2)
at both profiles while its withheld bundle produces 0.1. Incentives therefore
fail despite the preserved budget. Duplicating the shared capacity instead
preserves incentives but can lose the desired funding constant. This remains a
research supplement, with two subsidiary claims corrected after opposite-model QA.
See reviews/COMPOSITION_ROOT_VERIFICATION.txt. Neither obstruction is an
unrestricted lower bound for all mechanisms.
