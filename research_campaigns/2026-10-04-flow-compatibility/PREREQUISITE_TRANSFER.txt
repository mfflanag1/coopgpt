PREREQUISITE TRANSFER AND A TRANSPORTATION FACE
Coordinator derivation, 2026-10-04. Conventional proof, independently reviewed;
see review/parallel-source-followup/ADJUDICATION.txt for scope and limitations.
A useful reduction, not a proof of endpoint compatibility. No novelty claimed.

Lemma1 (general elementary core transfer). Let v be a nonnegative monotone
TU game with nonempty nonnegative core. Suppose distinct players a,e satisfy
  v(A)=v(A minus {e}) whenever a is not in A.
For any x in C, define y_a=x_a+x_e, y_e=0, and leave other coordinates fixed.
Then y is in C.
Proof: efficiency/nonnegativity hold. If A contains both or neither, its payoff
is unchanged. If a in A and e notin A, payoff increases. If e in A and a notin A,
y(A)=x(A minus {e})>=v(A minus {e})=v(A). All coalitions are covered. QED.
This assumption says e cannot help a coalition unless a participates. It does
not say e is a null player or has zero original ideal.

Lemma2 (genuine network application). Consider a pure three-layer DAG with
arcs s->L, L->R, R->t. Suppose each L vertex has exactly ONE source arc a_i.
All other capacities and parallel middle/sink arcs may be arbitrary rational
nonnegative numbers. Every middle arc e leaving L_i requires a_i on every
source-to-sink path using e. Thus e satisfies Lemma1 with a_i. Sequentially
transfer every middle-arc payoff to its corresponding a_i. The resulting core
point has all middle coordinates zero and weakly increases every source payoff.
No capacity or ownership is changed by this map.

Let M be the middle players, S source players, B sink players. Define the
endpoint game w(Q)=v(Q union M) for Q subset S union B, making those middle
arcs available to endpoint coalitions. Because M alone has no s-t path,
w(empty)=0. The zero-middle face of the original core equals
  F={ (z,0_M) : z in C(w) }.
Proof: restriction follows by testing Q union M; extension follows from
v(K)<=v((K intersect(S union B)) union M) and nonnegative endpoint payoffs.
Grand values agree. This is a face since middle coordinates are nonnegative.

For every source coordinate a, max_C x_a=max_F x_a: inclusion gives one
inequality, and the transfer map gives the reverse. More strongly, every
source payoff vector attainable in C is coordinatewise dominated by one in F.
It follows that joint attainment of all ORIGINAL source maxima in the full
flow core is equivalent to joint attainment in C(w). This is not merely an
LP restatement: w is the concrete bipartite flow/transportation model with
fixed middle capacities and ONLY endpoint capacity owners.

If each R vertex also has exactly one sink arc, reversing the argument gives
the analogous sink-ideal preservation and joint-attainment equivalence to the
SAME face F. Source and sink transfer maps are separate; no single map is
claimed to weakly increase both endpoint sides at once.

Interpretation and remaining gap. With one source arc per L and one sink arc
per R, w is unweighted capacitated transportation: row and column capacities
belong to players; fixed public middle arcs enforce pair capacities. Pair
capacities are allowed, so the unrestricted transportation model is only a
subclass. Arbitrary weighted income matrices are not represented by flow volume.
This reduction alone does not establish side-optimal ENTIRE core allocations.
The unrestricted unweighted-transportation subclass is subsequently shown to
fail source-optimality in ENDPOINT_COUNTEREXAMPLE.txt (complete allowed pairs).
This reduction isolates that question without silently imposing Owen payoffs.

Limitations of the particular maps. Parallel source arcs into one L do not
supply a unique SOURCE prerequisite; the sourceward map cannot choose one of
those owners without a further proof. The sinkward map still applies if sink
ports are unique, as detailed below. The symmetric qualification applies to
parallel sink arcs and the sourceward map. Shortcuts or direct arcs are not included in
the stated pure-layer version. The frozen campaign permits these cases, so this
lemma does not resolve its universal target. A path decomposition by itself
cannot justify arbitrary transfers. The eight registered computations include
both bundle and pure-layer examples, but they do not prove these universal claims.

STRENGTHENING AFTER FIRST AUDIT. The sourceward map in Lemma2 leaves every
sink coordinate UNCHANGED, as well as weakly increasing sources. Thus unique
source ports alone suffice for preservation of BOTH endpoint coordinate ideals
on F: source maxima are dominated into F; sink projections are identical.
Conversely, when each R has exactly one sink arc, the sinkward map leaves all
source coordinates unchanged and weakly increases all sinks. Hence unique
sink ports alone suffice for both endpoint ideal preservation, even with
parallel source ports. The two-direction assumptions in the earlier statement
were sufficient but stronger than necessary. The updated quota theorem uses
only unique sink ports and the latter map. General both-sides parallel ports
remain outside this reduction unless another argument is provided.
