EXACT CORE EMBEDDING OF UNWEIGHTED TRANSPORTATION
Coordinator conventional derivation, 2026-10-04. Initial independent Fable
snapshot audit found no invalid step; see review/coordinator-proof/review.md. No historical novelty claimed. This is a reduction, not a settlement
of endpoint compatibility or C2.

Base game. A and B are disjoint finite player sets with rational capacities
r_i,c_j>=0; E subset A x B is an allowed-pair graph. For Q subset A, R subset B,
w(Q union R) is max sum z_ij subject to z>=0, allowed pairs, row sums<=r_i for
present i, column sums<=c_j for present j. All feasible trade units have value1;
several units may trade with the same partner. Assume T=w(A union B)>0.
This is unweighted transportation, not arbitrary weighted assignment, and not
the multiple-partners model with an extra one-unit bound per pair.

Construction. Let M=min(sum r_i,sum c_j), which is at least T and bounds every
coalition flow. Create source arc a_i=(s,L_i) capacity r_i and sink arc
b_j=(R_j,t) capacity c_j. For each allowed pair (i,j), introduce TWO parallel
arcs L_i->R_j, each capacity M and each owned by a distinct new player. Let D
be the set of these middle-arc players. All players own exactly one arc;
the network is acyclic and every s-t path has exactly3 arcs. Parallel arcs
are permitted, as in the campaign bundle fixtures and previous flow conventions.

Claim. Identifying base players with endpoint arcs,
  C(flow) = { (x,0_D): x in C(w) }.
Consequently the endpoint-coordinate ideals and their joint-attainment question
are preserved exactly; every new middle arc has original core ideal zero.

Proof. With all middle arcs present, the row/column constraints are precisely
the endpoint flow constraints. M is sufficient for every possible flow on any
pair, so the middle capacities never restrict a feasible transportation plan.
Thus v(Q union R union D)=w(Q union R), including grand value T.

Deleting any one middle arc leaves its parallel twin with capacity M. Therefore
v(N minus {d})=T. For any core allocation y, nonnegativity and efficiency imply
0<=y_d<=T-v(N minus {d})=0. Thus all y_D=0. Taking coalitions Q union R union D
shows y(Q union R)>=w(Q union R), with endpoint total T, so its endpoint
restriction belongs to C(w).

Conversely, extend x in C(w) by zero on D. For every flow coalition K,
v(K)<=w(K intersect (A union B)): adding every middle arc can only increase
flow. Hence extended x(K)=x(K intersect (A union B))>=v(K), and efficiency
and nonnegativity hold. This proves both inclusions and the ideal claim. QED.

What this does and does not establish.
- Any endpoint incompatibility in unweighted transportation would yield an
  in-class flow counterexample. A universal positive flow theorem would settle
  this transportation special case. ENDPOINT_COUNTEREXAMPLE.txt subsequently
  supplies such a failure for the complete allowed-pair transportation game;
  its direct two-hop realization already refutes the campaign target.
- The extra arcs are NOT null/dummy players in the characteristic function:
  they may contribute to coalitions lacking their twins. They have zero payoff
  in every grand core allocation, which is the only property used.
- This does not clone an original capacity owner into independent players or
  identify the full core with an Owen subset. It constructs a new game and
  proves the full-core correspondence in both directions.
- There is no claim that all three-hop flow games reduce back to this model;
  separately owned capacity-limited middle arcs remain an additional difficulty.
- Historical many-to-many open-problem statements cover larger weighted models.
  This reduction neither verifies current openness nor resolves those models.
