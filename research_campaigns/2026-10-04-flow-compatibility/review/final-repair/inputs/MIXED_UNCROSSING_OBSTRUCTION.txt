MIXED UNCROSSING: A CLASSICAL INEQUALITY AND A TIGHTNESS OBSTRUCTION
Coordinator corrected derivation, 2026-10-04. Independent review pending.
This records a blocked proof step, not a counterexample to endpoint compatibility.
The rank inequality is classical polylinking/bimatroid structure; no novelty claim.

MODEL AND INEQUALITY.
Sources I own separate arcs s->L_i of capacities r_i; sinks J own separate arcs
R_j->t of capacities c_j. Public middle arcs have capacities p_ij>=0. Parallel
public arcs may be combined by summing capacities. All trade units have value1.
Let w(Q,R) be maxflow with precisely Q subset I and R subset J endpoints active.
For any Q,Q',R,R',
 w(Q,R)+w(Q',R') >=
 w(Q intersect Q', R union R') + w(Q union Q', R intersect R').             (M)

DIRECT CUT PROOF WITH THE CORRECT CUT PAIRING.
Use the full fixed vertex set, even when endpoint arcs are inactive. Let U,V be
minimum-cut source sides for w(Q,R),w(Q',R'), with s in and t out. Use U intersect V
for (Q intersect Q',R union R') and U union V for
(Q union Q',R intersect R'). Both are valid cuts on that full vertex set.
The fixed public-middle cut-capacity function is submodular. This follows by
checking the four membership indicators of each directed arc and summing with
nonnegative capacities. For source i let a=1_(i in Q), a'=1_(i in Q'), and
u=1_(L_i notin U), v=1_(L_i notin V). Its two original cut contributions divided
by r_i sum to a*u+a'*v, while the two new contributions sum to
 (a AND a')*max(u,v) + (a OR a')*min(u,v).
The former is at least the latter: equality when a=a', otherwise min(u,v) is at
most the selected original indicator. This includes r_i=0 without division by
interpreting the inequality before multiplying by r_i.
For sink j put b=1_(j in R), b'=1_(j in R'), u=1_(R_j in U), v=1_(R_j in V).
The crossed terms are
 (b OR b')*min(u,v) + (b AND b')*max(u,v),
again at most b*u+b'*v. Sum these inequalities and the middle-cut submodularity.
The sum of original minimum-cut capacities is at least the sum of the two
crossed-cut capacities, each at least its crossed maximum-flow value. This is(M).

WHY CORE TIGHTNESS DOES NOT UNCROSS THIS WAY.
For any additive payoff (x,y), the sum of its payments to(Q,R),(Q',R') equals
the sum on the two crossed coalitions. If the originals are tight, (M) permits
STRICTLY SMALLER total worth on the crossed pair, so their payments may have
positive slack. Usual tight-set closure would need the opposite inequality.

EXACT CERTIFICATE USING THE ALREADY REGISTERED G01.
The network is the two-hop bundle with sources a1,a2:s->u capacity3 each, and
sinks b1,b2,b3:u->t capacities2,2,1. Thus
 w(Q,R)=min(3*|Q|,2*1_(b1 in R)+2*1_(b2 in R)+1_(b3 in R)).
Equivalently, these endpoint values are unrestricted unweighted transportation.
The payoff z=(0,0;2,2,1) is a core point: total5 and every coalition's payoff is
its available sink capacity, which is at least its maximum flow. Now take
 Q={a1}, R={b1,b3}; Q'={a1,a2}, R'={b2,b3}.
Both originals have worth3 and payoff3, hence are tight.
The crossed pair is
 ({a1},{b1,b2,b3}) with worth3 and payoff5,
 ({a1,a2},{b3}) with worth1 and payoff1.
Total worth drops from6 to4, while total payment remains6. The original
coalitions overlap, and neither contains the other. This is a strict obstruction
to tight-set closure in an actual already-certified flow game, not merely a
pathological abstract game. It is an analytic derivation, not a new LP or census.
This same game DOES have joint endpoint maxima; it refutes no compatibility claim.

CORRECTION OF THE DELEGATE PROOF.
The raw delegate report used restricted cuts X subset Q,Y subset R and paired
(X intersect X',Y union Y') with(X union X',Y intersect Y'). Its asserted
middle-arc indicator inequality is false. Counter-pattern: i in X but not X';
j in R intersect R', j in Y but not Y'. The original middle indicators sum0,
whereas the reported second crossed indicator is1. The theorem(M) remains true,
but that proof must not be used. The full-vertex proof above avoids the problem.

COMPLEMENTED UPPER CONSTRAINTS DO NOT REVERSE THIS SIGN PROBLEM.
Writing core constraints as payoff(A)<=g(A), where g(A)=T-w(N minus A),
turns(M) into g(A)+g(B)<=g(A crossed B)+g(B crossed A): the two complements
exchange which crossed coalition is which, without changing their sum. Thus
tight original UPPER constraints still allow slack in the crossed upper bounds.
For the G01 tight pair above, complementing gives coalitions {a2,b2} and {b1}.
Both have payment2 and upper bound5-3=2. Their crossed pair, in either order,
is {a2} (payment0, upper bound5-3=2) and {b1,b2} (payment4, upper bound5-1=4).
Total upper bound rises from4 to6 while payment stays4. Column complementation
similarly changes mixed submodularity to ordinary submodularity but does not
turn lower core constraints into the upper constraints of a base polyhedron.
These are sign/constant transformations, not a new directedness proof.

STOPPING CONCLUSION.
(M) is stronger than separate submodularity but already established mathematics.
Its strict-slack example rules out directly transferring the usual core tight-set
uncrossing argument. It does NOT prove that bisubmodularity is logically
insufficient for every possible endpoint-directedness theorem. A different
argument controlling slack might work; none has been established in this attempt.
