ONE-SIDED QUOTAS: ENDPOINT COMPATIBILITY AND SHARP PROTECTED SUBSIDY
Coordinator proof candidate, 2026-10-04. Independent audit pending.
This is a restricted-class theorem; the frozen arbitrary-capacity target remains
open in this campaign. Its principal ingredient is classical many-to-one core
geometry, not new machinery. Novelty and field-level significance unestablished.

THEOREM. Let q>0 be rational. Consider a finite pure three-layer DAG
s->L->R->t, with one arc a_i from s to each L_i and one arc b_j from each R_j
to t. Source capacities are q*r_i, r_i nonnegative integers. Each sink arc has
capacity q. Each middle arc has capacity0 or at least q (rational); parallel
middle arcs are allowed, but endpoint arcs at a given L/R are unique.
Each arc is a distinct player. Let T>0 be the grand maximum flow and H_e be
its ORIGINAL ENTIRE-core coordinate maxima. Then:
(1) All source ideals are jointly attained in the original core, and all sink
    ideals are jointly attained in another original core allocation.
(2) If m is the number of middle arcs with H_e>0, then
    s_(1/3) <= q*max(0,m-1)/3 <= (T-q)/3.
(3) The bound (T-q)/3 is sharp for each positive integer T/q, because the unit
    chain family in the completed scoping campaign, scaled by q, is a subclass.
The reversed version holds with unit source capacities q and integral-multiple
sink capacities. No statement is made for arbitrary capacities on both ends.

DEFINITIONS. v(A) is max flow using exactly the available arcs in A.
C={x>=0: x(N)=T, x(A)>=v(A) for every A}. H_e=max_C x_e is frozen.
s_(1/3)=min{y(N)-T: y>=H/3, y(A)>=v(A) for every A}.
All maxima exist because the finite core is nonempty, closed and bounded.

STEP1: SCALE AND NORMALIZE WITHOUT ALTERING THE GAME.
Divide capacities, coalition values and payoffs by q. Work at q=1, restoring
scale at the end. A positive middle arc ending at R_j can never carry more than1
in ANY coalition flow, because the sole outgoing arc b_j has capacity1. Hence
capping every positive middle capacity at1 leaves every coalition value unchanged.
Zero-capacity arcs may be retained; their marginal contribution is identically0
and their core coordinates are0. The resulting effective network has integral
capacities, middle capacities0/1, and sink capacities1. Thus T is a positive
integer. This cap normalization preserves the same players, v, C and H; it is
not an independent-player cloning operation.

STEP2: THE ZERO-MIDDLE FACE PRESERVES ENDPOINT IDEALS.
Let M be all middle players, S all source players and B all sink players. Every
middle arc leaving L_i needs a_i on any path using it. Transfer its payoff to
a_i. The elementary prerequisite-transfer lemma in PREREQUISITE_TRANSFER.txt
proves this preserves all core inequalities and weakly increases each source
coordinate. Doing this for every middle arc yields a point of C with x_M=0.
Similarly, transferring each middle payoff to its unique downstream b_j preserves
core and weakly increases every sink coordinate.

Define w(Q)=v(Q union M) on endpoint players Q subset S union B. The face
F=C intersect {x_M=0} equals {(z,0_M):z in C(w)}, by testing coalitions with all
M for restriction, and monotonicity for extension. The two transfer maps imply
max_C x_a=max_F x_a separately for each source a and each sink b. The source
and sink maps are distinct; a single map improving both sides is not asserted.

STEP3: IDENTIFY THE ENDPOINT GAME.
With all middle arcs available, let a_ij=1 if at least one positive middle arc
connects L_i to R_j, and a_ij=0 otherwise. A source firm i may serve at most r_i
workers, while each sink worker j may be served once. Every allowed trade has
value1; zero-value trades can be ignored. Integral flow/matching equivalence
therefore identifies w with the many-to-one assignment game with matrix a and
firm quotas r. Parallel positive middle arcs do not change this endpoint game.
No whole-core/Owen identification is made.

STEP4: SELF-CONTAINED MANY-TO-ONE CORE LATTICE ARGUMENT.
The next standard characterization and lattice consequence are included to make
the proof checkable. They are established ingredients; see Atay-Nunez-Solymosi,
J Global Optimization2026, Proposition2, and their references to Sotomayor.
https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10898-026-01632-7

This argument works for any nonnegative assignment matrix a, not just0/1. Remove
zero-quota firms (null players), and balance total quota and number of workers
by adding enough zero-value dummy workers if quotas exceed worker count, or a
zero-value dummy firm with the missing quota if workers exceed total quota.
These are null players: allowing zero-value matches cannot improve any coalition
value. Their core coordinates are necessarily zero, and projecting them away
preserves the original game and core. Fix an optimal matching mu that fills every
firm quota and assigns every worker, permitting zero-value matches. Write
mu(i) for the workers assigned to i, and i(j) for the firm assigned worker j.

Claim: (x,y) is in the balanced market core iff
  (i) 0<=y_j<=a_i(j),j for every worker j;
  (ii) y_k-y_j >= a_i,k-a_i,j whenever j in mu(i), k notin mu(i);
  (iii) x_i=sum_{j in mu(i)}(a_i,j-y_j).
Necessity: the disjoint coalitions {i} union mu(i) together yield the optimal
value. Their core inequalities and efficiency force equality separately, giving
(iii). Nonnegativity gives y_j>=0. Compare equality with coalition i plus
mu(i) minus j to obtain y_j<=a_i,j. Compare with the feasible replacement
mu(i) minus j plus k to obtain(ii). These are feasible coalition lower bounds;
no assertion that the replacement itself is optimal is needed.

Sufficiency: (i),(iii) imply nonnegative firms/workers and total optimal value.
For any firm i and worker set U of size at most r_i, pair each k in U minus mu(i)
with a distinct j in mu(i) minus U. There are enough such j. Condition(ii) says
  a_i,k-y_k <= a_i,j-y_j.
Every remaining matched surplus is nonnegative by(i), so
  x_i >= sum_{k in U}(a_i,k-y_k).
Thus every one-firm feasible team is covered. Any feasible matching of ANY
coalition partitions its matched players into disjoint one-firm teams; unused
players have nonnegative payoffs. Summing the preceding inequalities covers that
matching's value, and hence the optimum for the coalition. This proves the claim.

Let Y be the salary vectors satisfying(i),(ii). It is a nonempty compact set
(nonemptiness follows from the standard assignment dual core construction, or
from the nonempty flow core and Step2 in our application). Y is closed under
coordinatewise minimum AND maximum: if y_k>=y_j+d and z_k>=z_j+d, both the
minimum and maximum of the left coordinates dominate the corresponding minimum
and maximum of the right coordinates plus d. Box bounds are also preserved.
Take one salary minimizer per coordinate and successively take their componentwise
minimum; this constructs y^- in Y minimizing every salary simultaneously. The
corresponding x^- from(iii) simultaneously maximizes EVERY firm payoff, since
each x_i is a constant minus a sum of salaries. Similarly y^+ constructed from
coordinate maximizers simultaneously maximizes every worker payoff. Dummy
coordinates remain zero by core membership. Projecting away dummy players gives
the desired two side-optimal core allocations for w. Step2 identifies their
coordinate maxima with the original entire-flow-core H at the endpoints. This
proves(1).

STEP5: INTERNAL IDEALS ARE BINARY AFTER NORMALIZATION.
For any arc e, the complement coalition gives H_e<=T-v(N minus e). For a
normalized middle arc its capacity is at most1, so its deletion marginal is0
or1 (integral maxflow and a maximum flow loses at most its flow on e when the
paths through e are removed). If this marginal is0 then H_e=0. If it is1,
take a minimum cut in the network with e deleted; its capacity is T-1. Arc e
must cross this cut, otherwise the original network would also admit this
cut of capacity T-1. Restoring e of capacity1 makes a minimum cut of capacity T.
The allocation paying each cut arc its capacity and others0 is in the ORIGINAL
core of the normalized game: its total is T and its payment to every coalition
is the capacity of that coalition's restriction of the cut, hence at least its
maxflow. It pays e exactly1. Thus H_e=1. Therefore every middle H_e is0 or1.

A fixed integral grand maximum flow f uses every positive-ideal middle arc:
otherwise deleting it would not lower the maximum flow. Its flow there is1.
Every unit of flow crosses exactly one middle arc in this pure-layer network,
so m<=sum_{e in M} f_e=T.

STEP6: PROTECTED PAYOFF CONSTRUCTION.
Let p^S,p^B in C jointly attain source and sink maxima as proved above. If m=0,
(p^S+p^B)/2 already dominates H/3 and yields subsidy0. If m>=1, choose a middle
arc e with H_e=1 and a core point p^e attaining that value (Step5 gives a cut
point). The average z=(p^S+p^B+p^e)/3 is core. It dominates H/3 on all endpoints
and on e. Raise each other middle coordinate, if necessary, to H/3. Only the
m-1 remaining positive-ideal middle arcs can need a raise, of at most1/3 each.
The resulting y is nonnegative, covers every coalition, and has all frozen
floors, with y(N)-T<=max(0,m-1)/3. Since m<=T and T is a positive integer, this
is at most(T-1)/3. Rescaling proves(2).

STEP7: SHARPNESS AND SCOPE.
For integer n=T/q>=1 use vertices s,L_i,R_i,t and arcs sL_i,L_iR_i,R_it,
plus L_iR_(i+1) for i<n, all capacity q. The prior campaign gives a conventional
upper/lower certificate with subsidy q(n-1)/3. The family has unique endpoint
arcs, and its source quotas are1, so it lies in this theorem's class. This proves
sharpness. It does NOT show arbitrary unequal endpoint quotas are covered.

LOAD-BEARING UNRESOLVED STEP OUTSIDE THE CLASS.
When both endpoint sides have quotas above1, optimal transportation flows no
longer partition players into one-firm teams: one worker-owner can serve several
firms. The salary-difference characterization in Step4 then fails to follow.
Splitting that worker-owner into separate players changes the original entire
core. This proof does not supply the missing many-to-many compatibility result.
With parallel source/sink ports, Step2 may also lose its unique prerequisite.
