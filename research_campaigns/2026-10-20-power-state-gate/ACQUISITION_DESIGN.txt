OFFLINE ACQUISITION AUDIT — FIXED JUNE 1–14 CANDIDATE

Decision

The declared complete-column-chunk reader cannot acquire the fixed June 1–14
candidate under the inherited remaining allowance of 6,393,615 bytes. Its exact
uncached chunk union is 107,625,469 bytes, an excess of 101,231,854 bytes before
any additional requests, gaps or retries. This is a necessary byte cost within
that reader class, conditional on the supplied metadata. It is neither a claim
that all possible readers fail nor a universal compression lower bound.

This audit uses only the five designated safe inputs: the metadata projection,
candidate summary, byte totals, candidate gate text, and request ledger. No
archive, Parquet member, footer binary, page, node dictionary, or dataset value
was opened. No package was installed, no network request made, and no source
file changed. The report hashes those inputs and checks the metadata hash
against the inherited candidate summary. Ledger assertions are provenance and
arithmetic checks on supplied records; sealed response bodies are not rehashed.

The candidate remains [2020-06-01T00:00:00Z, 2020-06-15T00:00:00Z), with the
fixed 980-node design. No date/node selection, cap change, shorter reliability
window, holdout qualification, or data acquisition is authorized by this audit.
The 980 count is a design constraint, not an observed active-node cardinality.
Active-node counts, common-node support, power/state codes and their statistics
remain unread. This June-only arithmetic does not establish the completeness
or acquisition feasibility of a fixed training-plus-holdout pair.

Exact definition and reproducible ranges

A row group is selected when its reported closed timestamp extrema [lo,hi]
overlap the fixed half-open candidate [start,end): lo < end AND hi >= start.
For every selected group and projected column, the complete chunk begins at
the dictionary-page offset when present, otherwise the data-page offset. Its
end is that start plus the supplied total_compressed_size. These are byte
ranges [start,end), not inclusive HTTP endpoints. For HTTP, the last requested
byte would be end-1. Reported extrema are relied on as valid for this plan;
global envelopes alone do not prove complete interior coverage.

acquisition_audit.json records every selected group id and the canonical exact
per-column union in member-relative coordinates, plus the complete selected
union for each member. Add archive_member_start to both endpoints for archive
coordinates. It also records all-month complete-chunk unions and the already
cached marker/footer ranges. The exact source offsets are:

  state:           897,921,024
  ps0_input_power: 3,269,122,048
  ps1_input_power: 4,010,451,456

Duplicate identical metadata entries cost once, order is immaterial, and
overlapping/adjacent ranges are canonically unioned. Conflicting duplicates,
overlapping distinct chunks, missing groups/columns, reversed or malformed
ranges, invalid offsets, and known metadata/chunk overlaps fail closed.
No selected chunk overlaps the cached June marker/footer/header union, so
there is no previously-paid-byte credit against the selected-chunk total.

Reader alternatives and limits

1. Timestamp-first, complete selected chunks
   The first stage alone needs 33,114,069 uncached bytes, exceeding the remaining
   allowance by 26,720,454. Each PSU timestamp column alone exceeds the remaining allowance.
   Therefore this particular timestamp-first strategy cannot start and finish
   its timestamp stage within the current allowance. In addition, every
   selected group's reported minimum is inside the candidate (96/96, 442/442,
   477/477). Conditional on valid extrema, each group has at least one target
   timestamp, so timestamp decoding cannot eliminate an entire selected group
   while recovering all target records at complete-chunk granularity. Savings
   in later reads are not assumed. A strategy reading only selected timestamp
   pages is a different, currently uncosted reader class.

2. Drop all extra state columns
   Dropping description, host_group, nagiosdrained and state_type would save
   exactly 81,037 bytes. Keeping only timestamp/value/node for state and both
   PSU members still costs 107,544,432 bytes, exceeding the remaining allowance
   by 101,150,817. This fails even before deciding whether the research semantics
   permit omitting those columns. Treating them as optional for this favorable
   byte calculation does not establish that they are scientifically optional.

3. Complete projected column chunks
   The inherited selected-group plan is the exact 107,625,469-byte union above.
   Reading every column in every June row group, without timestamp pruning,
   costs 128,050,719 chunk bytes: state 836,502, PSU 0 63,082,168, PSU 1
   64,132,049. These are necessary payload unions; neither figure guarantees
   that a specific client will avoid additional body bytes. A complete-member
   or a reader that coalesces byte gaps pays more.

4. Whole members
   The three complete members total 128,776,583 bytes: state 974,255; PSU 0
   63,375,454; PSU 1 64,426,874. Reading them as three complete responses pays
   that amount, including repeated metadata bytes. If a reader instead assembles
   them using all cached member bytes, 378,489 bytes can be credited and the
   exact new-byte cost is still 128,398,094. Both variants fail the cap. Tar
   padding and unrelated archive members are excluded. A whole-archive download
   would be a separate, larger plan and is not proposed.

5. Indexed-page or page-header-scanning alternatives
   The five audited inputs do not supply page counts, offsets, compressed page
   lengths, index existence, timestamp-to-row/page mapping, dictionary reuse,
   or cross-column page alignment. No exact byte budget or feasible savings
   follows from this projection. The cost is UNIDENTIFIED, not proved zero,
   feasible, or impossible. An alternative needs a separately checked map of
   required pages and decoder dependencies; a bounded cost for discovering that
   map; accounting for requests, gaps and retries; and an exposure plan before
   any power/node/state decoding. Scanning headers may itself consume dataset
   bytes, and a selective record predicate does not imply selective physical
   byte retrieval. Independent structural-index findings, if obtained by the
   parent audit, should be stated separately with their own provenance.

Overhead and cumulative accounting

The ledger replays to 93,138,130 new dataset response-body bytes. Adding the
inherited 468,255 bytes gives 93,606,385 consumed under the 100,000,000-byte cap.
Documentation response bodies total 45,851 and are separate by contract.
June's 544,889 already charged bytes are 325 header requests totaling 166,400,
plus nine marker/tail/footer requests totaling 378,489. The audit checks exact
206 responses, Content-Range endpoints, requested bytes and body lengths for
successful dataset entries. Unique-cache unions and actually-read-body sums
are distinct accounting quantities; repeated reads would still be charged.

Selected chunk cost uses supplied compressed lengths, with no extra allowance
for gap coalescing, new footer checks, page indexes, retries, failed-response
bodies, repeated requests, or any reader-specific additional body bytes. These
would add to future dataset consumption. Previously charged marker/footer bytes
can be reused without charging them again. HTTP/TLS and response-header overhead
are excluded under inherited BYTE_TOTALS accounting, not silently counted as
zero network traffic. The difference between whole-member lengths and all-month
chunk unions is 725,864 bytes; 378,489 of that is cached member metadata/markers.
The remaining 347,375 is other member space outside the supplied chunk unions.
Its internal interpretation is not inferred by this safe projection.

Exposure and execution boundary

Selected groups extend through June 30, with 90 state, 338 PSU 0 and 416 PSU 1
group maxima at or after the exclusive candidate end. Current use of structural
metadata and timestamp extrema is not June outcome evaluation. If a later
authorized reader decodes those chunks, all exposed dates must be declared
