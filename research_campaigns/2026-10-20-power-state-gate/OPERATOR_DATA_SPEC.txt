CONDITIONAL RESTART SPECIFICATION — DRAFT, NOT SENT

Purpose

Identify useful, reliability-qualified shared power headroom and its net value.
This is a request specification for future evidence, not a report of available
rights, measured savings, owner-independent truthfulness, or an agreed contract.

Required before another performance campaign

1. Fix the physical boundary. Provide the delivery-point diagram, all loads and
   losses charged to its capacity, fixed equipment roster and meter-to-equipment
   mapping. Include cooling/auxiliary loads if they consume the claimed capacity.
   A PSU sum is not automatically the facility delivery-point measurement.

2. Define the product. Specify delivered quantity, response delay, sustained
   duration, ramp/restore requirements, reliability target, service constraints,
   penalty/liability and the minimum net useful benefit. No numerical product or
   threshold is invented in this gate. A sampled low-load instant is insufficient.

3. Establish the instrument contract. Supply calibrated readings or interval
   energy with exact interval boundaries, UTC clock synchronization and error/lag
   bounds, instrument firmware/configuration versions, and documented validity
   flags. Pair power with telemetry freshness/communication health. If off-state
   classification is used, validate it against a physical state signal; do not
   use scheduler drain or absent data as its substitute. Off may still consume
   standby power at the relevant boundary.

4. Acquire complete prespecified windows. Retain all fixed equipment over the
   14-day training and 14-day holdout design with explicit missingness. Use a
   versioned, hashed export with known storage costs; freeze product, estimator,
   uncertainty procedure and exposure ledger before holdout outcomes. Preserve
   April/May exposure. June is presently only a metadata-inspected candidate.
   Any redesign, source change, cap increase or holdout replacement is explicit.

5. Identify the actual institution. Name independently controlled owners and
   affiliates, their firm capacity entitlements and observable withdrawal rights,
   feasible joint outside operation, dispatch/restoration authority, binding
   commitments, costs and failure liability. Identify sponsor benefit and funding.
   Labeling subsets of one operator's nodes as owners does not supply these facts.

6. Validate useful output. For an AI claim, link workload class, completions,
   latency/service requirements, energy and dispatch decisions. Estimate net value
   including opportunity cost and operating/payment/penalty costs under one
   consistent accounting baseline. HPC telemetry alone is not AI-serving evidence.

A conservative missingness route worth testing once these prerequisites exist

Physical off classification is not logically necessary if every equipment load
has a validated upper envelope. At a single timestamp, suppose p_i lies in
[l_i,u_i], every load at the boundary is included, and capacity is C. Then

             sum_i l_i <= total load <= U := sum_i u_i.

If U <= C, at least C-U is certified unused electrical capacity at that instant.
For missing readings use a validated hardware/physical upper bound, not zero.
If U > C, these bounds certify neither positive headroom nor baseline feasibility;
actual load might nevertheless be below C. This is a failure to certify, not
proof that no headroom exists.
This is elementary interval bounding, not a new research theorem, dispatch
guarantee, additional useful compute, or pooling gain.

For a duration product the envelopes must hold throughout its interval, including
sensor error, sampling gaps and future load changes. A validated ramp envelope
could supply this; nominal cadence cannot. We have not established these bounds
for M100 and have not computed a claimed reserve from them. If conservative
envelopes leave no useful headroom, that is a legitimate stopping result.
