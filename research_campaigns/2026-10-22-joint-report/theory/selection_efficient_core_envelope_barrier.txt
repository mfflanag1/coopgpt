EVERY EFFICIENT-CORE SELECTOR'S OWN-REPORT ENVELOPE CAN COST LINEARLY
22 October 2026. Exact extension of the coordinator's norm-selector example.

THEOREM. For each k>=2 there is a fixed concave homogeneous Leontief-pool
technology with n=2k actual owners such that, for EVERY all-profile efficient-
core selection q, there EXISTS a report profile b with
 sum_i sup_{0<=a_i<=b_i} q_i(a_i,b_-i) >= 2+(k-1)/2,
while grand production is 2. Thus the funding factor is at least
(k+3)/4=(n+6)/8. The selection need not be symmetric, continuous, measurable,
unique, or chosen by any particular optimization. Randomized selections
interpreted through their expected allocations are included.

Fix k pools, each with two owners h_j and l_j. The scalar resource reports
are capacities, and production is the minimum of the k pool sums.
Let F_j be the profile in which pool j has capacities (1,1) and every other
pool has capacities (4,1), in the order (high,low). Let L_ij, for i!=j,
have capacities (1,1) in both pools i,j and (4,1) in all remaining pools.
All these profiles have production 2, and L_ij is obtained from F_j by only
owner h_i underreporting its capacity from 4 to 1. It is also obtained from
F_i by only owner h_j making that underreport.

The following exact core descriptions were established in
selection_contextual_failures.txt, section 2, and are repeated for clarity.
At F_j the efficient core is the singleton paying h_j=l_j=1 and everyone
else zero. At L_ij the efficient core consists precisely of allocations
paying (lambda,lambda) to pool i, (1-lambda,1-lambda) to pool j, zero to all
other owners, where 0<=lambda<=1. To see the latter, a worth-2 coalition
containing all four small-pool owners and all remaining high owners forces
the remaining low owners' payments to zero. The four worth-1 coalitions
containing one owner from each small pool and all remaining low owners then
force the four displayed pair inequalities. Efficiency makes their two
disjoint pair sums equal to 1, and solving gives the claimed interval.
Conversely each displayed allocation covers every coalition. The singleton
description is the analogous one-small-pool argument.

For any selector q, define a_ij=q_{h_i}(L_ij). The core interval implies
 a_ij+a_ji=1 for every unordered pair {i,j}.
At F_j its own-report envelope E has
 E_{h_j}(F_j)>=1, E_{l_j}(F_j)>=1,
 E_{h_i}(F_j)>=a_ij for every i!=j.
Therefore sum_owner E_owner(F_j)>=2+sum_{i!=j}a_ij. Summing these k
inequalities and using the pair identity gives
 (1/k)sum_j sum_owner E_owner(F_j)>=2+(k-1)/2.
At least one F_j meets the asserted bound. No symmetry argument or norm
objective appears in this averaging.

MORE GENERAL DOMINATION STATEMENT.
The same proof applies to any eligible mechanism M with the additional
property that at every report profile it coordinatewise dominates some
efficient-core allocation (the allocation may depend on the profile).
Choose such an allocation at each of the finitely many F_j,L_ij profiles;
own-monotonicity transfers the high-owner lower payments to F_j exactly as
above. This is an architecture restriction, NOT a condition imposed on the
original mechanism problem. Arbitrary covering subsidy vectors need not
dominate an efficient core, and no lower bound for them is claimed here.

QUANTIFIERS AND OWNERSHIP.
One fixed technology and the same 2k actual owners serve every comparison.
The family F_j consists of different full profiles of the same domain.
The bad index j can depend on the selector. Every comparison uses a genuine
one-owner underreport and leaves every other actual report unchanged.
No fairness floor, outside-option guarantee, or new mechanism requirement
is added to the original problem.
