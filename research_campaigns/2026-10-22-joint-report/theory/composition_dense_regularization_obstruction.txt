WHY DENSE LINEAR REGULARIZATION DOES NOT GIVE AN UNRESTRICTED IMPROVEMENT
22 October 2026. Exact parameterized obstruction; actual owners stay fixed.

Let G(B)=min{B_1,B_2} and for any M>0 put

  F_M(B)=M(B_1+B_2)+G(B)
        =min{(M+1)B_1+M B_2, M B_1+(M+1)B_2}.

All coefficients are strictly positive. The weighted-deficiency theorem
has w_1=w_2=M+1, kappa=(2M+1)/(M+1), and factor

  beta_M=1+1/(M+1) -> 1.

One might hope to subtract M sum_r b_ir from the resulting payments and
obtain a nearly efficient mechanism for the original G. This fails for
the bottleneck-threshold payments, even with two actual owners and along
one genuine own-report edge.

Fix owner 2 at b_2=(0,1), and let owner 1 report b_1=(t,0), with

  1 <= t <= (M+1)/M.

The bottleneck threshold is exactly

  tau_M(t,1)=min{max{(M+1)t,M},max{Mt,M+1}}=M+1.

Thus owner 1's original threshold payment is M+1 throughout this interval.
After the proposed linear subtraction its payment is

  M+1-Mt,

which strictly DECREASES from 1 to 0 when that owner's report increases
from 1 to (M+1)/M. Owner 2's adjusted payment is constantly 1. These
adjusted payments remain nonnegative and cover coalitions at the two
endpoints, so the obstruction is specifically the required own-report
monotonicity, not an issue of illegal reports or changing ownership.

The simplest exact instance is M=1, t:1->2, with the SAME technology
F=min{2B_1+B_2,B_1+2B_2} at both profiles.

Subtracting the baseline from GRAND MARGINALS does preserve monotonicity,
but gives precisely the old grand marginals of G:

  [F_M(B)-F_M(B-b_i)]-M sum_r b_ir = G(B)-G(B-b_i).

The improved factor for F_M does not imply a nearly efficient bound for G:
after subtracting the baseline, the bound becomes

  sum_i U_i^G <= beta_M G(B) + M/(M+1)(B_1+B_2).

The second term has no uniform bound relative to G over all real profiles.
For example take B=(t,1) and let t grow. Therefore neither subtraction
route converts the dense-form guarantee into a new unrestricted rate.

This does not rule out every conceivable regularization or transport
argument. It rules out these direct algebraic transfers with their exact
quantifiers, and avoids interpreting a ratio close to one for a large-
baseline technology as a small subsidy relative to the original output.
