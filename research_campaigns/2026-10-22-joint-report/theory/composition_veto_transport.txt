VETO SUBSTITUTION AND AN EXACT NONAMPLIFYING TRANSITION GADGET
22 October2026. Structural follow-up to composition_obstructions.txt.
Fixed actual owners, fixed technology, all-real own-underreport domain.
No generic LP search or numerical optimization is used in this note.

1. ALL-REAL VETO-SUBSTITUTION THEOREM
Let g:R_+^k->R_+ be a monotone concave homogeneous outer technology on k
scalar owners. In disjoint owner blocks j, let q_j be any admissible inner
technology with a distinguished actual owner c_j satisfying
  q_j(b_j with the report of c_j set to0)=0
for every nonnegative profile of that block. Thus c_j is a veto owner of
the inner technology, throughout the all-real domain. Define
  F(b)=g(q_1(b_1),...,q_k(b_k)).
This is admissible; for finite nonnegative min-linear g and q_j it remains
finite nonnegative min-linear, by distributing sums and minima.

THEOREM. If g has an all-real own-monotone coalition-covering mechanism
of funding factor beta, then so does F, with exactly the same factor beta.
Consequently the optimal all-real factor of F is at most that of g.

Proof. Set y_j=q_j(b_j), run the outer mechanism P(y), and pay P_j(y) only
to c_j. Pay every other actual owner0. A report increase by c_j increases
only y_j, so its payment is nondecreasing by outer own-monotonicity.
Every helper's own payment is identically0. These are all actual owners.

For any actual coalition S, let C be the outer indices whose distinguished
owner lies in S. Veto implies q_j(b_S)=0 for j outside C. For j in C,
monotonicity gives q_j(b_S)<=y_j. Hence
  F(b_S)<=g(y_C)<=sum_{j in C}P_j(y)=sum_{i in S}x_i(b).
Total payments are sum P_j(y)<=beta*g(y)=beta*F(b). This also handles
zero-output profiles. No regrouping of actual owners or simultaneous
report manipulation is used. The payment rule is defined on all real
profiles, not merely on a calibration grid. QED.

The theorem can be iterated through arbitrarily many layers of disjoint
veto-child substitutions without increasing the outer factor. Report
dynamic ranges, numbers of helper owners, and inner dimensions may all
grow. Therefore a transition architecture in which each abstract owner
is controlled by one actual veto owner cannot amplify a lower bound.

2. REPLICATED SUBSTITUTE HELPERS: A VALID APPROXIMATE TRANSFER
A natural alternative to a single frozen helper is
  y_j=min(u_j, sum_{r=1}^m v_jr),
where u_j belongs to one actual controller and the v_jr belong to m
different actual helpers. Fix every helper report at R, and restrict
controller profiles to0<=u_j<=R. Then y_j=u_j at the designated profiles.
For a macro coalition S, any actual coalition consisting of its controllers
plus ONE chosen helper in each selected group produces g(u_S).

Write a_j for controller payments and H_j for the sum of its helper
payments. Average coverage over all m^|S| helper choices to obtain
  sum_{j in S}a_j + (1/m)sum_{j in S}H_j >=g(u_S).            (1)
Controller payments satisfy the exact legal outer own-report comparisons:
changing u_j changes only actual controller j. Helper reports never change.
There is no assumption that helper payments stay fixed when controls vary.

If the full actual budget is at most beta*T, where T=g(u), grand coverage
in(1) and the budget give, writing A=sum a_j,H=sum H_j,
  A+H/m>=T,     A+H<=beta*T.
For m>1 this implies
  H/m <=(beta-1)T/(m-1).                                    (2)
Thus controllers alone satisfy every outer coalition constraint with an
additive deficit no larger than (beta-1)T/(m-1). This is a rigorous
payment-concentration estimate. It uses actual coalitions and legal own
comparisons, and it improves the cruder deficit beta*T/m.

For example, adding (beta-1)T/(m-1) to every controller payment repairs all
nonempty outer coalition constraints and preserves own-monotonicity, since
T is nondecreasing in every control. This produces a genuine outer-grid
mechanism with factor at most beta+k(beta-1)/(m-1). If every productive
outer coalition has at least s0 owners, divide that correction by s0.
This gives an approximate transfer of lower bounds as m grows, not a
multiplicative amplification. The all-real theorem in Section1 already
shows why amplification by this gadget alone is impossible: each controller
is a veto owner of its child.

3. AN EXACT PARAMETERIZED TRANSITION OPTIMUM
Take the outer technology
  g(u)=min(u0+u1,u2+u3),
and substitute the replicated-helper children above. There are4(m+1)
actual scalar owners and one fixed technology F_m. Let R>=2 be arbitrary.
Every helper reports R in all three profiles; controllers report
  P0=(1,1,1,1), PA=(R,1,1,1), PB=(1,1,1,R).
All three full outputs are2. R may grow arbitrarily with m. The complete
finite joint-report LP on these three profiles, including ALL actual
coalitions and ALL actual-owner comparisons, has exact optimum
  beta_m=(9m-7)/(8m-6)=1+(m-1)/(8m-6).                       (3)
In particular beta_1=1, beta_2=11/10, and beta_m increases to9/8.
Increasing helper replication or dynamic range does not multiply9/8.

LOWER BOUND, VALID WITHOUT HELPER SYMMETRY.
Use a_j^p,H_j^p for the controller and aggregate helper payments at p, and
A^p=sum a_j^p,H^p=sum H_j^p. The averaged coverage inequality(1) for
coalition{0,3} at P0, together with legal own-monotonicity, gives
  a0^A+a3^B >=1-(H0^0+H3^0)/m.
At PA sum(1) for{1,2},{1,3},{0,2,3}. At PB use{0,2},{1,2},{0,1,3}.
Their right sides are respectively1,1,2 for every R>=2. Adding all these
inequalities yields
  2(A^A+A^B)+(2/m)(H^A+H^B)
    >=9-(H0^0+H3^0)/m+(H0^A+H3^B)/m
    >=9-H^0/m.
Because m>=1 and A^p+H^p<=2beta at PA and PB, the left side is at most
8beta. For m>1, apply(2) at P0 with T=2 to obtain
  8beta>=9-2(beta-1)/(m-1).
Rearrangement gives beta(8m-6)>=9m-7. For m=1, ordinary grand-coalition
coverage gives beta>=1, equal to(3).

MATCHING PRIMAL FOR EVERY INTEGER m>=1 AND REAL R>=2.
Put delta=1/(8m-6) and v=1/2-delta=4(m-1)/(8m-6). At P0 pay controllers
  (v,1/2,1/2,v).
Pay EACH helper in groups0 and3 delta; all other helper payments are0.
At PA pay controllers
  (v,v/2,1-v/2,1-v/2),
and pay every helper0. At PB pay controllers
  (1-v/2,1-v/2,v/2,v),
and pay every helper0.

The only nontrivial actual own-comparisons are controller0 from P0 to PA
and controller3 from P0 to PB. Both preserve that owner's payment v.
All other actual reports remain fixed across the relevant comparisons.

To check all actual coalitions, a group produces its controller amount
exactly when both the controller and at least one helper are present.
Other partially present groups produce0 and add only nonnegative payments.
At P0 each complete group pays at least1/2, so every productive coalition
is covered by the efficient uniform-half outer allocation. At PA, a
complete group pays its controller amount from the displayed vector.
For a cross-pool pair using controller1 the payment is exactly1; a pair
using controller0 pays1+v/2>=1. Coalition{0,2,3} pays exactly2; adding
other groups cannot violate coverage, and the other coalitions have worth
at most these bounds. PB is its reversed counterpart. Thus every actual
coalition is covered, with no endpoint-only truthfulness assumption in
the lower argument: these are actual comparisons from the all-real domain.

The P0 total is2+2(m-1)delta=2beta_m. Each other total is2+v/2=2beta_m.
This proves the exact optimum(3). The primal is a finite-grid certificate,
not a globally factor-beta_m mechanism; the all-real substitution theorem
is a separate statement with a separate proof.

4. RELATION TO THE PROPOSED RECURSIVE FAMILY
For m=2, one child min(u, v1+v2) is obtained from f1 by setting one leaf
in its first pool to0 and using the other as u. Thus the m2 transition
appears as a literal restricted-profile family inside f2, with four extra
zero-report owners. Paying those extra owners0 gives the primal above;
discarding their nonnegative payments from the budget preserves the lower
bound. Its exact factor remains11/10, below the original9/8 calibration.
No ownership reassignment is involved in this embedding.

The exact general lesson is that a controllable scalar-output gadget can
repair the missing individual comparison edges and force payments toward
the controller, yet still fail to amplify. If the controller is a veto
owner, the global substitution theorem rules amplification out completely.
An escaping construction must use non-veto children and must explicitly
account for coalitions retaining productive partial children; ordinary
outer covering constraints only cover unions of whole children. This is
an additional obligation beyond growing report range and adding hybrids.

5. A SECOND FAILURE: PROPORTIONAL DISAGGREGATION NEEDS FRACTIONAL COVERAGE
Even additive children cannot automatically use a macro allocation.
At outer g=min(u0+u1,u2+u3), profile u=(4,1,4,1), the efficient outer-core
payment vector(3/4,0,13/4,1) covers every outer coalition and totals5.
Split outer owner2 into four additive actual owners each contributing1,
and divide its macro payment proportionally, giving each13/16. Keep the
other macro owners unchanged. The actual coalition consisting of owner1
and one such clone produces1 but receives only13/16. Coverage fails.
This exact example does not prove additive substitution amplifies any
lower bound; it isolates why an efficient macro core cannot simply be
disaggregated. A valid payment lift would require inequalities for partial
child outputs, not only the outer game's Boolean coalition constraints.

REPLAY AND STATUS
Run python3 theory/composition_veto_check.py. It verifies the exact
parameterized primal and lower-bound identity for many m and growing R,
plus the proportional-disaggregation counterexample. The proofs establish
the full parameter statements and the all-real veto-substitution theorem;
the checks are arithmetic diagnostics. No growing arbitrary-mechanism lower

legal hybrid report comparisons, and an explicit partial-child charging
