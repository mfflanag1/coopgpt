OWN-LOCAL ADAPTIVE WEIGHTS: VALIDITY AND A MATCHING SQUARE-ROOT BARRIER
22 October 2026. Complete arguments; no improved funding theorem claimed.

1. OWN-WEIGHT MONOTONICITY.
Fix the coalition game v and every weight except w_i. If w_i increases, then
the weighted lexicographic minimax cover payment to i weakly increases.
Proof. Compare the pinning algorithms with old weight t and new weight t'>t.
Before i first freezes in the old algorithm, all maximizing rows omit i.
Increasing its weight weakly lowers every incident residual row density and
does not affect off-i row densities. Therefore all preceding stages are
identical (including ties), until the old stage first freezing i. Let its
level be delta, frozen previous set F, and tight witness row S containing i.
Every new remaining row density is at most delta. Thus all eventual new
payments to j outside F are at most delta*w_j for j!=i, while payments to F
are unchanged. Feasibility of S forces
 x'_i >= v(S)-sum_{j in S\{i}}x'_j
       >= v(S)-sum_{j in S\{i}}x_j = x_i.
If the old payment is zero, nonnegativity suffices. This proves the claim.

Together with the own-incidence RHS monotonicity in
selection_weighted_lex_route.txt, it gives a precise adaptive invariant:
choose any functions w_i(b_i)>0, finite, componentwise nondecreasing, depending
only on owner i's own report (and fixed public technology/labels). Then
 x(b)=L_{w(b)}(v_b)
is covering and own-monotone on ALL real report profiles. To compare an own
increase, first increase incident coalition values holding weights fixed,
then increase only w_i holding the new game fixed. Each step weakly raises
the same owner's payment. A fixed, report-independent distribution of such
own-local functions also gives an eligible expected rule.

2. THE FAILURE GATE: OWN-LOCAL ADAPTATION STILL CANNOT BE o(sqrt n).
For every integer r>=1, set k=2r, n=rk+1=2r^2+1, and M=k^(r-1). There is one
fixed Leontief technology on d=(k+1)M resources with the following property:
for EVERY fixed probability mixture of arbitrary positive own-local weight
functions, there EXISTS a report profile at which its expected total payment
is at least (r+1)/4, although total production and minimum cover cost equal 1.
The bad profile may depend on the mixture, while the technology does not.
Monotonicity of the weight functions is not needed for this lower bound.

Construction. Partition resources into a root set R of size M and an outside
set Q=[k]^r of size kM. Use the symmetric fixed technology
 f(X)=min_{q in R union Q} X_q.
Define n binary report bundles: the root bundle is 1_R; for l=1,...,r and
j=1,...,k, the petal bundle is
 b_(l,j)=1_{ {a in [k]^r : a_l=j} }.
Every bundle has exactly M entries equal to one. A coalition has positive
production iff it includes the root and an entire petal P_l={(l,j):j in[k]}.
Indeed, without the root a root resource is missing; if every petal has a
missing j_l, the outside resource (j_1,...,j_r) is missing; if one full petal
is present every outside resource is covered. Every positive coalition has
production exactly 1 because only the root contributes to root resources.
Thus the complete cover polyhedron is the petal polyhedron from
selection_weighted_lex_obstruction.txt, and its exact total cost is
 (w_root+r*m)/(w_root+m), m=min_l sum_{i in P_l}w_i.

Randomize a bijection between actual owner labels and the n roles, and
independently apply one uniformly random permutation of all d resource
coordinates to every report. The technology f stays literally unchanged.
Fix any realized random seed determining all own-local weight functions.
For a fixed l, the root bundle and the k bundles in P_l partition the d
resources into k+1 disjoint blocks of equal size M. Under the resource
permutation they form a uniform ordered equal-block partition. Their owner
labels are an independent uniform ordered (k+1)-tuple of distinct owners.
Consequently the resulting k+1 numerical weights are jointly exchangeable,
even when each owner's local function is different and highly asymmetric
in resource coordinates. Strict positivity implies
 {w_root > sum_{i in P_l}w_i} subseteq
 {w_root > max_{i in P_l}w_i}.
Exchangeability bounds the latter probability by 1/(k+1); ties only help.
No independence between different petals is assumed. A union bound gives
 P(m>=w_root)>=1-r/(k+1)>1/2.
On this event the exact lexicographic cost is at least (r+1)/2. Thus the
average over role/resource permutations exceeds (r+1)/4. Integrating over
the fixed distribution of own-local functions, one fixed report profile
has expected cost at least (r+1)/4.

Since n=2r^2+1, this is Omega(sqrt n). It excludes an o(sqrt n) guarantee for
the complete own-local weighted-lex architecture, including randomized
functions, arbitrary fixed public resource scores, norms, and owner labels.

3. SCOPE AND NEXT CONSTRUCTIVE GATE.
The construction keeps one fixed f and fixed actual owners throughout. Its
profile averaging selects a bad full profile and does not pretend that role
permutations are underreports. No arbitrary-mechanism lower bound follows.
It does not establish a square-root obstruction in the resource parameter d,
which is much larger than n here.
Report-context-dependent weights can evade the exchangeability argument.
However, their eligibility is not supplied by the own-local invariant. A
valid constructive extension must prove that contextual changes of OTHER
owners' relative weights cannot decrease the reporting owner's selected
payment, and must beat this explicit Leontief petal family. Merely allowing
all weights to adapt to the profile is not a proof of that property.
