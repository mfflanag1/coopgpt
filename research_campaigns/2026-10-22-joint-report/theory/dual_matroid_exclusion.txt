MATROID BASIS TECHNOLOGIES WITH DISJOINT RESOURCE OWNERSHIP
22 October 2026. Structural exclusion; classical rank/greedy ingredients.

Let M=(E,r) be a matroid. Let
  f(z)=min_{B a basis of M} sum_{e in B} z_e.
Assume the resource set is partitioned into fixed owner blocks E_i; owner i
reports arbitrary nonnegative amounts only on E_i. This support restriction
is essential and is preserved under all permitted underreports here.

At a profile b, define H_i(t)={e in E_i:b_ie>=t}. For any S of owners,
the minimum-weight-basis layer-cake identity gives
  v(S)=integral_0^infinity [r(E)-r(E\union_{i in S}H_i(t))] dt.
Proof of identity: a minimum-weight basis greedily maximizes its intersection
with every lower-weight level set. The minimum number of selected elements
in a strict upper level H is r(E)-r(E\H); integrate the selected weights.
Changing strict to weak levels affects only finitely many t and no integral.

For fixed t, the H_i(t) are disjoint. Hence unions and intersections of
coalitions correspond to unions and intersections of their resource sets.
Submodularity of rank makes S -> r(E)-r(E\union_{i in S}H_i(t)) supermodular.
Integrating proves the whole coalition game v is supermodular.

Fix one owner ordering independently of reports, and let P_i denote the
owners preceding i. Pay
  x_i(b)=v_b(P_i union {i})-v_b(P_i).
These payments are nonnegative, sum to v_b(N), and cover every coalition:
supermodularity says the marginal increment after all predecessors is at
least the marginal increment after just predecessors belonging to S; sum
these inequalities over i in S. The payment is own-monotone because its
second term omits i and its first is monotone in i's bundle.

Thus this entire fixed-support report domain has an efficient eligible rule.
Exponential numbers of uniform-subset cuts, graphic basis cuts, or other
matroid basis cuts do not alone create arbitrary-mechanism hardness when
each resource has one owner. This conclusion must NOT be extended to
overlapping owner bundles: the disjointness step above then fails.

Converse at unit coordinate profiles: for an r-uniform binary row family A,
v(S)=min_{a in A}|a intersect S| is supermodular iff A is the full basis family
of a matroid. Indeed r'(S)=r-v(E\S)=max_{a in A}|a intersect S| is then a
normalized nondecreasing integer submodular function with r'(S)<=|S|, hence
a matroid rank function. Every row is a basis. Conversely any r-set B with
r'(B)=r contains a row a in A, and cardinality forces a=B. This does not
classify all domains with efficient rules; it only characterizes this convex
game argument.

STATUS. Useful exclusion, not a new general rate theorem. No priority claim.
