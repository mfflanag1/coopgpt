TWO CONTEXTUAL SELECTION CANDIDATES: EXACT FAILURE GATES
22 October 2026. These are architecture obstructions, not universal bounds.

1. SHAPLEY WEIGHTS IN WEIGHTED LEX ARE NOT OWN-MONOTONE.
Take fixed technology f(y,x,z)=min{y,x+z}. There are three owners: a root
reporting y=1+epsilon and two unit leaves, where 0<=epsilon<=1/4. Singleton
values and the leaf-pair value are zero, each root-leaf pair has value 1,
and grand production is 1+epsilon. The Shapley weights are
 w_root=(2+epsilon)/3,
 w_leaf=(1+2epsilon)/6 for each leaf.
The two pair-row densities are 6/(5+4epsilon), and the grand-row density
is 1, so the pair rows are maximizing throughout this interval (with a tie
at its right endpoint). Weighted lex therefore pays the root
 (4+2epsilon)/(5+4epsilon).
This strictly decreases from 4/5 to 3/4 as its own report increases. Thus
using an efficient, own-monotone contextual score vector as the weights is
not enough: the other owners' changing relative weights can reverse the
own-payment comparison. There is no supporting-price issue in this example.

2. EFFICIENT-CORE MINIMUM-NORM ENVELOPE HAS LINEAR FUNDING COST.
Construction supplied by the coordinator and independently verified here.
For k>=2 use n=2k owners in k disjoint pools, two scalar-capacity owners per
pool. The fixed production technology is the minimum of the k pool totals.
For each profile b let q(b) minimize sum_i x_i^2 over the full efficient core
 {x>=0 : x(N)=T(b), x(S)>=v_b(S) for all S}.
Its actual own-report envelope is E_i(b)=sup_{0<=a_i<=b_i}q_i(a_i,b_-i).

Full profile: pool0 has capacities (1,1); every other pool has capacities
(4,1), whose owners are called high and low. Here T=2. A coalition taking
both pool0 owners and the high owner from each other pool produces 2.
Efficiency and nonnegativity force every other pool's low owner to receive
zero in every core allocation. Next, either one pool0 owner together with
all these low owners forms a worth-1 coalition. Each pool0 owner must
therefore receive at least 1. Efficiency forces the unique core allocation
to be (1,1) on pool0 and zero elsewhere.

For any other pool j, let only its high owner underreport from 4 to 1.
There are now exactly two (1,1) pools, pool0 and poolj, while T remains 2.
A coalition taking all four owners of these small pools and the high owner
of each remaining pool produces 2, forcing all remaining low payments to
zero. Each of the four coalitions consisting of one owner from each small
pool plus all remaining low owners produces 1. Their pair constraints imply
the four small-pool owners receive at least 2 in total, so all remaining
high payments also vanish. With total exactly 2, the four pair constraints
are equivalent to the allocations
 (lambda,lambda) on pool0, (1-lambda,1-lambda) on poolj,
 0<=lambda<=1.
These allocations do cover every coalition: any productive coalition
contains an owner from each small pool, and a worth-2 coalition contains
both owners from each of them. The unique squared-norm minimizer has
lambda=1/2. In particular the underreporting high owner receives 1/2.

Consequently, at the original full profile,
 sum_i E_i >= 2+(k-1)/2,
 (sum_i E_i)/T >= (k+3)/4 = (n+6)/8.
All comparisons hold on one fixed technology with the same actual owners,
and all are constant-production (T=2) underreport comparisons. This rules
out a universal O(sqrt n) bound for this efficient-core envelope architecture.
It is stronger than a variable-grand-value issue. It also refutes the
constant-T pointwise hypothesis E_i<=C*q_i(full) for every finite C, since
the original high-owner payment is zero. The squared envelope satisfies
sum_i E_i^2>=2+(k-1)/4, so a bound sum_i E_i^2<=T^2 fails for k>=10.

This does not rule out arbitrary efficient-core selections with different
tie behavior, or a genuinely joint all-profile construction. The first
candidate fails eligibility; the second is eligible after enveloping but
fails funding. Neither yields a general mechanism lower bound.
