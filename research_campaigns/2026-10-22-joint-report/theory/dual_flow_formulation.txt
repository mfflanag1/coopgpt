FINITE JOINT-REPORT DUAL AS OWNERWISE UPWARD TRANSPORT
22 October 2026. Standard LP/flow consequence; no novelty or rate claim.

Let P be a finite collection of profiles of one fixed admissible production
function with fixed actual owners. Write T_p=v_p(N). For each owner i, put a
directed edge p->q whenever p_-i=q_-i and p_i<=q_i coordinatewise. Adjacent
cover relations suffice if their transitive closure contains all comparisons.
The finite mechanism LP minimizes beta>=0 over x_ip>=0 subject to
  sum_{i in S} x_ip >= v_p(S),
  x_iq-x_ip >= 0 for every owner-i edge p->q,
  beta T_p-sum_i x_ip >= 0.

Give these rows multipliers lambda_pS>=0, f_i,pq>=0, mu_p>=0, respectively.
Its dual maximizes sum_p,S lambda_pS v_p(S), subject to
  sum_p mu_p T_p <= 1,
  ell_ip + in_f(i,p)-out_f(i,p) <= mu_p,
where ell_ip=sum_{S containing i}lambda_pS. All signs follow by collecting
the coefficient of nonnegative x_ip in the primal Lagrangian. Finite LP
strong duality applies whenever the primal optimum is finite (it is: paying
each owner T_p is feasible and own-monotone because T_p is monotone).

TRANSPORT FORM. Flow can be decomposed into paths separately for each owner
and each same-others report fiber, eliminating cycles if redundant equality
edges were included. Equivalently, there exist tau_i(p,q)>=0, supported on
same-others pairs p_i<=q_i, with
  sum_q tau_i(p,q)=ell_ip,   sum_p tau_i(p,q)<=mu_q.
Staying at p is permitted. To see exact source equality despite balance
inequalities, discard unneeded source flow or route it directly to unused
capacity; more formally, use the bipartite source/destination max-flow test.
For any set A of source profiles in a fiber, its destination neighborhood is
the upward closure up(A). Hall's condition is ell_i(A)<=mu(up(A)). Since ell
is nonnegative, it is equivalent to checking all upper sets U:
  sum_{p in U}ell_ip <= sum_{p in U}mu_p.
This proves transport existence directly and hence the equivalence, including
partially ordered multidimensional own reports.

LOAD-BEARING INTERPRETATION. Coverage at p creates ell_ip load on owner i.
It may move upward only in that owner's own-report fiber. It cannot change
owner, move between other-owner configurations, or move downward. The same
mu_p is available as a separate capacity for each owner, while its objective
cost mu_p T_p is paid once. This reuse is the source of possible lower bounds.

Zero-output profiles do not create positive dual reward and cannot receive
upward transport from a positive-worth source: monotonicity gives T_q>=T_p.
Thus they can be deleted for positive-objective constructions, or retained
without changing the formulation. No endpoint realization claim is implicit:
all finite profiles must already belong to one actual admissible technology.

NO RATE CONSEQUENCE YET. The formulation alone is standard and fails the
campaign continuation gate. A useful lower construction must coordinate
coalition loads across many intersecting owner fibers; a useful upper proof
must charge those loads while preserving their source/fiber dependence.
Replacing all source-specific prices by a single concavity inequality loses
that dependence and only bounds the reward by f(sum_p mu_p aggregate_p),
which need not be comparable to sum_p mu_p T_p.
