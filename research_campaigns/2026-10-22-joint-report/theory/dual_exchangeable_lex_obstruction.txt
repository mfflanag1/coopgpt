EXCHANGEABLE WEIGHTED-LEXICOGRAPHIC COVERS HAVE AN n/log n OBSTRUCTION
22 October 2026. Exact architecture obstruction; NOT an arbitrary-rule lower.

THEOREM. Fix integers r>=1 and k>=1, and N=rk+1 owners. Give one owner u the
role of a veto, and partition the others into r disjoint petals P_1,...,P_r,
each of cardinality k. Consider the unit cover constraints
  x_u + sum_{i in P_l}x_i >= 1       (l=1,...,r),   x>=0.
For ANY exchangeable law of strictly positive finite weights W on these N
owners, let L_W be weighted lexicographic minimax: lexicographically minimize
the decreasing list x_i/W_i. Its expected total satisfies
  E sum_i L_W,i >= (r+1)/2 * p_{r,k},
  p_{r,k}=integral_0^1 (1-t^k)^r dt
         =sum_{j=0}^r (-1)^j binom(r,j)/(kj+1).
The law may depend on N and may have ties or arbitrary dependence. If
  k=ceil(log_2(4r)),
then p_{r,k}>=3/8 and
  E sum_i L_W,i >= 3(r+1)/16.
Since N=rk+1 and the minimum total cover is 1, this is Omega(N/log N).

PROOF OF THE DETERMINISTIC FORMULA. Put
  m=min_l W(P_l), a=W_u/(W_u+m).
The first lex level is 1/(W_u+m): the rows with minimum petal weight are
tight, forcing the veto and all owners in those petals to this level. In
particular x_u=a. Every remaining row is disjoint from the others apart from
the already frozen veto. Therefore its remaining owners have ratios equal
to (1-a)/W(P_l), and its petal payment sum is 1-a. These levels do not exceed
the first level because W(P_l)>=m. Hence the entire solution is
  x_u=a,  x_i=(1-a)W_i/W(P_l) for i in P_l,
and its total is exactly
  F(W)=a+r(1-a)=(W_u+r m)/(W_u+m).
On W_u<=m, F(W)>= (r+1)/2 (for r=1 equality is automatic).

PROOF OF THE LAW-INDEPENDENT EVENT. Give ties independent continuous random
tie breakers and order by (W_i,tie_i). Exchangeability implies the resulting
rank permutation is uniform. The rank event
  rank(u)<=max_{i in P_l}rank(i) for every l
implies W_u<=max_{i in P_l}W_i<=W(P_l) for every l, and hence W_u<=m.
Its probability depends only on a uniform permutation. Generate such a
permutation by independent Uniform(0,1) auxiliaries U_i. Conditional on U_u=t,
each petal has some U_i>=t with probability 1-t^k, independently across
petals. This gives p_{r,k}. No coupling between U_i and the actual W_i is
claimed or needed: only their rank permutations have the same law.
For the stated k, the sufficient auxiliary event U_u<=1/2 and every petal
containing some U_i>=1/2 has probability
  (1/2)(1-2^{-k})^r >= (1/2)(1-r2^{-k}) >= 3/8,
using the union bound / Bernoulli inequality. This proves the theorem.

EXPLICIT PRODUCTION REALIZATION. Use d=N resources y and z_lj, one for each
owner, and the single fixed technology
  f(y,z)=min{y, sum_{l=1}^r min_{1<=j<=k} z_lj}.
It is finite, nonnegative, monotone, concave, and homogeneous of degree one.
For example it is the minimum of the y-coordinate linear form and all k^r
linear forms sum_l z_l,j_l, so it belongs to the exact manuscript class.
At the all-unit basis profile, owner u reports e_y and owner (l,j) reports
e_lj. A coalition has value 1 exactly when it contains u and all members of
at least one petal; otherwise it has value 0. Therefore the full coalition
cover polyhedron is exactly the displayed r-row cover: all other positive
coalition constraints are implied by one of its minimal winning rows.
The grand output is 1. The profile and actual owners are fixed throughout;
no endpoint extrapolation or reassignment is involved. Weighted lex itself
is defined on every real profile, and this single profile disproves its
proposed uniform expected-budget bound under exchangeable weights.

ALL REPORT-INDEPENDENT LAWS, ON ONE FIXED TECHNOLOGY. The exchangeability
assumption can be removed when testing the mechanism on the full manuscript
report domain. Fix the displayed f and ANY positive finite weight law indexed
by the N owners, independent of reports; the law may depend on f. For each
bijection sigma from resource roles to actual owners, form the basis profile
in which owner sigma(a) reports the unit vector of resource role a. Average
expected funding over all sigma. Equivalently, keep the canonical basis
profile and randomly permute the weights among its roles. This symmetrizes
the law, so the theorem lower-bounds this average. At least one fixed basis
profile therefore has expected funding at least 3(r+1)/16. No owners change
inside a comparison or incentive chain; this is existence of one profile
among the profiles on which the same rule is required to work. Consequently
NO report-independent mixture of positive weighted-lex selectors has a
universal o(N/log N) funding guarantee, even if its weight law is chosen after
the fixed technology f is known. This applies to deterministic weights too.

Padding makes the rate valid for every N>=8, not just a parameter subsequence.
Take k=ceil(log_2(4N)), r=floor((N-1)/k), use rk+1 active roles, and add ignored
resource coordinates/owners to reach N. Such dummy owners always receive 0
under weighted lex. Average all owner assignments of active and dummy roles;
the induced weights on active roles are exchangeable. Since r2^{-k}<=1/4,
the same argument gives a bad fixed profile with expected funding at least
  3(r+1)/16 >= 3(N-1)/(16 ceil(log_2(4N))).
The production has d=N, ignoring the dummy coordinates, and unit output.

SCOPE. The comparator e_u is a static efficient nonnegative cover. Paying
all output to u is also an eligible efficient rule on the pure-coordinate
fixed-support subdomain, where only u can report y. That statement is NOT
extended to all of (R_+^d)^N: in the unrestricted manuscript domain other
owners can report y at other profiles. No arbitrary eligible-rule lower
bound is established here. On a restricted fixed-support domain with a known
veto owner, asymmetric weights can evade the canonical-profile lower bound;
that restricted domain does not contain all the basis profiles used above.
Report-dependent weights and selectors outside this mixture class remain
possible escapes, provided they preserve own-report payment monotonicity.

CONSEQUENCE FOR THE LIVE CANDIDATE. The proposed iid law W_i=U_i^(1/alpha)
is exchangeable for every alpha>0, even when alpha depends on N. Therefore
no alpha tuning gives an O(log N) universal bound for this selector. The
failure already holds with k=2 at order sqrt N: using t=(2r)^(-1/2) gives
p_{r,2}>=t/2, hence expected cost >=(r+1)/(4 sqrt(2r)). Growing k yields the
stronger N/log N obstruction above. This is a structural rejection of the
candidate distribution family, not a bounded numerical anomaly.

VERIFICATION / PRIORITY. The proof is elementary and exact. The separate
stdlib checker verifies small rational instances, the exact rank integral,
and the weight formula; these checks supplement rather than replace the
argument for all parameters. No historical novelty claim has been checked.
