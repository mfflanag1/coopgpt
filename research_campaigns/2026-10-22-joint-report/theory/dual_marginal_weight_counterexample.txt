MARGINAL-LOSS WEIGHTS FAIL OWN-REPORT MONOTONICITY
22 October 2026. Exact four-owner real-production counterexample.

Tempting adaptive law: M_i(b)=T-v_b(N\{i}), and use weighted lexicographic
minimax with W_i=M_i+epsilon. These weights incorporate other owners' reports
and correctly concentrate on a unique veto in simple games. Nevertheless
the selector is not eligible, even with strictly positive regularization.

Fix f(z)=min{z_1+z_2,z_3+z_4}. Owner i reports only resource i in the two
profiles below, so the comparison is a genuine unilateral increase with
fixed owners and a single monotone concave homogeneous technology.

At b=(e_1,e_2,e_3,e_4), T=2 and M=(1,1,1,1). Every weight is 1+epsilon.
All coalition values obey v(S)<=|S|/2, and the grand coalition attains this
ratio. Hence weighted lex pays x=(1/2,1/2,1/2,1/2).

Increase only owner 1's report from e_1 to 4e_1. Still T=2, but
  M=(1,0,1,1), W=(1+epsilon,epsilon,1+epsilon,1+epsilon).
For 0<epsilon<1/2, the highest first ratio is 1/(1+2epsilon), attained by
the coalitions {2,3} and {2,4}; they freeze
  x_2=epsilon/(1+2epsilon),
  x_3=x_4=(1+epsilon)/(1+2epsilon).
The largest remaining demand on owner 1 comes from {1,3,4}, of value 2,
giving
  x_1=2epsilon/(1+2epsilon)<1/2.
All coalition constraints are covered: the cross-pool pairs {1,3},{1,4}
need only epsilon/(1+2epsilon), the displayed {1,3,4} row is tight, and the
grand coalition has extra x_2. The other rows are implied or zero. The next
ratio x_1/(1+epsilon) is below the first level in the stated epsilon range.
Thus the freezing computation is exact and owner 1 loses from increasing.

For the clean rational choice epsilon=1/4, the two payment vectors are
  (1/2,1/2,1/2,1/2)   and   (1/3,1/6,5/6,5/6).
This is a strict violation with no zero-weight convention required. Raw
M weights, defined by forcing zero-weight coordinates to zero, also fail:
the second payment is (0,0,1,1).

The general valid observation remains: every efficient nonnegative cover c
satisfies c_i<=M_i, because c(N\{i})>=v(N\{i}). Hence M is itself feasible.
That static domination does not control how other owners' weights change
when an owner reports more. No funding or incentive conclusion follows from
it alone.
