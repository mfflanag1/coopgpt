Sharp subsidy for short unit-capacity flow games -- candidate conventional proof
3 October 2026. Fable supplied the T/3 proof and independently found the chain
family. Codex QA found proof-completeness issues; the coordinator supplies those
steps here and strengthens the finite bound. Final independent review pending.
Historical novelty is NOT established. The unequal-capacity conjecture is open
within this campaign and is not replaced by this special-case theorem.

THEOREM. Let a finite DAG have distinct source s and sink t, unit arc capacities,
positive maximum flow T, and every directed s-t path of length at most three.
Players are arcs; coalition value is maximum flow using its own arcs. Let H_e be
the maximum over the original entire core. Then
                 s_(1/3) <= (T-1)/3.
For every positive integer T this bound is attained by the chain family in
FLOW_CHAIN_CANDIDATE.txt. In particular the supremum of s/T is 1/3.
Parallel arcs are allowed and counted as distinct players. Unused arcs are not
removed from the game. No core=Owen-set characterization is assumed.

1. Core points and individual ideals.
For any minimum cut K, its capacity is T. Its indicator x^K has total T. For
every arc coalition A, the flow across this same vertex cut uses only A intersect
K, so v(A)<=|A intersect K|=x^K(A). Thus x^K belongs to the original entire core.
Fix any integral maximum flow f and decompose it into T arc-disjoint s-t paths;
acyclicity precludes flow cycles. Removing a used arc's entire path leaves a
feasible flow of value T-1. An unused arc can be deleted without losing flow.
For every x in the core, x_e<=T-v(N\{e})<=f_e. Hence H_e<=f_e<=1.
The deletion marginal is an integer in {0,1}. If H_e>0, it equals 1. Take a
minimum cut after deleting e. Its capacity is T-1. Reinserting e must increase
that cut capacity by 1 (otherwise the original maximum flow would be <=T-1),
so it is an original minimum cut containing e. Its indicator attains payoff 1.
Therefore H_e is exactly the deletion marginal, belongs to {0,1}, and H_e=1
iff e belongs to some minimum cut. This proves attainment using the entire core.

2. The first and last residual cuts.
Let R be the residual graph of f. There is no residual s-t path. Let S0 be the
nodes reachable from s in R, and let S1 be the complement of nodes that can reach
t in R. They satisfy s in S0 subset S1, t outside S1. Both sets are closed under
outgoing residual arcs. Consequently their outgoing original arcs are saturated
and their incoming original arcs carry zero flow. Conservation shows both cuts
K0=delta+(S0) and K1=delta+(S1) have capacity T.
Along any used flow path, membership of either set can change from inside to
outside but cannot re-enter: an incoming positive-flow arc would give a reverse
residual arc from inside to outside, violating closure. Thus each path crosses
each cut exactly once. If positions are k and l respectively, inclusion gives
k<=l. This proves the cut-crossing assertion explicitly.

3. Ideals vanish before the first and after the last cut on a used path.
Write a path P as n0=s,n1,...,nm=t, arcs e1,...,em, where m<=3. Let ek be its
K0 crossing. If k=1 there is nothing before it. Otherwise choose a simple residual
path R0 from s to n_(k-1). Every vertex on R0 lies in S0. It cannot traverse any
forward arc of P, all of which are saturated. It cannot traverse a reverse e_j
for j>=k, whose tail n_j is outside S0. It cannot traverse reverse e_(k-1),
which leaves R0's endpoint; simplicity excludes an earlier visit to that endpoint.
The only other possible reversed P arc is reverse e1 when k=3, and it enters s,
which a simple path starting at s cannot do. Thus R0 uses no arc of P in either
direction. Delete P's unit of flow and augment by one along R0 followed by the
suffix ek,...,em. All residual capacities on R0 remain sufficient, and the suffix
has just been freed. The two path pieces do not meet except at their junction:
R0 lies in S0 while suffix vertices after the cut lie outside S0. This gives a
value-T feasible flow avoiding e1,...,e_(k-1). Their deletion marginals and H vanish.
For the other end, reverse all network arcs, swap source and sink, and reverse f.
The new residual graph is the reversal of R. Its source-reachable set is exactly
the original set of nodes that residual-reach t, namely the complement of S1.
Its first cut corresponds to K1. Applying the just-proved prefix argument in the
reversed network proves H_ej=0 for j>l in the original network.

4. Two cuts leave at most one positive ideal per flow path.
An arc with H_e=1 belongs to f. If it is outside K0 union K1, paragraph 3 puts
it strictly between positions k and l on its used path. A path of length <=3
has at most one such position. Consequently the set
                 B={e:H_e=1, e not in K0 union K1}
has size r<=T. This is the structural lemma that improves the elementary bound.
It uses unit saturation in paragraph 3, not just the path-length bound.

5. A third cut yields the finite correction.
If r=0, x=(x^K0+x^K1)/2 is already a core point above H/3, so subsidy is zero.
Otherwise choose e in B. Paragraph 1 supplies a minimum cut K2 containing e.
Let z=(x^K0+x^K1+x^K2)/3, a core point. Every arc in these three cuts has
z_e>=1/3>=H_e/3. At most r-1 positive-ideal arcs lie outside their union.
Set y=max(z,H/3). This covers every coalition because y>=z, and adds exactly
1/3 for each of those outside arcs. Hence s_(1/3)<=(r-1)/3<=(T-1)/3.
Together with the chain family this proves the theorem.

SCOPE FAILURE FOR UNEQUAL CAPACITIES.
A serial three-arc path with capacities (2,2,1) is a unanimity game of worth 1,
so the entire core is the unit simplex and H=(1,1,1). Only the final arc is in
a minimum cut. Its Owen payoff (0,0,1), clipped to H/3, has total 5/3 and subsidy
2/3, while the true protected subsidy is zero at (1/3,1/3,1/3). Thus our residual
cut covering lemma and cut-only clipping construction do not extend as stated.
Splitting capacities into independent unit-arc players changes the core and H;
it is not an authorized equivalence. This example does not refute C2.
