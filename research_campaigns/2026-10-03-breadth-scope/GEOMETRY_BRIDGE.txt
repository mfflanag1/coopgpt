Candidate obstruction to an ideal-only bridge, 3 October 2026
Coordinator derivation; pending finite verification and independent review.

The geometry question is sourced in Vazirani 2402.11437v6, Section 11, item 1.
It asks for a characterization of leximin/leximax positions in the optimal-dual
polytope. Existing algorithms already compute those points. Our maxima-comparison
mechanism supplies constraints on H; a putative reconstruction from H alone fails.

Let A=[[4,0,0],[0,10,0],[0,5,0]], and let B differ only by B[1,2]=9
(one-based notation). Ordinary two-sided assignment games on three players each
side; missing edges have value zero. The diagonal matching has total 14.
A cover of total 14 exists for each game, so T=14 and its diagonal pairs are tight.
Write row payoffs (p,q,0), column payoffs (4-p,10-q,0).
For A the exact core is 0<=p<=4, 0<=q<=5. The cap q<=5 is the (3,2) edge.
For B add q-p<=1, from edge (1,2). These constraints are necessary and sufficient
by covering all nonnegative matching edges, then summing disjoint edge covers.
Both cores have H=(4,5,0;4,10,0): attain p=4 at (4,0), p=0 at (0,0),
q=5 at (4,5), q=0 at (0,0); all satisfy both sets of inequalities.
The respective minima are also the same: (0,0,0;0,5,0).

Both always-zero coordinates precede the positive coordinates in sorted order.
Among the others the minimum cannot exceed 2 because p+(4-p)=4. Both cores
allow minimum 2, forcing p=2. In A choose q=5, giving (2,5,0;2,5,0).
In B q<=3, so choose q=3, giving (2,3,0;2,7,0). These are their unique
leximin allocations. Thus identical total, diagonal values, coordinate maxima
and minima do not determine leximin. The coupling constraint matters.

This does not solve or refute the author's open question and does not establish
novelty of the example. It refutes only an ideal/extrema-only route. A useful
bridge would need to recover successive tight faces or additional coupling data;
our aggregate subsidy inequality by itself does not do that.
