Baseline derivations -- established ingredients, checked 3 October 2026
These checks are not new campaign contributions.

1. Core existence. For any nonnegative-capacity max-flow game, fix a minimum
vertex cut K and pay x_e=c_e on K, zero elsewhere. Its total is T. Every coalition
A can send at most the capacity of its own arcs crossing the same cut, which is
x(A). Thus this allocation is in the original entire core, even with unequal
capacities, zero-capacity arcs, and parallel arcs treated as distinct players.

2. Ideal budget. In a DAG choose any maximum flow f and a finite path-flow
decomposition. The total weight of paths using arc e is f_e. Removing all these
paths leaves a feasible flow of value T-f_e without e. Every core point x has
x_e=T-x(N minus e)<=T-v(N minus e)<=f_e, hence H_e<=f_e. Every s-t path has at
most three arcs, so sum_e H_e<=sum_e f_e<=3T. Arcs on no s-t path have f_e=H_e=0.
No claim is made that the first inequality is equality for unequal capacities.

3. Elementary subsidy. For x in the original core, 0<=x_i<=H_i. For 0<=alpha<=1,
(alpha H_i-x_i)_+ <= alpha(H_i-x_i). Therefore y=max(x,alpha H) covers every
original coalition and has subsidy at most alpha(sum H-T). At alpha=1/3 the
previous budget yields 2T/3. This baseline is excluded from the significance gate.

4. Path calibration. A single m-arc unit-capacity path is the unanimity game of
worth1. Its entire core is the simplex of nonnegative vectors with total1, so
each H_i=1. The only positive coalition is the grand coalition. Thus the exact
protected subsidy is max(0,m alpha-1). This proves that fixed positive alpha
cannot admit a size-independent bound when path length is unrestricted.
