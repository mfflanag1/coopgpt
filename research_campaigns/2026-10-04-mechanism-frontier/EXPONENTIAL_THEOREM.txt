OWNERSHIP-DEPENDENT EXPONENTIAL PAYMENTS -- 2026-10-04
Conventional theorem with model QA and independent rational-interval checks.
Historical novelty and independent human specialist validation are unresolved.

1. EXACT MODEL
Let N=L union R be a finite partition into nonempty owner pools. Each original
owner i has capacity c_i>=0 in exactly one pool. Put A=c(L), B=c(R), T=min(A,B),
and v(S)=min(c(S intersect L),c(S intersect R)). Capacities are real.
The ORIGINAL efficient real TU core is
 C(v)={x:x(N)=T and x(S)>=v(S) for every S subset N}.
Define H_i=max{x_i:x in C(v)} and M_i=T-v(N without i).
The bottleneck-pool capacity vector belongs to C(v), so C(v) is nonempty.
Singleton constraints imply x>=0; efficiency makes C(v) compact. For every x
in C(v), x_i=T-x(N without i)<=M_i; hence H_i<=M_i.

Payments are nonnegative functions of reported capacities. Utility is payment;
reports cannot exceed actual capacity; there are no operating costs. The required
incentive condition is monotonicity of each payment in its OWN report. Coalition
coverage uses the original v and fairness uses the original H. Ownership is fixed.
No group-report truthfulness, overreport robustness or cloning invariance is claimed.

2. RULE
If T=0, set every payment to zero. If T>0, let
 d_i=min(c_i,T).
Call a pool with total T short and the other long. Short owners are untruncated.
Write C=sum_{i in long} d_i, rho=C/T, u=rho-1, and p=(1/2)exp(-u).
Pay
 y_i=(1-p)c_i      for i in short,
 y_i=p d_i         for i in long.
When A=B, each owner's capacity is at most T, C=T, rho=1 and p=1/2. The
formula gives y_i=c_i/2 independently of which pool is called short.

THEOREM. For every such finite ownership instance and every real c>=0:
 (a) y_i>=0 and y(S)>=v(S) for every coalition S;
 (b) y_i is nondecreasing in c_i, keeping all other reports fixed;
 (c) y_i>=M_i/2>=H_i/2;
 (d) T<=sum_i y_i<=(1+1/(2e))T.
The upper subsidy constant 1/(2e) is attained by this rule at (1,1|1).
It is NOT asserted to be optimal among arbitrary mechanisms.

Consequently the unrestricted project minimax interval with original H/3 floors
is now [1/8,1/(2e)], approximately [12.5%,18.394%]. The exact 1/8 certificates are
retained and independently rechecked. The new upper bound also improves the
separate 2+2 bound 1/5 for the H/3 contract. That older rule protects 3H/5, whereas
this theorem protects H/2; no dominance at the stronger 3H/5 floor is asserted.

3. COALITION COVERAGE AND CAPACITY CAPPING
First C>=T. If any long owner has capacity at least T, its capped capacity is T.
Otherwise none is capped and C equals the long total, which is at least T.
Thus rho>=1 and 0<p<=1/2.

For every owner subset X, min(sum_{i in X}d_i,T)=min(sum_{i in X}c_i,T).
Indeed, if X contains an owner of capacity>=T, both sides equal T; otherwise
all capacities in X are unchanged. Also v(S)<=T for every coalition, because
its capacity in the globally short pool is at most T. Therefore
 v(S)=min(c(S intersect L),c(S intersect R),T)
     =min(d(S intersect L),d(S intersect R),T)
     =min(d(S intersect L),d(S intersect R)).
The final equality uses d(S intersect short)<=T. In particular, capping preserves
the entire characteristic function at this fixed profile and does not split owners.

Now y(S)=(1-p)d(S intersect short)+p d(S intersect long), a convex combination
of the two capped totals, so y(S)>=v(S). Nonnegativity follows from 0<p<=1/2.
When T=0 every coalition value and every original-core ideal is zero, so all
claims about coverage and floors are immediate.

4. EXACT SUBSIDY IDENTITY
Since d(short)=T and d(long)=rho T,
 sum_i y_i=(1-p)T+p rho T=T[1+(1/2)u exp(-u)].
For u>=0 the derivative of u exp(-u) is exp(-u)(1-u), so its maximum is 1/e,
attained at u=1. This proves (d), including its lower bound.
At (1,1|1), the left pool is long, C=2, T=1, u=1, and payments are
 (1/(2e),1/(2e)|1-1/(2e)). Total payment is exactly 1+1/(2e).
Each cross pair receives exactly 1, so this is compatible with coalition coverage.

5. ORIGINAL-CORE FAIRNESS
For a short owner, M_i=c_i. Hence y_i=(1-p)c_i>=c_i/2=M_i/2.
For a long owner i, let a be the total of the other owners in its pool. Since
its pool total is at least T, M_i=(T-a)_+.
If a>=T, then M_i=0 and the claim follows from y_i>=0.
If a<T, every other owner in its pool is uncapped. Put m=d_i. Then
 C=a+m, M_i=T-a, M_i<=m<=T, and u=(m-M_i)/T.
Its payment is
 y_i=(1/2)exp(M_i/T) m exp(-m/T).
The derivative of t exp(-t/T) on [0,T] is exp(-t/T)(1-t/T)>=0. Since m>=M_i,
 y_i>=(1/2)exp(M_i/T) M_i exp(-M_i/T)=M_i/2>=H_i/2.
We used H_i<=M_i as a bound, not as a replacement definition of H_i.

6. OWN-REPORT MONOTONICITY
Fix an owner in L. Let x be its report, a the fixed sum of the other L capacities,
and B the fixed right total. If B=0, every payment is zero for all x.
Assume B>0 and put A=x+a.

LONG REGION A>=B: T=B is fixed. Let alpha=sum_{k in L without i}min(c_k,B),
which is fixed as x varies, and m=min(x,B). The own payment is
 y_i(x)=(1/2)exp(1-alpha/B) m exp(-m/B).
The last factor is nondecreasing in m on [0,B], and m is nondecreasing in x.
This also handles the kink at x=B and the zero report of a redundant owner.

SHORT REGION 0<A<=B: define
 rho(x)=sum_{j in R}min(c_j/A,1), p(x)=(1/2)exp(1-rho(x)).
The own payment is y_i(x)=x[1-p(x)]. It is continuous on this region and C^1
away from the finitely many points A=c_j. On each smooth interval,
 rho'(x)=-sum_{j:c_j<A}c_j/A^2,
so 0>=rho'(x)>=-rho(x)/A. Since p'=-p rho' and 0<=x<=A,
 y_i'=1-p+x p rho' >=1-p(1+rho).
For rho>=1, h(rho)=(1/2)(1+rho)exp(1-rho) has h(1)=1 and
h'(rho)=-(1/2)rho exp(1-rho)<=0. Thus y_i'>=0 on each smooth interval.
Continuity and the finite number of kinks give monotonicity throughout this region.

SEAM A=B: no owner on either side exceeds T=B; both formulas pay x/2. Thus
monotonicity holds across the change of bottleneck. At A=0, necessarily x=a=0;
the assigned payment 0 is at most every later nonnegative payment. This covers the
remaining boundary. The argument applies symmetrically to owners in R. Under
binding underreports/no costs, truthful reporting is therefore a dominant strategy.

7. WHY THIS AVOIDS THE EARLIER OBSTRUCTIONS
The capped long total depends on individual ownership. For example, (2|1) and
(1,1|1) have identical raw pool totals but different capped totals and different
payments. Long owners' payments saturate when their own capacity reaches T.
Hence this is not a per-unit rule whose prices depend only on raw pool totals,
nor a scalar multiple of individual Shapley. The earlier 50% and 100% restricted-
class lower bounds do not apply to this rule. This is a direct class comparison,
not an assertion that those earlier results were incorrect.
