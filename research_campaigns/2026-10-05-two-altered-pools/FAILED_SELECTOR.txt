WHY SELECTING JUST THE MINIMUM POOL FAILS
Independent Codex attempt, opposite-model Fable QA, and direct exact check.

Choose an altered pool with minimum total, break ties deterministically, and use
its R^j vector on all owners. Coverage and budget <=2T follow from the auxiliary
profile argument in TWO_POOL_THEOREM.txt. The zero/one-altered boundary is retained.
But a change of the selected pool violates own-report monotonicity.

Take k=4,r=2. Two pools A,B both have reports (1/2,1/2); the others are all unit.
Whichever altered pool wins the tie, choose one of its low owners. It receives
1/4. Raise its report to 1. The other altered pool now has strictly smaller total,
so is selected, and the changing owner receives 1/[2(k-1)]=1/6.
Both endpoints STILL have two altered pools, and this is an allowed single-owner
increase. Thus every deterministic tie rule has such a failure. The same example
works for k>=4. No impossibility of all two-pool assignments follows.

The final product-weight construction repairs this exact edge: its changing owner
receives 5/24 before and 5/24 after. verify_two_pools.py checks both statements.
The independent QA briefly described the second endpoint as one altered pool;
that incidental description is incorrect and is corrected here. Its arithmetic
and main conclusion, as well as the independent delegate's statement, are valid.
