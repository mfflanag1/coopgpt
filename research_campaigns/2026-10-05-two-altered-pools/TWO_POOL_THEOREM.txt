TWO ALTERED POOLS AT THE SAME FACTOR TWO
2026-10-05 -- conventional coordinator proof, independently reviewed

STATEMENT
For every k>=2,r>=2, the complete labeled family in CONTRACT.txt admits a
nonnegative covering payment assignment, monotone along all retained own-report
increases, with total payment <=2T. It agrees EXACTLY with the earlier nonlinear
assignment on the zero/one-altered boundary. Max with M_i/k gives the original
H_i/k floor and total payment <=3T. This is NOT an all-real-report mechanism.

DEFINITIONS
For 0<=h<r define
 T_h=1+(r-1)*h/r, delta_h=(r-h)/r, z_h=T_h/r,
 a_h=(delta_h-z_h)_+/(r-h), b_h=1-z_h, c_h=z_h/(k-1).
An altered pool with h unit owners has r-h low owners, each capacity 1/r.
For such pool j define the vector R^j on ALL owners: give its own low owners
 a_h, its own unit owners b_h, and every owner outside j the SAME amount c_h,
even when some of those other owners have low reports. Its total cost is
 F_h=h*b_h+(r-h)*a_h+(k-1)*r*c_h.

For 0<=s<=r-2 define q_s=a_s/b_{s+1}; the denominator is strictly positive.
For 0<=g<h<r put
 e(h,g)=(1/2)*product_{s=g}^{h-1}q_s.
The assignment is:
 * No altered pool: pay every owner 1/k.
 * One altered pool j: use R^j, exactly as before.
 * Two altered pools with the same h: use (R^j+R^l)/2.
 * Two altered pools with h>g: give the h-pool vector weight e(h,g), and
   the g-pool vector weight 1-e(h,g).
T=T_g when h>=g. No division by a vanishing q_s is ever used; a zero product
simply assigns zero weight to the more advanced pool.

LEMMA 1: EACH R^j COVERS THE ACTUAL TWO-POOL-ALTERED GAME
Temporarily set every report outside j to 1, keeping j unchanged. On this
auxiliary profile the previous one-pool covering proof applies to R^j.
Every actual outside report is <=1, so every actual coalition value is <= its
auxiliary value, while R^j itself is unchanged. Hence R^j covers every actual
coalition. Nonnegative convex combinations preserve this property.
For completeness, if a coalition takes b unit owners and low capacity s from
j, and n is the minimum selected-owner count outside j, its auxiliary worth is
min(b+s,n). Its payment is >=b*(1-z_h)+s*(1-z_h/delta_h)_++n*z_h.
If n<=b this is >=n; if n>=b+1 it is >=b+s since s<=delta_h. Thus all labeled
coalitions, including ones omitting pools, are covered.

LEMMA 2: PAYMENT RATIOS AND SINGLE-POOL COST
For 0<=s<=r-2, direct substitution yields
 q_s = (1/(r-1))*[1-s**2/((r-s)*(r-s-1))]_+.
Consequently 0<=q_s<=1/(r-1)<=1, and b_{s+1}>=(r-1)*a_s.
Also a_{r-1}=0. As in the earlier proof, F_h<=2*T_h.

We will use the slightly more precise facts:
 (i) 2*T_g-F_g >=1/r for every integer 0<=g<r;
 (ii) F_h-F_g <=2*(h-g) whenever h>g;
 (iii) if a_g>0, F_g=T_g*(2-(g+1)/r).
To prove these, extend h to a real x in [0,r), using the same definitions.
Where delta_x>=z_x,
 F(x)=T_x*(2-(x+1)/r), so 2*T_x-F(x)=T_x*(x+1)/r>=1/r,
 and F'(x)=((r-1)/r)*(2-(x+1)/r)-T_x/r<=2.
Where delta_x<=z_x,
 F(x)=x+(r-x)*T_x/r and F'(x)=2*(r-1)/r*(1-x/r)<=2.
For (i) at an integer g in the second region,
 2*T_g-F_g=(T_g-g)+g*T_g/r=(r-g)/r+g*T_g/r>=1/r.
The formulas agree at their transition, so their derivative bounds prove (ii).
They also immediately prove (iii), including equality cases by continuity.

LEMMA 3: EVERY RETAINED OWN-REPORT EDGE IS MONOTONE
An increase changes one low report to 1, so its pool count advances h to h+1.
Other owners need not retain their payments; the proof imposes no such condition.
All edges with two altered pools have the following four forms.

A. Tied counts s,s become s+1,s, with s<=r-2.
The changing owner's old payment is (a_s+c_s)/2. Its new payment is
 e(s+1,s)*b_{s+1}+(1-e(s+1,s))*c_s
 =a_s/2+(1-e(s+1,s))*c_s >=(a_s+c_s)/2,
since e(s+1,s)=q_s/2<=1/2.

B. The changing pool is already ahead: h>g and h<=r-2.
Write e=e(h,g), e'=e(h+1,g)=e*q_h<=e. Its old payment is
 e*a_h+(1-e)*c_g. Its new payment is
 e'*b_{h+1}+(1-e')*c_g=e*a_h+(1-e')*c_g, which is at least as large.
This equality also holds when a_h=0 or the earlier product has become zero.

C. The changing pool is behind: g<h and g<=r-2.
Let e=e(h,g). If g+1<h set e'=e(h,g+1); if g+1=h set e'=1/2.
The product definition gives e=e'*q_g<=e'<=1/2, even if q_g=0.
The changing owner's old payment is (1-e)*a_g+e*c_h; its new payment is
 (1-e')*b_{g+1}+e'*c_h.
For r>=3, b_{g+1}>=(r-1)*a_g>=2*a_g, so the new own-pool term is >=a_g,
while the old own-pool term is <=a_g; the external term also increases.
For r=2 the only such pair is g=0,h=1, with q_0=1 and e=e'=1/2;
b_1=a_0=1/4, so equality holds.

D. The changing pool's last low owner becomes unit: h=r-1 -> r.
Its own R^j payment before the change is a_{r-1}=0. If the other altered pool
has count g, the old payment is a weight <=1 times c_g; after deletion of this
altered pool it is exactly c_g under the old one-pool rule. Thus it increases.
This includes a tie at r-1. The last-low edge from one altered pool to none
has old payment zero and new payment 1/k.

For the remaining one-altered internal edges, the old proof gives a_s<=b_{s+1}
(the ratio bound suffices), and the boundary assignment is identical. There are
no other directed one-report increases inside this binary profile family.

THEOREM BUDGET PROOF
One or zero altered pools were already bounded by 2T. Equal altered counts
also satisfy the bound, since the two vectors have the same cost F_g<=2T_g.
For h>g the cost is F_g+e(h,g)*(F_h-F_g). If the difference is negative, the
bound is immediate. Otherwise put d=h-g and use
 e(h,g)<=1/[2*(r-1)**d], F_h-F_g<=2*d.

Case 1: r>=4 and d>=2. Then the extra cost is <=d/(r-1)**d<=2/(r-1)**2<=1/r,
which is paid for by Lemma 2(i). The middle sequence decreases for d>=2;
the last inequality is 2r<=(r-1)**2, valid for r>=4.

Case 2: d=1 and g>=1. If a_g=0 then e=0. Otherwise the slack is
T_g*(g+1)/r>=2/r, whereas the extra cost is <=1/(r-1)<=2/r.

Case 3: d=1,g=0,r>=3. Here e=1/[2*(r-1)], F_0=2-1/r,
F_1-F_0=(2r-1)*(r-2)/r**2. The extra cost is at most 1/r because
(2r-1)*(r-2)<=2r*(r-1). This is precisely the available slack at g=0.

Case 4: the two small exceptions. For r=2,h=1,g=0, the cost is
(F_1+F_0)/2=(7/4+3/2)/2=13/8<=2*T_0.
For r=3,h=2,g=0, q_0=1/2,q_1=1/4, e=1/16,
F_0=5/3,F_2=25/9, hence the cost is 5/3+(1/16)*(10/9)=125/72<2*T_0.
These cases exhaust all integer r>=2,0<=g<h<r, proving total payment <=2T.

FAIRNESS AND SHARPNESS OF THIS BOUNDARY-PRESERVING ASSIGNMENT
Let M_i=T-v(N without i) and replace each payment by max(y_i,M_i/k).
M_i is own-report monotone; max preserves this property on retained edges.
Every original efficient core allocation has x_i<=M_i. In a pool of total C,
M_i=(c_i-(C-T))_+ and sum_pool M_i<=T, hence the top-up adds at most T overall.
Thus the original H_i/k floor is obtained with total payment <=3T.
At the one-altered boundary h=0 the base cost is (2-1/r)*T, tending to 2T.
So the factor 2 is sharp for any assignment required to agree exactly with that
boundary, and for the specific assignment above. This is NOT a minimax lower
bound over all assignments allowed to change the boundary.

SCOPE
Both altered pools may change in any order, and the construction uses all owner
labels. Raising a capacity remains inside this family, but some underreports
would create a third altered pool and leave it; those deviations are not covered.
It is a proof for the complete binary profile family, not merely one
path or a numerical grid. Arbitrary intermediate real reports, three or more
altered pools, arbitrary owner counts per pool, and the unrestricted constant
question remain unresolved by this theorem. No historical novelty claim.
