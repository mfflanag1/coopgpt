What carries over

Our cooperative-game work asks how a joint surplus can be divided so that no subgroup prefers its own alternative. The newer mechanism work also asks whether the rule remains incentive-compatible as actual owners reduce their contributions, possibly together. Ownership-preserving constructions matter because splitting a bank or infrastructure owner into fictional agents can change its incentives.

Those ideas fit cash, collateral, capacity and enforceable contract rights. They do not yet supply bankruptcy law, debt pricing, private-information mechanisms or dynamic commitment. The graph funding constants cannot be inserted directly into a credit spread or regulatory-capital formula.

1. Collateral has joint value. Who receives it?

Closest mathematical fit

Imagine several institutions controlling different eligible securities, cash or optional intermediation capacity. Together they can support useful transactions that none can support alone. The practical question is whether the rewards for contributing those resources keep the arrangement attractive when one owner, or a coalition, contributes less.

The first model should concern an actual optional resource-pooling arrangement, with public eligibility rules, haircuts and fees. A transparent production benchmark is:

F(c) = max pᵀx subject to Ax ≤ c, x ≥ 0.

Here each column is a genuinely feasible activity, each row a binding resource, and p measures transferable net revenue. Repo principal is not that revenue. Static core allocation in linear production is classical; our possible contribution concerns a single rule across contribution profiles and joint partial withdrawals.

Candidate result. Characterize how eligibility or substitution structure controls the funding required for coalition-stable, withholding-resistant rewards. Alternatively prove an unrestricted impossibility under a financially meaningful contract. An immediate application of an existing theorem would be a useful corollary.

Institutional boundaries. An institution can withhold optional, unencumbered resources before a stated cutoff. It cannot erase an existing liability, reuse segregated collateral, or evade a binding clearing obligation. A platform-owned indispensable resource can also trivialize the coalition model; that must be checked at the outset.

Budget. If an applicable theorem gives total rewards at most KF, and F is available cash revenue, the outside contribution is bounded by (K−1)F. This is a conditional theorem bound. It is not an estimate of a taxpayer subsidy or social loss.

Prior work and data. Fleming and Keane already analyze Treasury clearing/netting benefits. Garratt studies cooperative liquidity-cost allocation and voluntary acceptance; his discussion notes that a Shapley allocation need not lie in the core. The OFR repo counterfactual holds underlying trades fixed. That leaves an interesting participation question, but does not establish novelty for our proposed mechanism.

Public OFR data support segment-level descriptions, not reconstruction of every institution's positions and legal rights. The current SEC implementation page gives December 31, 2026 for eligible cash transactions and June 30, 2027 for eligible repo transactions. A model must respect the applicable scope rather than assume clearing is always voluntary.

Stop if: the permitted deviation is fictitious, public fixed prices already solve the incentive problem, or a claimed empirical exercise needs an unavailable owner-level network.

2. Debt secured on pieces of an AI project

Recommended application priority

A functioning AI facility needs more than equipment: deliverable power, a usable site, cooling and connectivity, and customers whose contracts actually generate revenue. The owner of each piece can differ from the borrower, guarantor or creditor. Distress can leave valuable hardware without the rights needed to operate it.

The question: holding physical technology and demand constant, how much does enforceable control over the complete operating bundle change debt capacity and recovery?

Compare three arrangements: separate claims with limited step-in rights; a bundle of enforceable step-in rights and cross-default provisions; and unified ownership. The benefit of bundling is a hypothesis. Cross-default can also transmit trouble across assets, so the sign should come out of the model.

A first model has financing at date 0, a readiness/demand shock and explicit renegotiation or enforcement at date 1, and cash flow or recovery at date 2. It must identify actual ownership, legal withdrawal options, replacement costs, seniority and the lender's participation condition. A minimum of physical inputs is only an operating-capacity approximation after conversion to common service units.

Possible contribution. A many-owner theorem identifying which sets of enforceable rights are sufficient to preserve operating value, or an exact example showing that a familiar covenant package fails once several complementary owners interact. The broader ambition would be a characterization of financeable surplus under fragmented control.

A budget issue we caught: the current production theorem's grand-coalition coverage already requires owner payments of at least the full production value F. Paying a separate creditor from that same F cannot simply be added. Creditors must enter the ownership/contract model, or the relevant residual cash-flow technology must be rebuilt and checked.

What already exists. Hart and Moore already connect withdrawal and asset specificity to debt capacity. Van Nieuwerburgh's 2026 conference draft discusses AI financing architectures and execution risks. Gete and Mercader's preliminary 2026 paper examines data-center mortgage underwriting using proprietary CoStar data. “AI debt is risky” is not a contribution.

Grounded first deliverable. Trace one public financing through borrower, subsidiaries, guarantors, collateral, power rights, tenant termination and lender remedies. CoreWeave's 2025 10-K and incorporated credit exhibits are a possible starting point; complete site-level rights have not yet been verified. Public issuer disclosures do not automatically supply a clean project-level loan panel.

Stop if: the key rights are inaccessible, the result is ordinary two-party hold-up, or the model needs unjustified assumptions about breaching contracts. A source-backed case study and a clear data-insufficiency finding remain useful outcomes.

3. Who gets the value of a duration hedge?

Clean calibration · higher rediscovery risk

Suppose desks or institutions hold verifiable signed rate exposures. A common coherent risk measure ρ assigns capital to each combined portfolio. Let:

C(S) = ρ(Σᵢ∈S Lᵢ) v(S) = Σᵢ∈S C({i}) − C(S).

The second expression is the coalition's diversification saving. A stable cost allocation k satisfies k(N)=C(N) and k(S)≤C(S). Defining yᵢ=C({i})−kᵢ maps those constraints exactly to the profit core of v. This maps savings; fairness rules on raw charges need not survive the owner-specific change of coordinates.

A direct mathematical connection

With one common factor, Lᵢ=aᵢZ, write A₊=Σ max(aᵢ,0), A₋=Σ max(−aᵢ,0), and κ₊=ρ(Z), κ₋=ρ(−Z). Positive homogeneity gives:

v(S) = (κ₊ + κ₋) min{A₊(S), A₋(S)}.

This identity was checked algebraically. One-factor hedging benefits therefore have exactly the two-input minimum structure used in our work. It is an elementary calibration, not a proposed discovery.

The less routine candidate. Fix Z∼N(0,I₂) and a common Expected Shortfall tail probability, allow the number of actual owners to grow, and ask about the two specific opposing egalitarian core selectors from our existing paper. Can their normalized disagreement approach the unrestricted frontier? Or does this fixed geometry impose a stronger bound? These are proposed targets whose priority is still unconfirmed.

v(S) ∝ Σᵢ∈S ‖aᵢ‖ − ‖Σᵢ∈S aᵢ‖.

Classical boundaries. Csóka, Herings and Kóczy (2009) already connect general risk-allocation games to totally balanced games, and riskless aggregate portfolios to exact games. Csóka and Pintér's working paper already proves a broad incompatibility of allocation axioms. Those general results do not automatically settle a fixed Gaussian two-factor domain or our binding-withdrawal incentive contract.

False leads removed. A one-factor core can already have dimension growing with owner count; large core diameter does not show that our particular selectors differ. Also, for centered symmetric exposures with zero aggregate exposure, the Shapley allocation of savings is simply the standalone risk vector and lies in the core. Asking only whether that allocation is stable would not justify a campaign.

Public Federal Reserve yield curves can illustrate a specified factor model later. They do not reveal institutions' actual books, and a capital-allocation theorem is not a return forecast.

How this can become macro research

4. Margin liquidity insurance

Members commit cash or credit before stress, and subsequent calls arrive at different times. The useful question is whether a reserve mechanism remains viable when several members can reduce uncalled commitments before new information. A theorem must specify dates, recall rights and statewise service, and survive common shocks. Static subadditivity is only the starting benchmark.

The Bank of England's gilt-repo discussion paper highlights netting benefits alongside cash-margin pressures. Funding/market-liquidity feedback is already central to Brunnermeier and Pedersen. Our candidate would need a new commitment mechanism or sharp obstruction.

5. Rates and unfinished projects

A financed project may need several components ready before revenue begins. A minimal timing variable is:

τ = max{τpower, τfacility, τcompute, τcustomer}.

Compare τ with debt maturity and remaining cash runway. The hypothesis is that otherwise similar financing shocks have larger effects where refinancing precedes joint readiness and owners cannot coordinate completion. This could connect credit structure to delayed capital formation.

Production-network amplification and complementarity are already established themes in Baqaee and Farhi. A new result must isolate financing/control rights, then distinguish that channel empirically from leverage, demand, location and construction risk. An event study alone would not do that.

6. Fiscal guarantees for complementary investment

Would a limited public guarantee be more effective when it covers the missing power-delivery or completion obligation rather than subsidizing equipment? Under a fixed fiscal-risk budget, compare support packages for whole operating systems.

This connects our coalition compensation and robust bottleneck-policy work. It requires explicit external benefits, private surplus, guarantee payouts and crowd-out. Government infrastructure risk allocation and guarantees are established subjects. The new question would concern many-owner complementarity and incentive-compatible support, not merely making a project cheaper to finance.

7. Clearing architecture and monetary transmission

After solving the collateral problem, add constrained intermediaries and endogenous prices. Ask when a private allocation of netting gains releases balance-sheet capacity, and whether that changes repo rates, liquidity or policy transmission.

First separate private fee savings, regulatory balance-sheet treatment and real resource savings. Then supply an equilibrium model and a feasible identification design. The existing funding factor supplies none of those quantities by itself.

Longer-range possibilities: reserve/swap pooling across countries, sovereign restructuring coalitions, and utility/municipal financing. They introduce enforcement, political economy or institutional details far beyond the current contracts. They should wait until one closer application earns its place.

A bounded first campaign

My preferred applied starting question is: When do lender rights over complementary power and compute assets increase financeable cash flow, and when does bundling spread distress? It is a question, not an announced theorem.

  1. First 90 minutes: pick one public financing and build the ownership, cash-flow and enforcement map. Record every missing document. Do not begin a broad spread regression.
  2. Next 90 minutes: compare the proposed mechanism to Hart–Moore and the closest AI/project-finance papers. State the exact additional friction and observable prediction.
  3. Next three hours: build the smallest two-date model with an explicit lender participation constraint. Recover the classical benchmark, then attempt one new many-owner result or covenant counterexample.
  4. Final one to two hours: independent correctness review and a decision. Continue for a checked new implication plus a credible contract interpretation; otherwise retain the case study or record the obstruction.

If we prioritize immediate reuse of our coalition mathematics, run the equivalent gate on optional collateral pooling instead: actual rights first, original-source comparison second, mechanism/proof third. These are alternatives for the next commitment, not seven simultaneous campaigns.

A field-level outcome would be a reusable characterization of how fragmented control changes financeable surplus, or a general incentive-compatible allocation theorem with a real institutional interpretation. A relabeled minimum-production example would not meet that standard.
