Independent risk-allocation research: final disposition, 24 October 2026

Outcome: rejected as a complete research design. Retain only the checked source
map and elementary transformations. Candidate artifacts remain unchanged; this

Verified useful substance
Root inspected the free original Csoka-Herings-Koczy (2009) published PDF:
risk-allocation games = totally balanced games, and the riskless-aggregate subclass
= exact games. The savings-core affine transformation was separately derived in
ROOT_CALIBRATION_AND_BOUNDARIES.txt. This broad connection is classical.
Root also inspected Csoka-Pinter's 2010 working paper and Buch-Dorfleitner's
original paper. Their allocation-axiom impossibilities have explicit domain and
axiom hypotheses, and do not themselves establish truthful resource reporting
or impossibility on a fixed two-factor subdomain.

Why the proposed gate fails
The candidate proposes a modular-complement test as a decisive reason to abandon
a two-factor selector problem. Under a centered symmetric law with zero aggregate
exposure, put b_i=h(beta_i), A=sum b_i. Then
 v(S)-v(N\S)=2*b(S)-A.
Passing this necessary condition does not give a two-dimensional representation
or a separated-selector example. Failing it excludes only that candidate family.
Codex QA independently caught this same invalid gate. Indeed every exact game
whose core is a segment with endpoints u,w satisfies the same form with center
(u+w)/2: v(S)=min(u(S),w(S)). So this screen can pass in broad situations without
resolving the geometric question.

Further scope corrections
Fix the actual law, e.g. Z ~ N(0,I_2), a common ES tail probability, fixed actual
owners, signed exposures, and the two selectors from the project's exact-game
paper. Optimizing exposures is nonlinear; a fixed-book core test is an LP.
Do not equate a large core diameter or dimension with separation of those selectors.
The fixed-alpha representation sketch was not independently completed and is
not included as a verified result. Strong monotonicity is not the same incentive
contract as binding resource underreports.

Root also rejects the QA's substitute problem as a substantial next target
QA suggested asking whether the Shapley value is always in the core for five
centered Gaussian desks with zero aggregate exposure. It is immediate for every n.
Let C(S)=h(sum_{i in S} beta_i), with h even and nonnegative, and sum beta_i=0.
Then C(S)=C(N\S) and C(N)=0. For each i, pair a predecessor coalition S with
T=N\(S union {i}). Their Shapley weights are equal and their marginal costs
are opposite:
 C(T union {i})-C(T) = C(S)-C(S union {i}).
Thus phi_i(C)=0. For v(S)=b(S)-C(S), linearity gives phi_i(v)=b_i. This allocation
is efficient and coalition-stable because b(S)>=v(S). Hence the proposed M_5 is
zero, as is M_n for every n, with the empty/grand coalitions attaining zero.
This is an elementary calibration, not a novelty claim. It removes that substitute
from the shortlist; it does not solve the actual opposing-selector problem or
any across-report incentive question.

Revised disposition
The literature scout helped identify relevant sources, but did not deliver its
requested precise, decisive next gate. Original artifacts and opposite-model QA
are retained. The final user report uses the verified sources and transformations,
labels the two-factor selector question as provisional, and prioritizes collateral
participation and complementary-asset credit instead.
