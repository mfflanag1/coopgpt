Relevant project results (internal research; historical novelty/human review pending):
1. Efficient core fairness: for singleton-normalized exact TU games with n players,
opposing egalitarian core selections have normalized L1 separation approaching 2,
with 2-s_n=2/n+O(log n/n^2). Whole-core representations matter.
2. Matching/sector graphs: selected tree/cycle ideal-share and opposing-selector
results have precise different domains. Do not conflate resource and sector graphs.
3. Divisible concave homogeneous production F(c): actual fixed owners hold vectors
of nonnegative inputs; binding coordinatewise underreports only. Nonnegative
payments cover every coalition at every report. Full OUT means a coalition's
payment increase on restoring d is at least its independent production F(sum d).
Funding factor bounds total payments relative to total production F(c). It is not
a direct welfare loss, taxpayer cash cost, or capital requirement. Simpler TCWM
only prevents a coalition payment decrease. Private costs/values, risk and timing
are not covered automatically. Latest unit-profit P4/complete-blowup rule gives
factor1+1/sqrt2; arbitrary trees remain open. Tested leaf recursion/composition fail.
4. Reserve pooling: deterministic public factor vectors and firm capacity cone
give value t_sum - norm(x_sum), subject to both retained and removed subportfolios
remaining feasible. Economic ownership, rights and sponsor surplus must be real.

Potential risk allocation bridge to audit, not yet a novelty claim:
For a fixed public coherent risk measure rho on losses, C(S)=rho(sum_{i in S}L_i).
Capital charges k_i satisfy sum k_i=C(N), k(S)<=C(S) (cost core).
Define y_i=C({i})-k_i and v(S)=sum_{i in S}C({i})-C(S).
Then y is a profit-game core allocation of diversification savings. Establish
precise scope; no automatic transfer of a chosen fairness selector under unequal
shifts/sign changes. Few-factor interest-rate portfolios might be a genuinely new
restriction: do bad fairness extremes survive one/two public yield factors and a
fixed common ES rule? General arbitrary-state risk representation alone is classical.
