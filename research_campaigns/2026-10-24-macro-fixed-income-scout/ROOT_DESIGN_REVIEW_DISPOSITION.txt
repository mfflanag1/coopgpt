Rejected mathematical suggestion
The reviewer asserts that the general weighted one-factor glove game has a core
of dimension<=1 and that the riskless aggregate case leaves a segment. This is
false for fixed owners with unequal position sizes. Take n=m+1 actual owners:
m owners each have one positive unit and the final owner has m negative units.
Normalize the common positive savings scale to1. Coalition value equals the
number of positive owners in the coalition when the negative owner is present,
and0 otherwise. The grand value is m. Its entire core is
     0<=x_i<=1 for i=1,...,m; x_(m+1)=m-sum_i x_i.
Necessity: nonnegativity follows from singleton values; the coalition containing
everyone except positive owner i has value m-1, hence x_i<=1 by efficiency.
Sufficiency: a coalition of positive owners has nonnegative allocation; if it
contains the negative owner and positive subset T, its allocation is
m-sum_{i notin T}x_i>=m-(m-|T|)=|T|. All coalition constraints therefore hold.
This is an m-dimensional cube inside the efficiency hyperplane. Already m=2
gives a rectangle, contradicting the claimed segment. Its balanced aggregate
position is zero and the one-factor representation holds exactly.

Also reject inference from distant core endpoints to distant opposing egalitarian
selectors: in this example the equal allocation m/(m+1) is feasible. Both of the
project's opposing selectors choose it, so their distance is zero. Core size or
dimension alone does not settle the targeted fairness comparison. The proposed
few-factor research remains scoped to the actual selection objectives, not a
disproved dimension bound. No historical novelty is claimed for this calibration.
