DISPOSITION
PASS: no substantive mathematical error or missing hypothesis found in the
stated universal dimension equalities. The proof covers the full class in
the statement, including discontinuities between support faces. The full-OUT
lower is an implication from hypothetical uniformly funded arbitrary rules
on every finite pure-owner model; it is not a restriction of the original
mechanisms to price formulas. Two optional presentation clarifications are
listed at the end. Neither requires a new argument or changes a claim.

1. MODEL AND QUANTIFIERS
The section's D_d and D_d^OUT agree with the core model: every coalition is
covered and protected, all actual owner endowment boxes remain fixed, and
unchanged members of a coalition are permitted. The s in the finite TCWM
obstruction is a construction parameter, not a protected-size restriction.
All original payment rules can be arbitrary maps; regularity is proved only
for a derived price array after the finite-owner compactness argument.

The completion argument correctly handles infima that are not attained.
If the supremum of the finite models' infimal factors is below the claimed
constant, choose kappa strictly between them. Every model then has some
feasible factor strictly below kappa and hence a kappa-funded rule. This is
precisely the price-reduction hypothesis and leads to a contradiction.
Thus a finite model defeats every proposed subcritical uniform kappa.
No infinite-owner mechanism or strategic comparison between different owner
rosters is asserted. The explicit upper works for arbitrary fixed mixed
owners, while pure-owner minimum technologies suffice for the OUT lower.

2. PAYMENT-ONLY UPPER AND FINITE LOWER
The coalition contribution a_j/(a_j+o_j) times A_j(a+o) is nondecreasing
in every coalition coordinate. The support-face comparison is valid:
newly active coordinates add nonnegative contributions, and inserting a
nonnegative coordinate in an old integrand cannot lower its value.

At an interior differentiability point, the gradient majorizes boundary
values without a continuity assumption: the supporting inequality at x
for (1-t)x+tz and concavity along that segment imply
F(z)<=F(x)+g(x).(z-x)=g(x).z. Gradients are nonnegative. Integrating a
partial derivative from epsilon to one bounds its integral by the endpoint
value because the omitted lower endpoint is nonnegative. Monotone passage
to zero and Tonelli are legitimate. The two Jensen inequalities give the
factor (k+1)/2. Their means are in the positive relative face, so boundary
upper semicontinuity is not being assumed.

The finite obstruction's actual-owner counts and restorations are valid.
P_0 contains (d-2)s+2 active owners and s-1 zero owners, totaling
(d-1)s+1. P_1 restores only the latter owners in resource d-1. P_2 restores
the same coalition S within its original full unit cubes. All dropped
payments are nonnegative. With m=(d-1)s, the final constraints give
mp<=m delta/(m-1). Combining with
2b>=1-delta/(s-1) yields exactly
delta >= (s-1)((d-1)s-1)/(2s^2+(d-3)s-1).
The d=2 case remains valid with no first groups. Its limit is (d-1)/2.

3. SCALAR LAW AND HINGE INEQUALITY (lines 180--261)
For h(c)=c(2-exp(-1/c)), h'(c)=2-exp(-1/c)(1+1/c)>1 for c>0.
The endpoint comparisons at 1/2 and 1 give a unique root. The density has
unit mass and mean c since c(1+t_0)=1. Direct integration gives the two
displayed formulas for q(t). The second-smallest coordinate contributes
ct, including ties, and the displayed ray lower follows.

The potentially discontinuous zero endpoint is handled correctly. A finite
nondecreasing H has a finite right limit at zero; replacing H(0) by that
limit preserves concavity and does not affect either integral. For t>0 the
inequality is unchanged, while for t=0 the replacement increases the right
side and hence proves a stronger statement. At one, concavity rules out an
upward jump and monotonicity rules out a downward jump. Thus the filled
function is continuous on the closed interval.

Integration by parts on [epsilon,t_0], followed by epsilon->0, gives the
identity because epsilon H(epsilon)->0. For a hinge min(u,s), the integral
is s(t_0-s) if s<=t_0 and zero otherwise. Subtracting the claimed right
side yields exactly the three residuals printed in lines 253--255, all
nonnegative in their stated ranges. Constant and linear residuals are
also correct. Continuous monotone concave piecewise-linear functions have
the required nonnegative constant, linear, and hinge representation;
uniform polygonal interpolation passes the value-form inequality to H.

4. FULL-OUT DIFFERENTIATION AND SMOOTHING (lines 283--387)
Differentiating a_j H_j(C)/C_j when both a and C increase by z yields
(1-alpha_j)H_j(C)z_j/C_j + alpha_j D H_j(C)[z], exactly the displayed
holding derivative. Its dependence on alpha is affine, so the corners
are sufficient. Gradient majorization gives M_j>=c F(z). Two or more
occupied coordinates therefore suffice because 2c>1. The zero corner
follows from the ray lemma.

For a singleton occupied corner, normalization and common rescaling are
valid because G is degree one and its gradient is degree zero. With
min_{ell!=j} x_ell=1, the other coordinate terms are at least cV. If
V<t_0, necessarily t<1 and monotonicity gives 0<t<=V. The displayed H
satisfies H(t)>=t and H(1)=V. Euler's identity gives
t partial_j G=H-uH', including when H has a downward jump at zero.
Since (1-u)f_U(u)=c, the hinge lemma supplies c(t_0-V), which closes
the singleton corner exactly: c+cV+c(t_0-V)=1.

Differentiation under the expectation has a valid uniform local bound.
Nonnegative gradients and Euler imply C_j F_j<=F(C) and
u C_ell F_ell<=F(C). The differentiated off-j terms contain u, so possible
growth of F_ell as u->0 is canceled. On a compact positive capacity box,
these bounds dominate each integrand. Directions containing zero entries
follow by linearity of the derivative and monotonicity of F; no assertion
F(z+eta 1)->F(z) across faces is required.

The finite restoration path has a fixed positive relative face for every
s>0. Integrating on [epsilon,1] and using previously established finite
TCWM gives Y(1)-Y(0)>=(1-epsilon)F(z), without taking a continuity limit
of Y at zero. The zero and one-coordinate faces have direct formulas.

Multiplicative smoothing preserves each support face and all four needed
properties of F. On a face, F(exp(x)) is locally bounded and convolution
with the marginal smooth compactly supported density is smooth in log
coordinates. The coordinate sandwich e^-epsilon x <= diag(e^epsilon Z)x
<= e^epsilon x proves the multiplicative bounds on every face, even at a
downward boundary jump. Each face average and each nonnegative payment
converges individually, so their finite differences and the outside value
pass to the limit. Coalition coverage follows by restoring that coalition
from zero holdings, whose payment under the explicit formula is zero.

5. COUNT-ARRAY COMPACTNESS AND PRICE REGULARITY (lines 398--489)
Within-type averaging is a lawful symmetry of each fixed finite institution.
At binary profiles, the count inequalities are genuine consequences of
coverage, budget, and OUT for the same actual coalition. Turning u_j of its
active members off leaves a_j-u_j active members; the off members' payments
are only dropped from the right side by nonnegativity. No dummy-zero axiom
is imposed. Every finite collection of integer-count constraints is present
in a sufficiently large finite roster.

The bounds kappa F_r(m)/m_j for m_j>0 are independent of roster size;
unused coordinates are fixed at zero. Thus the countable diagonal limit
and compact convex constraint set are valid. Integer dilations preserve
each bound and each inequality after multiplication/division of counts.
For the prime-box averages, cancellation leaves two boundary layers; their
coordinate values both lie in the same [0,B] interval, so the discrepancy
is at most B/L. Hence a subsequential limit is fixed by every prime and
every integer dilation. Rational rescaling is then well-defined: for two
denominator clearers L,L', compare both arrays at LL'C.

The relative-comparison inequality follows from the actual price inequality
using low coalition holdings lambda C'_j e_j and restoring
v=C-lambda C'. Its high holdings are lambda C'_j e_j+v<=C. Dropping the
nonnegative outside term yields exactly the printed sign and denominator.
On [a,b]^r, lambda>=a/b and
|1-lambda|<=||C-C'||_infinity/a, so all printed bounds are valid.
Swapping C,C' supplies the second side of a local Lipschitz estimate.

The real extension retains every finite inequality. One precise way to
implement the stated rational approximation is to approximate the three
nonnegative bundles a-u, u, and C-a separately, and then add them to form
a and C. This preserves all containment inequalities. If the low aggregate
has zero minimum, its zero price contribution is legitimate and coverage
of a bounds F_r(u). Zero high production forces every relevant value to
zero. Resource symmetrization preserves the entire system.

6. ABUNDANT-COORDINATE LIMIT (lines 491--576)
At (A,M,...,M), coverage and budget imply
(r-1)(M-A)b<=(kappa-1)A. Restoring only the second-resource coalition
from B to M proves the prelimit expenditure cap. For r=2 no limit array
is needed. For r>=3, the first two price coordinates are uniformly bounded
at every rational pair, permitting one common countable diagonal subsequence.
Extra coalition holdings min(a,b) have payment O(1/M), so coverage survives.

Both shift estimates have been rederived with their displayed orientation.
For C=z and C'=z', lambda=1 and the residual has w units only in extra
coordinates, giving w kappa min(A,B)/(M z_j). For C=z' and C'=z,
lambda=(M-w)/M and the residual is (w/M)(A,B,0,...,0); the denominator
is ((M-w)/M)z_j, giving the reverse bound with M-w. These estimates
hold for M>w, and in particular tend to zero for each fixed w and pair.

For pair withdrawals (u,v), assigning and withdrawing w=min(u,v) in every
extra coordinate makes the original outside production exactly w. Its
high added payment vanishes, its low retained extra holdings are zero,
and the shifted low price limit equals the unshifted one by the preceding
estimates. A zero-production low pair has zero payment by budget.
This supplies all rational pair OUT inequalities on the same subsequence.
The second compact dilation averaging is needed and valid: the first pair
limit need not be homogeneous. Every finite pair constraint and the cap
is closed, linear in prices, and invariant under common rational scaling.
The same relative-comparison argument supplies regularity only afterward.
