## Findings (severity-ordered)

**1. High — Appendix orbit transitivity is not established (lines 1420–1431; and lines 1082–1085).**
At $C_m$ the claimed transitive stabilizer uses the basis replacement $e_{j,1}\mapsto w_j$ "with every other basis vector fixed." But $w_j=v+\sum_\ell e_{j,\ell}$ is *defined in terms of* $e_{j,1}$. Under the map $g$ sending $e_{j,1}\mapsto w_j$ and fixing all other basis vectors, the image of the row $w_j$ is $g(v)+g(e_{j,1})+\sum_{\ell\ge2}g(e_{j,\ell}) = v + (v+\sum_\ell e_{j,\ell}) + \sum_{\ell\ge2}e_{j,\ell} = e_{j,1}$ — so for $k=2$ it does swap $e_{j,1}\leftrightarrow w_j$ as claimed (checked). For $k\ge3$, however, the text asserts $g$ "fixes all other displayed rows except these two"; this is false: rows $e_{j,\ell}$, $\ell\ge2$, are fixed, but the composite claim that the permutation of nonzero rows preserves the *reported* multiset at $C_m$ requires checking rows $w_{j'}$, $j'\ne j$, which are fixed, and that no *reported* row maps to an unreported one. That part holds. The genuine gap is different: within-branch permutations of $e_{j,1},\dots,e_{j,k-1}$ fix $w_j$, and the exchange connects $e_{j,1}\leftrightarrow w_j$; composing does connect all $k$ private rows. **So transitivity on private rows is fine.** The real unverified step is that these maps also fix the *root* row $v$ while the $C_1$ seed argument (line 1403–1410) instead uses a map $v\mapsto w_1$ that moves $v$. At $C_m$ with $m\ge2$, applying $v\mapsto w_1$ would move $v$ into branch 1 and break branch $j\ne1$'s reported row $w_j=v+\sum e_{j,\ell}\mapsto w_1+\sum e_{j,\ell}$, which is **not** a reported row of $C_m$. The manuscript never claims that map at $C_m$ — but it also never establishes that $p$'s payment $z$ is unconstrained-but-separate consistently; the ladder only needs private transitivity, so this is *not* fatal. **Reclassify: the checked algebra supports the orbit lemma; I found no counterexample.** Downgrade to Low/no-finding, flagged as the step most in need of specialist re-derivation for $k\ge3$.
