BOUNDED COALITIONS: A FINITE FUNDING GUARANTEE FOR SPARSE PRODUCTION
17 October 2026. Internally accepted after root rederivation, exact replay and
independent Fable review023829. Original-source novelty remains unconfirmed.

1. MODEL AND THEOREM

There are n fixed actual owners with nonnegative divisible resource bundles
b_i in fixed eligibility sets. Reports are binding coordinatewise underreports.
Production is the optimum of one fixed finite packing LP

 F(c)=max{pi.x: x>=0, A x<=c},  A>=0, pi>=0,

with no positive-profit zero-resource activity. Every positive-profit activity
uses at most r resource rows. All constrained resources, including any power
cap, are owned and additive across coalitions. Thus coalition S can produce
F(b_S), b_S=sum_{i in S}b_i. Public coefficients and profits are fixed; private
costs and unverifiable overreports are not modeled.

Fix an integer s>=1. The incentive requirement concerns EVERY actual coalition
S with |S|<=s, but coverage concerns ALL coalitions of every size. Put p=1/s.
Define the established p-binomial semivalue / probabilistic Banzhaf ingredient

 m_i(p;b)= E_R [F(b_i+b_R)-F(b_R)],                         (1)

where each other owner independently belongs to R with probability p. Set

                   y_i(b)=p^(1-r) m_i(p;b).               (2)

Then, for every real report profile:

 (a) y_i>=0;
 (b) sum_{i in S} y_i >= F(b_S) for EVERY coalition S;
 (c) sum_i y_i <= r*s^(r-1) F(b_N);
 (d) for EVERY S with |S|<=s and simultaneous withdrawals 0<=d_i<=b_i,

     sum_{i in S}[y_i(b)-y_i(b-d)] >= F(sum_{i in S}d_i).   (3)

Here d_i=0 outside S. Thus even the coalition's production from its pooled
withheld resources cannot offset its lost payments. Equation(3) implies TCWM
for these coalitions and ordinary individual underreport monotonicity.
Owners and resources may both grow without limit while r and s remain fixed.

For example, r=3 and s=2 gives factor12, independent of the number of owners
and pods. This is an existence bound, not a sharp constant. For r=2 stronger
unrestricted-coalition graph theorems are already available in this campaign.
The new candidate statement is the combination of full coverage, bounded-
coalition outside-production protection, and a uniform sparse-production bound;
the binomial semivalue itself and the arity inequality are established tools.

2. TWO ESTABLISHED PACKING INGREDIENTS, WITH PROOFS

For a coalition R, write v(R)=F(b_R) and U_i(R)=v(R)-v(R\{i}) for i in R.
An optimal nonnegative dual resource price w has w.b_R=v(R), and
v(R\{i})<=w.b_(R\{i}). Hence U_i(R)>=w.b_i. Dual feasibility gives

 sum_{i in T} U_i(R) >= v(T) for every T subset R.         (4)

Also the standard bounded-arity grand-marginal inequality gives

                  sum_{i in R} U_i(R) <= r v(R).          (5)

One direct proof fixes an optimal production plan and divides each activity's
resource usage among actual suppliers. Couple its supplier assignments across
its at most r required rows. This decomposes its value into nonnegative pieces,
each supplied by at most r actual owners. Removing all pieces involving i leaves
a feasible plan without i, so U_i is at most their total value. Summing counts
each piece at most r times. This is an analytic allocation of usage, not a change
to strategic owner identities. The existing sparse-production note connects
(5) to the established MPH-r inequality of Ezra et al., arXiv:1811.09871,
Lemma6.2. These ingredients are not claimed as new here.

For any fixed coalition S and independent p-thinning R, one furthermore has

                     E v(S intersect R) >= p^r v(S).      (6)

To verify(6), choose an optimal plan x for S. For each used activity e, assign
its required row v to a supplier i in S with probability b_iv/(b_S)_v,
independently across required rows. All denominators are positive for a used
activity. Each assignment creates a positive hyperedge supported on at most r
distinct ACTUAL owners, of weight pi_e*x_e times its assignment probability.
Let h(T) be the sum of weights supported inside T. For any T subset S, its
activity-e weight equals pi_e*x_e times the product of its row fractions.
This product is at most each individual row fraction, so those scaled activities
are feasible using b_T. Thus h(T)<=v(T), whereas h(S)=v(S). Under p-thinning,
an owner-supported hyperedge of size k<=r survives with probability p^k>=p^r.
Taking expectations proves(6). Ezra et al., arXiv:1811.09871, Lemma6.4 states
the more general established MPH-r sampling inequality: if every subset of at
most r members survives with probability at least q, expected value is at least
q times the original value. Equation(6) is its Bernoulli specialization. The
positive-hypergraph support representation of sparse packing is proved above
for completeness, and is not asserted historically new.

3. COVERAGE AND BUDGET

Now R denotes a p-thinning of ALL n owners. Inclusion/exclusion conditioning
in(1) gives, for every S,

 p sum_{i in S}m_i = E sum_{i in S intersect R} U_i(R)
                  >= E v(S intersect R) >= p^r v(S).

Multiplication by p^(1-r) proves coverage of every S, including those larger
than s. For the total, (5), Jensen concavity, and homogeneity give

 p sum_i m_i <= r E F(b_R) <= r F(p b_N)=r p F(b_N).

Therefore sum_i y_i <= r p^(1-r) F(b_N), proving(c). All inequalities apply
also when production is zero. Nonnegativity follows directly from monotonic F.

4. FINITE COEFFICIENT PROOF OF OUTSIDE-PRODUCTION PROTECTION

Assume s>=2, so 0<p<1. For a fixed coalition S write M_S=sum_{i in S}m_i.
Expand the finite expectation as a linear functional of coalition values:

 M_S(b)=sum_{A subset N} c_S(A) F(b_A),

where, for 0<|A|<n,

 c_S(A)=p^(|A|-1)(1-p)^(n-|A|-1)[|A intersect S|-p|S|].   (7)

At the endpoints the same expansion gives explicitly

 c_S(empty)=-|S|(1-p)^(n-1),    c_S(N)=|S|p^(n-1).

These follow by counting the positive occurrence of F(b_A) for i in A intersect S
and the negative occurrence for i in S\A in(1). For |S|<=s=1/p, whenever
A intersects S, one has |A intersect S|-p|S|>=1-p|S|>=0.
Thus every coefficient of a coalition value affected by S's reports is
nonnegative. Coefficients for A disjoint from S can be negative, but those
coalition values do not change during S's withdrawal.

Concavity and degree-one homogeneity imply superadditivity of F. Therefore

 F(b_A)-F(b_A-d_A) >= F(d_A),  d_A=sum_{i in A}d_i.

Multiplying this by the nonnegative affected coefficients and summing gives

 M_S(b)-M_S(b-d) >= sum_A c_S(A)F(d_A)=M_S(d).

For A disjoint from S, both the actual value difference and F(d_A)=F(0) vanish,
so their negative coefficients create no issue. The last expression uses the
SAME n fixed owners and the SAME mechanism, evaluated at the report profile
consisting of the withheld bundles and zero bundles outside S. Applying the
already proved full coverage at that profile gives

 p^(1-r) M_S(d) >= F(d_S).

This proves(3) directly for arbitrary simultaneous coalition withdrawals.
There is no derivative, genericity, positive-stock, boundary-continuity,
zero-owner-payment, or price-field assumption in this argument.

For s=1, p=1 and(1) is exactly U_i(N)=F(b_N)-F(b_(N\{i})). Its excluded term
is fixed when i withdraws, and superadditivity gives
U_i(b)-U_i(b-d_i)>=F(d_i). Equations(4)--(5) prove coverage and factor r.
This handles the endpoint without interpreting singular coefficient powers.
If there are no positive-profit activities, F and all payments are zero;
the arity-zero case uses this zero rule. Empty owner/coalition cases are also zero.

5. THE BINOMIAL PARAMETER THRESHOLD IS SHARP FOR THIS ARCHITECTURE

Assume the production class permits activity arity at least2. For integer s>=2
and p>1/s with p<1, the unscaled rule(1) fails TCWM already
on a unit star. There is one center resource owner with capacity1 and s leaf
resource owners, with production

                  F(c,h)=min(c, sum_{j=1}^s h_j).

Each activity uses two resources. Set all leaf capacities to1 except leaf i,
which increases from0 to1. Let S be the coalition of all s leaves, excluding
the center. Before the increase, each of the s-1 positive leaves receives
p(1-p)^(s-2); after it, each of the s leaves receives p(1-p)^(s-1). Consequently

 M_S(high)-M_S(low)=p(1-p)^(s-2)(1-sp)<0.                 (8)

Any fixed positive scaling preserves this violation. For p=1, use a two-leaf
star: before restoring the missing leaf the existing leaf's grand marginal is1;
afterwards both leaf marginals are0. This coalition has size2<=s. Thus choosing
p>1/s cannot repair the constant in(c) while keeping this binomial architecture
valid for every coalition of size at most s. This is a RULE-SPECIFIC obstruction,
not a lower bound of r*s^(r-1) for arbitrary mechanisms.
For activity arity1, production is additive across resource rows and owners,
so the binomial payment is independent of p and this threshold obstruction
does not apply.

6. COMPUTATION, ECONOMIC SCOPE, AND PRIORITY

For rational p,A,pi,b, every expectation is a finite rational sum of exact LP
values. Enumerating the 2^n coalition values suffices; one can then evaluate all
payments by finite sums. This is an exact deterministic all-profile rule. No
polynomial-time algorithm, efficient sampling guarantee, or FPTAS is claimed.
Approximate Monte Carlo payments would require separate coverage and incentive
analysis; replacing this formula by a numerical estimator does not inherit(3).

A GPU/memory/power model could have r=3 if each activity truly uses only those
three owned resource rows. Multiple sites, time slots, network links and other
constraints can increase r and must be counted. The guarantee concerns all
groups of at most s fixed actual owners; it does not protect larger colluding
groups. Nevertheless every large coalition still receives at least its feasible
standalone production. Public net values/costs may be included in LP profits,
but private opportunity-cost reports and measured serving gains are absent.

The contrast with the common-cap rank-three obstruction is precise: unlimited
TCWM coalitions force unbounded funding as owner count grows; bounding the
incentive coalition size gives a uniform finite bound, even while preserving
full outside-production protection for every permitted coalition. The external
funding above production is at most [r*s^(r-1)-1]F, so the existence theorem is
not a claim that the current constant is economically attractive.

Established ingredients include p-binomial/Banzhaf semivalues, LP core prices,
MPH-r support, and the bounded-arity grand-marginal inequality. The proposed
contribution, pending original-source comparison, is their all-profile synthesis
in(2)--(3), together with the bounded-versus-unbounded coalition consequence.
