EXACT P6 OBSTRUCTION TO RECURSIVE PROTECTED-LEAF PROJECTION
24 October 2026. Bounded tree-extension gate. Read-only canonical work;
this file concerns a specified recursive selector, not arbitrary mechanisms.

OUTCOME AND STOP.
The accepted P4 protected-leaf selector does not extend by sequentially
projecting leaf uses to a fixed fractional reserve on the asymmetric
maximum-flow face. An exact all-positive P6 example has zero payment loss
for a fixed mixed owner withholding a productive middle-edge bundle.
The same failure holds for every 0<alpha<1 and every fixed reserve fraction
0<=beta<1. This closes this recursive branch; it does not disprove a uniform
constant below2 for arbitrary full-OUT mechanisms on all trees.

1. PRECISE SELECTOR UNDER TEST.
For a unit-profit tree T and capacity c, form the asymmetric bipartite double
cover with left capacities c_v and right capacities alpha*c_v. Select maximum
flow. A tree is bipartite, so its double cover has two orientation components.
In each component, visit its left vertices according to a fixed leaf-peeling
order of the original tree, with any deterministic tie-breaking. At a visited
vertex v, among maximum flows consistent with all already fixed left uses,
compute the feasible interval [l_v,h_v] of its left use. Fix that use to
 clamp(beta*c_v; l_v,h_v).
Continue until all left uses are fixed; pay each actual owner i
 y_i=sum_v b_iv*w_v/c_v,
with zero contribution at c_v=0. On P4, choosing alpha=1/sqrt(2), beta=1/2
and visiting its two original leaves before the two internal vertices gives
exactly the accepted protected-leaf selector (one leaf in each orientation).

The universal double-cover funding identity remains true for this rule:
 sum_v w_v <= (1+alpha) F_T(c).
It is not an incentive certificate. The counterexample below shows that its
naive recursive extension loses full OUT, despite retaining flow feasibility.

2. CANONICAL EXACT COUNTEREXAMPLE.
Let T be the six-vertex path 0--1--2--3--4--5. Set alpha=1/sqrt(2), beta=1/2,
and use the full capacity vector
 c=(1, sqrt(2), 1/4, 1/4, sqrt(2), 1).
The same three actual owners are used at both profiles:
 owner I holds all resources0,2,3,5;
 owner II holds resource1, capacity sqrt(2);
 owner III holds resource4, capacity sqrt(2).
Only owner I deviates, reducing both middle resources2 and3 from1/4 to1/8.
It keeps its resources0 and5 unchanged. Thus
 c'=(1, sqrt(2), 1/8, 1/8, sqrt(2), 1),
 d=c-c'=(0,0,1/8,1/8,0,0), and F_T(d)=1/8.

At the full profile, the selected left-use vector is
 w=(3/4, 5alpha/4, 1/4, 1/4, 5alpha/4, 3/4).
At the lower profile it is
 w'=(7/8, 9alpha/8, 1/8, 1/8, 9alpha/8, 7/8).
Therefore owner I's payment is
 y_I(c)=3/4+1/4+1/4+3/4=2,
 y_I(c')=7/8+1/8+1/8+7/8=2.
Full OUT requires their difference to be at least1/8, but it is zero.
This is already singleton OUT with one mixed owner, not merely a coalition
failure. No owner or resource is reassigned during the comparison.

3. ELEMENTARY MAXIMUM-FLOW AND SELECTION CERTIFICATE.
In the orientation with left vertices{0,2,4}, all right capacities can be
saturated. At the full profile take directed flows
 0->1 =3/4, 2->1 =1/4,
 4->3 =alpha/4, 4->5 =alpha,
and all other directed flows in this component zero. The right capacities
are alpha*c1=1, alpha*c3=alpha/4, alpha*c5=alpha, so this is a maximum flow.
Any maximum flow saturates right1. Its only incoming sources are0 and2;
source2 has capacity1/4. Consequently w0>=1-1/4=3/4. The displayed flow
attains this bound. The first visited original leaf0 therefore has interval
lower endpoint3/4>beta*c0=1/2, so the selector fixes w0=3/4. Saturation of
right1 then forces source2 to spend its entire1/4 on that right vertex.
The remaining right capacities are forced to come from source4. All left
uses in this orientation are thus fixed, regardless of subsequent order.

The other orientation is the reflected argument: its first original leaf5
has feasible minimum3/4>1/2, fixing w5=3/4 and forcing w3=1/4, with the
remaining flow from source1. The order between original leaves0 and5 is
irrelevant because they lie in different orientation components.

At the lower report, replace1/4 by1/8 in this argument. Right1 and right4
still have capacity1. Their adjacent middle sources now have capacity1/8,
so the original leaves have feasible minimum7/8. The displayed lower flow
saturates all right capacities and obeys every left capacity. In particular
5alpha/4<sqrt(2) and9alpha/8<sqrt(2), and both endpoint uses are below1.
The same selection argument forces exactly w'. Thus the finite report
comparison is proved without relying on numerical differentiation, an LP
solver, or the generic rank implementation used for discovery.

4. GENERAL FIXED-RESERVE OBSTRUCTION.
Fix arbitrary 0<alpha<1 and 0<=beta<1. Choose
 0<q<min(1-beta, alpha^(-2)-1, 1).
On the same P6, set
 c(q)=(1,1/alpha,q,q,1/alpha,1).
The useful strict inequalities are
 1-q>beta and alpha(1+q)<1/alpha.
Both orientation components therefore admit maximum flows saturating every
right capacity, with selected left-use vector
 w(q)=(1-q,alpha(1+q),q,q,alpha(1+q),1-q).
For example in the first orientation use0->1=1-q, 2->1=q,
4->3=alpha*q, 4->5=alpha; reflect for the other orientation.
Each component's maximum-flow value is1+alpha(1+q), its total right capacity.
Right vertices1 and4 have capacity1. Since their middle sources have capacity
q, every maximum flow has w0>=1-q and w5>=1-q. The endpoint reserve beta is
strictly below1-q, so each first original-leaf clamp selects that lower
endpoint. Right saturation then forces all remaining left uses, independently
of subsequent tie-breaking or reserve choices.

Let the same mixed owner hold0,2,3,5 and the same two outsiders hold1,4.
Reduce both middle reports from q to q/2. The new selected vector is
 w(q/2)=(1-q/2,alpha(1+q/2),q/2,q/2,alpha(1+q/2),1-q/2).
All capacities remain strictly positive and feasible; the original leaves'
uses stay below1. The maximum-flow and clamping proof is unchanged. The
mixed owner's payment is identically2 at both reports, while its withheld
bundle produces q/2 on edge2--3. Thus OUT fails for every stated alpha and
beta. A completely specified admissible parameter is
 q=min(1-beta,alpha^(-2)-1,1)/2.

This includes reserves beta=alpha^2, so increasing alpha toward1 or retuning
the fractional reserve cannot repair this recursive architecture. The proof
also allows different fixed endpoint fractions beta0,beta5<1: use their
maximum in the displayed q bound. It does not cover beta=1, different globally
coupled selections, reserves depending on other reports, mixtures of selectors,
or arbitrary admissible mechanisms. No successor search on these alternatives
is authorized by this obstruction.

5. INDUCTION BOTTLENECK EXHIBITED BY THE EXAMPLE.
The P4 proof reserves two outer leaves while the two residual central
vertices remain adjacent and coupled. In P6, fixing the first original leaf
in each orientation at its feasible minimum saturates both middle sources
into their outer neighbor sinks. This creates two independently tight cuts.
A mixed owner holding both endpoint leaves and both middle sources then has
payment determined only by the two unchanged outsider capacities. Reducing
the middle edge's two endpoint resources is exactly offset by increased
payment on its unchanged original leaves. Funding and flow feasibility do
not constrain this redistribution enough to imply OUT.
Any valid tree induction must retain a joint boundary-response condition
across the middle edge; fixing feasible endpoint reserves independently
loses that information. The example is an exact obstruction to that proposed
compatibility step, not a proof that every possible compatibility invariant
fails.

6. NO-GO LEDGER AND VERIFICATION SCOPE.
Inherited routes not reopened: balanced utilization selection, integrable
homogeneous gradient prices, independent component-local pair prices,
positive binary-min decompositions. The only new branch was the explicit
sequential protected-leaf projection above, and it is now closed by Section4.
The P4 theorem and its complete-blowup/disjoint-union corollary are untouched.
No all-tree upper below2 or new arbitrary-mechanism lower has been obtained.

Discovery: leaf_projection_probe.py/.json found finite P5/P6 failures using
piecewise-linear rank formulas. These are diagnostics only. The proof in
Sections2--4 uses direct displayed flows and a forced leaf minimum, and is
independent of the discovery implementation. An exact standalone checker
verifies the canonical finite report comparison.
