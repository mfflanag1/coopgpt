ALL-PROFILE POOLING THEOREMS -- 2026-10-04
Coordinator proofs checked independently by Fable 5.1; exact finite stress checks
passed. Conventional proofs, not formal verification. No priority claim.
The pool-total functional is established continuous Banzhaf; see BANZHAF_COMPARISON.txt.

MODEL
There are k>=1 disjoint finite owner sets N_1,...,N_k, with each owner i in
exactly one pool j and capacity c_i>=0. Put A_j=sum_{i in N_j}c_i and
T=min_j A_j. Coalition value is v(S)=min_j sum_{i in S intersect N_j}c_i.
For k stages, this is exactly a series of k parallel-arc bundles, with distinct
arc owners. It is a subclass, not all networks of path length k. Payments are
deterministic functions of reports, utility is payment, underreports only, no costs.
C and H are the ORIGINAL efficient real TU core and its coordinate maxima.
A bottleneck-pool cut payment (c_i in that pool, zero elsewhere) proves C nonempty.

THEOREM1. A UNIFORM-THRESHOLD PRICE FORMULA
If T=0 set y=0. Otherwise, for each pool j define
 p_j(A) = (1/A_j) integral_0^T product_{l != j}(1-t/A_l) dt,
 y_i(c)=c_i p_j(A) for i in N_j.
Then, at every real nonnegative capacity profile:
 (a) y>=0 and y(S)>=v(S) for every coalition S;
 (b) y_i is nondecreasing in c_i with every other capacity fixed;
 (c) y_i>=H_i/k;
 (d) T<=sum_i y_i<=((k+1)/2)T.
Thus underreporting is not profitable, all coalition outside options are covered,
and the external subsidy is at most (k-1)T/2. For k=2 it protects H/2 with
subsidy<=T/2; for k=3 it protects H/3 with subsidy<=T. The proofs below cover
all real profiles, independently of the finite stress tests. These are not
optimality claims over arbitrary mechanisms.

Proof of(a). Every p_j>=0. Differentiating P(t)=product_l(1-t/A_l) gives
 -P'(t)=sum_j (1/A_j)product_{l != j}(1-t/A_l).
Integrating from0 toT gives sum_j p_j=P(0)-P(T)=1. For any coalition,
 y(S)=sum_j p_j sum_{i in S intersect N_j}c_i >= sum_j p_j v(S)=v(S).
At T=0 every coalition value is0, so y=0 works.
Equivalent interpretation: p_j is the probability that independent uniform
U_l in[0,A_l] have their minimum at j. This is an analysis device; payments
use exact deterministic prices, not a lottery realized after reporting.

Proof of(b). Fix i in pool j, write A_j=x+a where x=c_i and a>=0 is fixed.
For all other pool totals positive, define
 g(t)=product_{l != j} max(1-t/A_l,0), t>=0.
Then y_i(x)=x/(x+a) integral_0^(x+a) g(t)dt.
Both factors are nonnegative and nondecreasing in x, hence so is their product.
This expression agrees with the displayed price formula, since g vanishes above
the least other total. If k=1 the empty product is1 and y_i=x. At x=a=0
define y_i=0, and the inequality across that boundary follows by nonnegativity.
If another pool has total0, all payments stay0 as x varies. No overreport, cost,
or group-report truthfulness is proved.

Proof of(c). For every x in C, x_i=T-x(N\{i})<=T-v(N\{i}). Put
 B=min_{l != j}A_l for k>=2. If A_j<=B, the resulting marginal bound is c_i.
If A_j>B, it is max(0,c_i-A_j+B), which is <=c_i*B/A_j because c_i<=A_j.
Thus H_i<=c_i*T/A_j in both cases. For0<=t<=T every A_l>=T gives
 product_{l != j}(1-t/A_l)>=(1-t/T)^(k-1).
Consequently p_j >= T/(k*A_j) and y_i>=c_i*T/(k*A_j)>=H_i/k.
For k=1 the core is the unique additive payoff c and the same claim holds.
For T=0 the original core is{0}, so the floor is0.

Proof of(d). Fix j* with A_j*=T. Since each factor lies in[0,1] on[0,T],
 A_j* p_j* <=T. For every j != j*, the integrand includes1-t/T, hence
 A_j p_j<=integral_0^T(1-t/T)dt=T/2. Sum these k inequalities.
The lower bound is the grand-coalition case of(a).
For this particular formula the upper constant is a supremum: take A_1=1 and
A_2=...=A_k=M and let M grow. The first integral tends1 and every other
integral tends1/2. This does not prove optimality among other mechanisms. QED.

TWO-POOL CLOSED FORM
For totals A,B>0 with A>=B,
 p_L=B/(2A), p_R=1-B/(2A),
 total payment=3B/2-B^2/(2A), subsidy ratio=(1-B/A)/2.
For A<=B interchange sides. At A=B both prices are1/2 and subsidy0.

THEOREM2. SHARP FAIRNESS/SUBSIDY TRADEOFF IN A SPECIFIED PRICING CLASS
Restrict to two-pool payment families, valid for EVERY finite partition of
capacities into owners, of the form
 y_i=c_i p(A,B) on the left, y_i=c_i q(A,B) on the right.
The same nonnegative price functions p,q depend only on the two totals, not
on the number, identities, or partition of owners. This is an EXTRA assumption;
it is not asserted to follow merely from DSIC, anonymity or split-proofness.
For any fixed alpha in[0,1], require all coalition inequalities, own-report
monotonicity, and original-core floors y_i>=alpha H_i for all these instances.
The infimum worst-case subsidy factor in this class is max(1/2,alpha).
Thus fairness up to H/2 is free within this class; above 1/2 the optimal worst-case
subsidy factor increases exactly with alpha.

Upper bound. Theorem1 with k=2 gives alpha=1/2 and beta=1/2, hence every
alpha<=1/2. The auxiliary rule z_i=c_i*T/A_j pays each pool total T. It is
own-report monotone: holding other capacities fixed, z_i=x*min(1,B/(x+a));
this increases as x or x/(x+a) in its two regimes and agrees at the boundary.
It covers coalitions because the bottleneck pool alone receives its capacities.
The marginal bound above gives z_i>=H_i, and total z=2T. For alpha>=1/2,
 y^alpha=(2-2alpha)y+(2alpha-1)z
is a convex combination preserving monotonicity and coalition covering. It
protects alpha H and has subsidy factor at most alpha. At zero flow use0.

Lower bound. At totals(1,1) with one owner in each pool, original ideals are(1,1).
Thus p(1,1),q(1,1)>=alpha and p(1,1)+q(1,1)>=1. One of these prices is at
least r=max(1/2,alpha); interchange pool names if necessary. Suppose p>=r.
Raise the sole left owner's capacity from1 to M>1, keeping the right capacity1.
Own-report monotonicity implies M*p(M,1)>=r. Now consider instead two left
owners of capacities1 and M-1, and the same right owner of capacity1. Because
prices depend only on totals, they remain p(M,1),q(M,1). The coalition of the
unit left owner and the right owner has value1, implying p(M,1)+q(M,1)>=1.
Therefore total payment at these totals is
 M*p(M,1)+q(M,1)>=1+(M-1)*p(M,1)>=1+r*(1-1/M).
Here T=1. Let M tend to infinity. Every universal beta is >=r. The argument
uses at most two owners per pool, but crucially uses partition-independent
prices across ownership instances. It does NOT prove beta>=1/2 for arbitrary
profile-dependent payments in the fixed four-player model. QED.
