ACCEPTED MATHEMATICAL PROGRESS
For k complementary capacity pools with value equal to the minimum coalition
capacity across pools, deterministic payments cover every coalition, are monotone
in each owner's capacity, protect H_i/k of each original efficient-core ideal,
and require subsidy no more than (k-1)T/2. T is the unsubsidized total value.
For two pools: at most 50% subsidy and at least H_i/2 for every owner.
For three pools IN SERIES: at most 100% subsidy and at least H_i/3.
These are conventional proofs for every real nonnegative profile; finite tests
provide additional stress checks, not the proof of universal quantification.

For two pools, within uniform per-unit prices depending ONLY on aggregate pool
totals and shared across EVERY ownership partition, the best worst-case subsidy
factor with alpha*H floors is exactly max(1/2,alpha), alpha in [0,1].
This identifies a real limitation of aggregate-only pricing. It is not an
impossibility for all truthful mechanisms or a fixed four-owner domain.

IMPLICATION FOR APPLICATIONS
The application is complementary resource pooling: e.g., separate pools of compute
and bandwidth, or compute and a necessary specialized resource, measured in
compatible job-throughput units. The mathematical production technology is a fixed
minimum of pool totals. This is a motivating model, not a validated cloud system:
positive operating costs, heterogeneity, latency, private values, false identities,
overreporting and coordinated misreports are outside the proof.
