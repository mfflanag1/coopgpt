## 1. Exact contribution under comparison

There are exactly three sectors, each of size 3q+1; the number of sectors is fixed while the number of players grows. Triple worth is A_ij+B_ik+C_jk, with nonnegative pair entries in {0,1,2}. The asserted core is the full strict-triple core, including nonnegativity and all triple covers. The family has a positive first leximin floor τ=1/[3(q+1)] and a labeled target with whole-core ideal H=1. That target is fixed at τ throughout the first maximizing face, hence also by full sorted ascending leximin. The asserted ratio therefore tends to zero.

The priority question is this simultaneous model, selector, and coordinate-ideal statement. A result on arbitrary multisided assignment games, existence of the core, or an allocation evaluated by a different bargaining concept is not automatically the same theorem. Nor is exclusion from one subclass a proof of novelty.

## 4. Direct exclusion of the new family from every three-sector chain ordering

This is a deduction from the source's exact model definition and the explicit target array, rather than an attributed old theorem.

If a nonnegative matrix has the form M_ij=max{r_i+s_j,0}, any 2×2 submatrix with both diagonal entries positive satisfies

    M_11+M_22 ≤ M_12+M_21.

Indeed, its two positive diagonal entries equal the underlying sums. Their total equals the total of the two underlying off-diagonal sums, each of which is at most its positive part. Thus a violation rules out even the signed-pair, truncated chain model.

For every q≥1, the target array has these slices:

| Omitted interaction if a chain existed | Fixed third player | Row labels; column labels | Slice |
|---|---|---|---|
| AB | c=L0 | a=(O1,L0); b=(O1,L0) | [[2,1],[0,1]] |
| AC | b=L0 | a=(O1,L0); c=(O1,L0) | [[3,1],[1,1]] |
| BC | a=L0 | b=(L0,R1); c=(L0,R1) | [[1,0],[0,1]] |

Every slice has positive diagonals and strictly larger diagonal sum. If C were the middle sector, the AB slice would have the forbidden truncated-separable form; analogous reasoning handles middle sectors B and A. The obstruction is existential, so within-sector relabeling cannot remove it. It also survives a different choice of the two pair matrices: it is a statement about the actual triple tensor.

Consequently the exact family cannot itself be a supplier–firm–buyer chain market under any sector ordering. This does **not** exclude an auxiliary-player representation preserving its labeled core, a strategically transformed game, or a theorem from an uninspected broader part of Stuart1997. Those would require a separate theorem with the necessary selector and ideal preservation.

## 6. Older formulas and the positive chain control

The older strict-chain nonemptiness theorem and bargaining-set/core coincidence do not, as stated, compare full sorted leximin with H_i. The campaign's earlier 1/3 chain protection argument uses additional full-core potential geometry and leximin optimality. Its slack-balancing method has already been credited to classical literature; this follow-up does not claim that application was first proved here.

A related older bilateral-composition formula applies to generalized markets with productive pairs. It cannot be transferred wholesale to the strict model: for a single player per sector, chain pair values1 and1 give strict core p≥0, sum p=2, whereas composition of the two bilateral cores forces each endpoint payoff≤1. Equal sector sizes do not fix this discrepancy. The previous SOURCE_GATE.md records the full primary Atay–Núñez2019 model comparison. No new search or priority claim is based on this elementary counterexample.

The cyclic family exclusion above is stronger than observing that its displayed representation has three nonzero matrices. It checks the tensor itself and the source's zero-truncation convention.
