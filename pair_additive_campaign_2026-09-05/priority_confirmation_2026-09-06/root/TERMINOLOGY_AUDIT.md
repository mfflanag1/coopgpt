# Additional original-source comparisons

6 September 2026. Eight focused queries plus primary reads. These checks are distinct from the earlier historical and modern audits; they do not create a new research branch or assert that the literature has been exhausted.

## Multilateral bargaining: original working text obtained

Tejada–Rafels, *Symmetrically multilateral-bargained allocations in multi-sided assignment markets*, [UB E09/216](https://diposit.ub.edu/bitstreams/92331b85-c564-429d-866c-e063a2d7b3b0/download), 2009 working version of the 2010 article, was read at Definitions1–3, Theorems5–7, and Example8 (printed pp.8–16). The selector is a fixed point of a threat-based bargaining map. The main results characterize its intersection with a generalized kernel and prove nonemptiness. They do not identify full sorted leximin.

Example8's displayed full core has three permanent zeros and active payoffs `(a,b,3-a-b)`, with `a,b>=0` and `2<=a+b<=3`. Direct deduction from that core: full leximin has active payoffs `(1,1,1)`, while their ideals are `(3,3,1)`; the minimum fraction is1/3. This example does not itself yield a vanishing family. These ratio calculations are ours, not statements attributed to the paper. No transformation from its bargaining characterization to the bounded-pair-data theorem has been established.

The original publisher article was subscription-only; its abstract was read, while the institutional working text supplied the theorem comparison. The outdated repository path returned an empty DSpace page; the listed current institutional download yielded indexed original full text. A screenshot request later failed and supplied no evidence.

## Leximin outside the core is a different problem

Dolgopolov and coauthors, [*Assignment Markets: Theory and Experiments*](https://arthurdolgopolov.net/papers/Dolgopolov_Assignment_Markets.pdf), author PDF, AppendicesB–C, printed pp.29–31, discusses leximin over attainable assignment outcomes, potentially outside the core. Its displayed bilateral example has a leximin allocation that does not maximize total surplus. This does not anticipate a statement about leximin restricted to the entire efficient payoff core. Only the cited conceptual/example passages were inspected, not the paper's experimental analysis or every proof. No numerical conclusion about our core selector is imported.

## Partition stability is not the payoff core

Aziz, Brandl, Brandt, Harrenstein, Olsen and Peters, [*Fractional Hedonic Games*](https://www.dominik-peters.de/publications/fhg.pdf), author draft dated7 September2018, introduction and preliminaries, defines outcomes as coalition partitions and utility as the average of individual valuations within a coalition. Its leximin/star-packing and stable-partition results concern that nontransferable-utility setting. They do not supply our convex set of transferable payoffs or coordinate ideals. The model distinction was established from the original text; no broad representation impossibility is claimed.

The 2015 assignment-market survey was used to locate original references, not as a substitute for their theorem statements. Already audited 2024–2026 leximin papers and the implementation thesis surfaced again but were not counted as newly closed gaps.

## Disposition

These additional comparisons remove several superficially similar results from direct contention. They do not close the original Quint/Lucas/Stuart/2023 core-representation gaps assigned to the other routes, and they do not certify global novelty. The exact claim remains the constrained realization of vanishing full-core leximin protection in complete equal-sector strict three-sided markets with pair entries bounded by two.
