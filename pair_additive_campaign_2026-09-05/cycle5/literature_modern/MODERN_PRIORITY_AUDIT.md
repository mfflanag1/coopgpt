## Two substantive precedents that narrow the claim

**Core-ideal protection is explicitly old.** Marina Núñez and Carles Rafels, [*Bargained stable allocations in assignment markets*](https://www.bse.eu/sites/default/files/working_paper_pdfs/153.pdf), Barcelona Economics Working Paper 153, dated 28 December 2004, Definition 6, printed p.19, defines the half-optimal core by `p_i>=H_i/2` for every player. Its nonemptiness follows there from the midpoint of the two side-optimal core allocations. Theorem 9, p.23, relates these allocations to Nash bargaining; Theorem 14, p.30, characterizes those reached by Kalai–Smorodinsky bargaining with the paper's allowed disagreement points. These are bilateral markets, initially with all agents active; Section 6 handles inactive agents. This directly anticipates the quantitative benchmark and its interpretation, but does not identify sorted leximin or give a strict triple example. Cite the inspected working version unless the journal version is separately checked.

**Vanishing protection is already implicit in a simple flow example.** Gangam, Garg, Shahkar and Vazirani, [*Equitable Core Imputations for Max-Flow, MST and b-Matching Games*](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2403.06037), arXiv v3, 11 February 2025, §3.1.4, printed p.7, considers a path of n unit-capacity edges and its equal `1/n` allocation. Direct deduction: every proper edge coalition has worth zero, so the full core is the nonnegative simplex of total one; its full leximin is uniform and each ideal is one. Thus the ratio is `1/n`. The example uses coalitions requiring all n path edges. It does not provide a three-sector pair-additive realization. Definitions 3.4–3.5, p.6, distinguish sorted selectors on the core and on the Owen set; Lemma 4.2, pp.9–10, supplies progressive dual-support freezing for general LP optima. Neither the selectors nor that certification principle should receive a new-method label.

## Terminology and deductive checks

Our `H_i=max_{p in P}p_i` is an attainable coordinate maximum over the full core. It is not automatically the TU-game marginal or utopia bound `v(N)-v(N\{i})`: the latter bounds H from above by a coalition inequality, with equality requiring additional structure. Nor is H a single team's output unless a separate upper bound and attainment proof establish that equality.

Raw-payoff sorted leximin and a lexicographic extension of ideal-normalized bargaining are different objectives: dividing coordinate i by H_i changes the order being optimized. The search found the primary Heidelberg record for Driesen's *The Asymmetric Leximin Solution*, but direct full-text access was denied by its access layer. Indexed text discusses utopia normalization; this is a terminology lead, not a verified theorem comparison. No conclusion depends on that unread theorem.

The three focused hypergraph/fractional-cover queries did not expose a directly matching primary theorem. This is a limited search outcome. Ordinary fractional matching algorithms, leximin of an arbitrary LP, and the flow-path example do not establish priority for the constrained realization by themselves. A deductive anticipation would need to preserve the entire feasible payoff set in the original labeled coordinates, the optimal integral matching total, all auxiliary/fixed-zero payoffs that enter sorting, and the pair-additive completion of every mixed triple. No inspected modern source supplies that interface. This is a missing implication, not a proof that no such implication exists.

The exact finite result is also more limited than a general statement that leximin is unfair: one designated coordinate is forced on the first max-min face, its ideal is proved on the whole core, and the observed ratio is `1/9`. A source demonstrating an arbitrary unfair core point, or a fair point in a proper subcore, does not anticipate that statement. Conversely, the campaign's rejected `1/6` conjecture was internal; its disproof must not be described as settling an established published conjecture.
