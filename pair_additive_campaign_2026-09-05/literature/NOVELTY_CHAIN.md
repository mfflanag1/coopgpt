**Finding:** selected-arc lexicographic potential-slack balancing is explicitly classical. ## Direct methodological predecessor

Held, Korte, Maßberg, Ringe, and Vygen, *Clock Scheduling and Clocktree Construction for High Performance ASICs*, ICCAD2003,232–239, has been retrieved in [full from a university archive](https://www.cs.york.ac.uk/rts/docs/SIGDA-Compendium-1994-2004/papers/2003/iccad03/pdffiles/03d_4.pdf). Section3.1,p233 models admissible potentials by difference inequalities and an origin node. Section3.2,p234 explicitly selects arcs with an indicator and maximizes their ascending-sorted slack vector. Its solution uses minimum-ratio cycles and contraction. Theorem3.1 confirms the lexicographic direction. The paper credits Albrecht et al.1999 and Young–Tarjan–Orlin1991; its reference10 identifies Held's2001 potential-balancing thesis. This is a direct predecessor, including the distinction between optimized arcs and structural constraints.

The current chain graph fits that optimization problem without removing payoff coordinates. Set the indicator to one on the three player-arc types and zero on structural arcs, and use the stated q-potentials. Hard inequalities remain hard inequalities; they are not extra fairness coordinates. The chain graph is strongly connected, and feasibility implies that every structural-only directed cycle has nonnegative constant sum. Diagonal structural loops can simply be omitted. These are checks on the current construction, not extra conclusions quoted from the old paper.

The current reverse-path argument is an elementary optimality certificate for partial slack balancing. If the required path is absent, a cut potential perturbation improves an eligible payoff without worsening any earlier fairness level. Telescoping a resulting cycle is the familiar potential cancellation. Although the inspected paper does not state the note's lemma in precisely that wording, calling the *general* cut/cycle principle new would be unsupported. The independent proof is useful as a self-contained justification, with classical-method attribution.

Full primary conference text inspected. Section4, Definition4.1 and Theorem4.2 (PDFpages3–4) optimize selected slacks while retaining other constraints; cycles fix successive levels. The extracted Definition4.1 says “minimal” although the surrounding maximization and algorithm indicate the opposite. The screenshot request failed, so this audit does not decide whether that is a printed typo or extraction error. The unambiguous 2003 source resolves the intended direction. - **Schneider and Schneider1991**, [*Max-Balancing Weighted Directed Graphs and Matrix Scaling*](https://people.math.wisc.edu/hans/paper_archive/other_papers/hs096b.pdf), MOR16(1),208–222. Full primary paper inspected at §1, Theorem2,p213, and §7. Potential reweighting, balanced cuts, cycle means, and contraction already appear here. Its main problem optimizes all arcs, so it is a foundational predecessor rather than the most exact source for selected payoff arcs. The paper was received in1987 and revised in1989.

- **Albrecht et al.2002**, *Maximum Mean Weight Cycle in a Digraph and Minimizing Cycle Time of a Logic Chip*, DAM123,103–127, DOI10.1016/S0166-218X(01)00339-0, is the journal expansion. ## Exact old chain model; different allocation concepts

Atay and Solymosi's [February19,2018 working paper](https://real.mtak.hu/75324/1/MTDP1805.pdf), *On Bargaining Sets of Supplier-Firm-Buyer Games*, MT-DP2018/5, later Economics Letters167,99–103, supplies a particularly useful exact comparison. Definition5 and equations1–2 (workingpp6–7) require full triples; bilateral contributions alone earn nothing. Triple worth is the positive part of the sum of two adjacent pair values. Thus nonnegative P,Q and equal sizes in the present theorem give a strict subclass of their model. Proposition6 credits nonempty core to Stuart1997. Theorem8,p9 establishes equality of the core and two bargaining sets. It does not identify either full sorted selector or compare leximin with coordinate ideals.

The generalized tree-market composition theorem discussed in `SOURCE_GATE.md` remains a different result: productive pairs impose additional constraints. It must not replace the current strict full core. Nonempty core, the strict locally additive model, and existence of bargaining allocations are established background; they are not candidate contributions here.
