Acyclicity and simultaneous protection
of individual core maxima
Maxwell Flanagan
New York City, United States
maxwell.flanagan@gmail.com

Abstract
A stable allocation need not protect each participant relative to that participant’s most
favorable stable payoff. We characterize when sector interactions permit a uniform simultaneous
guarantee of these individual core maxima. Fix a connected graph onK≥2equal-sized sectors.
Full teams contain one player from every sector, their values sum nonnegative pair values on the
allowed edges, and only full teams produce. Coalition worth is the maximum value of an integral
matching. Among markets with positive worth and nonempty core, both full leximin and the
best choice of any core allocation have sharp size-independent guarantee1/K on trees and zero
on graphs containing a cycle. The tree proof describes the entire core by cut coordinates and
uses clipping at every lexicographic level. For a triangular family with pair values in{0, 1, 2},
the entire core is a simplex and the optimal simultaneous fraction is exactly1/(2q + 3). Selective
subdivision transfers vanishing protection to every cyclic allowed graph while preserving a positive
core allocation. The cyclic obstruction therefore concerns what stability can achieve, not merely
the choice of a fairness rule. The classification is conditional on complete-team production and
permits zero-valued allowed edges.
Keywords:assignment game; core; leximin; individual ideal; simultaneous protection; sector graph.

1 Introduction
When production requires partners from several sectors, coalitional stability and individual protection
address different questions. The core rules out a group earning more by producing separately. It
does not ensure that a player receives a substantial share of the most that player could obtain
in another stable agreement. This distinction matters when the core is used as a feasible set for
bargaining: a payoff may be stable yet small relative to the same participant’s attainable stable
payoff.
For an entire coreC, writeHi = maxy∈Cyi. We ask whether one stable allocation can guarantee
pi≥λHi simultaneously for all players, withλdepending only on the number and pattern of
sector interactions, not the number of players. We also ask whether full leximin, which successively
maximizes the smallest nominal payoffs, attains such a uniform guarantee. The benchmarkHi is an
attainable core payoff, not an outside option or a claim that all maxima can be attained together.
We keep this benchmark fixed when comparing allocations within a market.
Our model describes a production project that needs one participant from every sector. Pair
values describe contributions to a complete project’s surplus; an edge does not by itself authorize
1

a productive bilateral coalition. The same sharp graph boundary governs both questions. Trees
guarantee1 /K, and full leximin attains that bound for everyone at once. Every cyclic allowed
graph instead admits growing markets with vanishing best simultaneous protection, even though
each market has a strictly positive core allocation. Thus the negative result cannot be repaired by
selecting a different point of the same core. The poorly protected player may depend on that point;
each individual maximum is nevertheless attained somewhere in the core.
The proof has three parts. A cut-coordinate description of the entire tree core yields a clipping
operation that improves any violation of the1/K floor. A fixed-alphabet triangular construction
has an explicit simplex core and exact optimal fraction1/(2q + 3). Finally, selective subdivision
transfers a unit partial-matching shortfall to all cyclic allowed graphs. The latter two steps keep the
number of sectors fixed as market size grows. The results measure distributional possibilities under
stability, not a loss in aggregate surplus, typical-market performance, or a change in productive
efficiency.
Related work.The multisided assignment model and its core have a substantial literature,
including Quint (1991) and Lucas (1995). The strict supplier–firm–buyer model is associated with
Stuart (1997); Atay and Solymosi (2018), Definition 3 and equations (1)–(2), provide a later full
formulation with signed adjacent-pair values and zero-truncated triple totals. Our nonnegative
three-sector path is a subclass. We claim neither the model nor chain-core nonemptiness as new.
The closest graph comparison is Atay and Núñez (2019). Their Theorem 1 expresses tree
cores through bilateral cores, Corollary 1 gives a tree/cycle strong-balancedness boundary, and
Proposition 2 gives simultaneous maxima within a sector. Their connected partial-sector teams
also produce value. Our core instead enforces strict full-team production and the cyclic theorem
is conditional on its nonemptiness. Thus neither their core decomposition nor their empty-core
cyclic examples establish the protection classification here. The elementary comparison below shows
explicitly how partial-team production changes the individual maxima.
Simultaneous ideal protection has a direct bilateral precedent: the half-optimal core of Núñez
and Rafels requires at least half of each player’s maximum core payoff. The original working text is
Núñez and Rafels,Bargained stable allocations in assignment markets, Barcelona Economics Working
Paper 153, December 28, 2004, Definition 6, printed p.19, with sharpness in Example 7, p.20 (https:
//www.bse.eu/sites/default/files/working_paper_pdfs/153.pdf). The bilateral bargaining
midpoint is also studied by Núñez and Rafels (2008). Neither the benchmark nor the bilateral half
guarantee is proposed as a new fairness concept.
Gangam et al. (2026), p.1621, give a unit-capacity path whose equitable payoff is1/r on each of
its r edges. For that instance, every proper coalition has worth zero, so its entire core is the unit
simplex. Thus each ideal equals one and the best simultaneous fraction is1/r; these are our inferences
from their stated example. Its productive coalition size grows withr, whereas our obstruction
already uses strict triples and pair values in{0, 1, 2}. The distinction is fixed production-team size
within a restricted class, not the first occurrence of vanishing individual protection in cooperative
games.
Extreme-core descriptions for assortative multisided markets (Martínez-de-Albéniz et al., 2020)
and simultaneous extrema under supermodularity and local monotonicity (Martínez-de-Albéniz et al.,
2026) impose different restrictions. A general 2023 core-redescription preprint of the same authors
also retains original inequalities outside a central strip: Martínez-de-Albéniz, Rafels and Ybern,
Extreme core points in multisided assignment problems, 2023 preprint, Theorem 2 and Theorems 3–
4 (https://doi.org/10.2139/ssrn.4369524). That general representation may apply to our
inputs; non-supermodularity does not exclude it. It does not itself give the quantitative protection

Matching duality, individual ideals, leximin, convex averaging and closure of difference constraints

distinct new labels. The same unit budget and first-stage pinning arguments apply; the target’s
diagonal worth and attainable ideal stay one.
Choose a simple cycle in any fixed cyclic allowed graphG. Embed the cycle construction there,
add the other sectors with zero interactions, and put zero matrices on all other allowed edges,
including chords. This is allowed by the model and gives the stated leximin target1/[K(q + 1)]
with ideal one.
For simultaneous protection retain the2q + 2original b,c players inU. Their old ideal-one
witnesses lift without changing those payoffs; they remain unmatched. Thus their enlarged-core
ideals are at least one, and(9) givesρ(MG,q)≤1/(2q + 2). We do not assert equality for longer
cycles. Letting q grow givesR(G) = 0, while nonnegativity and c(G)≤R(G)give c(G) = 0.
Together with the tree proof, this proves Theorem 2.1.
7 Economic scope and reproducibility
The classification identifies when stability itself permits a guarantee relative to every participant’s
individually most favorable stable agreement. For a tree, a single familiar fairness selection meets
the sharp guarantee. For a cyclic graph, the obstruction applies to the best possible selection. Every
finite market still has a positive optimal fraction; the zero guarantee is a worst-case statement as the
number of players per sector grows. The strictly positive witness allocations separate this conclusion
from an empty core or a player who can never be paid positively.
The theorem concerns the entire labeled core and nominal leximin. It does not cover productive
partial-sector coalitions, unequal sector sizes, strictly positive matrices on every allowed edge,
ratio-normalized leximin, or a selected proper face of the core. It does not determine the best
constant at each finite market size. These restrictions delimit the economic application, rather than
follow from the proof. In particular, an arbitrary transportation or extreme-point representation
cannot be assumed to preserve all of them.
