Please consider “Sharp subsidies for truthful production and network cooperation”
by Maxwell Flanagan as an original research article.

The paper asks how much external funding is needed when one payment rule must
both cover every coalition's production value and make payments monotone in
each fixed owner's binding capacity report. For technologies represented by
K nonnegative linear forms, the worst-case total-payment factor is asymptotic
to log K / log log K, with leading constant one. A matching lower bound already
holds for simple directed acyclic flow networks with fixed strategic owners.
The model has payment-only utility and binding underreports. The paper also
gives owner-count and resource-dimension upper bounds whose unrestricted
optimal rates remain open. It makes no polynomial-preprocessing claim.

The economic issue is incentive-compatible selection across profiles: each
individual game already has an efficient core. The paper explicitly separates
this problem from static cost of stability, population monotonicity, and the
restricted architecture barriers in the supplement. Its main argument is
self-contained. Online Resource 1 develops the supplementary results, and
Online Resource 2 supplies scoped exact checks and a source/version map.

Related unpublished manuscripts from this research program study acyclicity
and protection within an original core, and static additional payments for
original-core floors in three-sector matching. They ask different questions
and are not submissions of this cross-report incentive result. Their relationship
is available in the accompanying local overlap note if requested.
