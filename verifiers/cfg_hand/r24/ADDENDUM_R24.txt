ROUND 24, FINAL ROUND -- THE ATTAINMENT QUESTION
Answer: empirically YES at the argmin (0 failures in 165), and the selection
effect is strong (chain saturation fails ~15% of the time in general).  Two
further hand results collapse the question's shape; it is not proved.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
S.  THE SUBADDITIVITY COLLAPSE (proved; it merges the lead's angles 1 and 2)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
f is subadditive on disjoint sets: f(A u B) = max_core y(A u B)
<= max y(A) + max y(B) = f(A) + f(B).  Hence f(A u p) - f(p) <= f(A), so

      f_w(A) = min( f(A), f(A u p) - f(p) )  =  f(A u p) - f(p)   ALWAYS.

The f(A) branch is NEVER strictly active.  Verified on 1050 (game, p, A)
triples.  Three consequences.

 (a) LEMMA M needs only its second branch; branch 1 is vacuous.  The proof is
     now a single line:  2f(p) + (f(B) - f(p))/(|B|-1) >= 3*lambda for every
     B containing p, by f(p) >= lambda and f(B) >= lambda|B|.

 (b) lambda_w = min over B containing p, |B| >= 2, of
     ( f(B) - f(p) ) / ( |B| - 1 ):  the smallest PER-CAPITA MARGINAL over p.

 (c) The lead's angle (1) does not exist.  Attainment at B0 always reads as the
     NESTED chain {p} inside B0 u {p}, never as a disjoint pair.  (Consistently:
     simultaneous attainment of the disjoint caps f(p) and f(B0) would force
     f(B0 u p) = f(p) + f(B0), which by subadditivity is exactly the tie case
     where the two branches coincide.)  So the question is entirely angle (2):
     SINGLETON-ANCHORED chain saturation.

      ATTAINMENT AT B0  <=>  some core point has y_p = f(p) and
                             y(B0 u p) = f(B0 u p)
                        <=>  f({p}) is attained on the face where B0 u {p}
                             sits at its cap.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
N.  LEMMA N (new, proved; an IDENTITY, tight in 4500 of 4500 checks)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Let M contain p, B = M\{p}, C = N\M, and let wh be the reduced game on M at the
face G' = {y in Core : y(M) = f(M)}:
      wh(A) = max over D subset C of [ v(A u D) + v(C\D) - v(C) ],  wh(M) = f(M).
Then
      wh(B)  =  f(M) - f({p}) ,  and hence  f_wh({p}) = wh(M) - wh(B) = f({p}).

PROOF.  ">=" is the single choice D = C: v(B u C) + v(empty) - v(C)
= v(N\p) - v(C) = (T - f(p)) - (T - f(M)) = f(M) - f(p).
"<=" is the content.  Fix D subset C.  The coalitions {p} u (C\D) and
{p} u B u D meet only in p and cover N, so their loads are 2 at p and 1
elsewhere.  The cover bound sum_S f(S) >= sum_k c_k y_k, evaluated at the core
point attaining f({p}) (exactness at N\{p}), gives
      f(p u (C\D)) + f(p u B u D) >= T + f({p}).
Since f(p u (C\D)) = T - v(B u D) and f(p u B u D) = T - v(C\D), this is
      v(B u D) + v(C\D) <= T - f({p}) = v(C) + f(M) - f({p}),
which is the claim for that D.  Take the maximum over D.                     []

WHAT LEMMA N BUYS.  The reduced game at the face has EXACTLY the same singleton
cap for p as the original game.  So chain saturation can never fail for
numerical reasons -- the lower bound the face forces on B always leaves p room
to reach f({p}).  It can fail ONLY through non-exactness of wh at B, i.e.
      min over G' of y(B)  >  wh(B) = f(M) - f({p}).
Equivalently: chain saturation of {p} inside M is exactly the statement that
the face reduced game INHERITS the attainment of p's cap.

and the effect is a genuine selection, not an absence of failures.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
X.  SCOPE CORRECTION: (R) NEEDS STRICTLY LESS THAN ATTAINMENT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
THEOREM(4) has a level form, with the same proof: for any level
ell <= lambda_u, if max over the core of y(E) >= ell|E| for every nonempty E,
then mu_u >= ell.  (The cover reduction needs only ell <= lambda_u for
g_ell >= 0 and g_ell(N) >= 0; Lemmas B, C, E1 then need only (EXell).)
Applying it to the face with ell = 3*lambda - 2 f(p), which is <= lambda_w by
LEMMA M, gives (R) from
      (EXell-FACE)   h(A) >= (3 lambda - 2 f(p)) |A|  for every A subset U,
which is VACUOUS when f(p) >= 3 lambda / 2 and which, at the argmin, has slack
(lambda_w - ell)|B0| to spare.  So attainment at the argmin is SUFFICIENT for
the tight form (EXlam-FACE) but is NOT necessary for (R).  If attainment ever
does fail at an argmin, the reduction is not thereby dead -- the level-ell form
is the fallback, and it is the honest target.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Y.  WHY THE ARGMIN CLAIM IS NOT FREE, and what it is not
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The natural general principle -- "for any game with a nonempty core, the cap at
a lambda-argmin is attained" -- is NOT free.  Dualizing max{y(B0)} gives, for a
dual solution with multiplier eta,
      cost >= f(B0) + eta(T - f(B0))            if eta >= 0   (so >= f(B0)),
      cost >= f(B0) - eps(T - n*lambda)          if eta = -eps < 0,
the same eta < 0 deficit that blocks everything else in this program.  It IS
free when lambda = T/n, since then the deficit vanishes -- another appearance
of the zeroth filter.
It is also NOT the same statement as (EXlam): the four-player balanced witness
of round 1 (core the single point (0,1/3,1/3,1/3), lambda = 1/6, mu = 0) has
its lambda-argmins ab, ac, ad and ALL THREE caps ARE attained, at 1/3 = f.  So
"argmin cap attained" holds there while (EXlam) and mu >= lambda both fail.
The two properties are genuinely different, and the argmin one is the weaker.
