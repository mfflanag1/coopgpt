================================================================================
THE n=6 EXTREMAL CLASS AT RATIO 2/3 -- profile (task #32, Stage B side-task)
================================================================================
Five harvested games attain ratio exactly 2/3.  They fall into TWO classes, and
within each class the normalized solution pair is IDENTICAL up to permutation --
the same rigidity that cor:cfg records at n=5.

CLASS A   Lmin strata (5,1),  Lmax strata (2,4),  overlap [[2,3],[0,1]]
  Lmin = ( 1/9, 1/9, 1/9, 1/9, 1/9, 4/9 )      mu = 1/9
  Lmax = ( 1/18, 1/18, 2/9, 2/9, 2/9, 2/9 )    M  = 2/9,  min Lmax = 1/18 > 0
  gap = 2(1/9 - 1/18) + 3(2/9 - 1/9) + (4/9 - 2/9) = 1/9 + 1/3 + 2/9 = 2/3
  NOT STARVED.

CLASS B   Lmin strata (4,2),  Lmax strata (2,4),  overlap [[2,2],[0,2]]
  Lmin = ( 1/12, 1/12, 1/12, 1/12, 1/3, 1/3 )  mu = 1/12
  Lmax = ( 0, 0, 1/4, 1/4, 1/4, 1/4 )          M  = 1/4,  min Lmax = 0 (twice)
  gap = 2(1/12) + 2(1/4 - 1/12) + 2(1/3 - 1/4) = 1/6 + 1/3 + 1/6 = 2/3
  STARVED, with TWO zeros.

THREE OBSERVATIONS WORTH THE WRITE-UP.

(1) THE EXTREMAL CLASS IS NO LONGER UNIQUE.  At n = 5 every game at 1/2 was in
    the single configuration CFG.  At n = 6 the maximum is attained in two
    structurally different configurations, and they disagree on the property
    that organized the entire n = 5 analysis: Class B is starved, Class A is
    not.  So "the extremal configuration has a zero in Lmax" -- true and
    load-bearing at n = 5 -- FAILS at n = 6.

(2) CLASS B IS THE NATURAL CONTINUATION OF CFG.  Both have exactly four players
    at Lmax = T/4 and the remainder at zero; the zero stratum grows from one
    player to two, the Lmin poor stratum from three to four, the rich stratum
    stays at two.  M = T/4 in both.  Read that way, 1/2 -> 2/3 is the same
    family evaluated at the next n, which is what s_2(n) is meant to capture.

(3) BOTH CLASSES SHARE Lmax SHAPE (2,4) -- a two-player bottom stratum and a
    four-player top -- while their Lmin shapes differ, (5,1) against (4,2).
    If a hand proof of the n = 6 bound is ever attempted, (2,4) is where to
    start: it is the only Lmax shape carrying the maximum.

Data: harvest6_601.json, harvest6_602.json (5 games); this profile is
descriptive only and asserts nothing about configurations the harvest missed.
================================================================================

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
DOES THE CLASS-B FAMILY CONTINUE?  A CLOSED FORM, DERIVED NOT FITTED.
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Define the family structurally, from what n=5 CFG and n=6 Class B share:
    Lmax : four players at T/4, the remaining n-4 at zero
    Lmin : a poor stratum of n-2 at mu, a rich stratum of 2 at rho
    overlap : the poor stratum holds all n-4 zeros plus 2 of the T/4 players;
              the rich pair sits at T/4
Both observed members have mu = 1/(4(n-3)) -- 1/8 at n=5, 1/12 at n=6 -- and
then rho = (1 - (n-2)mu)/2 reproduces 5/16 and 1/3 exactly.  The gap telescopes:

   gap = (n-4)mu + 2(1/4 - mu) + 2(rho - 1/4) = (n-6)mu + 2rho = 1 - 4mu

   RATIO(n) = (n-4)/(n-3).

    n        family      s_2(n)      verdict
    5           1/2         1/3      EXCEEDS s_2
    6           2/3         2/3      TIES s_2
    7           3/4         5/6      undershoots
    8           4/5           1      undershoots
    9           5/6       11/10      undershoots
   10           6/7         6/5      undershoots

SO n = 6 IS EXACTLY THE CROSSOVER.  The CFG family is the champion at n=5 --
where it beats the two-generator value, which is why s(5) = 1/2 > s_2(5) -- it
TIES the two-generator family at n=6, and it is overtaken from n=7 on.  It
saturates at 1 while s_2(n) climbs to 2.

That is a direct answer to "why the G-family shape wins from n=6": it wins
because the CFG family stops winning there.  The n=6 coincidence 2/3 = 2/3 is
the handover point, and it also explains the extremal class splitting in two --
at the crossover both families are champions simultaneously, which is precisely
what the harvest sees (Class B is the CFG family's last win, Class A is not
starved and is presumably the G-family's first).

CAVEAT: this is a claim about ONE family's closed form, verified at its two
observed members.  It predicts 3/4 at n=7 for the CFG shape and says nothing
about what the true s(7) is.  A single designed n=7 instance of the shape above
would confirm or refute the 3/4; not attempted here.
