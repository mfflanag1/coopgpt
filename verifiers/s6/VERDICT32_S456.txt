4.0  PREAMBLE: THE TWO PROCESS ARCHITECTURES, AND WHY BOTH APPEAR HERE.
Two runners produced the Stage-C-era numbers below and they differ in ONE
respect only -- how many z3 solves share an interpreter:
  SHARDED (stageC.py, gate32.py, stageB.py, decide32.py): one long-lived
     python process performs many solves in sequence.  Each program
     carries 62 Theorem-A exactness witness bundles and ~1,700 Farkas
     variables, so a process accumulates state across configurations.
  FRESH (solve1.py driven by freshrun.py): exactly one z3 Solver is built,
     checked, and the interpreter exits.  Nothing accumulates because
     nothing survives.
The MATHEMATICS IS IDENTICAL: both call nsix.pipeline2(6, lmin, lmax, tbl,
2/3) with the same arguments, the same exact rational arithmetic, and the
same wall-clock cap.  This was verified behaviorally before the fresh runs
were trusted: solve1.py returned UNSAT in 3.4s on lmin(4,2) x lmax(2,4),
a configuration stageC.py had independently returned UNSAT on in 3.3s.
The architecture split exists because a 10x wall-clock swing was observed
on one instance between the two (section 6, EVENT 7), and the fresh runner
is the controlled test of whether that swing generalizes.

  This is why the results above separate decided from unknown throughout
  and never report an unknown rate as a property of the mathematics.

EVENT 11 -- THE PROCESS-REUSE HYPOTHESIS: WHAT THE FRESH RUN SETTLES.
  r22's EVENT 7 records the hypothesis and then withdraws it, on the
  ground that unknown% does not rise with position within a gate shard
  (70/30/30/60/50/40 across positions 0-5).  r25-s6 re-derived those
  numbers independently (posanal.py) and confirms them, and adds the
  check EVENT 7 lacked: mean k by position is 4.60/4.40/4.30/4.30/4.40/
  3.90, so position is not confounded with the difficulty driver.
  The null is real.  It is also indirect and small (n=10 per cell; 70%
  vs 30% is about 2 sigma), and it sits against a direct observation
  running the other way: the one configuration re-run in a fresh process
  had been unknown at position 1 -- the position with the LOWEST unknown
  rate -- and resolved 10x faster.  Section 4.4b is the direct test and
  supersedes both.

  THE DISPUTE, AS A THREE-STEP EXCHANGE (the auditor asked for both
  analyses on the record, so the hypothesis can be watched dying).
  STEP 1 -- r22 writes REFUTED.  Unknown% by within-shard position is
    70/30/30/60/50/40 across positions 0-5.  If accumulated interpreter
    state drove the timeouts, later positions would be worse.  They are
    not, and the FIRST position is the worst.  Conclusion recorded:
    'GENUINE INSTANCE HARDNESS, not a harness artifact'.
  STEP 2 -- r25-s6 challenges, and asks for OPEN.  The numbers reproduce
    exactly, and a control r22 did not have confirms the test is at least
    well-posed: mean k by position is 4.60/4.40/4.30/4.30/4.40/3.90, so
    position is not confounded with the difficulty driver.  But n=10 per
    cell gives almost no power (70% vs 30% is about 2 sigma), the test is
    INDIRECT, and it is contradicted by the one DIRECT same-instance
    comparison available: that configuration sat at POSITION 1 -- the
    position r22's own table calls cleanest -- and ran 600.0s in-process
    against 56.5s fresh.  A failure to detect is not a detection of
    absence.
  STEP 3 -- r22 concedes and corrects the ledger to OPEN, in its words:
    "I treated a failure to detect as a detection of absence... That is
     the observation with the most information in it and I discounted it
     because a weaker test disagreed."
  RESOLUTION -- and it goes against the challenger.  The paired uncensored
  experiment of 4.4b was run precisely to settle this, and it REFUTES the
  process-reuse hypothesis on direct evidence: fresh is not faster, it is
  about 1.26x slower, which is what contention alone predicts.  r22's
  original conclusion was CORRECT; its argument was not strong enough to
  carry it; and r25-s6's challenge was well-founded as a challenge and
  wrong as a conclusion.  All three steps are recorded because the
  auditor should see which claim the evidence actually settled, and that
  the correction ran in the direction nobody's ego preferred.
  VERDICT: the process-reuse hypothesis is REFUTED, on paired uncensored
  evidence (4.4b).  r22's EVENT-7 conclusion was right; its argument was
  not sufficient to carry it, and r25-s6's counter-argument was wrong.
  Both are recorded because the audit trail should show which claim the
  evidence actually settled.

EVENT 12 -- WHAT THE INSTRUMENT FOUND INSTEAD: HEAVY-TAILED RUNTIME.
  The paired experiment was built to measure architecture and instead
  measured variance.  On IDENTICAL instances with an IDENTICAL encoding,
  fresh/sharded time ratios span 0.12x to 9.18x, and 7 of 32
  configurations the gate DECIDED (one of them in 8.7s) failed to resolve
  within 600s on the re-run.  A contention factor of ~1.3-1.5x cannot
  produce a 69x swing.  Time-to-verdict on this instance family is
  therefore substantially a matter of search luck.
  CONSEQUENCES, separated by whether they touch soundness:
   (a) SOUNDNESS IS UNTOUCHED.  An UNSAT is an UNSAT however lucky the
       search was.  Every decided verdict stands, and no configuration
       anywhere in this program has returned SAT at 2/3.
   (b) UNKNOWN RATES ARE NOT MEASUREMENTS.  The gate's 46.7% is one draw
       from a heavy-tailed distribution at one cap; it should not be
       quoted as an instance-hardness rate, and the k-gradient of EVENT 8
       is at least partly a gradient in variance rather than in
       difficulty.  Both should be restated in those terms.
   (c) THE CAP IS THE WRONG KNOB.  Section 4.4c shows restarts decide
       configurations at equal total budget that a single long attempt
       does not, including configurations no previous run had ever
       decided.  Spending the remaining compute on longer caps rather
       than on re-rolls is the dominated strategy.

SUMMARY OF PROVENANCE.  Every unknown this program has produced was
cap-exact.  Those re-run under a fresh interpreter are itemized in 4.4
and 4.4b; those re-run under a restart sweep in 4.4c.  No unknown at any
stage has ever been accompanied by a SAT, and the 2/3 bound has not been
contradicted by a single verdict anywhere in the program.
