================================================================================
THE STARVED FAMILY:  A THEOREM AT EVERY n >= 5
six rows, ratio (n-4)/(n-3), proved by progressive leximin
================================================================================

VERDICT.  PROVED.  The six-row constructor is upgraded from four verified
instances to a theorem for every n >= 5.  The proof needs only TWO coalition
values and is uniform in n; no solver appears in it.

A SIMPLIFICATION FOUND WHILE PROVING IT.  The n=7 design used two kinds of pin,
PAIR caps inside the Lmax zero-set and QUAD caps.  The pair caps are REDUNDANT:
the quad caps alone pin the entire poorer side, because y(Z u {k}) <= 2(n-3) is
a sum of n-3 terms each at least 2 under the stage-1 constraint, so every one of
them is exactly 2.  The theorem below therefore rests on two coalition values,
not five.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
THE FAMILY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Fix n >= 5 and put
      T = 8(n-3),   mu = 2,   M = T/4 = 2(n-3),   rho = 3n-10,
      Z = {1,...,n-4}  (the Lmax zero-set),  k1 = n-3,  k2 = n-2,
      r1 = n-1,  r2 = n,   P = Z u {k1,k2}  (|P| = n-2),  R = {r1,r2}.
Note rho > mu and M > 0 for n >= 5.  Let G(n) be the lower envelope of the six
allocations of common total T

    x  = ( mu on P,                 rho, rho )
    z  = ( 0 on Z,  M, M,           M,   M   )
    a1 = ( 0 on Z,  0, M,           h,   h   )        h := (T-M)/2 = 3(n-3)
    a2 = ( 0 on Z,  M, 0,           h,   h   )
    b1 = ( mu on P,                 2rho, 0  )
    b2 = ( mu on P,                 0,    2rho )

All entries are nonnegative and each row totals T:
x gives 2(n-2) + 2(3n-10) = 8n-24; z gives 4M = 8(n-3); a1, a2 give M + 2h =
2(n-3) + 6(n-3) = 8(n-3); b1, b2 give 2(n-2) + 2rho = 8n-24.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
THEOREM.  For every n >= 5, G(n) is exact and singleton-normalized, with
      Lmin(G(n)) = x,   strata (n-2, 2) at levels mu = 2 and rho = 3n-10,
      Lmax(G(n)) = z,   strata (n-4, 4) at levels 0 and M = 2(n-3),
      ||Lmin - Lmax||_1 / T  =  (n-4)/(n-3).
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
PROOF.

STEP 1 (exact, normalized).  By the envelope lemma G(n) is exact and every row
lies in its core; in particular x and z are core points.  Every coordinate
vanishes in some row -- each i in Z in z, k1 in a1, k2 in a2, r1 in b2, r2 in
b1 -- so G(n) is singleton-normalized.

STEP 2 (the two quad caps).  For k in {k1,k2} the set Z u {k} has n-3 elements,
and the row values of g(Z u {k}) are
      x : 2(n-3) = M      z : 0 + M = M       a1 : 0 or M      a2 : M or 0
      b1, b2 : 2(n-3) = M
so max over rows is exactly M, whence
      f(Z u {k}) = M,  equivalently  v(N \ (Z u {k})) = T - M = 3M = 6(n-3).
Since N \ (Z u {k}) consists of three players, this single value is
simultaneously a CAP of size M on the n-3 players of Z u {k} and a coalition
worth 3M on three players.  That coincidence -- T - (n-3)mu = 3T/4 = 3M -- is
what makes the family work, and it holds identically in n.

STEP 3 (Lmin = x), by progressive leximin.
Every core point y obeys y(Z u {k}) <= M for k = k1, k2.
  Stage 1.  If y >= lambda*1 then lambda(n-3) <= y(Z u {k1}) <= M = 2(n-3), so
  lambda <= 2; and x attains lambda = 2.  Hence the stage-1 level is mu = 2.
  Now let y be any core point with y >= 2*1.  Then y(Z u {k1}) <= 2(n-3) is a
  sum of n-3 terms each at least 2, so every one equals 2; the same with k2.
  As Z u {k1} u Z u {k2} = P, we get y = 2 on all of P.  Therefore
  max y_i = 2 for each i in P, and all of P is pinned at stage 1.  No player of
  R is pinned, since x_i = rho > 2 there.
  Stage 2.  With P fixed at 2, efficiency gives y_{r1} + y_{r2} = T - 2(n-2)
  = 2rho.  The largest common lower bound is rho, attained by x, and subject to
  both being at least rho with sum 2rho each equals rho.  So both are pinned at
  rho.
Hence Lmin = x, with n-2 coordinates at 2 and two at rho > 2.

STEP 4 (Lmax = z).
  (a) Every core point has max_i y_i >= M: from Step 2,
  y({k2,r1,r2}) >= v({k2,r1,r2}) = 3M, a sum of three terms.
  (b) Suppose y is a core point with y_i <= M for every i.  Then
  y({k2,r1,r2}) >= 3M with each term at most M forces y_{k2} = y_{r1} = y_{r2}
  = M, and y({k1,r1,r2}) >= 3M forces y_{k1} = M.  The four of them already
  total 4M = T, so y = 0 on Z; that is, y = z.
So the minimum over the core of max_i y_i equals M and is attained only at z.
Lmax minimizes the payoff vector sorted in decreasing order, lexicographically,
and its first entry is max_i y_i; every core point other than z has a strictly
larger first entry.  Hence Lmax = z, with n-4 coordinates at 0 and four at M.

STEP 5 (the ratio).  Coordinatewise, |x - z| equals
      2            on each of the n-4 players of Z,
      M - 2 = 2n-8 on each of k1, k2   (M >= 2 for n >= 4),
      rho - M = n-4 on each of r1, r2  (rho >= M for n >= 4),
so ||x - z||_1 = 2(n-4) + 2(2n-8) + 2(n-4) = 8n - 32 = 8(n-4), and
      ||x - z||_1 / T = 8(n-4) / (8(n-3)) = (n-4)/(n-3).                    []

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
REMARKS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1.  ONLY TWO VALUES ARE USED.  Steps 3 and 4 consume nothing about G(n) beyond
    membership of x and z in the core and the two values v(N \ (Z u {k})) = 3M.
    Any exact singleton-normalized game with those properties has the same Lmin,
    Lmax and ratio.  The six rows are one way to realize them, not the content.

2.  THE n = 5 BOUNDARY, PRECISELY.  At n = 5 we have T = 16, mu = 2, rho = 5,
    M = 4, and |Z| = n-4 = 1 -- the Lmax zero-set is a single player, which is
    the smallest case in which the construction is defined.  The member has
    Lmin = (2,2,2,5,5) = (T/8 x3, 5T/16 x2) and Lmax = (0,4,4,4,4)
    = (0, T/4 x4): exactly the CONFIGURATION CFG of paper5 thm:cfg, with ratio
    1/2 = s(5).  So the family realizes the five-player extremal configuration
    and its bound is TIGHT at n = 5.

    BUT IT IS NOT THE SAME GAME as paper5 thm:n5's extremal example.  Scaling
    that game (the envelope of (1,0,0,1,2) and (0,1,3,0,0)) to T = 16 and
    comparing as UNLABELLED games -- searching all 120 relabellings -- no
    permutation carries one v to the other; 19 coalitions differ even under the
    best alignment.  The two games share the configuration, the selections up
    to relabelling, and the ratio, and they differ as games.  Checked, not
    assumed (n5_boundary.py), because a matching profile is not a matching
    game -- a distinction this campaign has been caught by before.  The useful
    reading: CFG at n = 5 is realized by at least two essentially different
    games, and the family supplies one of them uniformly in n.

3.  WHAT THE THEOREM DOES NOT SAY.  It gives s(n) >= (n-4)/(n-3), a lower
    bound that increases to 1.  It is tight at n = 5 but NOT extremal in
    general: the program's s(n) is known to exceed 1 from n = 8 and to tend to
    2, so this family is beaten for large n.  The theorem is a clean, uniform
    lower-bound family and the n-generic mechanism behind the crossover; it is
    not a claim about the supremum at any n > 5.

4.  NO MINIMALITY is claimed for the six rows.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
MACHINE CHECKS (guards, not premises)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
verify_family.py rebuilds G(n) from the closed form and re-derives everything
from the rows alone, exact rational, single process, for n = 5,...,9:
zero cover; both quad-cap values equal 6(n-3); Lmin and Lmax equal to the
targets; ratio equal to (n-4)/(n-3); and exactness at DEFINITION level by LP
over the core for n <= 8 (at n = 9 exactness is already the envelope lemma).
All pass.  It also confirms Remark 2 numerically.

ONE BUG FOUND AND FIXED in the verifier, not the construction: k1 and k2 sit at
indices n-4 and n-3, and a first version of the sweep read the second quad cap
at index n-2 (a rich player), reporting 4 instead of 6(n-3).  The construction
was never affected; the mis-indexed line is corrected in verify_family.py.

design7.py (the earlier constructor and the standalone n=7 instance).
