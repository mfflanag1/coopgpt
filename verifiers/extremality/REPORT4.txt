1.  TARGET 1 -- DESIGNED EXTREMALITY STRESS-TEST
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Bounds under test: s_2(6) = 2/3 (attained by G(2,3)), s_2(7) = 5/6 (G(2,4) and
G(3,3)).  Driver d29_extremality.py.  Every game is an envelope of nonnegative
common-total rows with every coordinate vanishing in some row, so exactness and
singleton normalization are free; Lmin and Lmax are computed exactly, and any
game above the bound would be re-derived with the independent second algorithm
for each selection before being reported.

POSITIVE CONTROL.  Lmin, Lmax and rho were validated against paper5
thm:family's closed forms on five members of the master family:
   G(2,3) n=6 rho=2/3 | G(3,2) n=6 1/2 | G(1,4) n=6 1/2
   G(2,4) n=7 rho=5/6 | G(3,3) n=7 5/6
all matching 2(p(k-1)-1)/(k(p+1)) exactly, with Lmin by two independent
algorithms and Lmax by two more (staged pinning, and a mirror Sigma_m ladder
with a uniqueness proof) -- so both selections are double-computed before any
hunting starts.

FOUR DESIGNED REGIONS, chosen where neither the two-generator enumeration nor
random harvests look:
  A  starved-Lmax hybrids: one generator concentrated on a single player (the
     n=5 extremal's shape), two or three others spread; the region that
     carries the n=5 mechanism into n>=6.
  B  G-perturbations: the G(p,k) pair at this n under a random relabelling,
     usually with a third generator added.
  C  deep floors: a large coalition against its complement, plus extra rows.
  D  disjoint-support multigen: three or four generators with pairwise disjoint
     supports -- a shape the two-generator class cannot express at all.

RESULTS (snapshots of runs still in progress; extremal_6_1.json is a completed
70 s pilot, ext6.log/ext7.log the long runs):

ZERO games above either bound.  What the regions say, which is the part worth
reporting:

  * REGION A IS THE INFORMATIVE ONE, but the n=6 and n=7 readings differ and
    only the n=6 one is strong.  At n = 6 it tops out at 8/15 = 0.533 against
    a bound of 2/3, over 3,684 games of which 2,377 genuinely starve -- a
    ceiling well below the bound that has not moved as the run has grown.  So
    at n = 6 the starvation mechanism that makes n=5 anomalous does not merely
    fail to help; carrying it up actively COSTS ratio, which is direct evidence
    for paper5's remark that the n=5 extremal class is qualitatively unlike the
    n>=6 one, and it closes the most natural "hybrid" route to beating s_2(6).
    At n = 7 the same region has since reached 2/3 -- still below the 5/6 bound
    but no longer far below, so the "actively costs ratio" reading is NOT
    supported at n=7 and should not be claimed there.

  * CORRECTION (recorded against my own first report).  My initial message gave
    region A's n=7 ceiling as 23/45 = 0.511.  That was a mid-run snapshot and
    the same run has since climbed to 2/3; the qualitative claim (region A
    stays under the bound) survives, the number does not.  This is exactly the
    staleness failure I flagged as finding R3 in the splice audit, reproduced
    in my own reporting the same day: a number read off a running job is not a
    result.  Any figure from this table that is quoted anywhere must be taken
    from the completed extremal_*.json, not from ext6.log / ext7.log.
  * REGION B reaches the bound often (484 and 268 ties) and never exceeds it,
    even with a third generator added to the G pair -- i.e. perturbing the
    known extremal family does not improve it.
  * REGION C reaches 2/3 at n=6 from a completely different construction
    (4 ties, deep-floor shapes rather than G shapes), which is mild evidence
    that 2/3 is a genuine ceiling rather than an artifact of the G family.
  * REGION D IS A CLEAN STRUCTURAL FACT AT n=6: 4,820 games with three or four
    pairwise-disjoint-support generators, best rho EXACTLY 0 -- Lmin = Lmax on
    every one.  At n=7 the same region reaches 8/15, so this is not a general
    fact about disjoint supports; it is specific to n=6, and it is a
    conjecture-sized observation the campaign has not recorded.  Worth a hand
    look: disjoint-support envelopes at n=6 appear to be rigidity games.

HONEST LIMIT.  This is a designed zero-failure outcome, not a proof.  Per
campaign law it is worth exactly what its regions cover, which is why the table
above reports the regions and their ceilings rather than a single total.
Region A's ceiling is the substantive contribution; the rest is coverage.

2.  TARGET 2 -- FAITHFULNESS OF THE n=6 ENCODING (all four controls pass)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Driver d30_lab6.py, mode `control`.  The lean encoding fixes S (removing the 62
one-hot booleans and making v' concrete) and drops the hypotheses the ablation
showed are not consumed.  Controls, all with every value pinned:

  the n=5 Theorem-1 game, H*-left target      sat     (must be sat)
  the n=5 Theorem-1 game, Hhat-leftmin target unsat   (must be unsat)
  n=6 counterexample A, H*-left target        sat     (must be sat)
  n=6 counterexample B, H*-left target        sat     (must be sat)

The second row is the sharper control and it was not in the assignment: the
same game must be ACCEPTED by the H* target and REJECTED by the Hhat target,
because it refutes the first containment and satisfies the second.  An
encoding that accepted both would be vacuous.

TWO HARNESS BUGS THE CONTROLS CAUGHT (mine, not the encoding's; recorded
because both are easy to repeat):
  (a) I first pinned the C-C-LEFT counterexamples into the Hhat target.  They
      satisfy W subset Hhat, so unsat was correct and my expectation was wrong.
      A control's expected value is part of the control.
  (b) lib12.suffixes returns the STRATUM INDEX j, while the lab keys cuts by
      CUT POSITION (players below the cut).  These coincide only when every
      lower stratum is a singleton, so the conversion is invisible on exactly
      the examples one tests first.  And the encoding assumes players are
      indexed in ascending phi order; both n=6 games violate that, so pinning
      needs an explicit relabelling with v permuted along.  Until both were
      fixed the controls returned unsat -- i.e. they failed loudly rather than
      silently, which is the only reason this is a footnote and not a defect.

3.  TARGET 2 -- THE WALL, LOCATED
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Shape gaps=(3,4), c*=3, S={2,3,4,5}, target leftmin, 120 s cap, groups added
incrementally (d30_lab6.py plus wall6.txt / wall6b.txt):

    core,hmin                        sat        0.0 s     141 assertions
    core,cert,hmin                   sat        0.0 s     398
    core,cert2,hmin                  sat        0.2 s     957
    core,exv2,hmin                   sat        0.4 s   4,109
    core,exv2,cert,hmin              sat        0.7 s   4,366
    core,exv2,cert2,hmin         UNKNOWN      120.4 s   4,925
    core,exv2,cert,cert2,hmin    UNKNOWN      120.5 s   5,182

So it is NOT the exactness witnesses (4,109 assertions, 0.4 s) and NOT the phi'
certificate family (957 assertions, 0.2 s).  It is precisely their CONJUNCTION.
The mechanism is clear from the encoding: cert2's support conditions are 6
blocks of 62 implications "lambda_T > 0 => phi'(T) = v'(T)", each of which asks
which coalitions are phi'-tight, and every one of those tightness questions is
now coupled to the 62 witness blocks that place 62 core points of v'.  The
solver is doing a disjunctive search over tightness patterns with a 3,900-
constraint linear system hanging off each branch.

Confirming that reading, restricting cert2 to k of the 6 levels -- still a
NECESSARY condition, so an unsat there would still decide the theorem -- shows
the cost growing with the number of certificate blocks and not with anything
else (wall6b.txt):
    1 level  sat 1.0 s | 2 levels sat 1.6-3.4 s | 3 levels sat 3.6-5.9 s
    6 levels unknown at 120 s
All of those are sat, so the weakened systems do not decide anything; they only
localize the blowup.

CONSEQUENCE.  n=6 is not decidable by this encoding as written, and the
obstruction is structural rather than a matter of tuning: any encoding that
imposes exactness by existential witnesses AND leximin by support-conditioned
Farkas certificates will pay this product.  Breaking it needs one of the two
sides made non-disjunctive -- e.g. a sound a-priori bound on the certificate
multipliers permitting a Big-M/boolean formulation, or a Caratheodory-style
restriction of each certificate to at most n coalitions with a cheap selector.
I did not find a sound version of either within this round.

STATUS: C-C^ at n=6 remains a conjecture.  Per the assignment, reporting the
wall precisely and stopping.
