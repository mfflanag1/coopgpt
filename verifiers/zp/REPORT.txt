==============================================================================
TASK #21 - THE ZERO-PAYOFF RESIDUE AT n=7
==============================================================================

Two items were open from the n=7 census:
  (1) the j != e branch of the zero-payoff impossibility;
  (2) the two resisting admissible types, poorer-part profiles (4,0) and
      (2,2), to be realized or refuted.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
VERDICTS
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  (1) j != e branch                    : PROVED  (Theorem 8, PROOF.txt)
  (2) profile (4,0)  [type A]          : REFUTED - proved impossible
      profile (2,2)  [type B]          : OPEN - reduced to one conjecture

The j != e branch is closed not by analysing that case directly but by
showing it cannot recur: each occurrence deletes a null player and drops m
by one, while the phase theorem keeps m >= 3, so the descent is well
founded and must terminate in the j = e case, which is contradictory when
q = 0.  That same descent is what refutes profile (4,0).

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
WHAT WAS PROVED (full statements and proofs in PROOF.txt)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  Lemma 1  a ZP configuration forces phi_e = 0            [k-fold RO]
  Lemma 2  min phi = 0 forces a single player to be zero on the whole core,
           and that player is null                        [convexity]
  Lemma 3  deleting a null player preserves exactness, the core, Lmin,
           tightness and the slack of every boundary avoiding it
  Lemma 4  the converse: suspending a null player over a PART configuration
           creates a ZP configuration of the same profile
  Thm 5    no PART(p,0): it would make a rich singleton tight, forcing
           phi_g = v({g}) = 0 against strictness of the boundary
  Thm 6    every ZP(p,q) descends to PART(p,q) or to ZP(p-1,q) / ZP(p,q-1)
           with one fewer poorer player
  Cor 7    no ZP configuration at all when m = 3          [phase theorem]
  Thm 8    no ZP(p,0) at any m   ===>  the n=7 profile (4,0) is IMPOSSIBLE
  Cor 9    every ZP question is a PARTITION question one level down

Machine verification: Lemmas 2 and 3 were checked exhaustively on n=5
games - 116/116 games with min phi = 0 had a common zero core player, it
was null in every case, and deletion preserved exactness, Lmin, every
tight set and every slack boundary.  Lemma 1 was checked on all 32
harvested ZP witnesses: phi_e = 0 in 32/32.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
THE RESIDUE: ONE CONJECTURE
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  CONJECTURE.  PART(p,q) is realizable exactly when p,q >= 1 and
               max(p,q) >= 3.

  The half p,q >= 1 is Theorem 5.  The half max(p,q) >= 3 is open and is
  the entire remaining content of the ZP question, because

    ZP(2,2) at m=5 -> PART(2,2) at m=4              [max = 2]
                   or ZP(1,2)/ZP(2,1) at m=4
                        -> PART(1,2)/PART(2,1) at m=3   [max = 2]
                        or ZP at m=3                    [Corollary 7]

  so the conjecture implies type B is impossible and closes the residue.

EVIDENCE (h=2 slack boundaries in exact games; exact arithmetic):

  random scan       : 242141 games, 21862 h=2 slack boundaries
      n=5 (m=3): 137221 games, 6920 boundaries
      n=6 (m=4): 70359 games, 8431 boundaries
      n=7 (m=5): 34561 games, 6511 boundaries
  targeted scan     : 139422 games in the frozen-total slice, 11396 boundaries

  ZP configurations observed:
      n7m5profile(1,3)             105
      n7m5profile(3,1)             107
  PARTITION configurations observed:
      n6m4split(1,3)               65
      n6m4split(3,1)               75
      n7m5split(1,4)               134
      n7m5split(2,3)               205
      n7m5split(3,2)               233
      n7m5split(4,1)               145
  targeted-slice partition configurations:
      m4split(1,3)                 20
      m4split(3,1)                 19

  Read off: no configuration of either kind at m=3; at m=4 the splits
  (1,3) and (3,1) occur and (2,2) never does; at m=5 every split with both
  parts nonempty occurs.  And the ZP profiles realized at m=5 are exactly
  (1,3) and (3,1) - precisely the PART splits at m=4, as Corollary 9
  predicts.  That agreement is the strongest single check on the whole
  descent picture.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
THE FINITE-FEASIBILITY ROUTE: WHERE IT STOPS
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

On n <= 6 the cone of exact games is cut out by the catalogue:
      v exact  <=>  sum_{S proper} lambda_S v(S) <= r v(N)
for every permuted min-semi-balanced system - 280 inequalities at n=5 and
7,006 at n=6.  Validated in exactcone.py: 200 exact games violate none and
20 non-exact games each violate one at n=5; 60 exact games violate none at
n=6 (the converse direction is not run at n=6 because deciding exactness by
definition needs exponential vertex enumeration).

So everything in the PART(p,q) question is exactly encodable EXCEPT
phi = Lmin.  Three relaxations were tried: the plain cone LP; plus leximin
stage 1 as a quantified linear condition; plus the one-packet theorem as a
disjunction over all delta>0 packet certificates.  All three are
SATISFIABLE for PART(1,2) at n=5, and every model fails the reality check -
rebuilding the game from the model's v and recomputing Lmin gives a
different leximin point at which H is not even a strict boundary.  The
obstruction is the multi-stage leximin condition, not exactness and not the
packet structure.  A decisive LP needs the full leximin encoding: fix the
stratification of the poorer side and add one quantified pinning condition
per stage.  That is the recommended continuation.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
WHY THE THRESHOLD IS PLAUSIBLY 3
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

If a PART game were the direct sum of its two sides, each side would be an
exact game in which its rich player is uniquely richest and {f} is slack,
so the phase theorem at h=1 would force at least m_min(1) = 4 poorer
players per side.  PART games are not direct sums - their core is strictly
inside the product of the two side cores - which is why the observed
threshold is 3 rather than 4.  Quantifying that gap is the natural route to
a proof of the conjecture.
