CONJECTURE (paper4, end of the monotonicity section).  No monotonicity failure
occurs at an exact-partition phi -- one where every cumulative union of the
richest payoff strata is tight, i.e. every richer suffix H_j has
phi(H_j) = v(H_j), so no slack richer suffix and no Hhat exists.

HEADLINE
--------
PROVED AT n = 5, and non-vacuously so.  All 15 gap patterns UNSAT under the
complete encoding, while 7 of those 15 shapes do admit admissible exact bumps
at exact partition -- so there is something to decide and the decision is
"no failure".  Two structural lemmas about exact partition fall out for free
and explain the shape pattern.  n >= 6 is open; designed searches running.

COROLLARY (n = 5).  Every five-player exact-domain monotonicity failure has a
slack richer suffix.  Combined with Theorem 3 of task #12 this closes the
five-player story: Hhat always exists at a failure and W subset Hhat subset S
always holds.  It also discharges, at n = 5, the hypothesis paper4 needed in
order to apply the four/five threshold to ALL failures rather than only to
those with a slack boundary.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2.  TWO FREE STRUCTURAL LEMMAS ABOUT EXACT PARTITION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
LEMMA EP1.  If x in core(v) and every richer suffix of x is x-tight, then x is
the leximin-maximal element of core(v).

PROOF.  Apply Lemma L (PROOF.txt Part I), whose certificate condition is
sufficient as well as necessary.  At cut j take lambda = 1_{H_j} and nu = 1:
H_j is proper and x-tight, so the support condition holds; the cover is 1 = nu
at every i in H_j and 0 <= nu - 1 = 0 at every i in B_j.  At the top cut take
lambda = 0, nu = 1.  Every cut therefore has a certificate.              []

Consequence: at exact partition "phi = Lmin(v)" is not an extra hypothesis --
it follows from phi in core(v) plus tightness of the suffixes.  This is why the
group "cert" is not in the minimal set above, and it means any hand proof of
the conjecture may treat the base side as given.

LEMMA EP2.  Let v({i}) = 0 for all i and let phi be at exact partition with
k >= 2 strata.  Then the top stratum B_k has at least two players.

PROOF.  H_{k-1} = B_k is tight, so phi(B_k) = v(B_k).  If B_k = {r} then
phi_r = v({r}) = 0.  But for i in B_{k-1} we have phi_r > phi_i >= v({i}) = 0,
so phi_r > 0 -- a contradiction.                                          []

Consequence: exact partition is incompatible with a strict payoff gap at the
last cut.  That is precisely the 8 UNSAT rows of the bumpexists control, so the
control's shape pattern is fully explained rather than mysterious.

VERIFICATION (d24_ep.py part a): 400 random exact games, of which 355 are at
exact partition; EP1 holds 355/355 (a Lemma-L certificate exists at every cut)
and EP2 holds 355/355 (top stratum has >= 2 players).  Note in passing how
COMMON exact partition is -- 355 of 400 -- which is the right way to read
paper4's 15,824 exact-partition bases: they are cheap to sample, and it is the
admissible bump windows inside them that are scarce.

3.  WHY THE PREVIOUS EVIDENCE WAS WEAK, AND WHAT IS STILL OPEN
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
paper4's evidence was 15,824 exact-partition bases, 1,613 admissible bump
windows and 10,239 exact bumps with no failure, "against the two hundred
expected at the rate observed on slack-bearing bases".  That framing is now
sharper in two ways.  First, at n = 5 the answer is not a rate at all: it is
zero by decision.  Second, the expected-count argument was never sound, because
Lemma EP1/EP2 show exact-partition bases are a structurally different
population, not a random sub-sample of slack-bearing ones -- the rate observed
on slack-bearing bases is not the right null.

OPEN: n >= 6.  The lab does not scale (the C-C round established that: unknown
at a 300 s timeout on the first n=6 shape).  Designed searches are running:
  d24_ep.py n=6  1,729 games -> 1,200 exact-partition bases -> 40 windows
                 -> 320 exact bumps -> 0 failures   [snapshot]
  d24_ep.py n=7    346 games ->   200 exact-partition bases ->  2 windows
                 ->  16 exact bumps -> 0 failures   [snapshot]
  d25_epfam.py    the family trick applied here too: phase 1 collected 22+
                 window-bearing exact-partition bases at n=6, phase 2 perturbs
                 them (results in epfam_6_51.json)
Honest note on volume: paper4's existing random evidence at exact partition
(1,613 windows, 10,239 exact bumps) is still far larger than what these runs
have accumulated, so at n >= 6 this round adds method, not yet weight -- the
contribution at n >= 6 is the family generator, which is the only way seen so
far to manufacture window-bearing exact-partition bases in bulk.
NO SHORT HAND PROOF EITHER (d26_epcore.py).  The small hypothesis VOCABULARY
turns out not to mean a small proof.  Minimizing the core of the shape
gaps=(1,), S={1,2} greedily takes 1384 assertions -> 482 (z3) -> 302
IRREDUNDANT, each of which alone restores satisfiability.  Composition:
    223  cert2   (the Lemma-L certificate machinery for phi')
     44  exv2    (exactness witnesses of v')
     20  core_phi,  2 core_phi2,  1 expart,  and the shape/normalization rows
So exact partition enters through exactly one equation (there is a single cut
in this shape) and essentially the whole proof burden sits in phi's leximin
certificate.  That is a LARGER irredundant core than the C-C two-strata case
(302 vs 72), so a hand proof looks less plausible here, not more -- the
opposite of what the small hypothesis set suggested.  Recommendation: record
the exact-partition statement at n=5 with finite-check status, as was done for
mono:thm:cchat, rather than treating it as awaiting a hand proof.
