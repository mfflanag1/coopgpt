CONJECTURE (paper4, end of the monotonicity section).  No monotonicity failure
occurs at an exact-partition phi -- one where every cumulative union of the
richest payoff strata is tight, i.e. every richer suffix H_j has
phi(H_j) = v(H_j), so no slack richer suffix and no Hhat exists.

HEADLINE
--------
PROVED AT n = 5, and non-vacuously so.  All 15 gap patterns UNSAT under the
complete encoding, while 7 of those 15 shapes do admit admissible exact bumps
at exact partition -- so there is something to decide and the decision is
"no failure".  Two structural lemmas about exact partition fall out for free
and explain the shape pattern.  n >= 6 is open; designed searches running.

COROLLARY (n = 5).  Every five-player exact-domain monotonicity failure has a
slack richer suffix.  Combined with Theorem 3 of task #12 this closes the
five-player story: Hhat always exists at a failure and W subset Hhat subset S
always holds.  It also discharges, at n = 5, the hypothesis paper4 needed in
order to apply the four/five threshold to ALL failures rather than only to
those with a slack boundary.

2.  TWO FREE STRUCTURAL LEMMAS ABOUT EXACT PARTITION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
LEMMA EP1.  If x in core(v) and every richer suffix of x is x-tight, then x is
the leximin-maximal element of core(v).

PROOF.  Apply Lemma L (PROOF.txt Part I), whose certificate condition is
sufficient as well as necessary.  At cut j take lambda = 1_{H_j} and nu = 1:
H_j is proper and x-tight, so the support condition holds; the cover is 1 = nu
at every i in H_j and 0 <= nu - 1 = 0 at every i in B_j.  At the top cut take
lambda = 0, nu = 1.  Every cut therefore has a certificate.              []

Consequence: at exact partition "phi = Lmin(v)" is not an extra hypothesis --
it follows from phi in core(v) plus tightness of the suffixes.  This is why the
group "cert" is not in the minimal set above, and it means any hand proof of
the conjecture may treat the base side as given.

LEMMA EP2.  Let v({i}) = 0 for all i and let phi be at exact partition with
k >= 2 strata.  Then the top stratum B_k has at least two players.

PROOF.  H_{k-1} = B_k is tight, so phi(B_k) = v(B_k).  If B_k = {r} then
phi_r = v({r}) = 0.  But for i in B_{k-1} we have phi_r > phi_i >= v({i}) = 0,
so phi_r > 0 -- a contradiction.                                          []

Consequence: exact partition is incompatible with a strict payoff gap at the
last cut.  That is precisely the 8 UNSAT rows of the bumpexists control, so the
control's shape pattern is fully explained rather than mysterious.

VERIFICATION (d24_ep.py part a): 400 random exact games, of which 355 are at
exact partition; EP1 holds 355/355 (a Lemma-L certificate exists at every cut)
and EP2 holds 355/355 (top stratum has >= 2 players).  Note in passing how
COMMON exact partition is -- 355 of 400 -- which is the right way to read
paper4's 15,824 exact-partition bases: they are cheap to sample, and it is the
admissible bump windows inside them that are scarce.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
5.  THE phi-SIDE MACHINERY CANNOT CLOSE THIS CASE (a correction to route 3)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The natural suggestion is that at exact partition the master inequality (MA) is
unusually strong, because every richer suffix is tight and so Lemma-L
certificates exist with suffix support and nu = 1.  It is the other way round.

At exact partition the GUARANTEED certificate at cut j is exactly
lambda = 1_{H_j}, nu = 1 (Lemma EP1), and its (MA) instance reads
      sum_i cover_i Delta_i >= 0   with cover = 1_{H_j},   i.e.   Delta(H_j) >= 0,
which is literally Proposition D.  So at exact partition (MA) carries NO
information beyond Prop D: the very tightness that makes the certificate exist
also makes it trivial.

Decisive check: the whole system MINUS cert2 -- that is, core + superadditivity
+ exactness of v + exactness of v' + Lemma L for phi + "worse" + exact
partition -- is SAT at gaps=(1,), (2,3) and (1,2,3).  No combination of the
phi-side tools (Prop D, MA, L-out, Lemma WIN, Theorem A) can therefore prove
the conjecture; the entire force sits on the phi' side.  That matches the
minimal core exactly: 223 of its 302 irredundant assertions are cert2.

6.  THE "UN-EXCLUDED SHAPE" IS NOT RARE -- IT IS UNIVERSAL (route 2, sharpened)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
paper4 notes that Theorem A rules out a loser set that is a richer suffix but
not one that is "slack without being a suffix, which is the shape of
Example mono:ex:notstratum" -- the tight top stratum redistributing internally.
Lemma EP2 already says the top stratum always has >= 2 players at exact
partition, so the mechanism is structurally available everywhere.  Driver
d27_epredist.py measures how often it actually fires, over certified exact
bumps of exact-partition bases:

                                        n = 5        n = 6
   certified exact bumps                  200           80
   Delta identically zero                   0            0
   some player strictly loses             200           80
   ... a loser INSIDE S                     0            0
   a stratum redistributes internally     144           64
   loser set IS a richer suffix             0            0
   loser set slack-but-NOT-a-suffix       200           80

So the reading has to be inverted.  The dangerous shape is not a rare loophole
that the search failed to reach: at exact partition it is what happens EVERY
time.  Every admissible exact bump moves phi, every one of them produces strict
losers, the loser set is ALWAYS slack-but-not-a-suffix, and in about 75% of
them a stratum redistributes internally.  What never happens is the one thing
the conjecture forbids: a loser inside S.  The correct statement of the
phenomenon is therefore not "the un-excluded shape does not occur" but "the
un-excluded shape occurs always, and always outside the bumped coalition".

A typical instance (n = 5): rows (6,0,3,8,7), (0,0,5,19,0), (2,18,0,0,4),
(0,12,0,12,0); phi = (9/2, 5, 9/2, 5, 5) at exact partition; S = {2,4},
alpha = 17/8; phi' = (9/2, 81/16, 9/2, 81/16, 39/8).  The stratum {2,4,5}
redistributes internally: 2 and 4 (both in S) gain 1/16 each and 5 (outside S,
same stratum) pays 1/8.  The bump's gain to S is financed by the members of S's
own stratum that lie outside S.
