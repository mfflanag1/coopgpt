3.  NEW LEMMA (L-out): OUTSIDER SLACK, FREE STRENGTHENING OF THEOREM B
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
LEMMA L-out.  Suppose only that v'(T) is attained on core(v') for every T
containing S -- the same fragment Theorem B consumes.  Then for EVERY nonempty
A contained in N \ S,          s_phi(A) >= alpha - s_phi(S) = rho .

PROOF.  Put T = S u A.  If T is proper, exactness of v' at T gives z in core(v')
with z(T) = v'(T) = v(T) (T =/= S because A is nonempty); if T = N take any
z in core(v'), where z(N) = v(N) holds automatically.  Either way
  v(T) = z(S) + z(A) >= (v(S) + alpha) + v(A).
With phi(T) >= v(T) and phi(T) = phi(S) + phi(A),
  s_phi(A) = phi(A) - v(A) >= v(S) + alpha - phi(S) = rho.                []

paper4's Theorem B states only the two extreme cases: A = {j} (part ii) and
A = N \ S (part iii).  Every intermediate A is free.  Reading: not merely the
outsiders as a block, but every sub-coalition of them, must carry slack at
least rho -- a much stronger prefilter on where a failure can sit.
  VERIFIED 66/66 on every certified instance on record: paper4's two examples,
  the n=5 faithfulness witness, the n=5 C-C-left counterexample, both n=6
  counterexamples, and the 60-record n=5 corpus (driver d21_lout.py part a).

4.  HAND PROOF: A SHARP REDUCTION AND A SHARP OBSTRUCTION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
(4a) THE VIOLATION HYPOTHESIS IS SOMETIMES UNNECESSARY.  Running the lab with
the failure requirement dropped (target right_nofail: only an admissible exact
bump, alpha > s_phi(S)) separates the shapes:
   gaps=(4,) c*=4   -- TWO strata --            unsat  (no failure needed)
   gaps=(3,) c*=3                               sat    (failure needed)
So the right inclusion is not one statement but two regimes, and the two-strata
regime is a statement about exact bumps alone.

(4b) THE TWO-STRATA CASE, MINIMAL HYPOTHESES (driver d19_min.py).  For
n = 5, gaps=(4,), c*=4, target right_nofail:
   core,sup,exv,exv2,cert,cert2,worse,hstar   unsat
   core,sup,exv,exv2,cert,hstar               unsat
   core,sup,exv2,cert,hstar                   unsat
   core,exv2,cert,hstar                       unsat     <-- minimal
   core,sup,exv,exv2,hstar   (no cert)        SAT
   core,sup,exv,cert,hstar   (no exv2)        SAT
So the sharpest available statement of the five-player right inclusion is:

  CLAIM (two strata).  Let v satisfy v({i}) = 0, let phi be the leximin-maximal
  element of core(v), and suppose phi has exactly two payoff strata, B_1 at
  level mu and {r} at level nu > mu.  If v' = v^{S,alpha} is exact and
  alpha > s_phi(S), then r in S.

  Hypotheses needed: core membership, Lemma L for phi at the single cut, and
  exactness of v'.  NOT needed: exactness of the base game, superadditivity,
  phi' and its certificate, and the monotonicity failure itself.
  This is the cleanest remaining hand-proof target in the whole question, and
  it is a genuine reduction: no phi' means no two-sided master-inequality
  argument is required.

(4c) THE OBSTRUCTION, PINNED DOWN (driver d21_lout.py part b).  The natural
attempt is to replace the full exactness of v' by its consequence L-out (which
consumes only exactness at supersets of S).  It does not work:
   S = {1,2}    core+cert+exv2  unsat        core+cert+L-out  SAT
   S = {1,2,3}  core+cert+exv2  unsat        core+cert+L-out  SAT
   S = {1,2,3,4} core+cert+exv2 unsat        core+cert+L-out  SAT
So exactness of v' at coalitions that do NOT contain S is doing irreducible
work even in the two-strata case.  This is summary2's localization finding
("a proof must use exactness at coalitions NOT containing S") in its sharpest
form: not a statistical non-correlation over a harvest, but a two-line
SAT/UNSAT separation on a fixed shape.  Any hand proof must therefore use a
draining witness -- a core(v) point z with z(C) = v(C) for some C not
containing S, which by Lemma WIN still satisfies z(S) >= v(S) + alpha.
(4d) THE MINIMAL CORE, AND WHY THERE IS NO SHORT PROOF (d22_mincore.py).
Starting from z3's unsat core (76 of 1067 assertions) and dropping assertions
greedily while the system stays unsat leaves 72 IRREDUNDANT assertions for
S = {1,2}: every one of them, dropped alone, makes the system satisfiable.
(Greedy gives a minimal set, not necessarily a minimum one, so 72 is an upper
bound on the shortest such list -- but z3's core was already nearly
irredundant, only 4 came off.)  Its composition is the proof skeleton:
  * certificate rows ONLY at the rich player and at the members of S
    (cert_rich_cover, cert_poor_cover_1, cert_poor_cover_2) -- not at the
    members of B_1 outside S;
  * lam_support at every coalition CONTAINING the rich player, and mere
    nonnegativity at the coalitions inside B_1;
  * core_phi only at the supersets of S ({1,2,3}, {1,2,5}, {1,2,3,5},
    {1,2,4,5}) -- these are the L-out inequalities;
  * exactness witnesses of v' at the SINGLETONS of S and at those same
    supersets of S, each used through two or three of its core inequalities.
So a hand proof must combine an L-out bound at every superset of S, a DRAINING
witness at a singleton of S, and the certificate rows at the rich player and at
S's members -- and the irredundancy count says it will not be a three-line
argument in these terms.

STATUS: no hand proof of either half was obtained.  The reduction (4b) plus the
obstruction (4c) is the partial, and it is sharp in both directions -- it says
exactly which hypotheses a proof may use and which it may not do without.
