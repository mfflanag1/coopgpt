================================================================================
THEOREM D BY HAND:  NO PART CONFIGURATION EXISTS AT m = 3
================================================================================

VERDICT.  COMPLETE.  Theorem D is upgraded from finite-check status to a hand
theorem.  The proof is four pages of linear algebra in THREE variables, with no
case tree over stratifications, no enumeration of tight families, and no solver.
The finite checks of the previous round (48 UNSAT in decisive.py, 3,161 CEGAR
nodes in families.py) and the cone enumeration of normalform.py are now
CROSS-CHECKS, not premises.  Nothing below rests on a machine result.

The mechanism also answers the second open question of the previous round:
it identifies WHY the threshold sits where it does, and exactly which step
stops working at m = 4.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
STATEMENT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
THEOREM D.  Let v be exact and singleton-normalized, phi = Lmin(v), and let
H = {f,g} be a strict richer-suffix boundary with s_phi(H) > 0 and |L| = 3.
Then there is no PART configuration at H.  Equivalently, PART(1,2) and
PART(2,1) do not exist.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
NOTATION AND TWO REDUCTIONS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
N = L u H, H = {f,g}, |L| = 3.  A PART configuration is a pair of phi-tight
coalitions S1 = P u {f} and S2 = Q u {g} with P, Q partitioning L.  By
Theorem 5' of PROOF.txt (no PART(p,0)) both P and Q are nonempty, so
{|P|,|Q|} = {1,2}.

REDUCTION 1 (the f/g symmetry).  Interchanging the labels (f,P,S1) with
(g,Q,S2) is a relabelling of the same structure and carries a PART(p,q)
configuration to a PART(q,p) one.  So it suffices to treat |P| = 1.  Write

        P = {a},   Q = {b,c},   S1 = af,   S2 = bcg.

Interchanging b and c is likewise a game isomorphism fixing the configuration,
and is used once, explicitly flagged.

REDUCTION 2 (no null poorer player).  If min_{i in L} phi_i = 0 then Lemma 6 of
PROOF.txt drops the configuration to a PART(0,2) or PART(1,1) at m = 2, which
are impossible by Theorem 5' and Theorem 7 respectively.  So from here on

        phi_i > 0 for every i in L,  and phi_f, phi_g > 0,

and consequently NO SINGLETON IS phi-TIGHT.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
LEMMA N (THE THREE-DIMENSIONAL NORMAL FORM)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Every core point x satisfies x(S1) = v(S1) and x(S2) = v(S2).  Writing

        u = x_a - phi_a,   v_b = x_b - phi_b,   v_c = x_c - phi_c,

the core is a polytope K in the three variables (u, v_b, v_c) containing the
origin, with

        x_f - phi_f = -u,        x_g - phi_g = -(v_b + v_c).

Under d = (u, v_b, v_c, -u, -(v_b+v_c)) the thirty coalition functionals d(T)
collapse onto twenty-one linear forms: the zero form, attained exactly at
T = af and T = bcg, and ten forms up to sign,

    +-u,  +-v_b,  +-v_c,  +-(v_b+v_c),  +-(u+v_b),  +-(u+v_c),
    +-(u+v_b+v_c),  +-(u-v_b),  +-(u-v_c),  +-(u-v_b-v_c).

The forms that never occur are exactly the three separating b from c with
opposite signs: v_b - v_c, u + v_b - v_c, u - v_b + v_c.

PROOF.  Block rigidity is Theorem 1 of PROOF.txt: v(S1) + v(S2) = phi(S1) +
phi(S2) = phi(N) = v(N), and two core inequalities summing to an equality are
equalities.  So x(af) = v(af) and x(bcg) = v(bcg) on the whole core; solving
for x_f and x_g gives the displayed substitutions, and d(af) = u - u = 0,
d(bcg) = v_b + v_c - (v_b + v_c) = 0.  The remaining twenty-eight forms are read
off coalition by coalition.  The absent forms are absent because b and c enter
d only through v_b, v_c with coefficient +1 (membership) and through g with the
common coefficient -1, so no coalition can give them opposite signs.        []

The last sentence is the combinatorial fingerprint of S2: holding b and c
together in one tight block is exactly what removes the three separating forms,
and it is what the whole proof exploits.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
LEMMA X (THE EXCLUSION LIST)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Of the thirty proper coalitions, THIRTEEN cannot be phi-tight:

    a, b, c        phi_i > 0                              (Reduction 2)
    f, g           phi_f, phi_g > 0                       (strict boundary)
    fg             H is slack
    bfg, cfg, bcfg Lemma 3 of PROOF.txt
    afg            crossing budget with S2
    abf, acf       crossing budget with S2
    abcg           crossing budget with S1

Seventeen remain, carrying TWELVE distinct nonzero normals (af and bcg carry
the zero normal; bc and abcf share one, bg and abfg share one, cg and acfg
share one):

    id  coalition(s)  normal (u, v_b, v_c)   numeric consequence if tight
     1  ab            ( 1,  1,  0)           --
     2  ac            ( 1,  0,  1)           --
     3  bc | abcf     ( 0,  1,  1)           --
     4  abc           ( 1,  1,  1)           --
     5  bf            (-1,  1,  0)           phi_b <= phi_a
     6  cf            (-1,  0,  1)           phi_c <= phi_a
     7  bcf           (-1,  1,  1)           phi_f <= phi_g
     8  ag            ( 1, -1, -1)           phi_a <= phi_b + phi_c
     9  bg | abfg     ( 0,  0, -1)           --
    10  cg | acfg     ( 0, -1,  0)           --
    11  abg           ( 1,  0, -1)           phi_a <= phi_c
    12  acg           ( 1, -1,  0)           phi_a <= phi_b

PROOF.  Singletons: a tight singleton {i} means phi_i = v({i}) = 0, excluded by
Reduction 2 and by strictness at f, g.  H itself is slack by hypothesis.

Lemma 3 of PROOF.txt: no phi-tight T contains H and omits a.  For such a T,
S1 n T = {f}, and the crossing budget s_phi(S1 n T) <= phi(N \ (S1 u T)) gives
phi_f <= phi(Q \ T).  T = fg is slack; T = bfg gives phi_f <= phi_c; T = cfg
gives phi_f <= phi_b; T = bcfg gives phi_f <= 0.  Each contradicts phi_f >
max_L phi >= 0.  (THIS IS THE STEP THAT NEEDS |Q| = 2 -- see the last section.)

afg: crossing with S2 has S2 n afg = {g} and S2 u afg = N, so phi_g <= 0.
abf: crossing with S2 has S2 n abf = {b} and S2 u abf = N, so s_phi({b}) <= 0,
     making {b} tight and phi_b = 0.  Likewise acf forces phi_c = 0.
abcg: crossing with S1 has S1 n abcg = {a} and S1 u abcg = N, so phi_a <= 0. []

Since the tangent cone of the core at phi is
D = { d : d(T) >= 0 for every phi-tight T }, Lemma X says D is cut out by a
SUBSET of the twelve normals above -- and by nothing else.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
LEMMA I (THREE IMPROVING DIRECTIONS)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
In normal-form coordinates the three directions

        e1 = (1, 0, 0),     e2 = (1, 1, 0),     e3 = (1, 0, 1)

are leximin-improving whenever they lie in D -- for EVERY stratification of L.
Consequently, since phi = Lmin(v), none of them lies in D, and therefore

    (L4)  bf, cf or bcf is phi-tight;                       [ids 5, 6, 7]
    (P2)  cf, cg or acfg is phi-tight;                      [ids 6, 10]
    (P3)  bf, bg or abfg is phi-tight.                      [ids 5, 9]

PROOF.  Each e has all three L-coordinates >= 0.  Let B_1 < ... < B_k be the
strata of phi; every stratum of L precedes every stratum meeting H, because the
boundary is strict.  Let B_j be the first stratum on which e is nonzero.  Since
e is nonnegative on L and nonzero there, and since e vanishes on the strata
before B_j, B_j is a stratum of L, e|B_j >= 0 and e|B_j is nonzero.  Moving
from phi along e therefore raises the sorted payoff vector at the first position
where it changes, so phi + t e leximin-dominates phi for small t > 0; as
phi + t e lies in the core for e in D, this contradicts phi = Lmin(v).  Hence
e is not in D, i.e. some phi-tight T has e . nu_T < 0.

It remains to read off which of the twelve normals are negative on each e:

    on e1 = (1,0,0):  only ids 5, 6, 7   (the normals with u-coefficient -1)
    on e2 = (1,1,0):  only ids 6, 10
    on e3 = (1,0,1):  only ids 5,  9

For e1 this is immediate: nu . e1 is the u-coefficient, which is -1 for
ids 5,6,7, 0 for ids 3, 9, 10 and +1 for ids 1,2,4,8,11,12.  For e2, nu . e2 =
nu_u + nu_b, which is 2,1,1,2,0,-1,0,0,0,-1,1,0 for ids 1..12, negative exactly
at 6 and 10.  For e3, nu . e3 = nu_u + nu_c = 1,2,1,2,-1,0,0,0,-1,0,0,1,
negative exactly at 5 and 9.                                                 []

(L4) is Lemma 4 of PROOF.txt, recovered here as one instance of a single
mechanism.  Note what makes (P2) and (P3) sharp: the normals that could have
rescued e2 by being negative on it -- the ones separating b from c -- are
exactly the three forms Lemma N says do not exist.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
LEMMA E (THE EXACTNESS DIRECTION)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
There is d* in D whose normal-form coordinates satisfy u(d*) = phi_f > 0.

PROOF.  Exactness at the coalition {f} supplies a core point z with
z_f = v({f}) = 0.  Block rigidity gives z_a + z_f = v(af) = phi_a + phi_f,
so z_a = phi_a + phi_f and u(d*) = z_a - phi_a = phi_f, where d* = z - phi.
d* lies in D because z is in the core: d*(N) = 0 and d*(T) = z(T) - v(T) >= 0
for every phi-tight T.  Finally phi_f > 0 by strictness.                     []

This is the crux, and it is where |P| = 1 is used: because S1 is a PAIR, the
whole of phi_f released by exactifying {f} lands on the single player a.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
PROOF OF THEOREM D
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Write (u, v_b, v_c) for the coordinates of the direction d* of Lemma E, so

        u = phi_f > 0.                                              (*)

Since d* lies in D, every phi-tight coalition's normal is nonnegative on it.
Combine (P2) and (P3) of Lemma I; there are four cases.

CASE 1:  cf tight and bf tight.
  id 6 gives v_c - u >= 0 and id 5 gives v_b - u >= 0, so by (*)
  v_b >= phi_f > 0 and v_c >= phi_f > 0.  Thus d* is STRICTLY POSITIVE on all
  three coordinates of L.  By the argument of Lemma I -- d* is nonnegative on L
  and nonzero on the first stratum it meets, that stratum being a stratum of L --
  d* is leximin-improving, contradicting phi = Lmin(v).

CASE 2:  cf tight and (bg or abfg) tight.
  id 6 gives v_c >= u = phi_f > 0; id 9 gives -v_c >= 0, i.e. v_c <= 0.
  Contradiction.

CASE 3:  (cg or acfg) tight and bf tight.
  id 10 gives v_b <= 0; id 5 gives v_b >= u = phi_f > 0.  Contradiction.

CASE 4:  (cg or acfg) tight and (bg or abfg) tight.
  ids 10 and 9 give v_b <= 0 and v_c <= 0.  Now apply (L4).  If bf is tight
  then v_b >= u = phi_f > 0; if cf is tight then v_c >= phi_f > 0; if bcf is
  tight then v_b + v_c >= phi_f > 0.  Each contradicts v_b, v_c <= 0.

The four cases are exhaustive and each is contradictory, so no PART
configuration exists at m = 3.  By Reduction 1 this covers PART(1,2) and
PART(2,1) alike.                                                             []

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
WHAT THE PROOF SAYS ABOUT THE THRESHOLD
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The previous round left open "a structural reason why the threshold is 4".  The
proof above supplies the lower-bound mechanism and localizes precisely where it
stops.

The engine is Lemma E: exactifying the rich singleton {f} pushes the entire
amount phi_f onto the poorer side of S1, producing a tangent direction that
strictly enriches a poorer player.  Leximin must block it, and Lemma I says the
only blockers available are tight coalitions of the shapes bf, cf, bcf (against
e1), cf, cg (against e2) and bf, bg (against e3).  Every such blocker forces a
sign on v_b or v_c at d*, and the signs are jointly unsatisfiable.

The step that fails first as m grows is LEMMA 3, inside Lemma X.  Its proof
reads phi_f <= phi(Q \ T) and needs |Q \ T| <= 1 to contradict phi_f >
max_L phi.  With |Q| = 2 that is automatic.  With |Q| = 3 -- i.e. already at
PART(1,3), m = 4 -- the coalition T = H u {b} leaves |Q \ T| = 2 and yields only
phi_f <= phi_c + phi_d, no contradiction.  Tight coalitions containing H but
omitting a then become available, the exclusion list of Lemma X shrinks, new
normals appear that ARE negative on e2 and e3, and the four-case table loses its
exhaustiveness.  That is exactly the frontier: PART(1,3) and PART(2,2) are both
realizable at m = 4.

So the threshold is not a coincidence of the constant 4 = m_min(1).  It is the
point at which the poorer side of the OTHER block first has enough players to
finance a tight coalition over H, and both readings of "enough" -- three players
in Q with |P| = 1, or two and two -- arrive together at m = 4.

THE DIAGNOSIS IS CONFIRMED AT THE FRONTIER, AND IT IS RAZOR-THIN.
frontier_check.py inspects the harvested PART(1,3) and PART(3,1) witnesses at
m = 4.  In both, Lemma 3 does fail, and by exactly the predicted route: the
coalition cef is phi-tight, contains H, and omits the unique poorer member a of
the singleton side.  Its crossing budget reads

        phi_f = 7/4  <=  phi(Q \ cef) = 7/4,

an EQUALITY.  So at m = 4 the very inequality that kills every candidate at
m = 3 survives with nothing to spare -- had the two remaining players of Q been
worth any less, the m = 3 argument would have carried over and PART(1,3) would
not exist either.  The threshold is sharp in the strongest sense available: the
obstruction does not weaken gradually, it expires exactly at m = 4.

WHAT RESTS ON A FINITE CHECK
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
NOTHING in the proof above.  Lemmas N, X, I, E and the four-case table are hand
arguments; the only inputs from elsewhere are Theorem 1 (block rigidity),
Theorem 5', Theorem 7, Lemma 2 (protectors), Lemma 3 and Lemma 6 of PROOF.txt,
all of which are themselves hand-proved there, together with paper4's crossing
budget.

Three machine cross-checks were run, and all agree.  They are guards, not
premises:

  verify_m3_proof.py  re-derives every step mechanically: the normal-form
      identities and the 21-form collapse (V1, V2); the exclusion list and the
      count of twelve admissible normals (V3); the sign computation behind
      Lemma I (V4); that e2 and e3 are leximin-improving for all 39
      stratifications, including the non-canonical orderings of L (V5); that
      each of the four cases is unsatisfiable (V6); and consistency with the
      cone enumeration (V7).  ALL CHECKS PASSED.

  normalform.py  enumerates, for each of the 24 stratifications, every minimal
      subset of the twelve normals for which the leximin cone conditions hold.
      The answer is the SAME eight sets in all 24 cases -- which is the
      enumeration's way of saying what Lemma I proves, that the stratification
      never enters.  The eight are
          {bf,cf,ag}   {bf,ag,cg}   {bf,bg,cg}   {bf,cg,abg}
          {cf,ag,bg}   {cf,bg,cg}   {cf,bg,acg}  {bcf,bg,cg}
      Each satisfies (L4), (P2) and (P3), and each falls into one of the four
      cases of the theorem: the four containing both bf and cf or bf and cg go
      to Cases 1 and 3, those with cf and bg to Case 2, and {bcf,bg,cg} to
      Case 4.

  frontier_check.py  confirms the diagnosis of the previous section against the
      harvested m = 4 witnesses: Lemma 3 fails there exactly as predicted, via

  The earlier decisive.py (48 UNSAT) and families.py (3,161 CEGAR nodes, zero
      surviving candidates) verdicts are unchanged and now redundant.
