Maxwell Flanagan --- Specialist review brief --- 7 October 2026

\textbf{Review request.} Correctness, priority and significance assessment of a
sharp worst-case budget theorem. The manuscript now consolidates the earlier
weighted multi-glove results and the general production/network extensions,
with their algorithmic and structural consequences. Conventional proofs and
exact checks have internal model reviews. Historical novelty and human
specialist validation remain unconfirmed.

\textbf{Exact model.} Fix $n$ owners, $d$ resource types and a finite ordered
family $V\subseteq\mathbb R_+^d$ of $K$ supporting forms. Owner $i$ supplies
$b_i\ge0$; coalition production is
\[
 v_b(S)=f\left(\sum_{i\in S}b_i\right),\qquad
 f(c)=\min_{w\in V}w\cdot c,\qquad T=v_b(N).
\]
Payments are nonnegative and deterministic, cover every coalition, and are
componentwise nondecreasing in each owner's \emph{whole} report, with the
other owners fixed. This means no profitable binding underreport when utility
is payment and there are no use costs. There is no overreport, group-report,
false-name, split/merge or population guarantee. The same rule works at all
nonnegative real profiles, including zeros and ties.

\textbf{Main theorem.} For every integer $j\ge2$, there exists an eligible rule with
\[
 \sum_i M_i\le C_j(K)T,\qquad
 C_j(K)=j+\frac{\log K+2\log j+1}{\log[j^3/(j^2+j-1)]}.
\]
Choosing $j=\max\{2,\lceil\log K/[\log(2+\log K)]^2\rceil\}$ gives
\[
 \boxed{\quad\sum_i M_i\le(1+o(1))T\frac{\log K}{\log\log K}.\quad}
\]
A matching lower holds for worst cases with \emph{at most} $K$ structural
cuts even on simple directed acyclic networks, with one distinct strategic
owner per edge. Thus leading constant one is sharp in $K$. Owner and resource
counts may grow without bound. $K$ can be exponential in a compact input;
this is not a sharp bound in owner count. The factor bounds total payments;
external subsidy is total payments minus $T$.

\textbf{Upper proof's key steps.} Delete each whole owner independently with
probability $q$ and select a fixed-priority minimum \emph{raw} supporting form.
For owner $i$, let $p_i^q$ be its expected contribution, capped at $T$, when
$i$ is deleted; let $m_i^q$ be its expected on-minus-off production marginal.
Both are own-report monotone. The mixture
$r_i^q=qp_i^q+(1-q)m_i^q$ dominates the capped selected-form allocation and
therefore covers coalitions. For $g(q)$ equal to expected masked production
and $Z(q)$ the total capped selected-form payment,
\[
 \sum_i r_i^q=Z-g-(1-q)g',\qquad
 \int_0^\eta\sum_i r_i^q\,dq
 =\int_0^\eta Z\,dq+T-(1-\eta)g(\eta)-2\int_0^\eta g\,dq.
\]
A Chernoff union over the fixed $K$ forms bounds $Z$. Averaging over
$0<q<1/j$ proves the finite constant above. Selection is uncapped;
only contributions are capped. Removing this cap is unbounded at $K=n=2$.

\textbf{Lower proof's key steps.} A hierarchy of fixed binary-report owners
uses two actual coalition inequalities and multinomial weights from
$p_h\propto\lambda^h/h!$. Raising one original owner's report moves one
module from type $h$ to $h+1$; the identity
\[
 \mu(\nu)\nu_h=(p_h/p_{h+1})\mu(\nu-e_h+e_{h+1})(\nu_{h+1}+1)
\]
transports only the changing owner's payment. Shared fan modules make the
network simple while accounting for every auxiliary owner's compensation.
With $Y,Q$ the disjoint variable/auxiliary budgets, the key bound is
\[
 A\le(1+\alpha)\mathbb EY+(m/R)\mathbb EQ
   \le\max\{1+\alpha,m/R\}\,B\mathbb ET.
\]
Taking $R=m$ retains the coefficient. The module's cut count is at most
$2^{4m}$; a slowly growing owner multiplier preserves
$\log K\sim h\log h$ and the lower factor $(1-o(1))h$.
Ordinary full subdivision into separately owned edge pairs instead permits
an efficient truthful rule, so it cannot justify this reduction.

\textbf{Further consolidated results.}
\begin{itemize}
\item Every finite monotone concave degree-one homogeneous production function
admits an all-profile factor $(\log2+o(1))n/\log n$, including discontinuities
at boundary faces. The proof uses relative supporting forms extended by
infinity outside their face and capped before payment. It makes no arbitrary-
function oracle or regularity claim.
\item Exact finite sampling gives incentives for every seed. A uniform theorem
gives one seed covering every coalition at every real profile. Rational
arrangement/LP certification includes all tie faces and yields always-correct
Las Vegas preprocessing. Preprocessing can be exponential; subsequent
rational-input evaluation is polynomial in the stated sample parameters.
\item Weighted multi-glove and series-parallel special cases retain finer
bounds. Two-pool alternatives and restricted-domain nonlinear constructions
are compared explicitly. These are parts of this paper, not extra candidates.
\item The original continuous-noise own-report envelope has a polynomial-in-$K$
obstruction, even on simple DAGs, and a universal $KT$ upper. This blocks every
truthful pointwise-majorant repair of that base rule, not arbitrary mechanisms.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Established ingredients and closest comparisons.}
Dual production-core allocations, max-flow/min-cut, probabilistic marginals,
own-excluded selection, concentration, convex support theory and uniform
convergence are established ingredients. The weighted multi-glove model and
continuous Banzhaf functional are explicitly credited to Moretti--Norde and
Kurz. The closest strategic comparison is Huang--Mei--Yang--Cao,
\href{https://arxiv.org/html/2502.08248v1}{\emph{Mechanism Design in Max-Flows}}
(2025): binding capacity underreports with payment utility; efficient core
selection can fail truthfulness. Our proposal permits subsidy while retaining
all coalition inequalities. The ledger also compares population monotonicity,
cost of stability, capacity-demand cost sharing and revised-worth production
games. Some originals remain only partially inspected. No bounded search can
confirm historical priority.

\textbf{Specific review questions.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item Does an existing theorem imply the sharp general factor under these
exact fixed-owner, whole-bundle, all-profile assumptions?
\item Is the capped own-excluded/endpoint hybrid's coverage, integration
identity or fixed-form probability bound missing a hypothesis?
\item Does the simple-DAG lower correctly preserve owner identities and
account for auxiliary payments, symmetrization and cut-count asymptotics?
\item Are boundary supporting forms and all-tie-face seed certification valid
at the claimed scope? Is the sharp theorem significant enough for one main
paper, with the longer class comparisons as a supplement?
\end{enumerate}
