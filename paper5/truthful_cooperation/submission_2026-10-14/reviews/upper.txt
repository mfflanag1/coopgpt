VERDICT

No load-bearing gap found in the stated finite-min-linear upper theorem or
its production/network corollary. The raw selection, capped contributions,
own-report monotonicity, endpoint comparison, deletion/integration identity,
tail bound, parameter choice, ties and zero cases all check out under the
stated fixed-family, nonnegative-form, binding-underreport assumptions.

One local mathematical/model-domain correction is needed in the separate
uncapped-rule example at lines 309-316. Its bad-payment example should state
the owner bundles explicitly. Its final efficient-rule sentence is false on
the full-bundle domain defined at lines 160-170, although it is true when

1. FIXED MODEL, MONOTONICITY AND ENDPOINTS

Nonnegative forms imply f(x)>=0 and f(x)<=f(y) whenever 0<=x<=y.
Also f(0)=0 and f(x+y)>=f(x)+f(y). These statements do not require positive
reports, nondegeneracy or a unique minimizer.

For each owner and each fixed other-owner mask, deleting that owner's WHOLE
bundle removes all of its report from every selection score. Since both V
and its priority are fixed, the selected off form is the same before and
after any own-report increase, even at a tie or an all-zero mask. Its own
linear contribution and full-profile T are both componentwise nondecreasing;
the minimum of these two functions is nondecreasing. Therefore p_i^q is.

For m_i^q the off value does not depend on b_i; the on value is nondecreasing.
The endpoint difference is nonnegative, and is monotone in the whole own
bundle. The weights q, 1-q, j and the integration interval are all independent
of reports. This proves the required monotonicity of the final payment,
including simultaneous decreases in several coordinates and comparisons
across T=0. No monotonicity of the raw selected on form itself is asserted or
needed. Choosing j from report-dependent active forms would be a different,
unjustified procedure; the manuscript chooses it from fixed K.

The conditional expression at q=0 is formally unnecessary: all identities
are proved for 0<q<1 and the finite-mask polynomial extends continuously to
the integration endpoint. The value at one endpoint does not affect the
integral. Thus there is no zero-probability-conditioning gap.

2. CAP/RAW COMPARISON AND COALITION COVERAGE

At any fixed full profile and for any form w, the vector
a_i(w,b)=min(w.b_i,T) covers every coalition S. If one of S's contributions
is at least T, its cap is T>=f(sum_S b_i); otherwise no S contribution is
capped and their sum is the linear upper support w.(sum_S b_i).
The argument includes empty S, equality at the cap, and T=0.

Let x be the masked aggregate of owners other than i and let w1 be any
priority-selected raw minimizer at x+b_i. Then

 f(x+b_i)-f(x) = w1.(x+b_i)-f(x) >= w1.b_i,

because f(x)<=w1.x. Since all summands are nonnegative,

 0 <= w1.b_i <= w1.(x+b_i)=f(x+b_i) <= f(c)=T.

Consequently w1.b_i=a_i(w1,b). The comparison has the correct direction;
it does not substitute the off minimizer into the on value. Averaging over
the same B_-i gives m_i^q >= E[a_i(W,b)|B_i=1]. Together with the exact
off component this gives r_i^q>=z_i^q, coordinate by coordinate. Taking the
same convex mixture of covering z-vectors covers every coalition, and
j times the interval length 1/j is one. Coverage of M follows.

The related selected-open identity is exact, not an inequality. For the
selected raw minimizer W, its nonnegative open contributions sum to the
masked value, which is at most T. Thus no open contribution is affected by
capping and sum_i B_i a_i(W,b)=f(sum_i B_i b_i). Closed contributions may be
capped; the proof does not assume otherwise. Raw selection is essential to
this argument, and the manuscript consistently uses raw selection.

3. DELETION IDENTITY AND INTEGRATION

Taking expectations of the selected-open identity gives

 Z(q)-g(q)=sum_i E[(1-B_i)a_i(W,b)]=q sum_i p_i^q.

To differentiate g, first give each owner's deletion bit its own probability
q_i. The resulting expectation is multiaffine. Its partial derivative in
q_i is E[off value - on value]=-m_i. On the diagonal q_i=q, the ordinary
derivative is the sum of these partial derivatives, so g'=-sum_i m_i^q.
This avoids any differentiability assumption on f or the report-space
selection rule; only a finite mask polynomial is differentiated.

Hence R=Z-g-(1-q)g'. Set F(q)=(1-q)g(q). Since
F'=-g+(1-q)g', one has R=Z-2g-F'. Therefore

 integral_0^eta R = integral_0^eta Z + T
                   -(1-eta)g(eta)-2 integral_0^eta g.

The minus sign and factor 2 in the manuscript are correct. For eta<1 all
terms discarded in its upper bound are nonnegative. A linear one-form
sanity check gives g(q)=(1-q)T and Z=R=T, and the exact identity reduces to
eta*T on both sides. No unproved independence between W and B is used.

4. TAIL BOUND

For fixed b with T>0, each fixed form has deterministic coefficients
d_i(w)=a_i(w,b)/T in [0,1]. For any q in (0,1), lambda>0, set
alpha=-log(q+(1-q)exp(-lambda))>0. The function
h(u)=log(q+(1-q)exp(-lambda*u)) satisfies

 h''(u)=lambda^2*q*(1-q)*exp(-lambda*u)
        /(q+(1-q)*exp(-lambda*u))^2 >= 0.

With h(0)=0 and h(1)=-alpha, convexity gives h(u)<=-alpha*u on [0,1].
For fixed w, the variables B_i are independent, so Markov applied to
exp(-lambda sum_i B_i d_i(w)) gives

 P(sum_i B_i d_i(w)<=1) <= exp(lambda-alpha*rho_w).

The selected form's open sum is g's realized masked value divided by T,
hence at most one. Thus the event rho_W>=x is contained in the union,
over fixed w with rho_w>=x, of their small-open-sum events. A union bound
gives min(1,K exp(lambda-alpha*x)). The selected W need not be independent
of the bits; the fixed-form union is precisely what permits its dependence.

Since Z/T=E[rho_W], tail integration is valid for the bounded nonnegative
random variable rho_W. The break point is
x0=(log K+lambda)/alpha>0. Integrating 1 up to x0 and the exponential tail
afterwards gives exactly (log K+lambda+1)/alpha. This proves the stated
bound without hidden dependence on owner count, resource count, form
coordinates, or a positive lower bound on contributions.

5. PARAMETER CHOICE AND ASYMPTOTICS

With eta=1/j and exp(-lambda)=j^-2, the quantity
q+(1-q)j^-2 is increasing in q and is at most
(j^2+j-1)/j^3 throughout the integration interval. Thus alpha>=alpha_j.
Positivity follows from j^3-(j^2+j-1)=(j-1)^2(j+1)>0 for j>=2.
Multiplying the integrated budget inequality by j yields

 total M <= [j+(log K+2 log j+1)/alpha_j] T.

For L=log K tending to infinity, the stated integer choice satisfies
j~L/(log L)^2. Moreover
alpha_j=log j-log(1+1/j-1/j^2)=log L-2 log log L+o(log L),
so alpha_j~log L, 2 log j+1=o(L), and j=o(L/log L). Hence the coefficient
is (1+o(1))*log K/log log K. The o(1) depends only on K, proving the claimed
uniformity. The construction is a family indexed by j, as correctly stated
by the theorem's 'for every j there exists a rule' quantifier.

6. ZERO, SINGLE-FORM, RATIONAL AND LP CASES

If T=0, every masked aggregate is coordinatewise at most c, hence has zero
value. All caps and endpoint differences vanish; the payment is exactly
zero. Normalized d_i and tail formulas are only used for T>0.

For K=1, each w.b_i is a nonnegative summand of T, so the cap is inert and
p_i^q=m_i^q=w.b_i. The integral returns that contribution exactly. Arbitrary
ties for K>1 are already handled by a fixed priority, including duplicate
score values and the empty mask.

The displayed finite-mask formula has degree at most n in q. Its integral
has rational coefficients at rational 1/j. 'Rational input' must include
the technology forms as well as reports; under that ordinary reading the
rational-payment conclusion is correct. It gives no polynomial evaluation
algorithm, as the manuscript explicitly acknowledges.

For the LP corollary, a positive entry in each activity column bounds that
activity's nonnegative quantity separately at finite c. Thus the primal is
nonempty (x=0), closed and bounded. A sufficiently large multiple of the
all-ones price vector is dual-feasible. The dual polyhedron is pointed since
it lies in the nonnegative orthant. A finite attained optimum has an optimal
vertex; finitely many vertices supply the required fixed family, even when
some capacities are zero. The nonzero-column exclusion is sufficient and
explicit. For finite directed single-commodity flow, min-cut supplies the
representation directly; any nonminimal structural cut has a contained
minimal cut of no greater nonnegative capacity. Removing such forms does
not depend on reports. No ordinary cut-count interpretation is asserted for
arbitrary fractional production dual vertices.

7. VERIFIED LOCAL ISSUE AND REPAIR

Lines 309-313 specify aggregate c=(M,1) but not the owners' bundles. The
claimed event follows with b1=(M,0), b2=(0,1). Then at mask (0,1), form
(1,0) has score zero while (1/M,1-1/M) has score 1-1/M>0. The uncapped
own contribution is M, giving r1^q>=q(1-q)M. Its j=2 average is
2*M*integral_0^(1/2)(q-q^2)dq=M/6; T=2-1/M. This part is valid once
the owner assignment is specified.

The subsequent proposed efficient rule allocates all c1/M to owner 1 and
half of (1-1/M)min(c1,c2) to each. On the declared arbitrary-bundle domain,
take b1=(0,0), b2=(1,0). It pays owner 2 zero, but that singleton's worth is
1/M>0. For example, at M=2 the claimed coalition-covering payment is
(1/2,0) although coalition {2} has value 1/2. This is a concrete failure
of eligibility, not an issue involving a tie or an asymptotic limit.

Two straightforward repairs are available:
(a) explicitly restrict this optional comparison to fixed resource-owner
    domains b1=(x,0), b2=(0,y), and say eligibility here is on that domain;
(b) retain the full-bundle domain and replace the comparison by

    P_i(b)=[f(b_i)+f(b1+b2)-f(b_{-i})]/2,  i=1,2.

For (b), the payments sum exactly to T. Superadditivity yields
T>=f(b1)+f(b2), implying P_i>=f(b_i)>=0. Thus both singleton constraints
and the grand-coalition constraint hold. With the other owner's report
fixed, f(b_i) and T are nondecreasing, while f(b_{-i}) is constant, proving
all-profile own-report monotonicity. This supplies the intended comparison
on precisely the main theorem's domain, without changing the upper theorem.
