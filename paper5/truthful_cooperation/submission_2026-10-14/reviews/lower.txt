1. Fixed-owner symmetrization (369-380)
Write each original edge owner as (module, local edge). A module permutation
moves the entire local report/coalition pattern, including every auxiliary
owner, to another identically shaped module; a branch permutation also moves
its two incident auxiliary edge families. These are bijections of one fixed
owner set, not population changes, mergers, subdivisions or clone owners.
For every nonnegative capacity profile c and coalition S, series composition
satisfies v_c(S)=min_l f_l(c restricted to S in module l). One inequality follows
by restricting any global flow to a module; the reverse follows by scaling
feasible local flows down to their common minimum and concatenating at the
single shared terminals. Thus v_(pi c)(pi S)=v_c(S), including arbitrary
asymmetric auxiliary capacities, even though permuting modules need not be a
vertex automorphism of the entire directed graph.
Consequently averaging ybar_i(c)=average_pi y_(pi i)(pi c) preserves coalition
coverage, nonnegativity, the factor-B budget, and own-report monotonicity.
At the proof's auxiliary-H profiles, stabilizers and branch/module relabelings
make each low/high middle-owner payment depend only on its type and the type
histogram. This justifies x_h(nu), z_h(nu). No assumed equality among auxiliary
payments is needed for the argument.

2. Actual shared-fan coalitions and auxiliary budget (345-394)
A chosen fan pair supports precisely the selected middle branches J. Give each
branch c_j units of flow. Each branch's two incident auxiliary edges can carry
it, and the two common end edges carry sum_J c_j <= m*m=H. The middle edges
are an upper-bounding cut, so equality holds. Series coalition worth is the
minimum of these realized module values. Every endpoint fan edge occurs with
probability 1/R; every incident auxiliary edge has probability either 0 or
1/R for a fixed selected J. Their expected payment is therefore <=Q/R because
payments are nonnegative. Replacing that expectation by Q/R relaxes coverage
in the correct direction. Auxiliary owners remain strategic; fixing their
reports only on this witness family is a valid restriction of an all-profile
eligible rule, and their payments remain fully charged to Q.
One low exists in every module since h<=d_0<m. For h<d_0, there are at least
q_0=m-d_0+1 lows; a type-d_0 module has a high because d_0>=1. The second
coalition has value q_0 when any type is below d_0, and m>=q_0 otherwise.
Multiplying its relaxed bound by d_0 and the first by m-d_0 gives exactly
A=(d_0+1)m-d_0^2 and coefficient m/R on Q. The all-top-type case is stronger
than needed, not omitted: its extra weighted constant is d_0(d_0-1).

3. Weighted transition and disjoint budgets (396-420)
For nu_h>0 and h<d_0, choose one actual low owner in a type-h module and raise
only its report from 1 to m. The source and destination representatives can
be chosen so all other original reports, including auxiliaries, are identical.
Own-monotonicity and the preceding symmetrization give x_h(nu)<=z_(h+1)(nu').
The histogram bijection nu'=nu-e_h+e_(h+1) satisfies, by cancelling factorials,
mu(nu) nu_h = [p_h/p_(h+1)] mu(nu') nu'_(h+1).
Since p_h/p_(h+1)=(h+1)/lambda and lambda=A/alpha, the transported coefficient
is alpha(h+1). There is no missing (m-h) multiplicity: the relevant coalition
rows charge x_h per selected low, not the total payments of all low owners.
Summing transports gives alpha times total high payments. The remaining
boundary term is precisely the full middle-owner payment in type-d_0 modules.
Both are nonnegative sub-budgets of Y, giving (1+alpha)E[Y]+(m/R)E[Q]. Because
Y and Q are disjoint, this is at most max(1+alpha,m/R)E[Y+Q], not the sum of
those coefficients times the budget. The displayed factor-B inequality follows.
The integer tail-sum formula for min_l H_l gives the stated exact expectation.
Each positive tail is <=1-p_0, and (1-1/Z)^k<=exp(-k/Z), proving the bound.

4. Structural cuts (423-428)
For any inclusion-minimal disconnecting edge set F, let U be vertices reachable
from the source after F is removed. Then delta+(U) is a disconnecting subset of
F, so minimality forces equality. A module has exactly 2R+2m internal vertices,
hence at most 2^(2R+2m) such edge cuts. It has a path, so at least one exists.
In series, a disconnected graph must contain a disconnected module; otherwise
local surviving paths concatenate. A cut containing a disconnected module's
edges and additional edges elsewhere is not inclusion-minimal. Conversely,
every local minimal cut remains globally minimal because restoring any one of
its edges restores a local path, while all other modules still have paths.
The modules' edge sets are disjoint and local cuts are nonempty, so no duplicate
sets arise: the exact count is K=k*C_(m,R), not just an upper bound. Series
terminal identification creates neither parallel edges nor cycles here.

5. Parameters and at-most-K inversion (429-451)
Put d=d_0. For d>=2, g=ceil(sqrt(log(d+1)))>=2, m=dg>d, R=m and alpha=1/g.
Then A/m=d+1-d/g and the budget maximum is 1+1/g. With ell=ceil(log(d+1))
and k=ceil(2 ell Z), d exp(-k/Z)<=d exp(-2ell)<=d/(d+1)^2 ->0. This separate
ell factor makes the displayed coarse bound vanish; substituting g into that
bound would leave d*exp(-2g), which does not vanish.
Lambda=d^2*g*(g-1)+d*g^2>d. Thus its truncated Poisson terms increase through
height d, establishing the displayed two-sided bound on Z. More explicitly,
log(lambda)=2log(d)+2log(g)+log(1+1/d-1/g)
           =2log(d)+O(log log(d)).
Stirling and the Z bound give log Z=d log d+O(d log log d); multiplying Z by
2ell and taking a ceiling preserves log k=(1+o(1))d log d. Since m=O(d sqrt(log d))
=o(d log d), the structural-count bound yields log K_d=(1+o(1))d log d.
Combined with B>=(1-o(1))d, this gives the leading coefficient one along K_d.
For fixed 0<epsilon<1 and L=log K, choosing d=floor((1-epsilon)L/log L) gives
log K_d=(1-epsilon+o(1))L<L eventually. Hence this witness is within the
external budget without needing monotonicity of the sequence K_d, or any
exact-count padding theorem. Taking liminf and then epsilon down to zero
proves the at-most-K lower. The matching upper is conditional here on the
separately stated upper theorem, outside this audit's re-proof scope.
The production lower follows by taking structural cut indicators as the fixed
nonnegative forms and restricting each edge owner's resource bundle to its
own coordinate axis. An eligible rule on the full bundle domain would restrict
to an eligible network rule, so expanding that domain cannot evade this lower.

6. Subdivision boundary check (454-471)
With mate report fixed, the chosen virtual capacity min(a_e,b_e), and hence
Q_e of the whole virtual profile, is constant once an owner's report reaches
its mate. Its own payment is zero below, half that fixed nonnegative Q_e at
equality, and the whole Q_e above: own-monotonicity holds without assuming
Q itself monotone. For a coalition, an incomplete subdivided edge cannot carry
through-flow at its private intermediate vertex; complete pairs exactly induce
S*. Each complete pair contributes its whole Q_e to coalition payment, while
other payments are nonnegative. Coverage and efficiency follow. This proposition
does not undermine the shared-fan lower, whose auxiliary owners are explicitly
included in the budget rather than treated as harmless subdividers.
