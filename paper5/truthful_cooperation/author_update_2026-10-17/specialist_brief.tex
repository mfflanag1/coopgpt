{\Large\bfseries Sharp subsidies for truthful production\\and network cooperation}\\[5pt]
Maxwell Flanagan --- Claim and model comparison --- 17 October 2026
\end{center}
\textbf{Question for a specialist.} Do the sharp joint coverage--incentive--budget
theorems below follow from earlier work, and are the proofs and mechanism-class boundaries valid?
This is an internal-review candidate, not a claim of external endorsement.

\textbf{Fixed model.} There are $n$ fixed owners and $d$ resources. Each owner
reports $b_i\in\mathbb R_+^d$ to the same fixed ordered family
$V=\{w^1,\ldots,w^K\}\subseteq\mathbb R_+^d$:
\[
 f(c)=\min_{w\in V}w\cdot c,\qquad
 v_b(S)=f\left(\sum_{i\in S}b_i\right),\qquad T=v_b(N).
\]
A single deterministic rule on every real report profile has nonnegative
payments $M_i$, covers every coalition ($\sum_{i\in S}M_i\ge v_b(S)$), and
is componentwise nondecreasing in each owner's entire bundle with others fixed.
This means truthfulness for binding underreports and payment-only utility.
There are no resource-use costs, overreports, coordinated-report, false-name,
split/merge, other-owner or population guarantees. Owners are never replaced
by strategic capacity units. A total-payment factor $B$ corresponds to subsidy
factor $B-1$.

\textbf{Proposed sharp theorem.} For every integer $j\ge2$ an eligible rule satisfies
\[
 \sum_iM_i\le C_j(K)T,\qquad
 C_j(K)=j+\frac{\log K+2\log j+1}{\log[j^3/(j^2+j-1)]}.
\]
Choosing $j=\max\{2,\lceil\log K/[\log(2+\log K)]^2\rceil\}$ gives
$(1+o(1))T\log K/\log\log K$. Zero-value profiles receive zero.
The worst-case optimum with \emph{at most} $K$ forms has the same leading
constant one. The lower already holds for simple directed acyclic flow
networks with at most $K$ structural cuts and one strategic owner per edge.
It is not an exact-count padding theorem for every integer $K$.

\textbf{Upper proof to check.} Delete whole owners independently with probability
$q$; select a fixed-priority minimum \emph{raw} form. Let $p_i^q$ be the
expected contribution capped at $T$ conditional on deleting $i$, and $m_i^q$
the expected on-minus-off production marginal. Both are own-monotone.
The mixture $r_i^q=qp_i^q+(1-q)m_i^q$ dominates a coalition-covering capped
form allocation. For masked production $g$ and total capped payment $Z$,
\[
 R=Z-g-(1-q)g',\qquad
 \int_0^\eta R=\int_0^\eta Z+T-(1-\eta)g(\eta)-2\int_0^\eta g.
\]
A Chernoff union over the fixed forms bounds $Z$. The payment
$M_i=j\int_0^{1/j}r_i^q\,dq$ is deterministic. Selection is uncapped;
only payment contributions are capped.

\textbf{Lower proof to check.} Shared-fan modules keep the network simple and
all auxiliary owners strategic. Actual coalition inequalities and multinomial
weights $p_h\propto\lambda^h/h!$ transport only the changing owner's payment.
With disjoint middle-edge and auxiliary budgets $Y,Q$,
\[
 A\le(1+\alpha)\mathbb EY+(m/R)\mathbb EQ
 \le\max\{1+\alpha,m/R\}B\mathbb ET.
\]
Taking $R=m$ preserves the constant. Cut counts and hierarchy parameters
then give $\log K\sim h\log h$ and factor $(1-o(1))h$.

\textbf{New owner-count and dimension bounds.} For every finite-valued monotone
concave degree-one homogeneous $f:\mathbb R_+^d\to\mathbb R_+$, including
boundary-discontinuous functions, there is an eligible rule with
\[
 \sum_i M_i\le
 \min\{d,\ n\sqrt{2/(n+1)},\ 1+2\sqrt d\}\,T.
\]
The owner bound improves the project's earlier $O(n/\log n)$ upper; the
dimension bound improves $d$ to $O(\sqrt d)$, independently of owner count.
These are upper bounds, not unrestricted sharp-rate theorems. The main paper
now contains complete proofs.

\textbf{Proof to check.} For a fixed open-owner set $B$, take a supporting
price on the coordinate face of $x_B=\sum_{i\in B}b_i$, extended by infinity
off that face. Closed owners receive their capped support price; open owners
receive $f(x_B)-f(x_B-b_i)$. This preserves coverage and own-bundle monotonicity.
With missing probability $q$, let $C(q)$ be expected closed payments and
$g(q)=\mathbb E f(x_B)$. Then
\[
 R(q)=C(q)-(1-q)g'(q),\qquad
 \int_0^a-(1-q)g'(q)\,dq=T-(1-a)g(a)-\int_0^a g(q)\,dq.
\]
The owner bound uses $C(q)\le nqT$ and $g(q)\ge(1-q)^nT$, yielding
\[
 B_n(a)=na/2+\frac{n[1-(1-a)^{n+1}]}{(n+1)a}.
\]
For dimension, coordinatewise price domination and the identity
\[
 \mathbb E\sum_{i\notin B}\frac{u_i}{u_i+S_B}
 =\frac{q}{1-q}\Pr(S_B>0),\qquad S_B=\sum_{i\in B}u_i,
\]
give $C(q)\le2dqT/(1-q)$, hence
$D_d(a)=[1+2d(-a-\log(1-a))]/a$.
Parameters and the choice of an entire rule depend only on fixed public
$n,d,K$, never on the realized report profile. Exact zero conventions and
relative support existence are proved in the main text.

\textbf{What the new lower bounds restrict.} The supplementary constructions
force square-root growth for report-independent mask laws using common
supporting prices selected from open reports and endpoint payments to open
owners. The bounds allow asymmetric/correlated masks and randomized finite
support selection. Separate results cover the maximum-coalition-marginal cap.
They do not apply to arbitrary truthful payment maps or every report-dependent
sampling scheme. The unrestricted lower bounds remain logarithmic over
logarithmic in owner count and dimension. No matching unrestricted
square-root lower bound has been established.

\textbf{Priority question specific to these additions.}
\href{https://arxiv.org/pdf/0907.4385}{Bachrach et al. (2009), Theorem 3.5}
already establishes a square-root static cost-of-stability bound for
superadditive games; the published 2018 JAIR Theorem 7 gives total factor
$\sqrt n$. Each profile here has a nonempty core and static cost
zero. Does an earlier result nevertheless imply the same bound for one
own-resource-monotone rule across all fixed-owner reports? Static core
existence and population monotonicity alone do not answer this question.

\textbf{Closest original-source comparisons.}

\textbf{Production and stability.} Owen (1975) and
\href{https://www.tau.ac.il/~samet/papers/lp\%20games.pdf}{Samet--Zemel (1984)}
establish dual-price/core ingredients. Moretti--Norde (2021) studies the
weighted multi-glove game used as a special case. Fixed-game cost of
stability is established. Our issue links different reports even though
each individual production game has an efficient core.

\textbf{Same disclosure model.}
\href{https://arxiv.org/html/2502.08248v1}{Huang--Mei--Yang--Cao (2025 v1)}
uses binding capacity underreports and payment utility in flow games.
Its efficient mechanisms and core-selection obstruction are the nearest
strategic comparison. The present theorem keeps all coalition inequalities
and allows subsidy; it does not inherit their extra incentive guarantees.

\textbf{Different monotonicity.}
\href{https://immorlica.com/pubs/costshare.pdf}{Immorlica--Mahdian--Mirrokni}
compares an unchanged owner's payment when other owners join.
\href{https://doi.org/10.1007/s11750-007-0002-7}{Br\^anzei--Solymosi--Tijs (2007)}
compares participating subgames of unit owners (final Definition 1,
Theorems 5--6). Neither condition is silently identified with our
same-owner bundle comparison.

\textbf{Same own-resource condition, different technology.}
\href{https://hal.science/hal-01400498v1}{Amigo--Belzarena--Vaton (2016 author version)}
defines own-resource monotonicity and a strictly concave service model.
Its Theorem 1 proves Shapley own-resource monotonicity, while noting that
Shapley can fail core membership. The supplement's smooth strictly concave example fits that inspected model
and refutes a universal extension of the reported projection simulations,
not a proved theorem. The publisher final has not been compared in full.
This diagnostic is not homogeneous and is separate from the main theorem.

\textbf{Established tools versus proposed contribution.} Supporting prices,
max-flow/min-cut, marginal contributions, own-excluded selection,
concentration, weighted counting, convex support theory and uniform
convergence are established. The proposed contribution is the joint sharp
all-profile guarantee and matching fixed-owner simple-network lower bound.
The source search is bounded; historical priority remains unconfirmed.
The full technical comparison with Cao's \emph{Superadditive Market Games}
(2026, DOI 10.1002/nav.70010) remains open; only its abstract and metadata
have been inspected. Owen's 1975 full original and the ABV publisher-final
text also remain unreviewed. These gaps do not establish nonoverlap.

\textbf{What the result does not settle.} $K$ can be exponential in an encoded
technology. The remaining owner-count and resource-dimension gaps are now between logarithmic-over-logarithmic lower bounds and square-root upper bounds.
Exact enumeration and the supplementary certification procedure may require
exponential preprocessing. The paper asserts neither a recognized major
open-problem resolution nor a general theorem for private opportunity costs.

\textbf{Review evidence.} The main paper contains complete complexity, owner
and dimension upper proofs and the matching complexity lower. The supplement
states the restricted architecture classes separately. Source-hashed internal
model audits and exact rational checks are preserved in the review-update
packet. These are not formal verification, specialist endorsement or priority
confirmation. The 14 October PDFs and archives are historical snapshots;
the 17 October package exports the main paper, supplement and this brief
