25: \begin{abstract}
26: Owners of complementary productive resources may gain by withholding capacity
27: when a sharing rule selects efficient core allocations. We ask how much
28: external subsidy suffices to retain every coalition's production guarantee
29: while making each fixed owner's payment nondecreasing in its reported
30: resource bundle. For any technology represented by the minimum of $K$ fixed
31: nonnegative linear forms, we construct a deterministic rule satisfying both
32: requirements at all nonnegative real report profiles. Its total payment is
33: at most $(1+o(1))T\log K/\log\log K$, where $T$ is grand-coalition production.
34: A matching lower bound holds for the worst case with at most $K$ structural
35: cuts even on simple directed acyclic flow networks with distinct strategic
36: edge owners. Thus the leading constant is one. The upper combines capped
37: supporting prices selected without the recipient's report with endpoint
38: marginal payments. The lower transports payments across unilateral report
39: changes and explicitly accounts for auxiliary owners. The incentive model
40: permits binding componentwise underreports and payment-only utility.
41: The bound is uniform in owner count and resource dimension, but $K$ may be
42: exponential in a compact description. Exact finite evaluation exists;
43: polynomial preprocessing is not asserted. The result isolates the cost of
44: linking coalition-covering allocations across reports in games whose
45: individual profiles already admit efficient core allocations. For every finite
46: monotone concave homogeneous technology we also obtain total-payment factors
47: $n\sqrt{2/(n+1)}$ and $1+2\sqrt d$ in fixed owner count $n$ and resource
48: dimension $d$. Their unrestricted optimal rates remain open.
49: \end{abstract}

51: coalitional stability; subsidy; flow game.\\

54: \section{Introduction}
55: Resource owners can jointly create value that none can produce alone. A
56: sharing rule must then address two distinct problems: retaining the coalition
57: and eliciting the resources available to it. At a given capacity profile,
58: competitive supporting prices can allocate the entire production value while
59: meeting every coalition's outside option. The difficulty is choosing these
60: allocations consistently across profiles. An owner who receives less after
61: revealing additional resources has an incentive to withhold them.
62: 
63: We measure the external budget needed to reconcile these requirements.
64: Owners, resource types and technology are fixed. Each owner reports a
65: nonnegative bundle and can commit no more than its true resources. Its utility
66: is its payment; resource use has no private cost. A single deterministic rule
67: must work at every nonnegative real profile, including zeros and ties. Payments
68: are nonnegative, cover every coalition's production value, and are
69: nondecreasing in the reporting owner's entire bundle with the other reports
70: held fixed. These assumptions describe capacity disclosure by fixed partners;
71: they do not model hidden opportunity costs or the choice of who joins.
72: 
73: The production function is a minimum of $K$ fixed nonnegative linear forms.
74: This includes finite nonnegative linear production programs and
75: single-commodity flow networks. A form can represent supporting resource
76: prices; in a network it is a structural cut. Grand production is denoted by
77: $T$. If total payment is at most $BT$, the external subsidy is at most
78: $(B-1)T$. Coverage includes the grand coalition, so total payments cannot
79: be below $T$ at positive-value profiles.
80: 
81: \subsection{Result and economic interpretation}
82: We prove that the optimal worst-case total-payment factor is
83: \[
84:  (1+o(1))\frac{\log K}{\log\log K}
85: \]
86: for technologies with at most $K$ forms. The same leading constant is necessary
87: even on simple directed acyclic networks with one strategic owner per edge.
88: Theorem~\ref{prod:upper} provides an explicit finite-$K$ bound and a mechanism.
89: Theorem~\ref{dag:sharp} supplies the matching asymptotic lower bound for the
90: worst case with at most $K$ cuts. Owner and resource counts are unrestricted
91: finite integers in both statements.
92: 
93: The lower bound means that no universal constant subsidy factor preserves
94: all coalition guarantees and truthful capacity disclosure throughout this
95: class. This is not caused by an empty core at a fixed profile. For any
96: minimizing form $w$, the efficient payments $w\cdot b_i$ cover every
97: coalition. Subsidy is required because payments must also satisfy incentive
98: inequalities between profiles. Our lower bound connects those inequalities
99: using actual unilateral changes by the original owners. All auxiliary edges
100: in its network construction belong to strategic owners and can have arbitrary
101: reports; no free infrastructure is introduced.
102: 
103: The upper bound selects supporting prices after randomly deleting whole
104: owners. A recipient's excluded price does not depend on its own report, and
105: its contribution is capped at grand production. Endpoint marginal payments
106: repair coalition coverage. Averaging over a short interval of deletion
107: probabilities controls the additional payment. Randomness defines a finite
108: expectation: the implemented rule is deterministic. The proof uses a union
109: bound over the fixed forms, which explains the technology-complexity parameter.
110: 
111: A small $K$ gives a budget guarantee independent of the number of owners.
112: However, $K$ can be exponential in an encoded network or production program.
113: Theorem~\ref{general:sqrt} gives separate bounds of order $\sqrt n$ and
114: $\sqrt d$, including technologies without a finite-form representation.
115: These bounds improve the previous owner-count and dimension estimates,
116: but their unrestricted optimal rates remain open. No polynomial-time
117: preprocessing algorithm is asserted. It also makes no incentive claim for resource-use
118: costs, overreports, coordinated deviations, false names, or changes in the
119: owner population. These are separate economic questions.

171: \section{General production: a sharp complexity bound}\label{prod:section}
172: There are $n$ fixed owners and $d$ fixed resource types. Owner $i$ reports
173: $b_i\in\R_+^d$; write $c=\sum_i b_i$. Fix a nonempty finite family
174: $V=\{w^1,\ldots,w^K\}\subseteq\R_+^d$, with a report-independent priority,
175: and define
176: \[
177:  f(c)=\min_{w\in V}w\cdot c,\qquad
178:  v_b(S)=f\left(\sum_{i\in S}b_i\right),\qquad T=f(c).
179: \]
180: A rule is eligible if its payments are nonnegative, cover every coalition,
181: and are componentwise nondecreasing in each owner's whole bundle, with all
182: other reports fixed. These conditions hold on all of $(\R_+^d)^n$.
183: They express truthfulness for binding underreports and payment-only utility;
184: there are no operating costs, overreport, false-name, population-change or
185: coordinated-report guarantees. A factor $C$ bounds \emph{total payment} by
186: $CT$; external subsidy is at most $(C-1)T$. The family $V$ remains fixed
187: across reports. Its size may be exponential in a compact description. All logarithms are natural.
188: 
189: \begin{theorem}[General sharp-order upper bound]\label{prod:upper}
190: For every integer $j\ge2$, there exists an eligible deterministic rule with factor
191: \begin{equation}\label{prod:constant}
192:  C_j(K)=j+\frac{\log K+2\log j+1}{\alpha_j},\qquad
193:  \alpha_j=\log\frac{j^3}{j^2+j-1}>0.
194: \end{equation}
195: With $L=\log K$ and
196: $j=\max\{2,\lceil L/[\log(2+L)]^2\rceil\}$, this factor is
197: $(1+o(1))\log K/\log\log K$, uniformly over owner counts, dimensions,
198: technologies and reports. Zero-value profiles receive zero; for $K=1$ the
199: constructed rule is efficient.
200: \end{theorem}
201: 
202: \paragraph{The mechanism.}
203: For $q\in(0,1)$ let independent owner bits satisfy $\Prob(B_i=0)=q$.
204: Choose the first minimizer of the \emph{uncapped} objective,
205: \[
206:  W(b,B)=\operatorname*{first\,argmin}_{w\in V}
207:                \sum_i B_i(w\cdot b_i),\qquad
208:  a_i(w,b)=\min\{w\cdot b_i,T\}.
209: \]
210: Only the payment contribution $a_i$ is capped. Put
211: \begin{align}
212:  z_i^q&=\E a_i(W,b),&
213:  p_i^q&=\E[a_i(W,b)\mid B_i=0],\label{prod:p}\\
214:  m_i^q&=\E_{B_{-i}}\left[f\left(b_i+\sum_{h\ne i}B_hb_h\right)
215:                          -f\left(\sum_{h\ne i}B_hb_h\right)\right],
216:  &r_i^q&=qp_i^q+(1-q)m_i^q.\label{prod:hybrid}
217: \end{align}
218: The final payment is the deterministic finite expectation
219: \begin{equation}\label{prod:payment}
220:  M_i(b)=j\int_0^{1/j}r_i^q(b)\,dq.
221: \end{equation}
222: These quantities are finite polynomials in $q$ at a fixed profile; no
223: continuity or generic-position assumption about the reports is required.
224: 
225: \begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{prod:upper}]
226: Conditional on $B_i=0$, every score and the selected form are independent of
227: $b_i$. For that fixed nonnegative form, both $w\cdot b_i$ and $T$ are
228: nondecreasing in $b_i$, so their minimum is nondecreasing. In $m_i^q$ the
229: off term is independent of $b_i$ and the on term is nondecreasing. The
230: nonnegative fixed mixtures preserve this property and nonnegativity.
231: 
232: For any $w,S$, if some $i\in S$ has $w\cdot b_i\ge T$, then
233: $\sum_{i\in S}a_i(w,b)\ge T\ge v_b(S)$. Otherwise no term in $S$ is capped,
234: and the sum is $w\cdot\sum_{i\in S}b_i\ge v_b(S)$. Thus every capped form
235: covers coalitions. Fix $B_{-i}$ and let $w_1$ be the on minimizer. The off
236: optimum is at most the off objective of this same form, whence
237: \begin{equation}\label{prod:endpoint}
238:  f\left(b_i+\sum_{h\ne i}B_hb_h\right)-f\left(\sum_{h\ne i}B_hb_h\right)
239:  \ge w_1\cdot b_i=a_i(w_1,b).
240: \end{equation}
241: The equality holds because the contribution is a nonnegative summand of
242: the selected on value, which is at most $T$. Averaging proves $r_i^q\ge z_i^q$,
243: and hence coverage for $r^q$ and $M$.
244: 
245: For the budget define $g(q)=\E f(\sum_iB_ib_i)$, $Z(q)=\sum_i z_i^q$,
246: and $R(q)=\sum_i r_i^q$. Capping leaves every open contribution unchanged:
247: \[
248:  \sum_iB_i a_i(W,b)=f\left(\sum_iB_ib_i\right).
249: \]
250: Consequently $q\sum_i p_i^q=Z-g$. Differentiating the finite mask polynomial
251: gives $\sum_i m_i^q=-g'$, so $R=Z-g-(1-q)g'$. Since $g(0)=T$ and $g\ge0$,
252: for $0<\eta<1$ integration by parts gives the exact identity
253: \begin{equation}\label{prod:integral}
254:  \int_0^\eta R\,dq=\int_0^\eta Z\,dq+T-(1-\eta)g(\eta)
255:                          -2\int_0^\eta g\,dq
256:  \le\int_0^\eta Z\,dq+T.
257: \end{equation}
258: Both the sign and the factor two in this identity matter.
259: 
260: Suppose $T>0$. For each fixed form let
261: $d_i(w)=a_i(w,b)/T\in[0,1]$ and $\rho_w=\sum_i d_i(w)$.
262: The selected form satisfies $\sum_iB_id_i(W)\le1$. For $\lambda>0$ put
263: $\alpha=-\log(q+(1-q)e^{-\lambda})$. The convex function
264: $u\mapsto\log(q+(1-q)e^{-\lambda u})$ lies below its endpoint chord
265: $-\alpha u$ on $[0,1]$. Independence and Markov's inequality give
266: \[
267:  \Prob\left(\sum_iB_id_i(w)\le1\right)
268:  \le e^\lambda\prod_i(q+(1-q)e^{-\lambda d_i(w)})
269:  \le e^{\lambda-\alpha\rho_w}.
270: \]
271: A union over the fixed family and integration of the resulting tail yield
272: \begin{equation}\label{prod:chernoff}
273:  \Prob(\rho_W\ge x)\le\min\{1,K e^{\lambda-\alpha x}\},\qquad
274:  \frac{Z(q)}T\le\frac{\log K+\lambda+1}{\alpha}.
275: \end{equation}
276: Take $\eta=1/j$ and $\lambda=2\log j$. For $q\le1/j$,
277: $q+(1-q)e^{-\lambda}\le(j^2+j-1)/j^3$, so
278: \eqref{prod:integral}--\eqref{prod:chernoff} give \eqref{prod:constant}.
279: For the displayed choice of $j$, as $L\to\infty$,
280: $j\sim L/(\log L)^2$, $\alpha_j\sim\log L$, and
281: $j=o(L/\log L)$, proving the asserted asymptotic.
282: 
283: If $T=0$, all masked production values and capped contributions vanish.
284: If $K=1$, no contribution is capped, $p_i^q=m_i^q=w\cdot b_i$, and
285: $M_i=w\cdot b_i$. Finally each finite-mask term is
286: $q^{n-1-|S|}(1-q)^{|S|}[q a_i(W(b,1_S),b)+(1-q)(v_b(S\cup\{i\})-v_b(S))]$.
287: Its integral is a finite sum with rational coefficients when $j$ is integer.
288: This gives exact rational payments for rational input, without an efficiency
289: claim about enumeration of masks or forms.
290: \end{proof}

292: \begin{corollary}[Production LPs and networks]\label{prod:lp}
293: The theorem applies to
294: $f(c)=\max\{\pi\cdot x:x\ge0,Ax\le c\}$ for fixed $A\ge0$, with every
295: activity column nonzero and fixed positive profits $\pi$. Here $V$ can be
296: the vertices of $\{w\ge0:A^Tw\ge\pi\}$. For single-commodity directed flow,
297: one may instead use the characteristic vectors of the inclusion-minimal
298: structural source--sink edge cuts.
299: \end{corollary}
300: \begin{proof}
301: Each activity is bounded by a positive entry in its column, so every feasible
302: production set is compact at finite $c$. The dual is nonempty, pointed and
303: polyhedral. Strong duality and attainment at a vertex give the claimed finite
304: min-linear representation, including boundary capacities. Network max-flow/
305: min-cut gives the cut representation directly. Redundant nonminimal cuts may
306: be removed once and for all because capacities are nonnegative. All owners
307: and forms remain fixed across profiles. A positive-profit zero-resource
308: activity is excluded because it would make production unbounded.
309: \end{proof}
310: Dual core allocations are established ingredients~\citep{Owen,SametZemel}.
311: General dual vertices may be fractional; their count is not an ordinary
312: network-cut count. Constraints introducing capacity-independent dual offsets
313: are outside the homogeneous model here.

315: \begin{proposition}[Why the payment cap is essential]\label{prod:uncapped}
316: Removing the cap from the own-excluded contribution in
317: \eqref{prod:p} makes the uniform-$q$ hybrid unbounded even with two owners
318: and two supporting forms.
319: \end{proposition}
320: \begin{proof}
321: Take unit-profit activity columns $(1,1)$ and $(M,0)$, $M>1$, and two
322: fixed owners with bundles $b_1=(M,0)$ and $b_2=(0,1)$, so $c=(M,1)$. The two dual vertices are $(1,0)$ and $(1/M,1-1/M)$, and
323: $T=2-1/M$. If owner 1 is off and owner 2 on, $(1,0)$ uniquely minimizes the
324: raw score, giving uncapped contribution $M$. Thus $r_1^q\ge q(1-q)M$;
325: with $j=2$ its average is at least $M/6$, an unbounded ratio to $T$.
326: This obstructs only the uncapped rule. On the full two-owner bundle
327: domain, the Shapley payments
328: $P_i(b)=[f(b_i)+T-f(b_{-i})]/2$ are efficient and own-monotone.
329: They are nonnegative by monotonicity of $f$, and cover singleton coalitions
330: because $f(b_1+b_2)\ge f(b_1)+f(b_2)$. This last inequality follows by
331: minimizing nonnegative linear forms. Thus an efficient eligible alternative
332: exists without restricting each owner's report to a single resource.
333: \end{proof}

335: \section{Bounds in owner count and resource dimension}\label{general:section}
336: The number of supporting forms can be much larger than the economic description.
337: We now obtain bounds directly in the numbers of fixed owners and resources.
338: Here the production technology may be any finite-valued monotone concave
339: function $f:\R_+^d\to\R_+$ homogeneous of degree one; a finite min-linear
340: representation is not required. The report space and eligibility conditions
341: remain unchanged. In particular, there is no additional fairness floor.
342: 
343: \begin{theorem}[General owner and dimension bounds]\label{general:sqrt}
344: For every fixed $n,d\ge1$ and every such technology, there exists an eligible
345: deterministic rule on all nonnegative real report profiles with
346: \begin{equation}\label{general:combined}
347:  \sum_i M_i(b)\le
348:  \min\left\{d,\ n\sqrt{\frac{2}{n+1}},\ 1+2\sqrt d\right\}T.
349: \end{equation}
350: The individual bounds are uniform in the other parameter. They also hold when
351: $f$ has downward discontinuities at the boundary. More precisely, for every
352: fixed $0<a<1$ the supporting-price/endpoint rule averaged over $q\in[0,a]$
353: has both bounds
354: \begin{align}
355:  B_n(a)&=\frac{na}{2}+
356:  \frac{n[1-(1-a)^{n+1}]}{(n+1)a},\label{general:owner}\\
357:  D_d(a)&=\frac{1+2d[-a-\log(1-a)]}{a}.\label{general:dimension}
358: \end{align}
359: The owner expression remains valid at $a=1$ by polynomial integration.
360: No polynomial-time evaluation or preprocessing claim is made.
361: \end{theorem}

363: \subsection{Supporting prices on boundary faces}
364: For $x\ne0$, restrict $f$ to its coordinate face
365: $F_x=\{z\ge0:z_e=0\text{ whenever }x_e=0\}$.
366: At the relative-interior point $x$, finite concavity gives a supergradient $u$.
367: Monotonicity forces each active coordinate of $u$ to be nonnegative, and
368: homogeneity applied above and below $x$ gives $u\cdot x=f(x)$.
369: For a boundary point $z\in F_x$, apply the supporting inequality at
370: $(1-t)x+tz$, $0<t<1$, and the concavity lower bound on that segment to obtain
371: $u\cdot z\ge f(z)$. Thus boundary continuity is unnecessary.
372: Choose the unique minimum-norm vector in the nonempty closed convex set of
373: such nonnegative supports on $F_x$. Extend its linear form to
374: \[
375:  \ell_x(z)=\begin{cases}u\cdot z,&z\in F_x,\\+\infty,&z\notin F_x.
376:  \end{cases}
377: \]
378: Set $\ell_0(0)=0$ and $\ell_0(z)=+\infty$ for $z\ne0$.
379: This is one fixed selection depending only on $x$. For finite min-linear
380: technologies the original fixed-priority global minimizing form is an
381: alternative; the bounds below apply to either choice.
382: 
383: For any fixed mask $B\subseteq N$, put $x_B=\sum_{i\in B}b_i$ and
384: $A_i(B)=\min\{\ell_{x_B}(b_i),T\}$. This vector covers every coalition:
385: if any member's raw price reaches $T$, its capped payment alone suffices;
386: otherwise all relevant prices are finite on the support face and their sum
387: majorizes the coalition's worth. For $i\in B$,
388: \[
389:  f(x_B)-f(x_B-b_i)\ge \ell_{x_B}(b_i),
390: \]
391: because the support touches at $x_B$ and majorizes $f(x_B-b_i)$.
392: The marginal is at most $T$. Replace open owners' prices by these marginals
393: and keep $A_i(B)$ for closed owners. Coverage is preserved. A closed owner's
394: selected price excludes its own report, while its raw price and cap are
395: nondecreasing in that report. An open owner's marginal has a fixed off term
396: and a nondecreasing on term. This proves eligibility for every fixed mask
397: and every report-independent mixture of masks.
398: 
399: With independent missing probability $q$, retain the notation $p_i^q,m_i^q,r_i^q$
400: from \eqref{prod:p}--\eqref{prod:hybrid}, now using $\ell_{x_B}$.
401: At a fixed profile every capped mask payment is finite, so the expectations
402: are finite polynomials in $q$. Define $M_i^a=a^{-1}\int_0^a r_i^q\,dq$.
403: The endpoints have measure zero, including when $a=1$.
404: Writing $g(q)=\E f(x_B)$ and $C(q)=q\sum_i p_i^q$, differentiation of the
405: finite mask polynomial gives
406: \begin{equation}\label{general:endpoint}
407:  \sum_i r_i^q=C(q)-(1-q)g'(q),\qquad
408:  \int_0^a-(1-q)g'(q)\,dq
409:  =T-(1-a)g(a)-\int_0^a g(q)\,dq.
410: \end{equation}
411: No derivative of $f$ with respect to reports occurs here.
412: 
413: \subsection{Owner-count bound}
414: Every closed payment is at most $T$, whence $C(q)\le nqT$.
415: The all-open mask alone gives $g(q)\ge(1-q)^nT$.
416: Substituting both estimates in \eqref{general:endpoint}, integrating and
417: dividing by $a$ yields
418: \[
419:  \sum_i M_i^a\le\left(\frac{na}{2}+
420:  \frac{n[1-(1-a)^{n+1}]}{(n+1)a}\right)T.
421: \]
422: Dropping the bracketed power term gives
423: $B_n(a)\le na/2+n/[(n+1)a]$. The fixed choice
424: $a=\sqrt{2/(n+1)}\le1$ equalizes these terms and proves the owner bound
425: in \eqref{general:combined}. For $n=1$ the exact expression is $B_1(a)=1$.
426: The former $O(n/\log n)$ upper bound recorded in the supplement is therefore
427: superseded by $(\sqrt2+o(1))\sqrt n$.

429: \subsection{Resource-dimension bound}
430: The following scalar identity controls contributions from closed owners
431: without counting owners or supporting forms.
432: \begin{lemma}[Bernoulli reciprocal bound]\label{general:reciprocal}
433: Let $u_i\ge0$, independently retain each $i$ with probability $p=1-q$, and
434: write $S_B=\sum_{i\in B}u_i$. Define $\min\{u_i/S_B,1\}$ to be zero when
435: $u_i=0$ and one when $u_i>0=S_B$. Then
436: \[
437:  \E\sum_{i\notin B}\min\{u_i/S_B,1\}\le\frac{2q}{1-q}.
438: \]
439: \end{lemma}
440: \begin{proof}
441: With zero-over-zero interpreted as zero,
442: $\min\{u/S,1\}\le2u/(u+S)$. Conditioning on the own bit gives
443: \[
444:  \E\sum_{i\notin B}\frac{u_i}{u_i+S_B}
445:  =\frac qp\E\sum_{i\in B}\frac{u_i}{S_B}
446:  =\frac qp\Prob(S_B>0)\le\frac qp.
447: \]
448: In the first equality both conditioned ratios are
449: $u_i/(u_i+\sum_{j\ne i}B_ju_j)$; the other bits have the same law.
450: \end{proof}
451: 
452: Suppose $T>0$. For every mask and owner,
453: \begin{equation}\label{general:coordinate}
454:  \frac{A_i(B)}T\le
455:  \sum_{e=1}^d\min\left\{\frac{b_{ie}}{(x_B)_e},1\right\},
456: \end{equation}
457: with the lemma's conventions. If $b_i$ has a positive coordinate outside
458: $F_{x_B}$, the right side is at least one. Otherwise the selected nonnegative
459: support satisfies $\sum_eu_e(x_B)_e=f(x_B)\le T$, so each active term obeys
460: $u_eb_{ie}/T\le b_{ie}/(x_B)_e$. Apply
461: $\min\{\sum_e z_e,1\}\le\sum_e\min\{z_e,1\}$.
462: The case $x_B=b_i=0$ is immediate. The same argument applies to a finite
463: global minimizing form, since off-face prices remain capped by $T$.
464: 
465: Sum \eqref{general:coordinate} over closed owners and use
466: Lemma~\ref{general:reciprocal} separately in each resource coordinate:
467: $C(q)\le 2dqT/(1-q)$. The endpoint integral in
468: \eqref{general:endpoint} is at most $T$. Since
469: $\int_0^a q/(1-q)\,dq=-a-\log(1-a)$, this proves
470: \eqref{general:dimension}. Also
471: \[
472:  -a-\log(1-a)=\sum_{k\ge2}\frac{a^k}{k}
473:  \le\frac{a^2}{2(1-a)},\qquad
474:  D_d(a)\le\frac1a+\frac{da}{1-a}.
475: \]
476: Choosing $a=1/(1+\sqrt d)$ gives $1+2\sqrt d$.
477: At $T=0$, monotonicity makes all coalition values, caps and endpoint
478: marginals vanish, completing these cases.

480: \subsection{Baseline, selection and remaining gap}
481: The grand marginal $U_i=f(c)-f(c-b_i)$ is eligible: it is own-monotone and
482: dominates the support allocation at $c$. Put
483: $\alpha_i=\max_{e:c_e>0}b_{ie}/c_e$ when $T>0$.
484: Then $c-b_i\ge(1-\alpha_i)c$, so $U_i\le\alpha_iT$ and
485: $\sum_i\alpha_i\le d$. This proves the $dT$ baseline.
486: Choosing an entire rule using the fixed public $n,d$ before reports proves
487: \eqref{general:combined}; no pointwise minimum of payment vectors is used.
488: For a finite $K$ representation, the complexity bound in
489: Theorem~\ref{prod:upper} is another available choice.

491: The scalar leading constant two is already sharp as $q\downarrow0$:
492: two equal positive weights give expected closed ratio exactly $2q$.
493: This does not prove optimality of the dimension constant for the full mechanism.

507: \section{A matching lower bound on simple networks}\label{dag:section}
508: A network has one distinct fixed strategic owner per edge, and coalition
509: worth is its maximum source--sink flow using only its owned edges. Structural
510: cuts are inclusion-minimal edge sets disconnecting source from sink; their
511: number depends on the fixed graph, not its reported capacities. Define
512: $\mathcal B_{\rm dag}(K)$ as the supremum, over simple finite DAGs having at
513: most $K$ structural cuts, of the best all-profile eligible total-payment
514: factor on that graph. The analogous quantity for finite nonnegative
515: min-linear technologies with at most $K$ forms is $\mathcal B_{\rm prod}(K)$;
516: owner counts and dimensions are unrestricted finite integers.
517: 
518: \begin{theorem}[Sharp first-order worst-case factors]\label{dag:sharp}
519: As $K\to\infty$,
520: \[
521:  \mathcal B_{\rm dag}(K)=(1+o(1))\frac{\log K}{\log\log K},\qquad
522:  \mathcal B_{\rm prod}(K)=(1+o(1))\frac{\log K}{\log\log K}.
523: \]
524: The lower holds along an explicit sequence of simple DAGs at their exact cut
525: counts, and for the worst case with \emph{at most} $K$ cuts. It does not
526: assert an exact-count padding theorem for every integer $K$.
527: \end{theorem}
528: 
529: \begin{proof}
530: The upper follows from Theorem~\ref{prod:upper}. We give the simple-DAG lower,
531: which also supplies the production lower. Fix integers $d_0\ge1$, $m>d_0$,
532: $R\ge1$, and $k\ge2$. (Here $d_0$ is hierarchy height, not resource dimension.)
533: One module with terminals $a,b$ has vertices $L_1,\ldots,L_R$,
534: $u_1,\ldots,u_m$, $v_1,\ldots,v_m$, $W_1,\ldots,W_R$ and edges
535: \[
536:  aL_s,\quad L_su_j,\quad u_jv_j,\quad v_jW_t,\quad W_tb
537:  \quad(1\le s,t\le R,\ 1\le j\le m).
538: \]
539: Take $k$ copies in series by identifying consecutive terminals. Each middle
540: edge $u_jv_j$ has report in $\{1,m\}$; all auxiliary edges have report
541: $H=m^2$. Every edge, including every auxiliary edge, is a strategic original
542: owner whose full report domain is nonnegative. Only the finite profiles
543: used in this proof keep auxiliary reports fixed.
544: 
545: A type-$h$ module has $h$ high middle edges, $0\le h\le d_0$. For any subset
546: $J$ of middle edges, choose one left fan and one right fan, and include the
547: source/final fan edges plus the incident edges supporting exactly $J$.
548: This actual coalition has module flow $\sum_{j\in J}c_j$: the middle cut
549: bounds it above, and the selected branches carry it because their total is
550: at most $m^2$. In particular the full graph has
551: \begin{equation}\label{dag:value}
552:  T=m+(m-1)\min_{1\le l\le k}H_l.
553: \end{equation}
554: Choosing the two fans independently uniformly, any particular auxiliary
555: edge is included with probability at most $1/R$.
556: 
557: Symmetrize an arbitrary eligible factor-$B$ rule over module permutations
558: and branch permutations, relabeling each branch's incident auxiliary edges.
559: These are permutations of the fixed owner set preserving every coalition's
560: flow at all profiles: module flows combine by their minimum, so even module
561: permutations preserve coalition values. Averaging preserves all required
562: inequalities. At a histogram $\nu=(\nu_0,\ldots,\nu_{d_0})$ of module types,
563: write $x_h(\nu),z_h(\nu)$ for low and high variable-owner payments, and
564: \[
565:  Y(\nu)=\sum_h\nu_h[(m-h)x_h(\nu)+hz_h(\nu)],\qquad
566:  Q(\nu)=\text{total auxiliary-owner payment}.
567: \]
568: No auxiliary-payment symmetry is assumed; $Y+Q\le BT$.
569: 
570: First choose one low variable in each module, supported by random fans.
571: Averaged coalition coverage and nonnegativity give
572: $\sum_h\nu_hx_h+Q/R\ge1$. Next put $q_0=m-d_0+1$ and choose $q_0$ low
573: variables in each type below $d_0$, and one high variable in each type
574: $d_0$. These exist because $m>d_0$. The resulting coalition has worth at
575: least $q_0$, giving
576: $q_0\sum_{h<d_0}\nu_hx_h+\nu_{d_0}z_{d_0}+Q/R\ge q_0$.
577: Multiply the first inequality by $m-d_0$ and the second by $d_0$. With
578: $A=(d_0+1)m-d_0^2$,
579: \begin{equation}\label{dag:coalitions}
580:  A\le A\sum_{h<d_0}\nu_hx_h+
581:  \nu_{d_0}[(m-d_0)x_{d_0}+d_0z_{d_0}]+(m/R)Q.
582: \end{equation}
583: 
584: Fix $\alpha>0$, set $\lambda=A/\alpha$,
585: $Z=\sum_{h=0}^{d_0}\lambda^h/h!$, and $p_h=\lambda^h/(h!Z)$.
586: Average profiles with independent module types of probabilities $p_h$;
587: let $\mu(\nu)$ be their multinomial histogram weights. Raising one actual
588: low variable owner from $1$ to $m$ gives
589: $x_h(\nu)\le z_{h+1}(\nu')$ at
590: $\nu'=\nu-e_h+e_{h+1}$. Every other report, including every auxiliary report,
591: is unchanged. The exact identity
592: \[
593:  \mu(\nu)\nu_h=(p_h/p_{h+1})\mu(\nu')\nu'_{h+1},\qquad
594:  A p_h/p_{h+1}=\alpha(h+1)
595: \]
596: transports only this changing owner's payment. Averaging
597: \eqref{dag:coalitions} therefore yields
598: \begin{align}
599:  A&\le\alpha\E\sum_{h=1}^{d_0}h\nu_hz_h+
600:  \E\nu_{d_0}[(m-d_0)x_{d_0}+d_0z_{d_0}]+(m/R)\E Q\notag\\
601:  &\le(1+\alpha)\E Y+(m/R)\E Q
602:  \le\max\{1+\alpha,m/R\}B\E T.\label{dag:transport}
603: \end{align}
604: The last step uses the disjoint budgets $Y,Q$; their coefficients are not
605: added. By the tail-sum formula and $p_0=1/Z$,
606: \begin{equation}\label{dag:mean}
607:  \E T=m+(m-1)\sum_{r=1}^{d_0}\left(\sum_{h=r}^{d_0}p_h\right)^k
608:  \le m[1+d_0 e^{-k/Z}].
609: \end{equation}
610: 
611: Let $C_{m,R}$ be the number of structural cuts of one module. Any minimal
612: cut is the outgoing cut of its reachable source side after deletion, so
613: $1\le C_{m,R}\le2^{2R+2m}$. A minimal cut in a series composition lies
614: entirely in one module: otherwise each module retains a path, or an edge
615: removed elsewhere is redundant. Hence the full cut count is exactly
616: $K=kC_{m,R}$.
617: For $d_0\ge2$ choose
618: \[
619:  g=\lceil\sqrt{\log(d_0+1)}\rceil,\quad
620:  m=d_0g,\quad R=m,\quad \alpha=1/g,\quad
621:  \ell=\lceil\log(d_0+1)\rceil,\quad k=\lceil2\ell Z\rceil.
622: \]
623: The separate multiplier $\ell$, rather than $g$, ensures the tail error
624: vanishes. Equations~\eqref{dag:transport}--\eqref{dag:mean} give
625: \[
626:  B\ge\frac{d_0+1-d_0/g}{(1+1/g)(1+d_0e^{-2\ell})}=(1-o(1))d_0.
627: \]
628: Here $\lambda=(d_0+1)d_0g^2-d_0^2g>d_0$, so
629: $\lambda^{d_0}/d_0!\le Z\le(d_0+1)\lambda^{d_0}/d_0!$.
630: Stirling's estimate implies
631: $\log k=(1+o(1))d_0\log d_0$; since $m=o(d_0\log d_0)$,
632: \[
633:  \log k\le\log K\le\log k+4m\log2=(1+o(1))d_0\log d_0.
634: \]
635: This proves the lower along the sequence $K_{d_0}$. For any fixed
636: $0<\varepsilon<1$ and large external cut budget $K$, take
637: $d_0=\lfloor(1-\varepsilon)\log K/\log\log K\rfloor$.
638: Then $K_{d_0}\le K$ eventually, yielding lower coefficient $1-\varepsilon$.
639: Letting $\varepsilon$ decrease to zero proves the at-most-$K$ assertion.
640: \end{proof}

642: \begin{proposition}[A subdivision that destroys the lower bound]\label{dag:subdivision}
643: Fully subdividing every edge of any finite directed network into two edges
644: owned separately produces a network with an efficient eligible rule.
645: \end{proposition}
646: \begin{proof}
647: For pair reports $(a_e,b_e)$ set $d_e=\min(a_e,b_e)$. Choose the original
648: network's fixed-priority minimum-cut core allocation $Q(d)$, which pays
649: $d_e$ on the selected cut and zero elsewhere. Pay $Q_e(d)$ to the higher
650: reporting member of pair $e$, split equally at equality, and pay the lower
651: member zero. Holding the mate's report fixed, the payment is zero below it,
652: half the fixed $Q_e$ at equality and the fixed $Q_e$ above it: in the last
653: two cases all virtual capacities are fixed. Thus it is own-monotone, even
654: if $Q$ is not. For a coalition $S$, let $S^*$ contain the original edges
655: whose two members both lie in $S$. Its value is exactly $v_d(S^*)$, and
656: $p(S)\ge Q(S^*)\ge v_d(S^*)$. Total payment is $T$. This shows why the naive subdivision reduction fails and motivates the
657: explicit auxiliary-budget accounting in the shared-fan construction.
658: \end{proof}

661: \section{Implications, limitations and further questions}
662: The result identifies technology complexity as one sharp measure of the
663: budget required to combine truthful disclosure with coalition coverage.
664: For every fixed finite representation, the upper holds simultaneously over
665: all profiles and all owner bundles. The lower uses an increasing family of
666: fixed technologies; it does not change the graph when an owner changes its
667: report. Its asymptotic statement concerns a worst case with at most $K$ cuts,
668: not an exact-count construction for each integer $K$.
669: 
670: Two questions remain distinct from the sharp $K$ theorem. First,
671: Theorem~\ref{general:sqrt} bounds the factor by
672: $\min\{d,n\sqrt{2/(n+1)},1+2\sqrt d\}$, while the unrestricted lower bounds
673: remain logarithmic over logarithmic in $n$ and $d$. The supplement's matching
674: square-root barriers apply to specified mask/support architectures; they do
675: not close these unrestricted gaps. A better general rule must escape those
676: restrictions. Second, rational finite technologies permit exact evaluation
677: by finite enumeration, and the supplement develops finite sampling with
678: all-profile certification. The certification preprocessing can be exponential.
679: A polynomial preprocessing guarantee or a matching computational obstruction
680: would be a separate contribution.
681: 
682: The main theorem deliberately isolates capacity disclosure. Private operating
683: costs, endogenous participation, and coordinated reports require additional
684: incentive constraints. A resource-sharing application should justify the
685: payment-only assumption and its feasible deviations before interpreting the
686: bound as a policy prescription.

+\section{Bounds in owner count and resource dimension}\label{general:section}
+The number of supporting forms can be much larger than the economic description.
+We now obtain bounds directly in the numbers of fixed owners and resources.
+Here the production technology may be any finite-valued monotone concave
+function $f:\R_+^d\to\R_+$ homogeneous of degree one; a finite min-linear
+representation is not required. The report space and eligibility conditions
+remain unchanged. In particular, there is no additional fairness floor.
+
+\begin{theorem}[General owner and dimension bounds]\label{general:sqrt}
+For every fixed $n,d\ge1$ and every such technology, there exists an eligible
+deterministic rule on all nonnegative real report profiles with
+\begin{equation}\label{general:combined}
+ \sum_i M_i(b)\le
+ \min\left\{d,\ n\sqrt{\frac{2}{n+1}},\ 1+2\sqrt d\right\}T.
+\end{equation}
+The individual bounds are uniform in the other parameter. They also hold when
+$f$ has downward discontinuities at the boundary. More precisely, for every
+fixed $0<a<1$ the supporting-price/endpoint rule averaged over $q\in[0,a]$
+has both bounds
+\begin{align}
+ B_n(a)&=\frac{na}{2}+
+ \frac{n[1-(1-a)^{n+1}]}{(n+1)a},\label{general:owner}\\
+ D_d(a)&=\frac{1+2d[-a-\log(1-a)]}{a}.\label{general:dimension}
+\end{align}
+The owner expression remains valid at $a=1$ by polynomial integration.
+No polynomial-time evaluation or preprocessing claim is made.
+\end{theorem}

+\subsection{Supporting prices on boundary faces}
+For $x\ne0$, restrict $f$ to its coordinate face
+$F_x=\{z\ge0:z_e=0\text{ whenever }x_e=0\}$.
+At the relative-interior point $x$, finite concavity gives a supergradient $u$.
+Monotonicity forces each active coordinate of $u$ to be nonnegative, and
+homogeneity applied above and below $x$ gives $u\cdot x=f(x)$.
+For a boundary point $z\in F_x$, apply the supporting inequality at
+$(1-t)x+tz$, $0<t<1$, and the concavity lower bound on that segment to obtain
+$u\cdot z\ge f(z)$. Thus boundary continuity is unnecessary.
+Choose the unique minimum-norm vector in the nonempty closed convex set of
+such nonnegative supports on $F_x$. Extend its linear form to
+\[
+ \ell_x(z)=\begin{cases}u\cdot z,&z\in F_x,\\+\infty,&z\notin F_x.
+ \end{cases}
+\]
+Set $\ell_0(0)=0$ and $\ell_0(z)=+\infty$ for $z\ne0$.
+This is one fixed selection depending only on $x$. For finite min-linear
+technologies the original fixed-priority global minimizing form is an
+alternative; the bounds below apply to either choice.
+
+For any fixed mask $B\subseteq N$, put $x_B=\sum_{i\in B}b_i$ and
+$A_i(B)=\min\{\ell_{x_B}(b_i),T\}$. This vector covers every coalition:
+if any member's raw price reaches $T$, its capped payment alone suffices;
+otherwise all relevant prices are finite on the support face and their sum
+majorizes the coalition's worth. For $i\in B$,
+\[
+ f(x_B)-f(x_B-b_i)\ge \ell_{x_B}(b_i),
+\]
+because the support touches at $x_B$ and majorizes $f(x_B-b_i)$.
+The marginal is at most $T$. Replace open owners' prices by these marginals
+and keep $A_i(B)$ for closed owners. Coverage is preserved. A closed owner's
+selected price excludes its own report, while its raw price and cap are
+nondecreasing in that report. An open owner's marginal has a fixed off term
+and a nondecreasing on term. This proves eligibility for every fixed mask
+and every report-independent mixture of masks.
