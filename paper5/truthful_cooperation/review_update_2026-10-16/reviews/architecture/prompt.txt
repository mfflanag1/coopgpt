4: \section{Limits of fixed-mask common-support mechanisms}\label{arch:section}
5: 
6: This section proves lower bounds for a specified payment architecture.
7: The owners and technology remain fixed throughout each construction, and only
8: their reports change. The conclusions do not apply to every eligible mechanism.
9: In particular, they do not replace the unrestricted logarithmic lower bounds
10: by square-root lower bounds.
11: 
12: \subsection{The architecture, marginal caps, and profile averaging}
13: 
14: Fix $n$ owners and a continuous finite nonnegative min-linear technology
15: $F:\R_+^d\to\R_+$: thus $F(x)=\min_{w\in\mathcal W}w\cdot x$ for a fixed
16: nonempty finite family $\mathcal W\subseteq\R_+^d$.
17: For a report profile $b=(b_i)_{i=1}^n$, put
18: \[
19:  b(S)=\sum_{i\in S}b_i,\qquad x_B=b(B),\qquad
20:  T=F(b([n])),\qquad v_b(S)=F(b(S)).
21: \]
22: A \emph{global linear support at $x$} is a nonnegative finite linear form
23: $\ell$ such that $\ell(z)\ge F(z)$ for all $z\ge0$ and $\ell(x)=F(x)$.
24: The support need not belong to the displayed defining family $\mathcal W$.
25: 
26: \begin{definition}[Fixed-mask common-support architecture]\label{arch:def}
27: Fix any probability law on subsets $B\subseteq[n]$, independently of all
28: reports. Conditional on $B$, select a global linear support $\ell_B$ at $x_B$
29: using only the fixed technology, $B$, and the open reports $(b_i)_{i\in B}$.
30: The selection may randomize. A closed report may not enter that selection.
31: Choose one of the following two caps:
32: \begin{align}
33:  c_i(b)&=T,\label{arch:tcap}\\
34:  c_i(b)&=V_i(b):=
35:  \max_{S\subseteq[n]\setminus\{i\}}
36:        \{F(b_i+b(S))-F(b(S))\}.\label{arch:vcap}
37: \end{align}
38: For the chosen cap, the payment map is the expectation of
39: \begin{equation}\label{arch:maskpay}
40:  Y_i(B,b)=
41:  \begin{cases}
42:  \min\{\ell_B(b_i),c_i(b)\},&i\notin B,\\
43:  F(x_B)-F(x_B-b_i),&i\in B.
44:  \end{cases}
45: \end{equation}
46: The cap convention and all mask and support-selection laws are fixed before
47: the profile is evaluated.
48: \end{definition}
49: 
50: This definition permits arbitrary correlations and asymmetries in the mask
51: law, any fixed mixture of mask laws, and randomized or redundant supporting
52: forms. Conditional on $B=\varnothing$, it permits exactly one support law for
53: the fixed technology and owners: there are no open reports on which that law
54: could depend. It does not permit a different empty-mask support law for each
55: profile.
56: 
57: \begin{lemma}[Eligibility and simultaneous marginal truncation]
58: \label{arch:caplemma}
59: Both architectures in Definition~\ref{arch:def} are eligible: payments are
60: nonnegative, cover every coalition, and are nondecreasing in each owner's
61: own whole bundle with all other reports fixed. Moreover, if a nonnegative
62: vector $z$ covers every coalition at $b$, then
63: $(\min\{z_i,V_i(b)\})_{i=1}^n$ also covers every coalition.
64: \end{lemma}
65: \begin{proof}
66: For every $i$, monotonicity of $F$ gives $0\le V_i\le T$. Each term in
67: the maximum defining $V_i$ is nondecreasing in $b_i$, since its off aggregate
68: does not involve $i$. Thus $V_i$ is own-bundle monotone.
69: 
70: For simultaneous truncation, fix a coalition $S$ and write
71: $C=\{i\in S:z_i>V_i\}$ and $R=S\setminus C$.
72: Coverage gives $\sum_{i\in R}z_i\ge v_b(R)$. Add the members of $C$ to $R$
73: one at a time. Each increment of coalition value is at most the corresponding
74: $V_i$, so
75: \[
76:  v_b(S)-v_b(R)\le\sum_{i\in C}V_i.
77: \]
78: Adding proves $\sum_{i\in S}\min\{z_i,V_i\}\ge v_b(S)$.
79: 
80: For any global support $\ell$, the vector
81: $A_i=\min\{\ell(b_i),T\}$ covers every coalition. If a member of a coalition
82: has raw price at least $T$, its capped payment alone covers that coalition;
83: otherwise the uncapped sum equals $\ell(b(S))\ge F(b(S))$.
84: The preceding truncation argument shows the same fact for
85: $\min\{\ell(b_i),V_i\}$. For an open owner $i$, touching and majorization
86: give
87: \[
88:  F(x_B)-F(x_B-b_i)\ge\ell_B(b_i),
89: \]
90: and this endpoint marginal is at most both $T$ and $V_i$.
91: Replacing an open owner's capped support payment by its endpoint marginal
92: therefore preserves coverage. All payments are nonnegative.
93: 
94: For a closed owner, the selected support is independent of its report, while
95: its raw price and either cap are nondecreasing in that report. For an open
96: owner, the off aggregate $x_B-b_i$ is independent of its report and the on
97: value is nondecreasing. Hence every fixed-mask payment is own-bundle
98: monotone. Averaging over fixed mask and support randomness preserves the
99: three requirements.
100: \end{proof}
101: 
102: \begin{lemma}[Combining regular and empty-mask witnesses]
103: \label{arch:averaging}
104: Fix one technology and $n$ owners. Suppose a fixed finite distribution
105: $\mu$ of positive-value profiles has mean normalized total payment at least
106: $C>0$ for every nonempty mask and every admissible choice of supports at
107: those profiles. Suppose a fixed finite distribution $\nu$ of positive-value
108: profiles has mean normalized empty-mask total payment at least $A>0$ for
109: every one fixed empty-mask support. Then every rule in the architecture has
110: some profile with total-payment factor at least
111: \begin{equation}\label{arch:harmonic}
112:  \frac{AC}{A+C}.
113: \end{equation}
114: The same conclusion holds if the empty support is randomized.
115: \end{lemma}
116: \begin{proof}
117: Give total weight $A/(A+C)$ to $\mu$ and $C/(A+C)$ to $\nu$.
118: For every nonempty mask the regular contribution alone is at least
119: $AC/(A+C)$; for the empty mask the anchor contribution alone is at least
120: that number. Other payments are nonnegative. At the empty mask, the finite
121: anchor average commutes with integration against its one fixed support law.
122: The regular bound is valid for every support choice separately, so it also
123: survives profile-dependent support selection using open reports. Finally
124: average over the report-independent mask law. The resulting finite weighted
125: average of normalized expected payments is at least \eqref{arch:harmonic};
126: at least one profile attains that bound.
127: \end{proof}

129: \subsection{A finite technology forcing all nonempty-mask prices}
130: 
131: The following lemma supplies the common finite construction. It is useful
132: because every supporting form at the regular profile is forced on its active
133: resource block, regardless of the chosen support-selection rule.
134: 
135: \begin{lemma}[Forced-support realization]\label{arch:realization}
136: Let $n\ge5$, and let $a_1\ge\cdots\ge a_{n-1}\ge0$ satisfy
137: \begin{equation}\label{arch:gapassumption}
138:  v_l:=(n-l)a_l\le\frac{n-l}{n}-\frac1{n^3}
139:  \qquad(1\le l<n).
140: \end{equation}
141: Set $v_0=1$, $v_n=0$, $\delta_l=v_l-v_{l+1}$ for $0\le l<n$, and
142: \[
143:  \varepsilon=\frac1{100n^6},\qquad
144:  t=1+(n-1)\varepsilon,\qquad \eta=\frac1{n^3}.
145: \]
146: For every nonempty proper subset $S\subset[n]$ define the form
147: \[
148:  w^S_j=
149:  \begin{cases}n^2/|S|^2,&j\in S,\\a_{|S|},&j\notin S,
150:  \end{cases}
151:  \qquad
152:  f(x)=\min\left\{\frac1n\sum_jx_j,
153:               w^S\cdot x:\varnothing\ne S\subsetneq[n]\right\}.
154: \]
155: At the regular profile
156: \begin{equation}\label{arch:regularprofile}
157:  b_i=e_i+\varepsilon\sum_{j\ne i}e_j,
158: \end{equation}
159: grand production is $T=t$. If $B$ is nonempty and proper, write
160: $C=[n]\setminus B$, $l=|C|$ and $r=n-l$. The unique minimizing form at
161: $x_B$ is $w^C$. Consequently every global linear support at $x_B$ is
162: this same form, and every closed owner's raw price exceeds $T$.
163: For every nonempty mask, including $B=[n]$, each open endpoint marginal
164: divided by $T$ is at least $\delta_l-\eta$.
165: 
166: These conclusions remain valid on the active block if $f$ is one summand
167: of a sum of technologies on disjoint resource blocks and the other reported
168: blocks are all zero.
169: \end{lemma}
170: \begin{proof}
171: The function $f$ is continuous, finite min-linear, nonnegative, monotone,
172: concave, and homogeneous of degree one. The full aggregate is $t\mathbf1$.
173: Its baseline value is $t$, whereas a subset form contributes at least
174: $n^2t/|S|>t$, so the baseline is uniquely minimal there.
175: 
176: At a nonempty proper mask, the aggregate has coordinate $r\varepsilon$ on
177: $C$ and $1+(r-1)\varepsilon$ elsewhere. The value of $w^C$ is
178: \begin{equation}\label{arch:maskedvalue}
179:  v_l+\varepsilon E_l,
180:  \qquad E_l=\frac{n^2r}{l}+(r-1)v_l,
181:  \qquad 0\le E_l\le2n^3.
182: \end{equation}
183: By \eqref{arch:gapassumption} and $2n^3\varepsilon<1/n^3$, this value is
184: strictly below $r/n$ and hence below the baseline $rt/n$; it is also below1.
185: Any subset $S$ meeting an open coordinate has value at least
186: $n^2/(n-1)^2>1$. If instead $\varnothing\ne S\subsetneq C$, its contribution
187: from the coordinates in $S$ is $n^2r\varepsilon/|S|$, strictly larger than
188: $n^2r\varepsilon/l$. Its off-$S$ coefficient is at least $a_l$; it multiplies
189: all open coordinates and additional nonnegative closed coordinates. Thus it
190: also costs strictly more than $w^C$. These cases exhaust the competitors.
191: 
192: Every coordinate of $x_B$ is positive. A finite minimum with a unique
193: minimizing linear form is differentiable there. If any other global linear
194: form majorizes $f$ and touches there, taking directional derivatives in both
195: directions forces its coefficients to equal that gradient. This proves the
196: assertion for all supports, including randomized selections. Each closed
197: owner has raw price at least
198: \[
199:  \frac{n^2}{l^2}\bigl(1+(l-1)\varepsilon\bigr)
200:  \ge\frac{n^2}{(n-1)^2}>t.
201: \]
202: 
203: Put $E_0=n-1$ and $E_n=0$. The exact masked values for all mask sizes are
204: $v_l+\varepsilon E_l$. Since $a_l$ is nonincreasing and nonnegative,
205: $(v_l)_{l=0}^n$ is nonincreasing and lies in $[0,1]$, so
206: $0\le\delta_l\le1$. Removing an open owner changes $l$ to $l+1$; its
207: unnormalized endpoint marginal is at least $\delta_l-2n^3\varepsilon$.
208: Normalization by $t$ loses at most an additional $(n-1)\varepsilon$:
209: \[
210:  \frac{F(x_B)-F(x_B-b_i)}{T}
211:  \ge\delta_l-(2n^3+n)\varepsilon
212:  \ge\delta_l-\eta.
213: \]
214: The same computation covers $l=0$ and $l=n-1$. Endpoint marginals are
215: nonnegative even when this displayed lower bound is negative.
216: 
217: For a sum of technologies on separate blocks, restriction of a global
218: support to the active block majorizes that summand and touches its value
219: at the active aggregate. The same interior-gradient argument therefore
220: forces precisely the support just computed. Inactive blocks contribute
221: zero to every reported bundle and every endpoint value.
222: \end{proof}

224: \begin{lemma}[Realizing decreasing endpoint increments]
225: \label{arch:increments}
226: Let $n\ge5$ and let $\delta_0\ge\cdots\ge\delta_{n-1}\ge0$ sum to1, with
227: $\delta_0\ge1/n+1/n^2$. Put
228: $v_l=\sum_{j=l}^{n-1}\delta_j$ and $a_l=v_l/(n-l)$ for $1\le l<n$.
229: Then the assumptions of Lemma~\ref{arch:realization} hold. At its regular
230: profile, every owner also satisfies $V_i/T\ge\delta_0-\eta$.
231: \end{lemma}
232: \begin{proof}
233: Since $v_{l+1}\le(n-l-1)\delta_l$, we have
234: $v_l/(n-l)\ge v_{l+1}/(n-l-1)$ whenever $l<n-1$.
235: Thus $a_l$ is nonincreasing. In particular
236: $a_l\le a_1=(1-\delta_0)/(n-1)$, whence
237: \[
238:  \frac{n-l}{n}-v_l
239:  \ge\frac{(n-l)(\delta_0-1/n)}{n-1}
240:  \ge\frac1{n^3}.
241: \]
242: Finally, $V_i$ includes the grand-coalition endpoint marginal. The $l=0$
243: case of Lemma~\ref{arch:realization} bounds it below by
244: $(\delta_0-\eta)T$.
245: \end{proof}

247: \subsection{A sharp owner obstruction already in dimension $d=n$}
248: 
249: \begin{theorem}[Global-cap owner and dimension barriers]
250: \label{arch:tbarrier}
251: For each $n\ge9$, let $m$ be the largest integer with $1\le m<n$ such that
252: \[
253:  S_m:=\sum_{l=0}^{m-1}\frac{m-l}{n-l}\le1,
254:  \qquad C_n=m-\frac1{n^2},\qquad A_n=n-2m.
255: \]
256: There is one fixed $n$-resource finite nonnegative min-linear technology on
257: which every global-$T$-cap rule of Definition~\ref{arch:def}, with the same
258: $n$ owners on all report profiles, has worst-profile factor at least
259: \begin{equation}\label{arch:tfinite}
260:  L_n=\frac{C_nA_n}{C_n+A_n}
261:       =(\sqrt2-o(1))\sqrt n.
262: \end{equation}
263: Since $d=n$, this is also a $(\sqrt2-o(1))\sqrt d$ architecture lower bound.
264: \end{theorem}
265: \begin{proof}
266: For $n\ge9$, $S_2<1$, so $m\ge2$. Also
267: $S_m\ge m(m+1)/(2n)$, implying $m<\sqrt{2n}<n/2$.
268: Define
269: \[
270:  \delta_l=\frac{m-l}{(n-l)S_m}\quad(0\le l<m),
271:  \qquad\delta_l=0\quad(m\le l<n).
272: \]
273: These increments sum to1 and are nonincreasing, because
274: $(m-l)/(n-l)$ decreases in $l$ when $m<n$. Moreover
275: $\delta_0=m/(nS_m)\ge2/n\ge1/n+1/n^2$.
276: Use Lemma~\ref{arch:increments} to define one technology $f$ and its
277: regular profile $P$. All closed raw prices at nonempty masks exceed $T$.
278: For a mask with $0\le l<m$ closed owners, its normalized total payment is
279: at least
280: \[
281:  l+(n-l)(\delta_l-\eta)
282:  =l+\frac{m-l}{S_m}-(n-l)\eta
283:  \ge m-\frac1{n^2}=C_n.
284: \]
285: The case $l=0$ is included. When $m\le l<n$, closed payments alone give
286: at least $l\ge m$. Thus every nonempty mask costs at least $C_n$ at $P$.
287: 
288: It remains to control the empty mask in this same technology. Index
289: coordinates cyclically modulo $n$. For each $j$, let
290: $S_j=\{j,j+1,\ldots,j+m-1\}$ and let $z_j$ have coordinate
291: $\tau=m/(4n^3)$ on $S_j$ and1 elsewhere. At the anchor profile $Q_j$, all
292: $n$ owners report this same bundle $z_j$. Since $a_m=0$, the form indexed
293: by $S_j$ gives
294: \[
295:  0<T_j=nf(z_j)\le n\frac{n^2\tau}{m}=\frac14.
296: \]
297: Strict positivity follows because $z_j$ is strictly positive and every
298: defining form has a positive coefficient.
299: 
300: For every empty-mask global support $w$, write $s=\sum_e w_e$.
301: We have $s\ge f(\mathbf1)=1$. Each coordinate lies in exactly $m$ cyclic
302: windows, so the uniform average of $w(S_j)$ is $ms/n$. Markov's inequality
303: shows that a fraction at least $1-2m/n$ of windows satisfies
304: $w(S_j)\le s/2$. At each such anchor,
305: \[
306:  w\cdot z_j\ge w([n]\setminus S_j)\ge s/2\ge1/2>T_j.
307: \]
308: All $n$ owners are therefore paid $T_j$ at the empty mask. The uniform
309: anchor average of normalized total payment is at least $n-2m=A_n>0$.
310: This is a pointwise statement for each one empty support, so it also holds
311: after randomizing that support according to its fixed law.
312: Lemma~\ref{arch:averaging} now proves \eqref{arch:tfinite} using only
313: $P,Q_1,\ldots,Q_n$.
314: 
315: Finally, uniformly for $m=O(\sqrt n)$,
316: \[
317:  S_m=\frac{m(m+1)}{2n}+O\left(\frac{m^3}{n^2}\right).
318: \]
319: Maximality of $m$ and this expansion give $m\sim\sqrt{2n}$.
320: Consequently $C_n\sim\sqrt{2n}$ and $A_n\sim n$, proving the stated
321: asymptotic factor.
322: \end{proof}

324: \subsection{Maximum-marginal caps retain the sharp owner obstruction}
325: 
326: The cap $V_i$ is a valid improvement to the payment rule, by
327: Lemma~\ref{arch:caplemma}. It can improve individual technologies. The next
328: result shows that it cannot improve the leading owner-count constant of the
329: fixed-mask architecture over general finite min-linear technologies.
330: 
331: \begin{theorem}[Sharp owner barrier with maximum-marginal caps]
332: \label{arch:vowner}
333: For $n\ge5$, let $m$ be the largest positive integer satisfying
334: $m(m+7)/2\le n-1$, and put
335: \[
336:  a=1-\frac{2m+1}{n},\qquad A_0=n-2,
337:  \qquad L'_n=\frac{amA_0}{am+A_0}.
338: \]
339: There is one fixed finite nonnegative min-linear technology on
340: $nm+n^2$ resources such that every $V_i$-cap rule of
341: Definition~\ref{arch:def}, with $n$ fixed owners, has worst-profile
342: total-payment factor at least
343: \begin{equation}\label{arch:vownerfactor}
344:  L'_n=(\sqrt2-o(1))\sqrt n.
345: \end{equation}
346: \end{theorem}
347: \begin{proof}
348: For $k=1,\ldots,m$, use the construction of Lemma~\ref{arch:realization}
349: with coefficients
350: \[
351:  a_{l,k}=\begin{cases}(n-l)/n^2,&l<k,\\0,&l\ge k.
352:  \end{cases}
353: \]
354: They are nonincreasing. If $l<k$, the gap in
355: \eqref{arch:gapassumption} is $(n-l)l/n^2\ge1/n^3$; if $l\ge k$, the
356: gap is $(n-l)/n\ge1/n^3$. Denote the resulting technology by $f_k$.
357: On separate coordinate blocks define
358: \begin{equation}\label{arch:ownertechnology}
359:  F(x^{(1)},\ldots,x^{(m)},z)
360:    =\sum_{k=1}^m f_k(x^{(k)})+\min_{1\le e\le n^2}z_e.
361: \end{equation}
362: A sum of finite minima on disjoint blocks is itself a finite minimum:
363: expand the finite product of form choices. Thus this is one fixed
364: finite nonnegative min-linear technology.
365: 
366: At profile $P_k$, activate the regular profile
367: \eqref{arch:regularprofile} only on block $k$, setting every other reported
368: block to zero. Grand production is $t$. At every nonempty proper mask,
369: Lemma~\ref{arch:realization} forces the active-block support and gives
370: closed raw prices exceeding $T=t$.
371: At the threshold $l=k-1$, with $r=n-l$, the unperturbed on value is
372: $r^2/n^2$ and the off value is zero. This also holds for $k=1$, because
373: the full-mask unperturbed value is1. Thus each threshold endpoint divided
374: by $T$ is at least $r^2/n^2-\eta$.
375: The total normalized open payment at that threshold is at least
376: \[
377:  r\left(\frac{r^2}{n^2}-\eta\right)
378:  \ge n-3l-\frac1{n^2}
379:  \ge n-3m-1=:D.
380: \]
381: Here $(n-l)^3/n^2\ge n-3l$.
382: 
383: Every owner has a coalition witnessing this same threshold marginal:
384: choose $n-k$ of the other owners and add the owner in question. Symmetry
385: of $P_k$ makes the value independent of those labels. Consequently
386: \[
387:  \frac{V_i}{T}\ge\frac{(n-k+1)^2}{n^2}-\eta
388:  \ge1-\frac{2(k-1)}n-\eta
389:  \ge1-\frac{2m+1}n=a>0.
390: \]
391: The last positivity follows from the defining inequality for $m$.
392: Since each closed raw price exceeds $T\ge V_i$, every closed owner
393: receives $V_i$, hence at least $aT$.
394: 
395: Let $S_0=m(m+1)/2$ and put
396: $\pi_k=(m-k+1)/S_0$ on the regular profiles. These weights sum to1,
397: and the choice of $m$ gives $S_0\le D$. For a mask with $0\le l<m$,
398: the mean normalized total payment across $P_k$ is at least
399: \[
400:  al+D\pi_{l+1}
401:  =al+D\frac{m-l}{S_0}
402:  \ge al+(m-l)\ge am.
403: \]
404: For $m\le l<n$, closed payments alone give at least $al\ge am$.
405: Thus every nonempty mask, including the full mask, has regular-profile
406: average at least $am$.
407: 
408: For empty-mask anchors, index the $n^2$ Leontief coordinates cyclically.
409: For each shift $j$, declare the $n$ coordinates
410: $S_j=\{j,\ldots,j+n-1\}$ scarce. Owner $i$ holds one unit of coordinate
411: $j+i-1$, zero of every other scarce coordinate, and two units of every
412: coordinate outside $S_j$. All other technology blocks are zero.
413: The grand production is1. Removing any owner eliminates its unique scarce
414: resource, reducing production to zero. Hence $V_i=T=1$ for every owner.
415: 
416: For an empty support, let $w$ be its restriction to the Leontief block.
417: Its nonnegative coefficient sum $s$ is at least1. Each coordinate lies in
418: exactly $n$ of the $n^2$ scarce windows, so the uniform mean scarce weight
419: is $s/n$. Markov's inequality gives
420: \[
421:  \Pr_j\{w(S_j)>s/2\}\le2/n.
422: \]
423: On every other anchor, each owner's abundant resources have raw price at
424: least $2(s/2)\ge1$. Its cap is1, so all $n$ owners receive1.
425: The mean normalized empty-mask payment is therefore at least $n-2=A_0$.
426: This holds for every realization of the one fixed empty-support law.
427: Lemma~\ref{arch:averaging}, with $C=am$, proves the finite lower bound.
428: 
429: Finally $m=\sqrt{2n}+O(1)$, $am=m-O(1)$, and $A_0=n-2$.
430: These give \eqref{arch:vownerfactor}.
431: \end{proof}

433: \subsection{A resource-dimension obstruction with maximum-marginal caps}
434: 
435: \begin{theorem}[Maximum-marginal dimension barrier]
436: \label{arch:vdimension}
437: For each $n\ge9$, put
438: \[
439:  m=\lfloor\sqrt{2n}\rfloor,\quad
440:  H=\sum_{l=1}^{m-1}\frac{m-l}{n-l},\quad a=\frac1{1+H},
441:  \quad R=\lceil n^{3/4}\rceil,\quad D=n+R,
442: \]
443: and define
444: \[
445:  C'_n=am-\frac2{n^2},\qquad A'_n=\frac{nR}{n+R}.
446: \]
447: There is one fixed finite nonnegative min-linear technology of dimension
448: $d_n=2n+R$ such that every $V_i$-cap rule of
449: Definition~\ref{arch:def}, with the same $n$ owners at every profile, has
450: worst-profile factor at least
451: \begin{equation}\label{arch:vdimensionfactor}
452:  \frac{C'_nA'_n}{C'_n+A'_n}
453:        =(1/2-o(1))\sqrt{d_n}.
454: \end{equation}
455: \end{theorem}
456: \begin{proof}
457: Set
458: \[
459:  \delta_0=a,\qquad
460:  \delta_l=a\frac{m-l}{n-l}\quad(1\le l<m),\qquad
461:  \delta_l=0\quad(m\le l<n).
462: \]
463: The increments sum to1 and are nonincreasing. Since $H\le m-1$,
464: $a\ge1/m$. Also $m<n$, so
465: \[
466:  a-\frac1n\ge\frac{n-m}{mn}\ge\frac1{n^2}.
467: \]
468: Lemma~\ref{arch:increments} therefore supplies a regular technology $f$.
469: Add a separate Leontief block:
470: \[
471:  F(x,z)=f(x)+\min_{1\le e\le D}z_e.
472: \]
473: This is a single finite min-linear technology on $n+D=d_n$ resources.
474: 
475: At the regular profile $P$, activate only the $f$ block. Each closed
476: raw price at a nonempty mask exceeds $T$, while
477: $V_i/T\ge a-\eta$. If $1\le l<m$, normalized total payment is at least
478: \[
479:  l(a-\eta)+(n-l)(\delta_l-\eta)
480:  =am-n\eta=am-\frac1{n^2}.
481: \]
482: For $m\le l<n$, closed payments alone give
483: $l(a-\eta)\ge am-1/n^2$. For $l=0$, endpoint payments give
484: $n(a-\eta)\ge am-1/n^2$. Thus every nonempty mask costs at least the
485: slightly weaker positive quantity $C'_n=am-2/n^2$.
486: Positivity follows from $am\ge1$.
487: 
488: For the empty-mask anchors, index the Leontief resources cyclically modulo
489: $D$. At shift $j$, the $n$ coordinates $S_j=\{j,\ldots,j+n-1\}$ are
490: scarce. Owner $i$ holds one unit of coordinate $j+i-1$, zero of every
491: other scarce coordinate, and $D$ units of every abundant coordinate.
492: All reports on the $f$ block are zero. Again $T=V_i=1$ for every owner.
493: 
494: The restriction $w$ of an empty support to the Leontief block has
495: nonnegative coefficients summing to at least1. Some coordinate $e$ has
496: $w_e\ge1/D$. It is abundant in exactly $R$ of the $D$ cyclic anchors.
497: On each of these anchors, every owner's raw price is at least
498: $D w_e\ge1$, so all $n$ capped payments equal1.
499: The uniform anchor average is at least $nR/D=A'_n$.
500: Although the coordinate $e$ can depend on the support realization, the
501: anchor list and its weights do not. The inequality thus holds pointwise
502: before averaging the fixed empty-support law.
503: Lemma~\ref{arch:averaging} proves the finite bound in
504: \eqref{arch:vdimensionfactor}.
505: 
506: For the asymptotic statement, $m\sim\sqrt{2n}$ and
507: \[
508:  H=\frac{m(m-1)}{2n}+O\left(\frac{m^3}{n^2}\right)\longrightarrow1.
509: \]
510: Hence $a\to1/2$ and $C'_n\sim\sqrt{n/2}$. Also
511: $A'_n\sim n^{3/4}$ and $d_n\sim2n$, yielding
512: \eqref{arch:vdimensionfactor}.
513: \end{proof}

515: \subsection{Scope of the matching statements}
516: 
517: The global owner-count upper factor $n\sqrt{2/(n+1)}$ is realized within
518: the fixed-mask support/endpoint architecture. Replacing its global cap by
519: $V_i$ only decreases closed payments and remains eligible by
520: Lemma~\ref{arch:caplemma}. Thus Theorems~\ref{arch:tbarrier} and
521: \ref{arch:vowner} show that the leading owner constant $\sqrt2$ is sharp
522: within the respective architectures. Likewise, the general
523: $O(\sqrt d)$ dimension upper and Theorems~\ref{arch:tbarrier} and
524: \ref{arch:vdimension} establish the square-root \emph{order} within those
525: architectures; they do not identify the optimal dimension constant.
526: 
527: The same lower constructions apply to the explicitly specified canonical
528: relative support: on the coordinate face of $x\ne0$, take the
529: minimum-Euclidean-norm nonnegative supporting form, extend its value to
530: $+\infty$ outside that face, and at zero use zero on the zero bundle and
531: $+\infty$ on every nonzero bundle. At each nonempty regular mask, the active
532: resource block is interior and all reported bundles lie in that face;
533: the unique-gradient argument in Lemma~\ref{arch:realization} therefore
534: forces the same prices. At the empty mask every nonzero anchor bundle has
535: infinite raw price. Each global-cap anchor then pays $T$ to all owners,
536: and each pivotal marginal-cap anchor pays $V_i=T$ to all owners, strengthening
537: the required anchor estimate. This observation concerns that particular
538: relative-support convention, not every unspecified extended-valued selector.
539: 
540: These lower bounds exclude improvements obtained solely by changing a
541: report-independent mask distribution, correlating its bits, choosing different
542: common supports from the open reports, or replacing the global cap by
543: maximum coalition marginals. They do not exclude report-dependent masks
544: whose incentives are separately proved, owner-specific supports coupled by
545: a new coverage argument, or a different payment architecture. No efficient
546: evaluation theorem, human specialist endorsement, or historical priority
547: claim is implied by these constructions.
