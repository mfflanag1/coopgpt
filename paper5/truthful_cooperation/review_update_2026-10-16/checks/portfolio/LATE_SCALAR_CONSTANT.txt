LATE SCALAR CONSTANT CHECK -- 15 October2026, separate from frozen results

Question. Can the factor2 in the scalar estimate be replaced by1+O(q),
uniformly over the number and size of nonnegative weights as q decreases to0?

Answer. No. The leading constant2 is exactly optimal for this scalar quantity.
This does not establish optimality of the full dimension-bound constant.

DEFINITIONS
Each weight u_i>=0 is independently closed with probability q and open with
probability p=1-q. Let S be the sum of open weights. Define
  X=sum_{closed i} min(u_i/S,1),
where a positive numerator over zero is capped at1 and a zero numerator
contributes0. Assume at least one weight is positive.

EXACT TWO-WEIGHT OBSTRUCTION
Take two equal positive weights u_1=u_2=1, with any additional weights zero.
When both are open, X=0. When exactly one is closed, X=1. When both are
closed, X=2. Therefore
  E[X]=2pq+2q^2=2q
for every q in[0,1]. In particular, no bound q+O(q^2), with a uniform finite
O-constant, can hold. The example remains within any fixed owner count n>=2
by adding zero-coordinate owners; those owners may have positive resources
in other coordinates. If all scalar weights must be positive, let the additional weights decrease
to0. The two distinguished weights alone have liminf contribution at least
2q; additional contributions are nonnegative. This suffices for the same
lower bound without asserting continuity of the entire zero-denominator sum.

UNIFORM SHARP FIRST-ORDER ASYMPTOTIC
For 0<q<1, the established reciprocal identity is
  E sum_{closed i} u_i/(u_i+S) = (q/p) P(S>0).
Pointwise min(u_i/S,1)<=2u_i/(u_i+S), including S=0 under the stated
conventions. Thus, for M(q)=sup_{n,u} E[X],
  2q <= M(q) <= 2q/(1-q).
Consequently
  lim_{q->0} M(q)/q = 2,
and M(q)=2q+O(q^2) uniformly over all finite owner counts and weights.
For q<=1/2 the explicit additive remainder bound is M(q)<=2q+4q^2.
The obstruction uses only two positive weights and needs no growing n.

IMPLICATION FOR THE DIMENSION PROOF
The sum over d coordinatewise scalar estimates cannot improve its leading
coefficient by replacing the scalar lemma alone. Any improvement of the
full total-payment upper from2sqrt(d) tosqrt(2d) must exploit compatibility
between coordinates, the selected support, normalized total value, or the
negative endpoint terms discarded in the existing proof. The scalar example
is NOT a technology/mechanism lower bound and does not establish2sqrt(d)
as the optimal unrestricted or architectural dimension constant.
