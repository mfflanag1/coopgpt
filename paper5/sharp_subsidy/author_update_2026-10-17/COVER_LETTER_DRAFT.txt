[After author approval: Please consider] “Sharp subsidies for protecting core
maxima in three-sided assignment markets” for the International Journal of
Game Theory.

The paper studies three equally sized finite sectors with nonnegative
pair-additive full-team values and a nonempty original core. If T>0 is the
grand-coalition worth and H_i is player i's maximum over that entire original
core, it asks for the least external subsidy preserving every original coalition
constraint while paying each player at least H_i/3. The reference core and its
maxima remain fixed before intervention.

The theorem gives s(M)<T/3 for every finite market, while the supremum of s(M)/T
over the class is exactly 1/3. A further sharp bound concerns integral partial-
matching obstructions, not the full fractional optimum at a fixed market size.
The proposed contribution is the assignment-specific aggregate compatibility
inequality: six comparisons of core maxima combine with separately attaining
core witnesses and residual budgets to control arbitrary fractional packings.
The manuscript credits the existing ideal-payoff protection and subsidy
frameworks, classical LP duality, and the earlier-project extremal family.
It does not claim a new general subsidy framework or assignment model.

The main text contains the complete central proof, finite strictness, integral
refinement and matching family. Online Resource 1 contains exact nonnegative
slack identities; Online Resource 2 supplies reproducible code and frozen inputs.
The finite checks corroborate the written proof. Broader higher-sector, flow,
transportation and ancillary appendices are preserved in a separate research
compendium and are not offered as additional contributions of this article.

Related work from the same research program includes “Acyclicity and
simultaneous protection of individual core maxima,” which studies protection
within the original core and classifies sector graphs, and “Sharp subsidies
for truthful production and network cooperation,” which imposes incentive
constraints across report profiles. This paper fixes the protection fraction
and minimizes static external funding. It uses shared concepts and tools;
its subsidy family differs from the Acyclicity triangle family.
