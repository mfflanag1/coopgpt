\section{Scope and notation}
This supplement accompanies the complete three-sided proof in the main paper.
It retains the exact slack identities from Section~7 of the October~7
compendium; it introduces no new theorem. The broader higher-sector, flow,
transportation and ancillary appendices remain in that preserved compendium
and are not part of this article's claimed contribution.

There are three finite sectors of equal size $n$, nonnegative pair-additive full-team
values, a nonempty original core and total matching worth $T>0$. Only full
teams produce value. For each player $(a,j)$, $H_{a,j}$ is its separately
attained maximum over the entire original core. Relabel an optimal perfect
matching diagonally; its team values $d_j$ sum to $T$. If $u_{a,j}$ denotes
the sum of the two diagonal pair values incident to $(a,j)$, then
\[
\sum_{a,j}u_{a,j}=2T,\qquad 0\le H_{a,j}\le d_j,
\qquad\delta_{a,j}=d_j-H_{a,j}\ge0.
\]
For a player $i=(a,j)$, write $u_i=u_{a,j}$ and $H_i=H_{a,j}$.
A fractional partial matching has nonnegative weights $\lambda_e$, player
loads $p_i=\sum_{e\ni i}\lambda_e\le1$ and residual weights $q_i=1-p_i$.
Define
\[
 k=n-\sum_e\lambda_e,\quad W=\sum_e\lambda_ev(e),\quad
 S=\sum_iq_iH_i,\quad V=3W+S,\quad
 \beta=\sum_iq_iu_i,\quad C_2=\sum_iq_i^2u_i,
\]
\[
 E=\sum_{a,j}\Bigl(\sum_{b\ne a}q_{b,j}\Bigr)\delta_{a,j}\ge0.
\]
The residual mass in each sector is $k$. For a team
$e=((1,j_1),(2,j_2),(3,j_3))$ and every ordered sector permutation $(a,b,c)$,
the main paper proves the six separately witnessed comparisons
\begin{equation}\label{eq:comparison}
 H_{c,j_c}-H_{c,j_b}\ge v(e)-d_{j_b}-u_{a,j_a}.
\end{equation}
It also proves, for $k>0$ and every player $i$,
\[
 kH_i\le T-W+(k-q_i)u_i.
\]
These inequalities use different attaining core points where necessary;
no simultaneous attainment is assumed. They imply the nonnegative slacks below.

\section{An exact slack certificate}\label{sec:slack}
For a team $e$ and a sector permutation $(a,b,c)$, let
\[
 \sigma_{e;a,b,c}
    =d_{j_b}+u_{a,j_a}+H_{c,j_c}-H_{c,j_b}-v(e)\ge0,
 \qquad
 L=\sum_e\lambda_e\sum_{(a,b,c)}\sigma_{e;a,b,c}.
\]
When $k>0$, define
\[
                  F=\sum_iq_i\bigl[T-W+(k-q_i)u_i-kH_i\bigr]\ge0.
\]
The two aggregations give the exact equalities
\[
       E+L=10T-2\beta-2V,\qquad F=3kT+k\beta-C_2-kV.
\]
Eliminating $\beta$ yields the nonnegative slack certificate
\begin{equation}\label{eq:slack}
                 2k(4T-V)=\frac{k}{2}(E+L)+C_2+F.
\end{equation}
For an integral partial matching, $C_2=\beta$ and the corresponding identity is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:integralslack}
                 (8k-5)T-(2k-1)V=\frac{k-1}{2}(E+L)+F.
\end{equation}
Both identities follow by substitution; their signs come from the
original-core arguments in the main paper, rather than a numerical feasibility test.
On the special partial matchings of the main paper's sharpness section, $E=L=F=0$,
consistent with equality in the integral bound.

The same calculation has a useful necessary-condition interpretation.
For an integral $P$ with $k\ge1$, suppose numbers $h_{a,j}$ obey the diagonal caps
$h_{a,j}\le d_j$, the six comparisons~\eqref{eq:comparison} for every
$e\in P$ with $h$ replacing $H$, and
$kh_i\le T-v(P)+(k-1)u_i$ for every unmatched player.
Then the proof of the main paper's integral residual-count theorem gives the same bound with
$\sum_{i\in U}h_i$ replacing $\sum_{i\in U}H_i$.
Thus these necessary conditions already imply the desired obstruction
bound; they need not characterize which vectors are actual core maxima.


\section{What the certificate does and does not prove}
For $k>0$, the first identity exhibits $V\le4T$. The strict finite-market
argument still requires the main paper's cases: if $C_2>0$, the inequality
is strict; if $C_2=0$, then $\beta=0$ and the residual bound gives $V\le3T$;
if $k=0$, all residual weights vanish and $W\le T$. Compactness of the
fractional matching polytope lets strictness be applied at its maximizing
point. Thus every fixed finite market has $s(M)<T/3$.

The integral refinement uses $C_2=\beta$ and an integer $k\ge1$. It bounds
an integral obstruction $J(M,P)$, not the full fractional optimum at a fixed
market size. The main paper includes a fractional packing with residual
mass $k=1$ and positive obstruction, ruling out that mistaken extension.

The family with parameter $\ell\ge1$ has $n=2\ell+1$, $T=3(2\ell+1)$,
all $H_i=3$ and $s(M)=2\ell$. Consequently $s(M)/T\uparrow1/3$ without
attaining it. It also attains the integral bound at $k=\ell+1$.
This is the current paper's family, not the different triangle family used
to bound protection within the original core in the Acyclicity companion.
