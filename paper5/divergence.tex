\title{Maximal divergence of the leximin and leximax\\
core selections in exact games}
\author{Maxwell Flanagan}
\date{August 10, 2026}

\begin{document}

\begin{abstract}
A singleton-normalized exact transferable-utility game carries two canonical
egalitarian core selections: the leximin allocation $\Lmin$ and the
leximax-min allocation $\Lmax$.  They coincide on every exact game with at
most four players.  We measure their separation by
$\rho(v)=\|\Lmin(v)-\Lmax(v)\|_1/v(N)$ and let $s(n)$ be its supremum on
$n$-player exact games.  We prove $s(5)=1/2$, $s(6)=2/3$, and
$\sup_n s(n)=2$.  An explicit two-parameter family $G(p,k)$ satisfies

\[
 \rho(G(p,k))=\frac{2(p(k-1)-1)}{k(p+1)},\qquad
 2-\rho(G(p,k))=\frac{2n}{k(p+1)},\quad n=p+1+k,
\]

and yields $s(n)\ge 2-8n/(n^2-(n\bmod2))$.  A common family of tight caps
drives both selections: leximin treats each cap as a ceiling and concentrates
the residual on one player, whereas leximax-min treats it as a budget and
spreads the residual, making the two supports asymptotically disjoint.  On
the two-generator class we also give a dual upper-cap description of the
core and a one-coalition certificate for the leximin floor; the mirror
leximax-min ceiling statement fails.

The fixed-dimensional upper bounds are computer-assisted.  At five players,
two exact pipelines exhaust all configurations and use independently
formulated Lmin-optimality components; they prove the sharp value and the
$1/3$ bound
in the leximin $(4,1)$ regime.
At six players, singleton exactness and lexicographic optimality map a strict
counterexample into one of fifteen sign-labelled blocker systems.  Exact
proof objects certify all fifteen infeasible; the largest systems reduce to
nineteen role-level exchange schemas and three negative-cut templates.  The
human counterexample-to-relaxation implication, finite certificates, and
novelty determination are kept as separate review obligations.
For the dimension-free upper-bound program, we prove the conjectured atomic
overlap inequality on every weighted four-role quotient satisfying the
canonical unit-role normalization.  A connected Lorenz-frontier argument
confines the two selectors to one pair-box edge.  The quantitative proof is
human for atomic weight at least ten and uses a complete quantified
linear-real audit of 1,152 cells for the six residual weight rows at weights
six through nine.  This quotient theorem makes common selector modules
inductively contractible in the general carrier program.  For strict-loss
carrier cycles, proper tight packets exist and their cardinality base is
automatically target-sized; comparison--residual cancellation reduces the
remaining direct arm to scalar nongainer-block selection.  A new exact
seven-player control refutes selection of the full maximum-overlap root block
and forces a labelled tight-support enlargement, locating the remaining gap
in staged transport congestion or a marked-facet alternative.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} cooperative games; exact games; core;
leximin; leximax; Lorenz order; computer-assisted proof.\\

\section{Introduction}

Let $(N,v)$ be a transferable-utility game on $N=\{1,\dots,n\}$ with
\[
 \core(v)=\{x\in\mathbb R^N: x(N)=v(N),\ x(S)\ge v(S)\ \text{for all}\ S\subseteq N\},
\]
where $x(S)=\sum_{i\in S}x_i$.  The game is \emph{exact} if every coalition
inequality is attained by some core allocation, and \emph{singleton-normalized}
if $v(\{i\})=0$ for every $i$; on that domain every core allocation is
nonnegative.  Exact games are the natural home for egalitarian core
selections, and two such selections are canonical.  The \emph{leximin}
allocation $\Lmin(v)$ maximizes the increasing rearrangement of $x$
lexicographically over the core; the \emph{leximax-min} allocation
$\Lmax(v)$ minimizes the decreasing rearrangement lexicographically over the
core.  Both are single-valued on a nonempty compact convex set.  The first
raises the poorest as far as possible, the second lowers the richest as far
as possible.

On small games the two agree.  The companion paper \citep{Flanagan2026}
proves that every singleton-normalized exact game on at most four players is
an exact partition game at $\Lmin$; by the established implication from exact
partition to Lorenz domination \citep{LlerenaMauri2017}, $\Lmin$ then
Lorenz-dominates every core allocation, and a Lorenz-greatest core point is
simultaneously leximin- and leximax-optimal\footnote{With equal totals,
Lorenz domination gives $\sum_{i\le k}x^{\uparrow}_i\ge
\sum_{i\le k}y^{\uparrow}_i$ for every $k$; induction on $k$ yields
$x^{\uparrow}\ge_{\mathrm{lex}}y^{\uparrow}$, and complementing partial
sums gives the leximax-min half.  See \citet{DuttaRay1989} for the
Lorenz-dominant core element on convex games.}, so
$\Lmin(v)=\Lmax(v)$ for every singleton-normalized exact game with $n\le4$.
Our complete enumerations at $n\le4$ reconfirm this rigidity as a control
(Section~\ref{sec:fixedn}).  The adjacent large-core chain --- exact games on
at most four players have large cores \citep{Biswas1999}, and on large-core
games the procedural egalitarian solution coincides with $\Lmax$
\citep{Dietzenbacher2021,ArinKuipersVermeulen2003} --- pins only the $\Lmax$
side: it does not deliver the coincidence, and indeed a five-player exact
game with a large core has $\Lmin\ne\Lmax$: the lower envelope of
$(2,0,12,6,0)$, $(0,0,0,0,20)$ and $(0,2,10,4,4)$, with
$\|\Lmin-\Lmax\|_1=v(N)/5$ (verified exactly; archived with the
repository).  The companion's lower-envelope realization technique is
used again here.

The pair itself has recent precedent.  \citet{Vazirani2024assignment} and
\citet{GangamEtAl2024} study leximin and leximax core imputations of
assignment, flow and matching games, observe on examples that the two
selections differ, and describe their opposition in exactly the direction
this paper quantifies; the leximax selection appears earlier as the leximax
solution of neighbour games \citep{KlijnEtAl2003} and as the lexmax
solution in the egalitarian-set literature \citep{LlerenaMauri2016},
besides its large-core characterization \citep{ArinKuipersVermeulen2003}.
The candidate contribution here is the quantitative worst case: the exact
domain, the supremum $2$ with its support-disjointness characterization, the
closed-form extremal family, and the direction result that the
\emph{concentrating} selection is the leximin one.  A targeted review of the
principal egalitarian-core, antiduality, exact-partition, large-core, and
exact-cone sources found no prior fixed-dimensional separation bound
\citep{ArinEtAl2008,DietzenbacherYanovskaya2020,StudenyKratochvil2022}.
The closest adjacent base-polyhedron theory studies one decreasingly minimal
or lexicographically optimal base and its canonical partitions
\citep{FrankMurota2022}.  More sharply, \citet{SchootUiterkamp2024}
characterizes compact convex sets having a least translated and rescaled
majorized point for every translation as base-polyhedra, and derives the
corresponding characterization of convex TU games.  That theorem concerns a
least-majorized point after every strategic translation, not the separation
of two opposing selectors in one arbitrary exact-game core.  The exact and
totally balanced cone papers classify
facets in game-value space \citep{KroupaStudeny2019,StudenyKratochvil2022}.
This is not a hidden change of vocabulary: if an exact game's core is directly
the base-polyhedron $B(f)$ of a submodular $f$, exactness gives
$v(S)=\min_{x\in B(f)}x(S)=f(N)-f(N\setminus S)$, so $v$ is convex.  Thus the
direct base-polyhedron transfer covers the convex subdomain, not arbitrary
exact games.
On the special two-bound domain, where the core is a box intersected with the
efficiency hyperplane, the egalitarian core is a singleton given by a clipped-
level formula \citep{GongDietzenbacherPeters2022}; hence the opposing Lorenz
selections coincide there.  The later one-bound class is a reduced-game-stable
subclass of the same coincidence domain \citep{GongDietzenbacherPeters2024}.
Exact-core-bound reductions now extend to all
balanced games, but the resulting coordinate-bound equality values can lie
outside the core already with three players \citep{GongDietzenbacher2026}.
Thus neither checked formulation states or implies a distance theorem for the
two opposing selections on arbitrary exact cores.  This negative search is
not a novelty proof, and the claim remains subject to specialist review.
The Lorenz-monotonic allocation-scheme framework of
\citet{IzquierdoMontesRafels2024} is another close terminological neighbor:
its allocations are indexed across subgames and its exactness strengthening
characterizes convexity, rather than bounding the two selections within one
exact core.  Likewise, the maximal population-monotonic egalitarian domain of
\citet{DietzenbacherDogan2025} characterizes a Dutta--Ray allocation scheme
across populations rather than the separation of the two selections in one
core.

The question of this paper is what happens after the rigidity breaks.  Write
\[
 \rho(v)=\frac{\|\Lmin(v)-\Lmax(v)\|_1}{v(N)},
 \qquad
 s(n)=\sup\bigl\{\rho(v):\ v\ \text{exact on $n$ players},\ v(N)>0\bigr\},
\]
the supremum running over singleton-normalized games.
Since $\Lmin(v)$ and $\Lmax(v)$ are nonnegative with the same total $v(N)$,
we have $\rho\le2$ always, and $\rho=2$ would mean disjoint supports.  The
rigidity says $s(n)=0$ for $n\le4$.  How large can $s(n)$ get?

\paragraph{Results.}
The answer is: as large as arithmetically possible.

\begin{itemize}[nosep]
\item $\sup_n s(n)=2$, and at fixed $n$, $s(n)\ge 2-8n/(n^2-(n\bmod2))$,
      which is $2-8/n$ for even $n$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:sup}); in particular
      $s(n)\ge1$ from $n=8$ and $s(n)>1$ from $n=9$; the bound is the full
      exact-class value at $n=6$, and is attained on the two-generator class
      at $n=7,8$ by complete enumeration.
\item The witnesses form a two-parameter family $G(p,k)$ whose two
      allocations are computed in closed form (Theorem~\ref{thm:family}),
      with the deficit identity $2-\rho(G(p,k))=2n/(k(p+1))$
      (Corollary~\ref{cor:deficit}).
\item On the two-generator class the core has a dual description as a system
      of upper caps (Theorem~\ref{thm:duality}), from which the leximin floor
      inherits a one-coalition certificate (Theorem~\ref{thm:floor}); the
      mirror statement for the leximax-min ceiling is false, and we give the
      smallest counterexample (Observation~\ref{obs:Mside}).
\item $n=5$ is anomalous: the family formula gives $1/3$, a game analysed
      by hand attains $1/2$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:n5}), and the value is the
      supremum --- $s(5)=1/2$, with $\rho\le1/3$ in the $(4,1)$ leximin
      regime (Theorems~\ref{thm:s5} and~\ref{thm:regime41}, by
      configuration-complete finite check, twice over) --- while the $n=5$
      extremal class is qualitatively unlike the $n\ge6$ one.
\item At $n=6$ the family lower bound is sharp: $s(6)=2/3$
      (Theorem~\ref{thm:s6}).  The upper bound is a table-free exact finite
      proof using singleton exactness witnesses and lexicographic exchange
      blockers; all fifteen sign-count cases have independently checked CPC
      proof objects.
\item In the weighted four-role quotient program, the conjectured atomic
      overlap bound holds for every exact nonnegative quotient satisfying
      the unit-role singleton hypothesis
      (Theorem~\ref{thm:atomic-four-role}).
      A human adjacent-gap theorem closes all atomic weights $n\ge10$; a
      stratum-free quantified linear-real audit closes the six residual rows
      at $n=6,7,8,9$.  Weighted common-face rigidity and a connected
      positive-Lorenz continuation show that the selectors always share two
      pair endpoints.  This completes the quotient theorem, not the full
      fixed-$n$ theorem.  Strong induction now discharges common carrier
      modules, and strict-loss cycles automatically have proper packets with
      target-sized cardinality base.  An exact role-irreducible seven-player
      control refutes the full-root-block scalar shortcut while preserving the
      target: its root budget is $-1$, but a labelled tight-support enlargement
      closes at base $5/6$.  The remaining arm is selected-support congestion
      or a marked-facet alternative.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{The mechanism, in one sentence.}
In $G(p,k)$ a single family of tight caps forces $p+1$ players to share only
$p$; leximin reads each cap as a ceiling, so the floor sticks at $p/(p+1)$
and the entire residual piles onto one player, while leximax-min reads the
same cap as a budget, loading half the players up to $p(k-1)/k$ and thereby
squeezing the capped players down to $1/k$ --- so the two supports become
asymptotically disjoint and $\rho\to2$.  It is worth stating the direction
explicitly, because it inverts the usual reading: the \emph{concentrating}
selection is the leximin one.  In $G(p,k)$ the leximin allocation gives one
player a share $p(k-1)/((p+1)k)\to1$ of $v(N)$ while everyone else sits just
below $1$; it is nevertheless leximin-optimal, because nothing better is
feasible for the poorest.  Both selections are driven into extreme shapes by
the same constraint, in opposite directions.

\paragraph{What is not proved.}
An upper bound matching Theorem~\ref{thm:sup} at fixed $n$ is open, and
Section~\ref{sec:obstruction} shows that one natural route to it is closed.
Section~\ref{sec:scope} separates the proved statements from the enumerated
and the conjectured ones, and lists the open problems.

\section{Preliminaries}\label{sec:prelim}

Throughout, $N=\{1,\dots,n\}$, all games are singleton-normalized, and
$T:=v(N)>0$.  Vectors are indexed by $N$, and $\supp(x)=\{i:x_i\ne0\}$.

\subsection{Exact games as lower envelopes}

The following generation fact is the only construction used in this paper.
It is standard; see \citet{CsokaHeringsKoczy2011} and
\citet{StudenyKratochvil2022} for the surrounding theory of the exact cone,
and \citet{Flanagan2026} for the same statement in the companion.

\begin{lemma}[lower-envelope generation]\label{lem:envelope}
Let $g_1,\dots,g_r\in\mathbb R_+^N$ have a common total
$g_1(N)=\dots=g_r(N)=T$, and set $v(S)=\min_{j}g_j(S)$.  Then $v$ is exact,
$v(N)=T$, every $g_j$ lies in $\core(v)$, and $v$ is singleton-normalized as
soon as every coordinate vanishes in some $g_j$.  Conversely, every exact
singleton-normalized game is of this form: one may take $g_1,\dots,g_r$ to be
the vertices of $\core(v)$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Each $g_j$ satisfies $g_j(S)\ge v(S)$ by definition of the minimum and
$g_j(N)=T=v(N)$, so $g_j\in\core(v)$; and the inequality at $S$ is attained
by whichever $g_j$ realizes the minimum, so $v$ is exact.  The singleton
statement is immediate from $v(\{i\})=\min_j (g_j)_i$.  Conversely, if $v$ is
exact then $v(S)=\min_{x\in\core(v)}x(S)$ for every $S$, and the minimum of a
linear functional over the polytope $\core(v)$ is attained at a vertex; the
vertices are nonnegative because $v$ is singleton-normalized, and all have
total $T$.
\end{proof}

We call a game generated as in Lemma~\ref{lem:envelope} by $r$ allocations an
\emph{$r$-generator} game.  Part of what follows is specific to $r=2$.

\subsection{The two selections}

For $x\in\mathbb R^N_+$ let $x^\uparrow$ be its increasing and
$x^\downarrow$ its decreasing rearrangement.

\begin{definition}
$\Lmin(v)$ is the allocation in $\core(v)$ whose increasing rearrangement is
lexicographically largest; $\Lmax(v)$ is the allocation in $\core(v)$ whose
decreasing rearrangement is lexicographically smallest.
\end{definition}

Both exist and are unique on a nonempty compact convex set: existence is
compactness applied stage by stage, and uniqueness follows by comparing two
putative optima with their midpoint, which is feasible by convexity and
strictly better at the first stage where they differ.  We write
$\mu(v)=\min_i\Lmin(v)_i$ for the \emph{leximin floor} and
$M(v)=\max_i\Lmax(v)_i$ for the \emph{leximax-min ceiling}; equivalently
$\mu=\max_{x\in\core}\min_i x_i$ and $M=\min_{x\in\core}\max_i x_i$.

\subsection{Normalization and the ratio}

Two conventions have to be stated honestly, because neither selection is
translation-equivariant: both are applied to the raw payoff vector $x$, not
to $x-(v(\{i\}))_{i\in N}$, and on a game that is not singleton-normalized
these are different objects --- the leximax-min allocation in particular
changes under the shift.  We therefore fix the domain once and for all: all
games are singleton-normalized, and both selections are computed on the
normalized game.  No claim below asserts invariance of $\Lmin$ or $\Lmax$
under adding singleton values.

Scaling, by contrast, is harmless: $\core(cv)=c\core(v)$ for $c>0$ and both
selections are positively homogeneous, so $\Lmin(cv)=c\Lmin(v)$ and
$\Lmax(cv)=c\Lmax(v)$.  Hence $\rho(v)=\|\Lmin(v)-\Lmax(v)\|_1/v(N)$ is
scale-invariant, which is what makes it comparable across games of different
size.  We record the trivial ceiling and the form of the deficit.

\begin{lemma}[ceiling and overlap]\label{lem:ceiling}
For any singleton-normalized exact $v$ with $T=v(N)>0$,
\[
 \rho(v)=2\Bigl(1-\frac1T\sum_{i\in N}\min\bigl(\Lmin(v)_i,\Lmax(v)_i\bigr)\Bigr)
 \ \le\ 2,
\]
with $\rho(v)=2$ if and only if $\Lmin(v)$ and $\Lmax(v)$ have disjoint
supports.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For nonnegative $x,y$ with $x(N)=y(N)=T$ one has
$|x_i-y_i|=x_i+y_i-2\min(x_i,y_i)$, and summing gives
$\|x-y\|_1=2T-2\sum_i\min(x_i,y_i)$.  The overlap term is nonnegative and
vanishes exactly when no coordinate is positive in both.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[zero-floor null-player descent]\label{lem:null-descent}
Let $v$ be singleton-normalized and exact.  If
\[
 \min_i\Lmin(v)_i=0,
\]
then some player $r$ is null.  If $v'$ is the restriction to
$N\setminus\{r\}$, then
\[
 \core(v)=\core(v')\times\{0_r\},
 \quad \Lmin(v)=(\Lmin(v'),0_r),
 \quad \Lmax(v)=(\Lmax(v'),0_r).
\]
In particular, deletion preserves total worth, overlap, and $\rho$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The first Lmin stage is
$\max_{y\in\core(v)}\min_i y_i$.  If its value is zero, no core allocation
is positive in every coordinate.  If each player $i$ were positive at some
$y^i\in\core(v)$, then the average of the finitely many $y^i$ would be a core
allocation positive in every coordinate, a contradiction.  Hence some fixed
$r$ satisfies $y_r=0$ throughout the core.  Exactness gives, for
$S\subseteq N\setminus\{r\}$,
\[
 v(S\cup\{r\})=\min_{y\in\core(v)}y(S\cup\{r\})
 =\min_{y\in\core(v)}y(S)=v(S),
\]
so $r$ is null.

Since $v(N\setminus\{r\})=v(N)$, efficiency and nonnegativity force the
$r$ coordinate of every core point to zero.  Restriction maps the core into
$\core(v')$.  Conversely, extending any point of $\core(v')$ by zero checks
all coalitions containing $r$ by nullity, so it lies in $\core(v)$.  This
proves the product identity.  Deleting a coordinate fixed throughout the
feasible set leaves both lexicographic comparisons unchanged, proving the
selection and overlap assertions.
\end{proof}

Put $c_n=n/\lfloor n^2/4\rfloor$.  These constants strictly decrease:
for $n=2k$,
\[
 c_{n-1}-c_n=\frac{2k-1}{k(k-1)}-\frac2k>0,
\]
and for $n=2k+1$,
\[
 c_{n-1}-c_n=\frac2k-\frac{2k+1}{k(k+1)}>0.
\]
Consequently the zero-floor branch in dimension $n$ follows inductively from
the hybrid-overlap theorem in dimension $n-1$.  The five-player overlap floor
$3/4$ proved below exceeds $c_6=2/3$, and Theorem~\ref{thm:s6} therefore
closes every zero-floor seven-player game.  More generally, a
minimal-dimensional counterexample to the proposed all-$n$ bound must have
strictly positive Lmin floor and no null player.  This elementary descent is
not claimed as a novel null-player result; its role is to remove the entire
zero-floor branch from the remaining packet argument.

So $\rho\to2$ is precisely asymptotic support disjointness of the two
egalitarian selections, and the whole content of the paper is that this can
be forced.

\section{The two-generator class}\label{sec:twogen}

Throughout this section $v$ is the lower envelope of exactly two nonnegative
allocations $g,h$ with common total $T$, so that $v(S)=\min(g(S),h(S))$ for
every $S$ (including $S=N$, where both sides equal $T$).

\begin{lemma}[support disjointness]\label{lem:disjoint}
If $v$ is singleton-normalized then $A:=\supp(g)$ and $B:=\supp(h)$ are
disjoint, and every player outside $A\cup B$ is null, receiving $0$ in every
core allocation.  Deleting null players changes neither $\Lmin$, $\Lmax$, nor
$T$, so we may and do assume $A\cup B=N$ and $A\cap B=\emptyset$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
$v(\{i\})=\min(g_i,h_i)=0$ forces $g_i=0$ or $h_i=0$, which is exactly
$A\cap B=\emptyset$.  If $i\notin A\cup B$ then $g_i=h_i=0$, so
$v(N\setminus\{i\})=\min\bigl(g(N\setminus\{i\}),h(N\setminus\{i\})\bigr)=T$;
any $x\in\core(v)$ has $x(N\setminus\{i\})\ge T$ and $x(N)=T$ with $x\ge0$,
hence $x_i=0$.  Both selections assign a null player $0$, so deletion is
harmless.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}
For $S\subseteq N$ put $f(S):=\max\bigl(g(S),h(S)\bigr)=\max\bigl(g(S\cap A),h(S\cap B)\bigr)$.
\end{definition}

\subsection{A dual description of the core}

\begin{theorem}[two-generator core duality]\label{thm:duality}
\[
 \core(v)=\bigl\{x\ge0:\ x(N)=T\ \text{and}\ x(S)\le f(S)\ \text{for all }S\subseteq N\bigr\}.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For $x$ with $x(N)=T$ and any $S$, the inequality $x(S)\ge v(S)$ is
equivalent to $x(N\setminus S)\le T-v(S)$.  It therefore suffices to prove
\[
 T-v(S)=f(N\setminus S)\qquad\text{for every }S\subseteq N.  \tag{1}
\]
Since $g$ and $h$ are modular with $g(N)=h(N)=T$,
\[
 T-v(S)=T-\min\bigl(g(S),h(S)\bigr)
 =\max\bigl(T-g(S),\,T-h(S)\bigr)
 =\max\bigl(g(N\setminus S),\,h(N\setminus S)\bigr)
 =f(N\setminus S).
\]
Replacing $S$ by $N\setminus S$ in (1) turns the family
$\{x(S)\ge v(S)\}$ into the family $\{x(S)\le f(S)\}$.  Finally $x\ge0$ is
exactly the family of singleton inequalities $x(\{i\})\ge v(\{i\})=0$.
\end{proof}

Identity (1) uses only modularity and the common total; the disjointness of
Lemma~\ref{lem:disjoint} is not needed for it, and enters only to normalize
the presentation of $f$.  Note also that $S=N$ gives $f(N)=T$, so the cap at
$N$ is implied by efficiency, and $S=\emptyset$ gives $f(\emptyset)=0$.

In fact the duality is fully general: for \emph{any} singleton-normalized
game, setting $f(S):=T-v(N\setminus S)$, the same complementation involution
gives $\core(v)=\{x\ge0:x(N)=T,\ x(S)\le f(S)\ \forall S\}$.  The
two-generator content of Theorem~\ref{thm:duality} is the computation
$f=\max(g,h)$.  The general form is used in Section~\ref{sec:n5closed}.

\subsection{The leximin floor}

\begin{theorem}[leximin floor: the easy direction]\label{thm:floor}
Let
\[
 \lambda:=\min_{\emptyset\ne S\subseteq N}\frac{f(S)}{|S|}
 =\min_{(A',B')\ne(\emptyset,\emptyset)}
   \frac{\max\bigl(g(A'),h(B')\bigr)}{|A'|+|B'|},
\]
the second minimum running over $A'\subseteq A$, $B'\subseteq B$.  Then
$\mu\le\lambda$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
$\Lmin(v)$ is a core allocation, so by Theorem~\ref{thm:duality}, for every
nonempty $S$,
$|S|\,\mu\le\Lmin(v)(S)\le f(S)$, i.e.\ $\mu\le f(S)/|S|$.  Take the minimum
over $S$.
\end{proof}

The bound is a \emph{one-coalition certificate}: a single tightest cap
already pins the floor from above.  Its natural habitat is polymatroid
theory: when $f$ is monotone submodular, the polytope of
Theorem~\ref{thm:duality} is a polymatroid base polytope, the leximin floor
\emph{equals} $\lambda$, and the decreasingly-minimal and
increasingly-maximal points coincide --- the theory of lexicographically
optimal bases \citep{Fujishige1980}, where $\rho=0$ identically.  The
two-generator $f=\max(g,h)$ is not submodular in general; the conjecture
below asks whether the floor identity survives the loss of submodularity,
and the rest of the paper measures exactly how far the dec-min/inc-max
coincidence fails.  Empirically the bound is exact.

\begin{conjecture}[leximin floor formula]\label{conj:floor}
$\mu=\lambda$ for every singleton-normalized two-generator exact game.
\end{conjecture}

\paragraph{Evidence and reduction.}
Conjecture~\ref{conj:floor} holds in $586$ of $586$ random two-generator
games at $n=4,5,6,7$ (sample sizes $120$, $218$, $165$, $83$), with all
arithmetic exact.  What remains to prove is a clean linear-programming
statement.  Equality means that the system
\[
 y\ge0,\qquad y(N)=T-n\lambda,\qquad y(S)\le f'(S):=f(S)-\lambda|S|
\]
is feasible; indeed, given such $y$, the allocation $x=y+\lambda\one$
satisfies $x(N)=T$, $x\ge\lambda\one\ge0$ and $x(S)\le f(S)$, hence lies in
the core with floor at least $\lambda$.  Note $f'\ge0$ by definition of
$\lambda$.  By linear-programming duality,
\[
 \max\{y(N):\ y\ge0,\ y(S)\le f'(S)\ \forall S\}
 =\min\Bigl\{\textstyle\sum_S w_Sf'(S):\ w\ge0,\ \sum_{S\ni i}w_S\ge1\ \forall i\Bigr\},
\]
the minimum running over fractional covers.  Taking $w=\one_{\{S=N\}}$ shows
the minimum is at most $f'(N)=T-n\lambda$, so Conjecture~\ref{conj:floor} is
exactly the assertion
\[
 \sum_S w_Sf'(S)\ \ge\ f'(N)\qquad\text{for every fractional cover }w. \tag{2}
\]

\begin{proposition}[balanced covers]\label{prop:balanced}
Inequality (2) holds for every exact (balanced) cover, i.e.\ whenever
$\sum_{S\ni i}w_S=1$ for all $i$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Balancedness gives $\sum_Sw_S|S|=\sum_i\sum_{S\ni i}w_S=n$, so, using
$f\ge g$ and the modularity of $g$,
\[
 \sum_Sw_Sf'(S)=\sum_Sw_Sf(S)-\lambda n
 \ \ge\ \sum_Sw_Sg(S)-\lambda n
 =\sum_i g_i\sum_{S\ni i}w_S-\lambda n
 =g(N)-\lambda n=f'(N),
\]
the last step because $g(N)=T$ and $f'(N)=T-\lambda n$.
The same computation works with $h$ in place of $g$.
\end{proof}

What is missing is the case of covers that strictly over-cover some players.
Writing $e_i:=\sum_{S\ni i}w_S-1\ge0$, the same computation gives
\[
 \sum_Sw_Sf'(S)\ \ge\ f'(N)
 +\max\Bigl(\sum_{i\in A}g_ie_i,\ \sum_{i\in B}h_ie_i\Bigr)
 -\lambda\sum_{i\in N}e_i,
\]
and since $\lambda\le f(\{i\})$, which equals $g_i$ for $i\in A$ and $h_i$
for $i\in B$, each inner sum dominates $\lambda$ times its \emph{own} block's
excess but not the other block's.  Closing that gap, or constructing the
attaining core allocation directly, is the remaining work.

\subsection{The leximax-min ceiling is not dual}

The mirror-image statement is an inequality but not an identity, and this is
the first place where the two selections part company.

\begin{observation}[$M$-side asymmetry]\label{obs:Mside}
For every core allocation, and in particular for $\Lmax(v)$,
\[
 M\ \ge\ \max_{\substack{P\subseteq A,\ Q\subseteq B\\ (P,Q)\ne(\emptyset,\emptyset)}}
 \frac{\min\bigl(g(P),h(Q)\bigr)}{|P|+|Q|}. \tag{3}
\]
Inequality (3) is not an identity: it fails strictly already at $n=6$.
\end{observation}

\begin{proof}[Proof of the inequality]
Let $x\in\core(v)$ and put $S=P\cup Q$.  Then $g(S)=g(P)$ and $h(S)=h(Q)$
because $g$ vanishes on $B$ and $h$ on $A$, so
\[
 \max_i x_i\ \ge\ \frac{x(S)}{|S|}\ \ge\ \frac{v(S)}{|S|}
 =\frac{\min\bigl(g(P),h(Q)\bigr)}{|P|+|Q|}.
\]
Apply this to $x=\Lmax(v)$.  Equivalently, (3) is Theorem~\ref{thm:duality}
read on complements.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{How to read (3).}
The maximum runs over pairs $(P,Q)$ other than $(\emptyset,\emptyset)$, with
the convention $g(\emptyset)=h(\emptyset)=0$.  There is no infinity
convention: a pair with an empty part contributes $\min(\cdot,0)=0$ and is
simply inactive, so only pairs with \emph{both} parts nonempty can bind.
This is visible in the proof, where an empty part makes $v(S)$ the minimum of
something and $0$.  Reading an empty part as $+\infty$ instead would reduce
(3) to a maximum over singletons and give a different --- and wrong --- value;
the misreading is easy enough that we spell the convention out.

\paragraph{The smallest counterexample.}
Scanning the complete two-generator enumeration in increasing $T$, no
counterexample to equality in (3) exists at $n\le5$ for any $T$, nor at $n=6$
for $T\le4$.  At $n=6$, $T=5$:
\[
 g=(3,2,0,0,0,0),\qquad h=(0,0,2,1,1,1),
\]
\[
 \Lmin(v)=\Bigl(1,\tfrac23,\tfrac43,\tfrac23,\tfrac23,\tfrac23\Bigr),
 \qquad
 \Lmax(v)=\Bigl(\tfrac65,\tfrac45,\tfrac65,\tfrac35,\tfrac35,\tfrac35\Bigr),
\]
so $M=6/5$, whereas the right-hand side of (3) equals $1$, attained for
instance at $P=\{1\},Q=\{3\}$ with $\min(3,2)/2=1$.  The gap is $1/5$.  One
further counterexample class occurs at $n=6$, $T=5$: $g=(3,1,1)$,
$h=(2,2,1)$ on the respective supports (its block-swapped relabelling is the
same game up to permutation), again with $M=6/5$ against a bound of $1$.  Over the same random samples used for
Conjecture~\ref{conj:floor}, (3) is tight in $120/120$ games at $n=4$,
$218/218$ at $n=5$, $160/165$ at $n=6$ and $74/83$ at $n=7$.

\begin{remark}[diagnosis]\label{rem:asymmetry}
Theorem~\ref{thm:floor} bounds the floor using only the upper caps
$x(S)\le f(S)$, and a single tightest cap can be saturated on its own, which
is why one coalition certifies the optimum.  Bounding the ceiling instead
requires the lower bounds $x(N\setminus S)\ge T-f(S)$, and \emph{minimizing}
a maximum must respect several of them simultaneously; no single one
certifies the optimum.  This is a genuine asymmetry between the two
selections, and it is the reason $\Lmax$ is the harder object throughout.
\end{remark}

\section{The master family}\label{sec:family}

\begin{definition}\label{def:family}
For integers $p\ge1$ and $k\ge2$ put $n=p+1+k$ and $T=pk$, and let $G(p,k)$
be the lower envelope of
\[
 g=\bigl(p(k-1),\ \underbrace{1,\dots,1}_{p},\ \underbrace{0,\dots,0}_{k}\bigr),
 \qquad
 h=\bigl(\underbrace{0,\dots,0}_{p+1},\ \underbrace{p,\dots,p}_{k}\bigr).
\]
We index the players $0,1,\dots,p+k$ and call player $0$ the \emph{head},
$O:=\{1,\dots,p\}$ the \emph{ones}, and $B:=\{p+1,\dots,p+k\}$; so
$A=\{0\}\cup O$ has $p+1$ elements and $|B|=k$.
\end{definition}

Both generators are nonnegative with total $p(k-1)+p=pk=T$, their supports
are disjoint, and every coordinate vanishes in one of them; by
Lemma~\ref{lem:envelope}, $G(p,k)$ is exact and singleton-normalized.

\begin{theorem}[closed forms]\label{thm:family}
For all $p\ge1$, $k\ge2$,
\begin{align*}
 \Lmin\bigl(G(p,k)\bigr)&=\Bigl(\tfrac{p^2(k-1)}{p+1},\ \bigl[\tfrac{p}{p+1}\bigr]^{p+k}\Bigr),\\
 \Lmax\bigl(G(p,k)\bigr)&=\Bigl(\tfrac{p(k-1)}{k},\ \bigl[\tfrac1k\bigr]^{p},\ \bigl[\tfrac{p(k-1)}{k}\bigr]^{k}\Bigr),\\
 \bigl\|\Lmin-\Lmax\bigr\|_1&=\frac{2p\bigl(p(k-1)-1\bigr)}{p+1},
 \qquad
 \rho\bigl(G(p,k)\bigr)=\frac{2\bigl(p(k-1)-1\bigr)}{k(p+1)},
\end{align*}
where the exponents indicate the number of coordinates, listed in the order
head, ones, $B$.
\end{theorem}

The proof occupies Sections~\ref{sec:keycap}--\ref{sec:gap}: a key cap, the
two optimizations, a membership check, and the arithmetic of the gap.

\subsection{The key cap}\label{sec:keycap}

For each $b\in B$,
\[
 f\bigl(O\cup\{b\}\bigr)=\max\bigl(g(O),h(\{b\})\bigr)=\max(p,p)=p,
\]
so by Theorem~\ref{thm:duality} every core allocation $x$ satisfies
\[
 x_1+\dots+x_p+x_b\ \le\ p\qquad\text{for every }b\in B. \tag{KC}
\]
That single family of $(p+1)$-player caps drives everything below.  Note that
the two generators \emph{tie} at these coalitions: the maximum defining $f$
is attained on both sides.

\subsection{Leximin}

Let $x\in\core(G(p,k))$ and $m:=\min_ix_i$.  Applying (KC) with any fixed $b$
gives $(p+1)m\le p$, so
\[
 m\le\frac{p}{p+1},\qquad\text{hence}\qquad \mu\le\frac{p}{p+1}.
\]
Suppose $m=p/(p+1)$.  Then in (KC) the $p+1$ numbers $x_1,\dots,x_p,x_b$ are
each at least $p/(p+1)$ and sum to at most $p=(p+1)\cdot p/(p+1)$, so all of
them equal $p/(p+1)$.  This holds for \emph{every} $b\in B$, hence
\[
 x_1=\dots=x_p=x_{p+1}=\dots=x_{p+k}=\frac{p}{p+1},
\]
and efficiency forces the head to
\[
 x_0=T-(p+k)\frac{p}{p+1}=pk-\frac{p(p+k)}{p+1}=\frac{p^2(k-1)}{p+1},
\]
using $k-(p+k)/(p+1)=(kp+k-p-k)/(p+1)=p(k-1)/(p+1)$.  Thus the max-min
allocation is \emph{unique}, and a unique max-min allocation is a fortiori
the leximin allocation.  Since $p(k-1)\ge1$, the head's coordinate is at
least $p/(p+1)$, so the floor of this allocation is indeed $p/(p+1)$.  It
remains to check that this point is in the core, which is done in
Section~\ref{sec:membership}.

\subsection{Leximax-min}

Let $x\in\core(G(p,k))$, $M:=\max_ix_i$ and $s:=x_1+\dots+x_p$.  By (KC),
$x_b\le p-s$ for every $b\in B$, and trivially $x_b\le M$ and $x_0\le M$.
Efficiency gives
\[
 pk=x_0+s+\sum_{b\in B}x_b\ \le\ M+s+k\min(M,\,p-s). \tag{4}
\]

\emph{Case A: $M\le p-s$.}  Then (4) reads $pk\le(k+1)M+s$.  The case
hypothesis is $s\le p-M$, so $pk\le(k+1)M+p-M=kM+p$, i.e.
$M\ge p(k-1)/k$.

\emph{Case B: $M>p-s$.}  Then (4) reads $pk\le M+s+k(p-s)$, i.e.
$(k-1)s\le M$, so $s\le M/(k-1)$.  The case hypothesis then gives
$M>p-s\ge p-M/(k-1)$, i.e.\ $Mk/(k-1)>p$, i.e.\ $M>p(k-1)/k$.

In both cases $M\ge p(k-1)/k$, so the leximax-min ceiling is at least
$p(k-1)/k$.  Equality can occur only in Case A with every inequality used
tight, that is with $x_0=M$, with $x_b=M$ for all $b\in B$, and with
\[
 s=pk-(k+1)M=pk-(k+1)\frac{p(k-1)}{k}=\frac{p\bigl(k^2-(k+1)(k-1)\bigr)}{k}=\frac pk .
\]
(Consistency: $p-s=p-p/k=p(k-1)/k=M$, so Case A holds with equality, as it
must.)  Thus at the min-max level the head and all of $B$ are pinned at
$p(k-1)/k$ and the ones share exactly $p/k$; since $p/k\le p(k-1)/k$ for
$k\ge2$, the ones occupy the last $p$ positions of the decreasing
rearrangement.  The next leximax stage minimizes their maximum subject to
their sum being $p/k$, whose unique optimum is $1/k$ each.  Hence $\Lmax$ is
as claimed, again modulo membership.

\subsection{Membership}\label{sec:membership}

This step is load-bearing: the two optimizations above determine the optima
only conditionally on the described allocations being feasible.

Index as in Definition~\ref{def:family} and, for $S\subseteq N$, put
\[
 \varepsilon:=[\,0\in S\,],\qquad i:=|S\cap O|,\qquad j:=|S\cap B|,
\]
so that $0\le i\le p$, $0\le j\le k$ and
\[
 g(S)=\varepsilon\,p(k-1)+i,\qquad h(S)=jp,\qquad
 f(S)=\max\bigl(\varepsilon\,p(k-1)+i,\ jp\bigr). \tag{5}
\]

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:membership}
Both allocations of Theorem~\ref{thm:family} lie in $\core(G(p,k))$ for all
$p\ge1$, $k\ge2$.  Moreover, for both of them the tight coalitions are
exactly
\[
\begin{array}{ll}
 \text{(T1)}\quad S=O\cup\{b\},\ b\in B, & f(S)=\max(p,p)=p,\\[2pt]
 \text{(T2)}\quad S=\{0\}\cup B',\ |B'|=k-1, & f(S)=\max\bigl(p(k-1),(k-1)p\bigr)=p(k-1),\\[2pt]
 \text{(T3)}\quad S=N, & f(N)=T.
\end{array}
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
\emph{The leximin allocation.}  Write $q:=p/(p+1)$ and
$H:=p^2(k-1)/(p+1)=p(k-1)q$, so $x^*=(H,q,\dots,q)$ with $p+k$ copies of $q$.
Efficiency:
\[
 H+(p+k)q=q\bigl(p(k-1)+p+k\bigr)=q(pk+k)=k(p+1)\frac{p}{p+1}=pk=T,
\]
and $x^*>0$.  It remains to check $x^*(S)=\varepsilon H+(i+j)q\le f(S)$.

$\varepsilon=0$, $j=0$: $S$ is a subset of $O$, and $x^*(S)=iq<i=f(S)$ for
$i\ge1$ because $q<1$; for $i=0$, $S=\emptyset$.  Strict.

$\varepsilon=0$, $j\ge1$: bound $f(S)\ge jp$ and use $i\le p$:
\[
 x^*(S)=(i+j)\frac{p}{p+1}\le(p+j)\frac{p}{p+1}\le jp
 \iff p+j\le j(p+1)\iff p\le jp,
\]
which holds since $j\ge1$.  Equality throughout requires $i=p$ and $j=1$,
i.e.\ $S=O\cup\{b\}$, which is (T1).

$\varepsilon=1$, $j\le k-1$: put $G_0:=p(k-1)+i=g(S)$, so $x^*(S)=q(G_0+j)$
and
\[
 x^*(S)\le G_0\iff p(G_0+j)\le(p+1)G_0\iff jp\le G_0=p(k-1)+i,
\]
which holds because $jp\le(k-1)p$.  Hence $x^*(S)\le g(S)\le f(S)$, with
equality exactly when $j=k-1$ and $i=0$, i.e.\ $S=\{0\}\cup B'$ with
$|B'|=k-1$, which is (T2).

$\varepsilon=1$, $j=k$: then $h(S)=kp=T$, so $f(S)\ge T\ge x^*(S)$, with
equality only for $S=N$ since $x^*>0$; this is (T3).

\emph{The leximax-min allocation.}  Write $L:=p(k-1)/k$ and $r:=1/k$, so
$y^*=(L,r,\dots,r,L,\dots,L)$ with $p$ copies of $r$ and $k$ copies of $L$.
Efficiency:
\[
 L+pr+kL=(k+1)L+\frac pk=\frac{p\bigl[(k+1)(k-1)+1\bigr]}{k}=\frac{pk^2}{k}=pk=T,
\]
and $y^*>0$.  Now $y^*(S)=\varepsilon L+ir+jL$.

$\varepsilon=0$, $j=0$: $y^*(S)=i/k\le i=f(S)$ since $k\ge2$, strictly for
$i\ge1$.

$\varepsilon=0$, $j\ge1$: comparing with the $h$-side,
\[
 \frac{i+jp(k-1)}{k}\le jp\iff i+jp(k-1)\le jpk\iff i\le jp,
\]
which holds since $i\le p\le jp$, with equality exactly when $i=p$ and $j=1$,
i.e.\ (T1).

$\varepsilon=1$, $j\le k-1$: $y^*(S)=(j+1)L+i/k$, and multiplying by $k>0$,
\[
 y^*(S)\le p(k-1)+i
 \iff (j+1)p(k-1)+i\le kp(k-1)+ki
 \iff p(j+1-k)\le i,
\]
after dividing by $k-1\ge1$; the left-hand side is at most $0\le i$, with
equality exactly when $j=k-1$ and $i=0$, i.e.\ (T2).

$\varepsilon=1$, $j=k$: as before $f(S)\ge h(S)=kp=T\ge y^*(S)$, with
equality only at $S=N$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The hinge: both selections saturate the same caps}

Lemma~\ref{lem:membership} contains the expository point of the paper.  The
active constraints are the same for $\Lmin$ and $\Lmax$: the two families
(T1) and (T2), plus efficiency.  Both are coalitions at which the two
generators \emph{tie}, which is the structural reason they are the active
ones --- there is no slack on either side to give.  What differs is only how
the two selections distribute mass \emph{inside} a tight coalition.  On
$S=O\cup\{b\}$, which has total exactly $p$:
\[
\begin{array}{lcc}
 & \text{each of the }p\text{ ones} & \text{the single }b\in B\\[2pt]
 \Lmin: & \dfrac{p}{p+1} & \dfrac{p}{p+1}\\[10pt]
 \Lmax: & \dfrac1k & \dfrac{p(k-1)}{k}
\end{array}
\]
Leximin splits the budget $p$ evenly over all $p+1$ members; leximax-min
gives the ones the least it can and puts the whole remainder on the one
member of $B$, because loading $B$ is how it lowers the global maximum.  The
divergence is visible in this one coalition.  Everything else is bookkeeping:
leximin's even split leaves the residual $T-(p+k)p/(p+1)$ with nowhere to go
but the head, while leximax-min's loading of every $b\in B$ up to
$p(k-1)/k$ consumes almost all of $T$ across $B$.

\subsection{The gap}\label{sec:gap}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:family}, conclusion]
Put $D:=p(k-1)-1\ge0$.  Coordinatewise,
\begin{align*}
 \text{head:}&& \frac{p^2(k-1)}{p+1}-\frac{p(k-1)}{k}&=\frac{p(k-1)D}{k(p+1)},\\
 \text{each of the $p$ ones:}&& \frac{p}{p+1}-\frac1k&=\frac{D}{k(p+1)},\\
 \text{each of the $k$ members of $B$:}&& \frac{p(k-1)}{k}-\frac{p}{p+1}&=\frac{pD}{k(p+1)},
\end{align*}
all three nonnegative.  Hence
\[
 \|\Lmin-\Lmax\|_1=\frac{p(k-1)D+pD+kpD}{k(p+1)}
 =\frac{D\bigl(p(k-1)+p+kp\bigr)}{k(p+1)}
 =\frac{2pkD}{k(p+1)}=\frac{2pD}{p+1},
\]
and $\rho=2pD/\bigl((p+1)pk\bigr)=2D/\bigl(k(p+1)\bigr)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[deficit identity]\label{cor:deficit}
$\displaystyle 2-\rho\bigl(G(p,k)\bigr)=\frac{2n}{k(p+1)}$, where $n=p+1+k$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
$2-\dfrac{2\bigl(p(k-1)-1\bigr)}{k(p+1)}
 =\dfrac{2\bigl[k(p+1)-p(k-1)+1\bigr]}{k(p+1)}
 =\dfrac{2(kp+k-pk+p+1)}{k(p+1)}=\dfrac{2(k+p+1)}{k(p+1)}$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[the deficit is twice the overlap]\label{rem:overlap}
Corollary~\ref{cor:deficit} also follows from Lemma~\ref{lem:ceiling}.  In
$G(p,k)$ the coordinatewise minima of the two allocations are $p(k-1)/k$ at
the head, $1/k$ at each one, and $p/(p+1)$ at each member of $B$, so
\[
 \sum_i\min\bigl(\Lmin_i,\Lmax_i\bigr)
 =\frac{p(k-1)}{k}+\frac pk+\frac{kp}{p+1}=p+\frac{kp}{p+1}<p+k=n-1,
\]
and $2\cdot\bigl(p+kp/(p+1)\bigr)/(pk)=2(p+1+k)/\bigl(k(p+1)\bigr)$.  The
overlap between the two selections is thus bounded by $n$ while $T=pk$ grows
quadratically: this is the precise sense in which their supports become
asymptotically disjoint.
\end{remark}

\subsection{The supremum}

\begin{theorem}[value of the supremum]\label{thm:sup}
For every $n\ge4$,
\[
 s(n)\ \ge\ 2-\frac{8n}{n^2-(n\bmod2)}
 =\begin{cases}
   2-\dfrac8n,& n\text{ even},\\[6pt]
   2-\dfrac{8n}{n^2-1},& n\text{ odd},
  \end{cases}
\]
attained on the two-generator class by $G(p,k)$ with the balanced split
$k=\lceil n/2\rceil$, $p+1=\lfloor n/2\rfloor$.  Consequently
$\sup_n s(n)=2$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By Corollary~\ref{cor:deficit} the deficit of $G(p,k)$ is $2n/(k(p+1))$ with
$k+(p+1)=n$, $k\ge2$ and $p+1\ge2$.  Minimizing the deficit means maximizing
the product $k(p+1)$ of two positive integers with fixed sum $n$, whose
maximum is $\lfloor n/2\rfloor\lceil n/2\rceil$, equal to $n^2/4$ for even
$n$ and $(n^2-1)/4$ for odd $n$, attained at the balanced split (for $n\ge4$
this split satisfies the constraints $k\ge2$, $p\ge1$).  Substituting gives
the displayed bound.  For the last claim, $\rho\le2$ always by
Lemma~\ref{lem:ceiling}, and $2-8/n\to2$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[the degenerate corner]\label{rem:degenerate}
$G(1,2)$ has $n=4$, $T=2$, $g=(1,1,0,0)$, $h=(0,0,1,1)$ and
$\rho=2(1\cdot1-1)/(2\cdot2)=0$, consistent with the four-player rigidity;
the deficit identity reads $2-0=2n/(k(p+1))=8/4=2$.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[there is nothing special about $|B|=4$]\label{rem:notfour}
An earlier version of this family fixed $k=|B|=4$, giving
$\rho=(3p-1)/\bigl(2(p+1)\bigr)\to3/2$.  Corollary~\ref{cor:deficit} shows
that $k$ is a free parameter and that the optimum is $k\approx n/2$, so the
limit $3/2$ was an artifact of the fixed block size; the $k=4$ slice is
already beaten from $n=10$ on ($7/6$ against $6/5$).  The number $4$ that
appears as $|\supp(\Lmax)|$ in the $n=5$ extremal class
(Section~\ref{sec:fixedn}) is a small-$n$ phenomenon and not a feature of the
asymptotics: in $G(p,k)$ every coordinate of $\Lmax$ is strictly positive, so
$|\supp(\Lmax)|=n$ and no player is starved at all.
\end{remark}

\section{Fixed \texorpdfstring{$n$}{n}: enumeration, certificates, and the
\texorpdfstring{$n=5$}{n=5} anomaly}
\label{sec:fixedn}

\subsection{Complete two-generator enumeration}

Write $s_2(n)$ for the supremum of $\rho$ over singleton-normalized
two-generator exact games on $n$ players.  Table~\ref{tab:enum} reports
complete enumerations, up to player permutation, generator swap and scaling,
of all integer generator pairs with common total in the stated range.  All
arithmetic is exact rational.

Two readings must be kept apart.

\paragraph{What is complete.}
Because $\rho$ is scale-invariant, every two-generator game with rational
generators has a unique primitive integer representative, and enumeration to
total $T_{\max}$ is exactly complete for the games whose primitive
representative has total at most $T_{\max}$ --- equivalently, for rational
shapes of denominator at most $T_{\max}$.  It says nothing about finer
denominators or about irrational generators, so enumeration-to-$T$ is a
lower-bound method for $s_2(n)$: the entries of Table~\ref{tab:enum} are
maxima over the enumerated range, not values of $s_2$.  As a partial probe of
the excluded region, shape hill-climbing at fine resolution was run from the
known witnesses (resolution $1/60$ at $n=5$; $1/56$ and $1/60$ at $n=6$;
$1/64$ at $n=7$).  None improved on the tabulated maximum; at $n=7$ the climb
from the $5/6$ witness stalled after $42$ steps, and a second start from a
base of $2/3$ climbed only to $3/4$.

\paragraph{What agrees.}
The enumerated maxima agree exactly with Theorem~\ref{thm:sup} at $n=6,7,8$,
so on the two-generator class that bound is attained there, and
\[
 s_2(n)\ \stackrel{?}{=}\ 2-\frac{8n}{n^2-(n\bmod2)}\qquad(n\ge6)
\]
is a live candidate, currently confirmed at three values of $n$.  At $n=5$
the two disagree, and the enumeration is the correct one; see
Section~\ref{sec:n5}.

\subsection{Searching the full exact class}

The two-generator restriction is not innocuous.  Adding a generator
\emph{lowers} the envelope and hence \emph{grows} the core, so it is not
automatic that two generators are extremal.  The following observation lets
one search the entire exact class rather than a bounded-generator slice.

\begin{lemma}[core closing]\label{lem:closing}
Fix $T>0$ and a vector $c=(c_S)$ indexed by the proper coalitions, and put
$P(c)=\{x: x(N)=T,\ x(S)\ge c_S\ \text{for all proper }S\}$.  If
$P(c)\ne\emptyset$, then the game $v$ defined by $v(S)=\min_{x\in P(c)}x(S)$
for proper $S$ and $v(N)=T$ is exact, and $\core(v)=P(c)$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Every $x\in P(c)$ satisfies $x(S)\ge v(S)$ by definition of $v$ and
$x(N)=T$, so $P(c)\subseteq\core(v)$; and each minimum is attained, so $v$ is
exact.  Conversely, $v(S)\ge c_S$ for every proper $S$ because every point of
$P(c)$ satisfies $x(S)\ge c_S$; hence $x\in\core(v)$ implies $x(S)\ge
v(S)\ge c_S$ and $x(N)=T$, i.e.\ $x\in P(c)$.
\end{proof}

Perturbing $c$ and re-closing therefore moves within the exact class and
reaches cores with arbitrarily many vertices.  Hill climbing in this
``$c$-space'', \emph{seeded at the extremal two-generator game} of
Table~\ref{tab:enum}, produced no improvement at $n=5$ ($240$ steps, stays at
$1/2$), $n=6$ ($496$ steps, stays at $2/3$) or $n=7$ ($301$ steps, stays at
$5/6$).  Since the search is over all exact games, this certifies the
two-generator optimum as a \emph{local} optimum in the full exact class at
those three values of $n$.  Independently, from the known extremal at each of
$n=5,6,7,8$, about $200$ structured and random third generators at fixed
common total were added: zero improvements.

One limitation must be stated.  Unseeded $c$-space restarts reach only $1/3$
at $n=6$, far below the known $2/3$; that variant of the search is
under-powered for $n\ge6$ and certifies nothing.  Only the seeded runs
support the local-optimality claim, and none of these runs bears on global
optimality.

\subsection{The \texorpdfstring{$n=5$}{n=5} anomaly}\label{sec:n5}

At $n=5$ the balanced-split family gives only $1/3$, but the truth is $1/2$,
and the witness has a non-uniform $h$-block.  The proof is by hand from two
flat splits of the game; no linear program and no vertex enumeration is
used.

\begin{theorem}[$n=5$]\label{thm:n5}
Let $w$ be the lower envelope of $g^1=(1,0,0,1,2)$ and $g^2=(0,1,3,0,0)$.
Then $w$ is exact and singleton-normalized with $w(N)=4$, and
\[
 \Lmin(w)=\Bigl(\tfrac12,\tfrac12,\tfrac54,\tfrac12,\tfrac54\Bigr),\qquad
 \Lmax(w)=(1,0,1,1,1),\qquad
 \|\Lmin-\Lmax\|_1=2,\qquad \rho(w)=\tfrac12 .
\]
In particular $s(5)\ge1/2>1/3$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Exactness and normalization are immediate from Lemma~\ref{lem:envelope}, each
coordinate vanishing in one generator.  Two flat splits control the core:
$w(\{1,2\})=1$ and $w(\{3,4,5\})=3$ sum to $w(N)=4$, so $x_1+x_2=1$ on
all of $\core(w)$, and likewise $w(\{2,4\})=1$ with $w(\{1,3,5\})=3$
gives $x_2+x_4=1$.  Putting $s:=x_1$, every core point therefore satisfies
\[
 x_1=x_4=s,\qquad x_2=1-s,\qquad x_3+x_5=3-s, \tag{6}
\]
with $s\in[0,1]$ by nonnegativity of $x_1$ and $x_2$.

\emph{Leximin.}  By (6), $\min_ix_i\le\min(s,1-s)\le1/2$, with equality
forcing $s=1/2$ and hence $x_1=x_2=x_4=1/2$.  Then $x_3+x_5=5/2$, and the
leximin continuation splits this evenly, giving $5/4$ each.  The resulting
allocation $x=(1/2,1/2,5/4,1/2,5/4)$ lies in the core, as direct
substitution against both generators confirms.  Hence $\Lmin(w)$ is as
claimed.

\emph{Leximax-min.}  By (6), $\max_ix_i\ge(x_3+x_5)/2=(3-s)/2\ge1$ since
$s\le1$.  Equality forces $s=1$, so $x=(1,0,x_3,1,x_5)$ with $x_3+x_5=2$; the ceiling
$\max_ix_i=1$ then forces $x_3=x_5=1$.  The unique candidate $(1,0,1,1,1)$
lies in the core, so $\Lmax(w)=(1,0,1,1,1)$.

Finally $\|\Lmin-\Lmax\|_1=\tfrac12+\tfrac12+\tfrac14+\tfrac12+\tfrac14=2$
and $\rho=2/4=1/2$.
\end{proof}

Four independent methods agree on this game: the hand argument above, exact
rational core-vertex enumeration, a staged exact solver over linear real
arithmetic, and a from-scratch exact-rational two-phase simplex with Bland's
rule.

The value $\tfrac12$ is in fact the supremum: $s(5)=\tfrac12$
(Theorem~\ref{thm:s5} below, by a configuration-complete exact finite
check).  Before that machinery, the searches already pointed there: roughly
$4\times10^4$ distinct exact five-player games evaluated in
exact rational arithmetic produced nothing above $1/2$ --- the complete
two-generator enumeration of Table~\ref{tab:enum}, complete three-generator
enumerations at totals $4$--$7$, $c$-space hill climbing in the sense of
Lemma~\ref{lem:closing}, and fine-denominator shape climbing from the
extremal witness --- with the $n=3,4$ controls returning maximum $0$ from
the same pipeline.

\paragraph{Structure of the extremal class.}
The following are computational findings over $360$ distinct profiled games
at $n=5$, of which $199$ have $\rho>1/3$ and $107$ attain $\rho=1/2$.  Among
the $107$ extremal games, without exception:
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
\item $\Lmin$ has stratum composition $(3,2)$, and $\Lmax$ has stratum
      composition $(1,4)$;
\item $\Lmax$ has exactly one zero coordinate, so $|\supp(\Lmax)|=4$;
\item normalized to $T=4$ the pair is unique up to permutation:
      $\Lmin\sim(\tfrac12,\tfrac12,\tfrac12,\tfrac54,\tfrac54)$ and
      $\Lmax\sim(0,1,1,1,1)$ --- that is, exactly the pair of
      Theorem~\ref{thm:n5}.
\end{itemize}
Among the $199$ games above $1/3$, the $\Lmin$ strata are $(3,2)$ in $196$
cases and $(2,1,2)$ in $3$; the pattern $(4,1)$ never occurs above $1/3$, and
$\Lmax$ is never the uniform allocation.

One tempting invariant is refuted.  Call $S$ a \emph{flat split} if
$v(S)+v(N\setminus S)=v(N)$; then $x(S)$ is constant on the core, which is
the affine seesaw visible in (6) --- in the game of Theorem~\ref{thm:n5},
$w(\{1,2\})+w(\{3,4,5\})=1+3=4$.  Only $76$ of the $107$ extremal games have
any flat split, and $31$ attain exactly $1/2$ with none, so the seesaw is a
feature of the particular presentation in Theorem~\ref{thm:n5} and not an
invariant of extremality.  When a flat split does exist its sizes are always
$\{2,3\}$, and the $\Lmax$-starved player lies on the size-$2$ side in all
$76$ cases.

\paragraph{Two regimes.}
Theorem~\ref{thm:sup} gives only $1/3$ at $n=5$ because the family $G(p,k)$
has a uniform $h$-block and the available splits $(k,p+1)\in\{(2,3),(3,2)\}$
are too coarse; the extremal five-player game has a non-uniform $h$-block.
The crossover to the $G$-family shape happens at $n=6$.  The two regimes
differ qualitatively and not just numerically: at $n=5$ the extremal $\Lmax$
starves one player to $0$ and has support of size $4$, whereas in $G(p,k)$
the extremal $\Lmax$ is strictly positive everywhere.  Explaining the
crossover is open (Section~\ref{sec:scope}).


\subsection{One-coalition certificates and the regime change}
\label{sec:n5closed}

Write, for any singleton-normalized exact game with the general dual bound
$f(S)=T-v(N\setminus S)$,
\[
 \lambda:=\min_{\emptyset\ne S}\frac{f(S)}{|S|},\qquad
 \nu:=\max_{\emptyset\ne S}\frac{v(S)}{|S|},\qquad
 \mu:=\min_i\Lmin(v)_i,\qquad M:=\max_i\Lmax(v)_i .
\]
The general duality gives the bracketing $\mu\le\lambda$ and $M\ge\nu$
at once (for $\mu\le\lambda$ the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:floor} applies
verbatim).  On a two-generator game the right-hand side of (3) is exactly
$\nu$, since $v(P\cup Q)=\min(g(P),h(Q))$.  Observation~\ref{obs:Mside} says the second bracket can be
strict from $n=6$ on.  Two facts sharpen this into a statement about the
regime change at five players.

\begin{proposition}[covering obstruction]\label{prop:cover}
Let $\tau_\nu:=\{S:v(S)=\nu|S|\}$ be the $\nu$-tight family.  If $\tau_\nu$
covers $N$ and $\nu>T/n$, then no core point satisfies $x\le\nu\one$, and
hence $M>\nu$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Sum the core inequalities $x(S)\ge v(S)=\nu|S|$ over a cover with weights
$w\ge0$: with $c_i:=\sum_{S\ni i}w_S\ge1$ this gives
$\sum_ic_i(x_i-\nu)\ge0$.  If $x\le\nu\one$, every term is nonpositive, so
$x_i=\nu$ for every $i$; then $T=\nu n$, contradicting $\nu>T/n$.
\end{proof}

Moreover no \emph{balanced} collection can witness $M>\nu$: for balanced $w$,
$\sum_Sw_Sv(S)\le T$ because the core is nonempty, and the same computation
then yields $\sum_Sw_S\bigl(\nu|S|-v(S)\bigr)\ge\nu n-T$, which is what
equality requires.  So a failure of $M=\nu$ needs a strictly over-covering
fractional cover: no balanced collection can witness one.  A covering
$\nu$-tight family is one way this arises, and it is what occurs at the
smallest counterexample of Observation~\ref{obs:Mside}: the four $\nu$-tight sets $\{2,3\},\{1,3,4\},\{1,3,5\},
\{1,3,6\}$ cover $N$ with per-player excess $(2,0,3,0,0,0)$, and player $2$,
lying only in $\{2,3\}$, blocks every balanced subfamily.  At the
five-player extremal game of Theorem~\ref{thm:n5}, by contrast, $\nu=1$
and the $\nu$-tight family $\{3,5\},\{1,3,5\},\{3,4,5\}$ misses
player~$2$, so Proposition~\ref{prop:cover} does not apply, and the dual
minimum $h(N)=1$ is attained at the balanced partition
$\{2,4\}\mid\{1,3,5\}$.  The covering test is sufficient for $M>\nu$
but not necessary: at six players, games with $M>\nu$ outnumber those with
a covering $\nu$-tight family.

\begin{theorem}[one-coalition certificates at five players]
\label{thm:n5closed}
For every singleton-normalized exact game on five players, $\mu=\lambda$ and
$M=\nu$: both egalitarian levels are certified by a single coalition.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof, by finite exact check]
By the duality above, $M=\nu$ fails if and only if some fractional cover $w$
has $\sum_Sw_Sh(S)<h(N)$, where $h(S)=\nu|S|-v(S)\ge0$; likewise for
$\mu$ with $f'(S)=f(S)-\lambda|S|$.  Both objectives are nonnegative on the
cover polyhedron, which is pointed with recession cone the nonnegative
orthant, so each minimum is attained at a vertex; on five players there are
$2287$ vertices, each of support at most five.  Fixing a vertex and a
coalition attaining $\nu$ (resp.\ $\lambda$) makes every hypothesis, and
the failure inequality, linear in $v$, and $v(N)=1$ is free by homogeneity.
Exactness is imposed through the $280$ inequalities that the facet theorem of
\citet{StudenyKratochvil2022} assigns to the sixteen indecomposable
min-semi-balanced systems on five players under all permutations; this
description was validated in both directions against independent exactness
tests.  Each of the $1142$ symmetry classes of (vertex, anchor) pairs, on
each side, is infeasible in exact rational arithmetic.
\end{proof}

Two remarks calibrate the proof.  First, deleting the $280$ exact-cone
inequalities makes $361$ (resp.\ $339$) of the over-covering classes
feasible, so it is exactness --- not the shape of the certificate --- that
forces the one-coalition property at five players.  Second, the $M$-side is
false from six players on (Observation~\ref{obs:Mside}), and the same
reduction detects that failure.  The theorem is therefore the formal content
of the regime change of Section~\ref{sec:n5}: at five players both
selections are governed by single bottleneck coalitions, while from six
players the ceiling requires genuinely fractional covers --- which is
precisely where $s(n)$ begins its climb toward~$2$.  One consequence localizes all the difficulty of $s(5)$.

\begin{corollary}[floor from ceiling]\label{cor:floorceiling}
For every singleton-normalized exact five-player game,
$\mu\ge\min_i\Lmax(v)_i$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
$\Lmax$ is a core point, so $f(S)=T-v(N\setminus S)\ge
T-\Lmax(N\setminus S)=\Lmax(S)$ for every $S$; hence
$\lambda\ge\min_S\Lmax(S)/|S|\ge\min_i\Lmax_i$, and $\mu=\lambda$ by
Theorem~\ref{thm:n5closed}.
\end{proof}

If $\Lmax$ has no zero coordinate, the leximin floor is therefore bounded
below for free and elementary gap arithmetic bounds the ratio; every
genuinely hard configuration for $s(5)$ has a \emph{zero} in $\Lmax$.  The
theorem does \emph{not} by itself deliver the starvation bound needed for
those configurations; that bound is a statement about a different functional
and requires its own argument, which the next theorem supplies in the
extremal one.

\begin{theorem}[the extremal configuration is pinned]\label{thm:cfg}
Let $v$ be a singleton-normalized exact five-player game whose $\Lmin$
strata form the pattern $(3,2)$ and whose $\Lmax$ strata form the pattern
$(1,4)$ with the singleton stratum at zero, at player $j$.  Then
\[
 f(\{j\})=T-v(N\setminus\{j\})\ \ge\ \tfrac{T}8 .
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Normalize $T=1$ and suppose $f(\{j\})<\tfrac18$.  The starved player $j$ is
$\Lmin$-poor by Corollary~\ref{cor:poorstarved} below (whose two-line proof
uses only Lemma~\ref{lem:anchors} and is independent of this theorem);
write $P=\{j,p_1,p_2\}$ for the poor stratum and $R=\{r_1,r_2\}$ for the
rich pair, with $x=\Lmin$ equal to $\mu$ on $P$ and
$\varrho=(1-3\mu)/2$ on $R$, and $z=\Lmax$ equal to $0$ at $j$ and
$\tfrac14$ elsewhere.  Call $S$ \emph{$f$-tight} when $x(S)=f(S)$,
equivalently when $N\setminus S$ is $v$-tight at $x$.

\emph{Step 0.}  $\lambda\le f(\{j\})<\tfrac18$, and $\mu\le\lambda$ holds
for every game (the easy half of Theorem~\ref{thm:n5closed}, i.e.\
Theorem~\ref{thm:floor}'s argument), so $\mu<\tfrac18$; hence
$\varrho>\tfrac5{16}>\tfrac14$ and $c:=1-5\mu>0$.

\emph{Step 1 (key lemma).}  Every $f$-tight $S$ satisfies
$|S\cap\{p_1,p_2\}|\le|S\cap R|$.  Indeed, $U=N\setminus S$ is $v$-tight,
and $z\in\core(v)$ gives, with $a=|U\cap P|$, $b=|U\cap R|$,
$\varepsilon=[\,j\in U\,]$,
\[
 \mu a+\varrho b\ =\ x(U)=v(U)\ \le\ z(U)\ =\ \tfrac{a+b-\varepsilon}4 .
\]
The claim is $b\le a-\varepsilon$.  A violation leaves four shapes, each
contradictory: $a-\varepsilon=0$ with $\varepsilon=0$ gives
$\varrho\le\tfrac14$; with $\varepsilon=1$ it gives
$\mu\le b(\tfrac14-\varrho)<0$; $a-\varepsilon=1$ with $\varepsilon=1$
(so $b=2$) gives $1-\mu\le\tfrac34$, i.e.\ $\mu\ge\tfrac14$; and
$a-\varepsilon=1$ with $\varepsilon=0$, $b=2$ --- the coalition
$U=\{p_i\}\cup R$ --- gives $1-2\mu\le\tfrac34$, i.e.\ $\mu\ge\tfrac18$,
against the failure hypothesis.  This last case is the only one that
mentions $\tfrac18$.

\emph{Step 2 (the pinning, dualized).}  That $P$ is the $\Lmin$ bottom
stratum means every core point $y\ge\mu\one$ has $y(P)=3\mu$.
Substituting $u=y-\mu\one$, this is
$\max\{u(P):u\ge0,\ u(N)=c,\ u(S)\le h(S)\}=0$ with
$h(S):=f(S)-\mu|S|\ge0$.  LP duality supplies $\alpha$ and $\beta\ge0$
with $\alpha+B_k\ge[\,k\in P\,]$ for every player $k$, where
$B_k=\sum_{S\ni k}\beta_S$, and $\alpha c+\sum_S\beta_Sh(S)\le0$.  Since
$c>0$ and $h\ge0$, necessarily $\alpha\le0$; put $A=-\alpha\ge0$, so
$B_k\ge1+A$ on $P$ and $B_k\ge A$ on $R$.

\emph{Step 3 (exact tightness).}  $x\in\core(v)$ gives
$h(S)\ge x(S)-\mu|S|=(\varrho-\mu)\,|S\cap R|$, so
\[
 \sum_S\beta_Sh(S)\ \ge\ (\varrho-\mu)(B_{r_1}+B_{r_2})\ \ge\
 2A(\varrho-\mu)\ =\ cA,
\]
using $2(\varrho-\mu)=1-5\mu=c$ \emph{exactly}.  Step 2 bounds the same
sum by $cA$, so both inequalities are equalities: $B_{r_1}+B_{r_2}=2A$,
and every support set of $\beta$ is $f$-tight.

\emph{Step 4 (counting).}  The key lemma now applies to every support
set, so
$B_{p_1}+B_{p_2}=\sum_S\beta_S|S\cap\{p_1,p_2\}|
\le\sum_S\beta_S|S\cap R|=2A$, while Step 2 demands
$B_{p_1}+B_{p_2}\ge2+2A$.  Contradiction.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[sharpness, and the archived check]\label{rem:cfghand}
The constant is not inserted; it falls out.  Exactly one line of the proof
mentions $\tfrac18$ --- the coalition shape $U=\{p_i\}\cup R$ --- and at
$\mu=T/8$ that inequality becomes an equality and the key lemma fails at
$S=\{j,p_{i'}\}$.  This is realized: in the extremal game of
Theorem~\ref{thm:n5} (which lies in the configuration with $\mu=T/8$) the
$f$-tight sets $\{1,2\}$ and $\{2,4\}$ are precisely of that shape.  The
proof also consumes strictly less than the original finite check did:
neither the hard direction of Theorem~\ref{thm:n5closed} nor the facet
description appears --- only core membership of the two selections, the
floor bracketing $\mu\le\lambda$, Corollary~\ref{cor:poorstarved}, and the
pinning.  The original check ($10{,}974$ exact programs over the $354$
pinning-dual vertices) remains archived as an independent confirmation.
\end{remark}

\begin{corollary}[the pinned solution pair]\label{cor:cfg}
In the configuration of Theorem~\ref{thm:cfg}, suppose in addition that the
starved player is $\Lmin$-poor and that some coalition attaining $\nu$ has
three players.  Then, up to relabelling,
$\Lmin=\bigl(\tfrac T8,\tfrac T8,\tfrac T8,\tfrac{5T}{16},
\tfrac{5T}{16}\bigr)$,
$\Lmax=\bigl(0,\tfrac T4,\tfrac T4,\tfrac T4,\tfrac T4\bigr)$, and
$\rho(v)=\tfrac12$ exactly.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Normalize $T=1$.  The $\Lmax$ pattern gives $M=1/4$ directly.  Core
membership of the $\Lmax$ vector gives, for $S\ne\{j\}$,
$f(S)\ge\bigl(|S|-[\,j\in S\,]\bigr)/4\ge|S|/8$, and
Theorem~\ref{thm:cfg} gives $f(\{j\})\ge1/8$, so $\lambda\ge1/8$.  By
the $M$-side of Theorem~\ref{thm:n5closed}, $\nu=M=1/4$, so a
three-player coalition $S_0$ attaining $\nu$ has $v(S_0)=3/4$ and
$f(N\setminus S_0)=1/4$ on two players, so $\lambda\le1/8$; hence
$\lambda=1/8$, and Theorem~\ref{thm:n5closed} gives $\mu=1/8$.  With the
starved player $\Lmin$-poor, $\mu=1/8\le1/6$ gives
$(1-3\mu)/2\ge1/4=M$, so the coordinatewise gap telescopes to
$\|\Lmin-\Lmax\|_1=T-4\mu=T/2$, and the two displayed allocations are
the unique ones consistent with the strata, the floor and the totals.
\end{proof}

The first additional hypothesis is automatic:
Corollary~\ref{cor:poorstarved} below proves, in two lines, that the
starved player is always $\Lmin$-poor.  The second --- a three-player
coalition attaining $\nu$ --- holds in every configuration game in the
combined harvests ($53$ of $53$) but remains open in general, and is
recorded in Section~\ref{sec:scope}.

For the ratio \emph{bound}, as opposed to the pinned pair, both hypotheses
can in fact be dispensed with.

\begin{theorem}[unconditional configuration bound]\label{thm:cfgbound}
In the configuration of Theorem~\ref{thm:cfg}, $\rho(v)\le\tfrac12$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof, by gap arithmetic]
Normalize $T=1$; the $\Lmax$ pattern gives $M=1/4$ and the $(3,2)$ pattern
gives $\Lmin=(\mu,\mu,\mu,\varrho,\varrho)$ with
$\varrho=(1-3\mu)/2$.  The starved player is $\Lmin$-poor by
Corollary~\ref{cor:poorstarved} below (a two-line consequence of
Lemma~\ref{lem:anchors}, independent of this theorem), so the
coordinatewise gap is $1-4\mu$ for $\mu\le1/6$ and $2\mu$ otherwise, and
Theorem~\ref{thm:cfg} gives $\mu\ge1/8$, so both branches are at most
$\tfrac12$.  (Historically the $\Lmin$-rich branch, whose gap $3/2-6\mu$
would exceed $\tfrac12$ for $\mu<1/6$, was first closed by a finite check
of the same design as Theorem~\ref{thm:cfg} --- the rich relabelling of
the pinning polyhedron, $31\times354$ exact programs, all infeasible,
with the drop-the-pinning control restoring feasibility.  The corollary
supersedes that check by showing the branch is vacuous; the check remains
archived as an independent confirmation.)
\end{proof}

Two calibrations.  First, the floor bound is not removable in favour of
soft arguments: from Theorem~\ref{thm:n5closed} and core membership of both
allocation vectors alone --- everything that is game-free --- the best
obtainable bound on $\rho$ at five players is exactly $\tfrac32$, so every
configuration needs a lower bound on the floor of the strength of
Theorem~\ref{thm:cfg}.  Second, the attempted refutation of the
$\nu$-triple hypothesis produced, once again, a certificate whose audited
model is a genuine exact game \emph{outside} the configuration (a
$(1,4)$-shaped $\Lmax$ with minimum $1/30$, ratio $5/12$) --- the third
certificate-not-realizable instance of this program, and the reason
hypotheses are retired here only by finite checks, never by feasible
certificates alone.

The configuration hypothesis is essential, not decorative: dropping the
$(3,2)$ pinning makes the same programs satisfiable, and their models are
genuine exact five-player games with $T-v(N\setminus\{i\})=T/16$ for some
player $i$ --- half the bound --- but with $\Lmin=\Lmax$, hence no
separation at all.  The cases outside Corollary~\ref{cor:cfg} --- the
$\Lmax$ stratum shapes $(1,2,2)$ and $(1,1,3)$, the $(4,1)$ $\Lmin$
boundary, and the $\Lmin$-rich starved player --- are closed in the next
subsection, which settles the supremum in full.

\subsection{The five-player supremum in full}\label{sec:s5full}

Three localization lemmas come first; all are consequences of
Theorem~\ref{thm:n5closed} together with core membership of the two
allocation vectors, and none needs a computation.

\begin{lemma}[anchor localization]\label{lem:anchors}
Let $v$ be a singleton-normalized exact five-player game, $S_0$ a coalition
attaining $\nu$ and $S_*$ a coalition attaining $\lambda$ (both exist by
Theorem~\ref{thm:n5closed}).  Then
\begin{enumerate}[nosep,label=(\alph*)]
\item $|S_0|\ge2$, and $\Lmax_i=M$ for every $i\in S_0$; consequently the
      $\Lmax$ top stratum contains at least two players;
\item $\Lmin_i=\mu$ for every $i\in S_*$, and $\Lmax(S_*)\le\mu|S_*|$;
      consequently
      $\mu\ \ge\ \min_{i\in P}\Lmax_i$, where $P$ is the $\Lmin$ bottom
      stratum;
\item $2M+3\mu\le T$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
(a) If $S_0=\{i\}$ then $M=\nu=v(\{i\})=0$, but $\Lmax(N)=T>0$ forces
$M\ge T/5$.  For the second claim, $\Lmax(S_0)\ge v(S_0)=M|S_0|$ by core
membership while $\Lmax_i\le M$ throughout, so $\Lmax$ is constant~$M$
on~$S_0$.
(b) Core membership gives $\Lmin(S_*)\le f(S_*)=\mu|S_*|$ while
$\Lmin_i\ge\mu$ gives the reverse, so $\Lmin=\mu$ on $S_*$; the same cap
applied to $\Lmax$ gives $\Lmax(S_*)\le\mu|S_*|$, whence
$\mu\ge\Lmax(S_*)/|S_*|\ge\min_{i\in S_*}\Lmax_i\ge\min_{i\in P}\Lmax_i$.
(c) By the floor side, $f(N\setminus S_0)\ge\mu(5-|S_0|)$, i.e.\
$M|S_0|+\mu(5-|S_0|)\le T$; since $M\ge T/5\ge\mu$ the left side is
nondecreasing in $|S_0|$, and $|S_0|=2$ is admissible by (a).
\end{proof}

Part (b) sharpens Corollary~\ref{cor:floorceiling}: the minimum ranges over
the $\Lmin$-\emph{poor} players only.  Combined with the trivial
$\mu\le T/5$ it closes, by two lines, the hypothesis that resisted the
configuration analysis.

\begin{corollary}[the starved player is $\Lmin$-poor]\label{cor:poorstarved}
In the configuration of Theorem~\ref{thm:cfg} --- $\Lmax$ strata $(1,4)$
with the singleton at zero, at player $j$ --- the player $j$ lies in the
$\Lmin$ bottom stratum.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The stratum pattern forces $4M=T$.  If $j\notin P$ then $\Lmax_i=M=T/4$
for every $i\in P$, so Lemma~\ref{lem:anchors}(b) gives $\mu\ge T/4$,
contradicting $\mu\le T/5$.
\end{proof}

In particular the $\Lmin$-rich branch of Theorem~\ref{thm:cfgbound}'s proof
is vacuous, the first additional hypothesis of Corollary~\ref{cor:cfg} is
automatic, and the corresponding finite check ($31\times354$ programs) is
superseded by the two lines above.

The second ingredient is an exact certificate form for the two selections,
replacing anchors and pinnings by linear identities.

\begin{lemma}[staged Farkas certificates]\label{lem:staged}
Let $v$ be a game with nonempty core, $\phi$ a core point with level sets
$B_1\prec\cdots\prec B_k$ in increasing order of payoff.  Then
$\phi=\Lmin(v)$ if and only if for every stage $t$ and every $i\in B_t$
there exist multipliers $y_A\ge0$ supported on proper $\phi$-tight
coalitions, $\eta\in\mathbb R$, $\rho_l\in\mathbb R$ for
$l\in B_1\cup\cdots\cup B_{t-1}$ and $\sigma_c\ge0$ for $c\in B_t$ with,
for every player $j$,
\[
 -\sum_{A\ni j}y_A+\eta+[\,j\in B_{<t}\,]\rho_j-[\,j\in B_t\,]\sigma_j
 \ =\ [\,j=i\,].
\]
The mirror statement characterizes $\Lmax$, with the level sets taken in
decreasing order, $\sigma$ entering with a plus sign, and right-hand side
$-[\,j=i\,]$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
Leximin is computed stagewise: with the earlier strata pinned, the level
$\mu_t$ is the largest uniform lower bound on the unpinned players, and
$B_t$ collects the players whose maximum subject to that equals $\mu_t$.
$\phi=\Lmin(v)$ is equivalent to the family of stage conditions
$\max\{x_i:x\in\core(v),\ x_l=\phi_l\ (l\in B_{<t}),\ x_c\ge\mu_t\
(c\in B_{\ge t})\}=\phi_i$ for $i\in B_t$.  Each is a linear program whose
feasible set contains $\phi$; the constraints active at $\phi$ are
efficiency, the $\phi$-tight coalitions, the pinned equalities, and the
level bounds on $B_t$ itself.  Dualizing over the tangent cone at $\phi$
and computing the polar cone gives exactly the displayed identity;
complementary slackness confines the support of $y$ to tight coalitions
and eliminates the bilinear terms $y_Av(A)$, which is what makes the
certificate linear in $(v,\phi,y)$ jointly.  The converse direction is the
same computation read backwards.  The certificate was derived independently
twice within the program (once per player and stage as displayed, once per
payoff cut) and the two forms verified against each other in both
directions on $30$ games and $6$ non-optimal core points; the mirror was
re-derived from scratch and validated on $1050$ games ($5{,}250$ ceiling
certificates, $10{,}500$ counting the floor form, no failures), with
negative controls rejecting a false
$\Lmax$ claim and the core midpoint $261/261$ times each.
\end{proof}

With these in place the supremum closes.  Recall that a \emph{configuration}
is a triple: the $\Lmin$ stratum composition, the $\Lmax$ stratum
composition, and the table of overlaps between the two stratifications.
At $n=5$ there are $2961$.

\begin{theorem}[the $(4,1)$ regime]\label{thm:regime41}
Every singleton-normalized exact five-player game whose $\Lmin$ strata form
the pattern $(4,1)$ satisfies $\rho(v)\le\tfrac13$, and the bound is
attained.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[five-player supremum]\label{thm:s5}
Every singleton-normalized exact five-player game satisfies
$\rho(v)\le\tfrac12$.  With Theorem~\ref{thm:n5}, $s(5)=\tfrac12$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof of both, by configuration-complete finite check]
Relabelling players reduces every game to the canonical layout of its
configuration, so it suffices to exclude $\rho>\tfrac12$ (resp.\
$\rho>\tfrac13$ on the $(4,1)$ class) in each configuration.  This was done
by two pipelines whose Lmin-optimality components are independently
formulated; no broader machinery-independence claim is made.  Coverage is
machine-audited:
every one of the $2961$ configurations lands in exactly one bucket, no run
errored, and none was left feasible.

The first pipeline is structural: $1953$ configurations are impossible by
Lemma~\ref{lem:anchors}(a) alone ($\Lmax$ composition ending in a
singleton); $800$ are excluded by one game-free exact program each ---
levels, orderings, totals and the anchor conditions of
Lemma~\ref{lem:anchors} against $\rho>\tfrac12$; $100$ fall to a finite
check with $v$ free, both core memberships, the certificates of
Theorem~\ref{thm:n5closed}, the floor and ceiling stage-one pinnings
(dualized as in Theorem~\ref{thm:cfg}), and the exact-cone facet
inequalities as lazy cuts; and the remaining $108$ fall to the same check
with the complete multi-stage pinning ladders --- every $\Lmin$ stratum
pinned given the strata below it, every $\Lmax$ stratum pinned given the
strata above it, the reduced-game caps encoded exactly as minima over
preimages.  All programs are infeasible in exact rational arithmetic with
no indeterminate verdicts.

The second pipeline re-proves the same verdicts from a minimal hypothesis
basis: singleton normalization and scale; core membership of both vectors;
exactness imposed \emph{existentially} (for each proper coalition $S$, an
attaining core point $w^S$ with $w^S(S)=v(S)$ --- the definition of
exactness, linear, with no appeal to the facet description); and
$\Lmin$/$\Lmax$ imposed through Lemma~\ref{lem:staged} and its mirror.
All $2961$ programs are again infeasible --- so the theorems depend neither
on Theorem~\ref{thm:n5closed} nor on the facet catalogue.  The two
pipelines agree configuration by configuration.

Soundness controls, both pipelines: dropping the pinning ladders (resp.\
certificates) restores feasibility wherever the configuration is possible
at all; the hypothesis sets hold, in exact arithmetic, on $1050$ profiled
random exact games spanning $21$ configurations --- the first pipeline's
$2874$ stage certificates in one run, the second pipeline's $10{,}500$
Farkas certificates in another, no failures in either; and on all eight
configurations for which a
game above some target ratio is known, the same programs run at that
target return feasible models that \emph{audit end-to-end} as genuine
exact games in the assumed configuration --- including the extremal
configuration at ratio exactly $\tfrac12$ and the $(4,1)$ regime at
exactly $\tfrac13$.  The configurations declared possible include every
configuration any harvest has produced.  Attainment in
Theorem~\ref{thm:regime41} is by explicit games ($111$ harvested at exactly
$\tfrac13$).  All drivers, logs and the coverage audit are archived with
the verifiers.
\end{proof}

The two-regime picture is therefore a theorem: $\Lmin$ strata $(4,1)$
force $\rho\le\tfrac13$ while $(3,2)$ allows $\tfrac12$, both attained.
One calibration deserves record: under the single-stage hypothesis set the
final $108$ configurations are all \emph{satisfiable}, and every one of the
$108$ models audits as a genuine exact game --- in the wrong configuration.
The complete ladders are what make the encoding decisive rather than
merely stronger; this is the fourth, and largest, instance of the
certificate-not-realizable phenomenon in this program, and the reason
every feasible model here is audited before it is believed.

\section{Obstructions to an upper bound}\label{sec:obstruction}

\subsection{The exact six-player bound}

Conceptually, this is a theorem about a core polytope inside the normalized
simplex.  Singleton exactness makes that polytope touch every coordinate
facet, through six witness allocations.  If two canonical Lorenz selections
were farther than $2/3$, their coordinatewise comparison would have one of
fifteen sign patterns.  In each pattern, every infinitesimal exchange that
improves one selection must be stopped by a tight coalition on which the
exchange has negative sum; the opposite selection and the six facet
witnesses impose seven necessary inequalities on that blocker.  The finite
part proves that no such blockers can coexist.  Thus the computation is a
fifteen-case convex-geometric exhaustion of necessary exchange obstructions,
not an enumeration of games or core vertices.

The first nontrivial fixed dimension can now be closed without a generator
normal form or a support-table census.

\paragraph{Proof architecture.}
There are three dependencies and only three.  A human reduction maps any
strict counterexample to one of fifteen sign-labelled systems of necessary
exchange blockers.  A finite linear-arithmetic lemma proves those systems
infeasible, with independently checked proof objects.  Finally, the explicit
game $G(2,3)$ supplies the matching lower bound.  The earlier generator
census, equality census, continuity arguments, and support tables are
historical discovery routes and are not used in this proof.

After orbit reduction, the nineteen-schema formulation of the three large
sign cases uses only the following hand-checkable exchange calculus.  The
original minimized labelled menus contain additional direction shapes; they
are proof-checked in the fifteen-case formulation but are not being classified
by this lemma.  Naming the reduced templates separately isolates what is
conceptual from the later blocker-coexistence certificates.

\begin{lemma}[Three exchange templates]\label{lem:orbittemplates}
Let $y\in\mathbb R^6$ and let $h(N)=0$.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*),nosep]
\item For $h=-e_a+e_r$, $h$ is Lmin-admissible exactly when $y_r<y_a$;
      it has $16$ negative nonempty proper cuts.  The Lmax condition is the
      same inequality $y_a>y_r$, read at the highest affected level.
\item For $h=-e_a-e_b+e_r+e_s$, $h$ is Lmin-admissible exactly when
      $\min(y_r,y_s)<\min(y_a,y_b)$; it has $20$ negative nonempty proper
      cuts.  For Lmax, the condition is
      $\max(y_a,y_b)>\max(y_r,y_s)$.
\item For
      $h=e_p+2e_{p'}-e_{m_1}-e_{m_2}-e_{m_3}$, $h$ is Lmax-admissible
      exactly when
      $\max_j y_{m_j}>\max(y_p,y_{p'})$; it has $24$ negative nonempty
      proper cuts.  Negating $h$ gives the Lmin dual.
\end{enumerate}
Ties make the displayed strict activation fail; they introduce no additional
case.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Admissibility asks whether the first affected payoff stratum contains a
coefficient of the improving sign and none of the opposite sign.  Reading the
lowest affected stratum for Lmin and the highest for Lmax gives the stated
minimum and maximum tests.  For (i), negativity means $a\in S,r\notin S$,
with the other four players free.  For (ii), if $d$ donors and $u$ recipients
belong to $S$, negativity is $d>u$; summing the cases $(d,u)=(1,0),(2,0),
(2,1)$ gives $8+4+8=20$.  In (iii), negativity means
$|S\cap\{m_1,m_2,m_3\}|>\mathbf{1}_{p\in S}+2\mathbf{1}_{p'\in S}$; the unused
sixth player is optional, and the three positive-side loads contribute
$14+8+2=24$ cuts.  Negation proves the dual statements.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Six-player bound]\label{thm:s6}
Every singleton-normalized exact six-player game satisfies
\[
 \|\Lmin(v)-\Lmax(v)\|_1\le \frac23v(N).
\]
The bound is attained by $G(2,3)$, and hence $s(6)=2/3$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Scale $T=v(N)$ to one and write $x=\Lmin(v)$, $z=\Lmax(v)$.  Put
\[
 P=\{i:x_i>z_i\},\qquad M=\{i:z_i>x_i\},\qquad E=\{i:x_i=z_i\}.
\]
If $x\ne z$, both $P$ and $M$ are nonempty.  Up to a common relabelling
there are therefore fifteen triples $(p,m,e)$ with $p,m\ge1$ and
$p+m+e=6$, and
\[
 \|x-z\|_1=2\sum_{i\in P}(x_i-z_i).                    \tag{28}
\]

Exactness at each singleton supplies a core point $q^i$ with $q^i_i=0$.
For a point $y$ and direction $h$, define the first order-crossing time
\[
 \Delta(y,h)=\min\left\{\frac{y_j-y_i}{h_i-h_j}:
 y_i<y_j,\ h_i>h_j\right\},
\]
with the empty minimum equal to $+\infty$.  Every
$0<\varepsilon<\Delta(y,h)$ preserves all strict orders between distinct old
payoff strata.

Three elementary selection anchors will be used.  First, $z$ cannot have a
unique top player $i$: along the core segment toward $q^i$, the direction
$q^i-z$ is negative at that unique top because $q^i_i=0<z_i$.  Taking
$0<\varepsilon<\min\{1,\Delta(z,q^i-z)\}$ lowers the first entry of the
decreasing rearrangement.  Second, some $z$-top player has $x_i\ge z_i$,
since otherwise the direction $x-z$ is negative throughout the old top
stratum and the same explicit bound lowers the first changed decreasing-lex
entry.  Dually, some $x$-bottom player has $z_i\le x_i$, using the direction
$z-x$.  Convexity keeps all three segments in the core; the crossing bound
handles tied strata without an informal limiting argument.

Now let $h(N)=0$.  On the Lmin side call $h$ admissible if its lowest affected
payoff stratum has a positive coefficient and no negative coefficient.  On
the Lmax side call it admissible if its highest affected stratum has a
negative coefficient and no positive coefficient.  Such a direction strictly
improves the relevant sorted lexicographic vector for every positive step
below $\Delta(y,h)$.  If all proper tight coalitions had nonnegative
derivative, set
\[
 \varepsilon_*=\min\left(\{1,\Delta(y,h)\}\cup
 \left\{\frac{y(S)-v(S)}{-h(S)}:
 \varnothing\ne S\subsetneq N,\ y(S)>v(S),\ h(S)<0\right\}\right).
\]
Every finite member is positive and the set contains $1$, so
$\varepsilon_*>0$.  Any $0<\varepsilon<\varepsilon_*$ preserves both the
payoff-stratum order and every core inequality, while $h(N)=0$ preserves
efficiency.  This would give a core improvement, a contradiction.  Optimality
therefore forces a proper tight coalition $S$ with $h(S)<0$.  Core membership
of the opposite selection and the six witnesses gives, respectively,
\[
 z(S)\ge x(S),\quad q^i(S)\ge x(S)\ (i\in N),           \tag{29}
\]
or
\[
 x(S)\ge z(S),\quad q^i(S)\ge z(S)\ (i\in N).           \tag{30}
\]

As a finite differential control, the pivot implementation of admissibility
was compared with a separately written first-affected-stratum criterion on
all $4{,}683$ labelled weak payoff orders, all $1{,}610$ bound-two directions,
and both selections: $15{,}079{,}260$ comparisons with no mismatch.

For each sign triple, impose nonnegativity and efficiency of $x,z,q^i$, the
strict signs, (28) with left side $>2/3$, the three anchors, and the necessary

 1,1,1,1,0,2,5,14,8,2,52,71,2,90,2,                  \tag{31}

most five; 150 use unit coefficients and five require coefficient two.  This
is a complete human explanation of the witness-side upper bounds, but the
allocation-side strict reductions have also been exactified: 53 rational
identities, checked solver-free at all 155 uses, show that the corresponding
lower sums strictly exceed their incidence bounds.  Of the uses, 118 need no
path comparison, 33 use ancestors only, and four also use the target
comparison.  An independent exact-arithmetic checker verifies that the
$11+155+47$ uses exhaust all 213 candidates, so every terminal no-blocker
closure is arithmetic rather than solver-only.  The coverage maps remain
relay-derived.
The same incidence geometry controls activation: anchor/order chains remove
84 of 124 elementary failure cases, four more are allocation-infeasible, and
each of the other 36 has a packet/ancestor over-cover of support at most four.
The activation incidence bounds are human; 26 solver-free rational identities
prove the 36 allocation lower bounds, and the four direct cases share one
more identity.  Thus no class-6 arithmetic leaf remains machine-only,
conditional on the frozen relay/use maps.  Of the full 40-use activation map,
19 uses are path-free and the 21 path-dependent uses split into three relay
families; the 27 identities reduce to 25 affine motifs after source-depth
aliases are forgotten.  Those maps remain encoding-shared and require
external human-to-machine review.
Both remaining rows also close after imposing the same root split
$x_3\le x_4$ versus $x_3>x_4$.  Indeed, that single split gives complete
forced-exchange relay trees for all eleven rows; class 10 alone needs the
nested $x_1$--$x_2$ split.  The last two trees still rely on the parent
encoding rather than separate exact hand certificates.  This does not replace
the human obligation to verify the counterexample-to-formula implication.

The single-witness filters have a common exact geometry.  For any fixed
coalition menu, the map $q\mapsto(q(C))_C$ sends
$\{q\geq0:q(N)=1,q_b=0\}$ onto the convex hull of the non-anchor incidence
columns.  Hence a nonnegative weighted sum of witness comparisons is bounded
exactly by its maximum non-anchor incidence load, attained at a unit vector.
The six witnesses are otherwise independent, so after choosing one blocker
for each active exchange this projection eliminates all witness coordinates
exactly; their six anchor-specific support bounds collapse to the
second-largest player incidence load.  This support system has an exact
finite reduction.  For anchor $i$ and a proposed maximizing non-anchor player
$k$, partition the nonnegative weight orthant by the cones
\[
 K_{i,k}=\{\lambda\geq0:
 \lambda\mathbin{\cdot}(a^k-a^\ell)\geq0
 \text{ for every }\ell\notin\{i,k\}\}.
\]
On $K_{i,k}$ the support bound is linear, so its extreme rays give a complete
rational inequality system.  A ray with $s$ positive coordinates requires
$s-1$ active player-comparison hyperplanes; because there are only $n-2$,
$s\leq n-1$.  Thus every six-player witness inequality uses at most five
blocker requirements, and a five-coalition incidence circuit shows this bound
is sharp.  This constructive ray statement strengthens the corresponding
pointwise Helly bound and gives an exact facet-enumeration algorithm.
The remaining system is a
finite cut-transversal problem coupled to this symmetric incidence support
function and the opposite-selection signs.  The class-5 crossing sums are
sparse incidence over-covers of this form.  This
standard support-function identity supplies an LP/facet algorithm.  Applied
exhaustively to class 6, it gives the 155 target-witness supports and eleven
context supports just described; these couple to 53 and nine exact allocation
identities, respectively.  The exact cut census identifies the remaining 47
candidates with the relay branches and settles every terminal no-blocker
closure.  This arithmetic exhaustion does not independently validate the
relay/use maps or the counterexample-to-formula implication.

Thus the upper bound holds.  The family formula gives
$\rho(G(2,3))=2(2(3-1)-1)/(3(2+1))=2/3$, proving equality.
\end{proof}

\subsection{No constant below \texorpdfstring{$2$}{2}}

Theorem~\ref{thm:sup} already rules out one shape of answer.  Any bound of
the form $\|\Lmin(v)-\Lmax(v)\|_1\le c\cdot v(N)$ with a constant $c<2$,
valid for all $n$, is false: choose $n$ even with $2-8/n>c$.  So the only
possible clean upper bound is dimension-dependent, and the right target is
the finite-$n$ statement
\[
 s_2(n)=2-\frac{8n}{n^2-(n\bmod2)}\qquad(n\ge6),
\]
now proved for the full exact class at $n=6$ by Theorem~\ref{thm:s6}, and
confirmed by complete two-generator enumeration at $n=7,8$, together with the
question of whether $s(n)=s_2(n)$ in larger dimensions.

\subsection{No bound linear in the leximin floor}

A second shape is also excluded, and this one was a live candidate.  The
inequality
\[
 \|\Lmin(v)-\Lmax(v)\|_1\ \le\ 4(n-4)\,\mu(v),\qquad \mu(v)=\min_i\Lmin(v)_i,
\]
holds with no violations in $900$ fresh random five-player games and is tight
both on the $n=5$ extremal class and on $G(3,4)$ at $n=8$, where both sides
equal $12$.  It fails from $n=9$ on:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}llll@{}}
\toprule
game & $n$ & $\|\Lmin-\Lmax\|_1$ & $4(n-4)\mu$\\
\midrule
$G(4,4)$ & 9 & $88/5$ & $16$\\
$G(3,5)$ & 9 & $33/2$ & $15$\\
$G(4,5)$ & 10 & $24$ & $96/5$\\
$G(5,5)$ & 11 & $95/3$ & $70/3$\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
The reason is structural rather than accidental: in $G(p,k)$ one has
$\mu=p/(p+1)<1$, bounded, while the gap grows like $2T$.  No bound of the
form $C(n)\mu(v)$ can track the divergence, whatever $C$ is.

\subsection{Non-monotonicity under majorization}

The most natural route to an upper bound on the two-generator class is to
argue that a uniform $h$-block is without loss of generality --- the extremal
family $G(p,k)$ has one --- and then to optimize over $g$ alone using the
floor formula of Conjecture~\ref{conj:floor}.  That route is closed.

Define a \emph{smoothing step} as a Robin Hood transfer of one unit from a
larger to a smaller $h$-coordinate, so that $h$ moves down in the
majorization order, toward uniform.  The premise that a smoothing step never
decreases $\rho$ is false, and not only for small blocks:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lrrrr@{}}
\toprule
$|B|$ & $2$ & $3$ & $4$ & $5$\\
\midrule
smoothing steps tested & $40$ & $76$ & $84$ & $84$\\
violations & $18$ & $27$ & $11$ & $21$\\
rate & $45\%$ & $36\%$ & $13\%$ & $25\%$\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
In particular it fails for $|B|\ge4$, so the block-size threshold one might
guess from the $n=5$ anomaly does not exist.  An explicit verified
counterexample at $|B|=4$, with $n=7$ and $T=10$, both allocations confirmed
in the core:
\[
 g=(0,5,0,3,2,0,0),\qquad h=(1,0,2,0,0,5,2),
\]
\[
 \Lmin=(1,2,1,1,1,3,1),\qquad
 \Lmax=\Bigl(\tfrac56,\tfrac52,\tfrac56,\tfrac43,\tfrac76,\tfrac52,\tfrac56\Bigr),
 \qquad \rho=\tfrac15 .
\]
Smoothing the $h$-block $(5,2)\mapsto(4,3)$, i.e.\ replacing $h$ by
$(1,0,2,0,0,4,3)$, gives
\[
 \Lmin=\Lmax=(1,2,1,1,1,2,2),\qquad \rho=0 .
\]
So a single Robin Hood step inside one generator collapses the ratio from
$1/5$ to $0$, and $\rho$ is not monotone under majorization of $h$.

The two facts sit together uncomfortably and both are true: smoothing makes a
game more symmetric and tends to \emph{collapse} the two selections onto each
other, yet the extremal family has a perfectly uniform $h$.  The dependence
is therefore genuinely non-monotone, and any upper-bound proof must handle
general $h$ directly rather than reducing to the uniform case.

\paragraph{A reduced formulation of problem (2).}
There is a dimension-free, exactly checkable reduction that narrows the
second problem without solving it.  Fix one complete staged tight-coalition
support $\mathcal C_x$ for $x=\Lmin(v)$, one sparse first-stage over-cover
support $\mathcal C_z$ for $z=\Lmax(v)$, and a singleton exactness witness
$w^r\in\core(v)$ with $w^r_r=0$.  It is sufficient to retain
\[
 x(S)\le z(S),w^r(S)\quad(S\in\mathcal C_x),\qquad
 z(S)\le x(S),w^r(S)\quad
 (S\in\mathcal C_x\cap\mathcal C_z),
\]
together with the common totals, nonnegativity, the two ordered strata and
the actual hybrid sign cut.  Indeed the actual triple $(x,z,w^r)$ is feasible
in this relaxation, so a lower bound on its hybrid-overlap objective is a
lower bound for the game.  On a common coalition the displayed rows reduce
to $x(S)=z(S)\le w^r(S)$: the sparse leximax-min certificate glues two rooted
tangents only where it meets the complete leximin packet.  The over-cover may
be chosen with at most $n$ coalitions, and affine Farkas duality gives a
support-minimal target certificate with at most $3n-3$ defining rows.  For a
fixed labelled branch, validity, equality vertices and the relative-facet
test are finite exact rational computations; projection to a facet of the
full exact-game cone still requires the indecomposable semi-balanced test of
\citet{StudenyKratochvil2022}.

There is a useful dimension-free reduction before any packet rows are used.
Write $\mu=\min_i x_i>0$, let $M=\max_i z_i$, and let $H$ be the top block of
$z$.  The normalized first-stage over-cover gives a probability $\alpha$
supported on $H$ with $\alpha\cdot x\ge M$, so some $r\in H$ satisfies
$x_r\ge M$.  At this ceiling root,
\[
 \min(x_r,z_r)=M,
 \qquad
 \min(x_i,z_i)\ge\min(\mu,z_i)\ge\frac{\mu}{M}z_i
 \quad(i\ne r).
\]
Consequently
\begin{equation}
 \sum_i\min(x_i,z_i)\ge M-\mu+\frac{T\mu}{M}.       \tag{CRI}
\end{equation}
In normalized variables $t=M/T$, $u=\mu/T$, this is
$O/T\ge t-u+u/t$.  If $c_n=n/\lfloor n^2/4\rfloor$, the target is therefore
automatic for $t\ge c_n$, and for $t<c_n$ it is automatic whenever
\[
 u\ge\frac{t(c_n-t)}{1-t}.
\]
Thus an unresolved positive-floor counterexample lies in the explicit wedge
$1/n\le t<c_n$ and $0<u<t(c_n-t)/(1-t)$.  For $0<\mu<M$, equality in (CRI)
forces every nonroot $z_i\in\{0,M\}$ and every nonroot top player to have
$x_i=\mu$, hence $T=qM$ for an integer $q$.  This rigidity does not prove the
target: on the two sharp seven-player orientations the right side of (CRI)
is respectively $79T/144$ and $37T/72$, both below $7T/12$.  Exact rational
replay on eight archived controls and three equality patterns is included as
an arithmetic audit; none of the eight hard controls is closed by (CRI)
alone.

The singleton witness can now be eliminated exactly.  Put
$\mathcal C_\cap=\mathcal C_x\cap\mathcal C_z$.  On this intersection the
direct rows already give $x(S)=z(S)$, so the $z$-to-witness comparisons are
duplicates.  For nonnegative weights $u$ on $\mathcal C_x$, let

\[
 p_i=\sum_{S\in\mathcal C_x:i\in S}u_S.
\]

There exists a nonnegative total-$T$ witness $w$ with $w_r=0$ dominating the
required $x$- and $z$-coalition sums if and only if

\[
 \sum_{S\in\mathcal C_x}u_Sx(S)
 \le T\max_{i\ne r}p_i                                      \tag{RHP}
\]

for every such $u$.  Necessity follows by weighting the witness rows;
sufficiency is separation from the closed down-set generated by the rooted
simplex.  After normalizing $p_i\le1$ for $i\ne r$, it suffices to check the
vertices of a rational rooted fractional packing polytope.  Every vertex
uses at most $n-1$ coalition weights, since its positive columns are
independent on the $n-1$ nonroot capacity rows.  Thus the witness projection
has a finite exact inequality description with a linear support bound.  On
the sharp family, the uniform extreme packing on the block-star hinges gives
both inequalities used in the hand proof and recovers the bound with
equality.  These are relative rooted-hinge inequalities; only projected
systems passing the published indecomposable semi-balanced test are
exact-cone facets.

The packing has a direct rooted balance form.  If
$p_i=\sum_{S\ni i}u_S$, $p_i\le1$ off the root, and
$\delta=p_r-1$, then (RHP) is

\[
 \delta x_r\le\sum_{i\ne r}(1-p_i)x_i.                 \tag{RDB}
\]

When the packing uses only common hinges, the same inequality holds with
$z$ in place of $x$.  For the master family the uniform block-star packing
has $\delta=1/(k-1)$, zero deficit on the block players, and unit deficit on
the $p$ outside players.  The two versions of (RDB) are exactly the hand
inequalities $X\le p(k-1)q$ and $M\le p(k-1)l$, giving a structural
explanation of the sharp family handover.  A universal proof cannot demand
$\delta>0$ on every nonspectral branch: one hostile control reaches its
target from the direct common-hinge gluing rows before any projection cut is
needed.

The block-star argument in fact needs only one common hinge.  This gives a
dimension-free mixed branch theorem.  Let
$N=\{r\}\mathbin{\dot\cup}O\mathbin{\dot\cup}B$, with
$|O|=p\ge1$, $|B|=k\ge2$, choose $b_0\in B$ and
$j\in B\setminus\{b_0\}$, and put
$C_b=\{r\}\cup(B\setminus\{b\})$.  Suppose a labelled branch has
\[
 x_r=c,\qquad x_b=b\ (b\in B\setminus\{b_0\}),\qquad
 x_i=a\ (i\in O\cup\{b_0\}),\qquad 0\le a\le b\le c,
\]
while $z_r=z_b=A$ on $B\setminus\{b_0\}$,
$z_{b_0}=B_0$, and $z(O)=L$.  Assign $B$ to $x$ and
$\{r\}\cup O$ to $z$ in the hybrid cut.  If a nonnegative total-$T$
witness $w$, with $w_r=0$, satisfies
\[
 x(C_b)\le w(C_b)\quad(b\in B),
 \qquad z(C_j)\le x(C_j),                              \tag{MBS}
\]
then
\[
 O(x,z)\ge\frac{nT}{k(p+1)}
          \ge\frac{nT}{\lfloor n^2/4\rfloor}.          \tag{MBB}
\]
Indeed, summing the $k$ witness rows and subtracting $(k-1)x(N)$ gives
$c\le p(k-1)a$.  Put $P=p(k-1)$ and $D=k(p+1)$.  The exact identity
\[
 (P-1)T-D(c-b)
 =[kP+p+1](b-a)+n(Pa-c)\ge0                            \tag{MBI}
\]
gives $c-b\le(P-1)T/D$.  Since
$T=kA+B_0+L$ and the one common hinge gives
$(k-1)A+B_0\le c+(k-2)b+a$, the fixed-cut overlap obeys
\[
 O=A+L+(k-1)b+a\ge T-(c-b)=nT/D.
\]
Finally $k+(p+1)=n$, so $D\le\lfloor n^2/4\rfloor$.
Equality at the universal target requires the balanced product,
$b=a$, $c=p(k-1)a$, and equality in the displayed packet rows.

A second repair pattern covers tied ceiling loads without a strict
over-cover leader.  Let
$N=\{r,u,j\}\mathbin{\dot\cup}Q$, where $|Q|=m\ge1$ and $n=m+3$.
Suppose nonnegative $x,z,w$ have common total $T$, $w_r=0$, and
\[
 x_r=c,\quad x_u=x_j=b,\quad x_q\le b\ (q\in Q),
 \qquad
 z_r=z_u=z_j=A,\quad z_q\le x_q\ (q\in Q),
 \qquad b\le A\le c.
\]
If
\[
 x(\{r,u\})\le w(\{r,u\}),\qquad
 x(\{r,j\})\le w(\{r,j\}),\qquad
 z(\{r,j\})\le x(\{r,j\}),                         \tag{TCS}
\]
then
\[
 \frac{O(x,z)}T\ge\frac{n}{2(n-2)}
                  \ge\frac{n}{\lfloor n^2/4\rfloor}. \tag{TCB}
\]
To see this, put $X_Q=x(Q)$.  The two witness rows give
$T-2(c+b)=X_Q-c\ge0$, the Lmin order gives $mb-c\ge0$, and the reverse
common pair gives $c+b-2A\ge0$.  Since
$O=T-2A+2b$, the full certificate is the identity
\[
 O-\frac{m+3}{2(m+1)}T
 =(c+b-2A)+\frac{2(mb-c)}{m+1}
  +\frac{m-1}{2(m+1)}[T-2(c+b)]\ge0.                 \tag{TCI}
\]
Finally $\lfloor n^2/4\rfloor\ge2(n-2)$ for $n\ge4$.
This theorem humanly closes two of the three positive-floor tied-repair
templates in the six-player census, records $601[103]$ and $602[129]$.
The irregular record $602[82]$ does not have the same two-spoke form.
The exact-cone row behind the theorem is
\[
 \one_{\{r,u\}}+\one_{\{r,j\}}
 +\sum_{q\in Q}\one_{\{r,q\}}-(m+1)e_r=\one_N.       \tag{TCSF}
\]
This is the $k=2$, $p=m$ member of the dimension-free block-star family
(GPL), so the theorem already proved that it is an indecomposable purely
min-semi-balanced facet of the exact-game cone.

That last template nevertheless has a separate human certificate.  On six
players $\{r,u,g,j,l_1,l_2\}$, suppose
\[
 x=(c,b,a,a,a,a)\quad\hbox{on }(r,u,l_1,l_2,g,j),
 \qquad z_r=z_u=z_g=A,\quad z_j=D,\quad z_{l_1}=z_{l_2}=E,
\]
with $0\le E\le a\le D$ and $a\le b\le A\le c$.  Let a nonnegative
total-$T$ witness satisfy $w_r=0$ and
\[
\begin{split}
 x(\{r,u,g,l_2\})&\le w(\{r,u,g,l_2\}),\\
 x(\{r,u,j\})&\le w(\{r,u,j\}),\\
 x(\{r,g,j\})&\le w(\{r,g,j\}),\\
 z(\{r,u,j\})&\le x(\{r,u,j\}).
\end{split}                                             \tag{TP}
\]
Summing the first three rows gives $3c+2b+5a\le2T$, hence $c\le3a$.
Writing $h=c+b+a-2A-D\ge0$, one obtains the exact identity
\[
 O-\frac23T=h+\frac{a+b}{3}+\frac23(3a-c)\ge0.       \tag{TPI}
\]
This proves record $602[82]$, whose actual overlap is $7/9$.  Thus all three
common-hinge repair templates isolated by the sequential census have human
branch proofs.  What remains open is the structural selection theorem forcing
an arbitrary residual into the Lmin-root-price or repair alternatives; these
branch theorems do not show that every residual has one of the displayed
geometries.

The last repair also has an exact-cone explanation.  Giving the three witness
rows in (TP) weight $1/2$ produces root excess $1/2$, deficit one on $l_1$,
and deficit $1/2$ on $l_2$.  Its rooted pair lift is
\[
 \one_{\{r,u,g,l_2\}}+\one_{\{r,u,j\}}+\one_{\{r,g,j\}}
 +\one_{\{r,l_2\}}+2\,\one_{\{r,l_1\}}-4e_r=2\,\one_N. \tag{TPF}
\]
The six support columns have rank six.  For a candidate exceptional
coalition with indicators $(e_r,e_u,e_1,e_2,e_g,e_j)$, the
Studen\'y--Kratochv\'il exceptional-set equations have nonnegative support
weights exactly when $L\le\rho\le U$, where
\[
\begin{split}
L=\max\{&-e_u-e_g+e_j,-e_u-e_j+e_g,-e_g-e_j+e_u,\\
        &-2e_2+e_u+e_g-e_j,-e_1\},\\
U={}&(e_r-e_j-e_2-e_1)/2.
\end{split}                                             \tag{TPE}
\]
For binary indicators the feasible sets are precisely the six support
coalitions and $N$.  Hence no new proper exceptional coalition exists:
(TPF) is indecomposable and defines a facet of the exact-game cone.  An
independently formulated exact-linear-arithmetic replay confirms all 56
unsupported proper candidates infeasible; the displayed interval argument,
not the solver result, is the proof.

Only the scalar root estimate is special to the star.  More generally,
suppose a branch has one root at $c$, $g$ players at $b$, and $m$ players at
$a$, with $n=1+g+m$, and its cross row gives $O\ge T-(c-b)$.  If a rooted
pair lift has root excess $\delta>0$, deficit weights supported on the
$a$-level loss players, and total deficit weight $Q$, then (PFRDB) and
nonnegative pair premiums give $c\le Pa$, where $P=Q/\delta$.  Putting
$D_P=P+n-1$ gives
\[
 (P-1)T-D_P(c-b)
 =[g(P-1)+D_P](b-a)+n(Pa-c)\ge0.
\]
Hence
\[
 O/T\ge n/(P+n-1),                                    \tag{MFP}
\]
which proves the universal target whenever
$P\le\lfloor n^2/4\rfloor-n+1$.  The ratio $Q/\delta$ is therefore an
effective \emph{marked} facet price.  For the block-star lift it is
$p(k-1)$.  Every one
of the nine extreme mixed-projection cuts in the hostile control suite has
price at most its dimensional threshold.  Eight put every deficit on a
nongaining player, but only five put every deficit on the first Lmin floor;
the still-missing property is therefore multistage payoff/sign alignment,
not a bound on the primitive coefficients.

The same calculation permits unstructured reserve players.  Suppose only a
subset $U$ has the root--middle--low form, with
$n_0=1+g+m$, every player outside $U$ is a nongainer, and the full gain set
$G$ consists of the $g$ middle players and one low player.  If a mixed row
gives $z(G)-x(G)\le c-b$, then the preceding identity applies to
$T_U=x(U)\le T$, while $O=T-[z(G)-x(G)]$.  Therefore
\[
 O/T\ge n_0/(P+n_0-1).                                \tag{MFR}
\]
If $T_U=0$ the cross row gives $O=T$ directly; otherwise the order and
$c\le Pa$ force $P\ge1$.
This proves the $n$-player target whenever
$P\le n_0\lfloor n^2/4\rfloor/n-n_0+1$.  Extra nongaining strata are thus
an overlap reserve.  What remains in a general branch is not a coefficient
bound, but simultaneous alignment: the rooted deficits must lie in the low
loss stratum and one mixed row must pay the entire gain by $c-b$.

This misalignment has an exact scalar penalty.  For an arbitrary rooted pair
lift put
\[
 e_q=\max\left\{0,\frac{\sum_iq_i(x_i-a)-
                    \sum_iq_i v(\{r,i\})}{\delta}\right\}.
\]
Then (PFRDB) gives $c\le Pa+e_q$, and the same identity yields
\[
 \frac OT\ge\frac{n}{P+n-1}
 -\frac{n e_q}{(P+n-1)T}.                             \tag{MPE}
\]
With a reserve subset, replace the numerator by $n_0(T-e_q)$ and the
denominator by $(P+n_0-1)T$.  Thus the missing multistage theorem must pay a
specific amount, not merely establish qualitative alignment.  In the full
$n$-player form, the denominator margin absorbs the defect whenever
$e_q/T\le1-(P+n-1)/\lfloor n^2/4\rfloor$.

Thus $k-1$ hinges may be Lmin-only: one aligned Lmax-tight hinge is enough
to couple the complete star to the target.  Eliminating the outside witness
coordinates produces the same rooted pair-spoke shear as (GPL), so the
support-minimal full-cone factor is the previously classified indecomposable
block-star facet despite the two-colour labelled proof.  A new exact
three-generator seven-player control realizes this branch away from the
sharp game: its actual overlap is $11/12$, its maximum-overlap root is unique,
and its rooted one-witness relaxation has exact optimum $7/12$, attained by
the normalized $G(2,4)$ triple.  Fourteen explicit staged identities and a
hard-coded exact primal/dual certificate are included with the verifier.

One exactness row strengthens this projection at no dimension-dependent
cost.  Put $m_i=v(\{r,i\})$ for $i\ne r$ and
$M_r=\sum_{i\ne r}m_i$.  A core witness with $w_r=0$ has
$w_i\ge m_i$, hence $M_r\le T$.  Translating the rooted simplex by $m$
shows that its support function in direction $p$ is
\[
 \sum_{i\ne r}p_i m_i+(T-M_r)\max_{i\ne r}p_i.
\]
Consequently the exact analogue of (RHP), with only these pair floors
retained from the witness core, is
\[
 \delta x_r+\sum_{i\ne r}q_i v(\{r,i\})
 \le \sum_{i\ne r}q_i x_i,                            \tag{PFRDB}
\]
with the same inequality for $z$ when the packing uses common hinges.  The
new term is also the exact price of transporting the exceptional singleton:
replacing $\{r\}$ by $\{r,i\}$ weakens the induced exact-game inequality by
$\delta[v(\{r,i\})-v(\{r\})-v(\{i\})]$, namely
$\delta v(\{r,i\})$ under singleton normalization.  Thus the pair-floor
projection retains precisely the premium lost by the canonical
semi-balanced decomposition.  It is sharp in the controls: seven of the
eight nontrivial hostile/sharp cuts satisfy (PFRDB) with equality, while the
neutral-endpoint cut has exact slack $9/9523$.

Crucially, (PFRDB) has a different canonical exact-cone lift.  If
$Q=\sum_{i\ne r}q_i$, then
\[
 \sum_{S\in\mathcal C_x}u_S\mathbf1_S
 +\sum_{i\ne r}q_i\mathbf1_{\{r,i\}}-(\delta+Q)e_r
 =\mathbf1_N.                                         \tag{RPL}
\]
This is obtained from (RMS) below by the invertible column shear
$e_i\mapsto e_r+e_i$.  Hence a vertex packing gives a min-semi-balanced
system of support at most $n$, with exceptional coalition $\{r\}$ when
$\delta+Q>0$.  Its induced exact-game inequality on the tight Lmin
coalitions is exactly (PFRDB), and the same inequality evaluates at Lmax on
common hinges.  By \citet{StudenyKratochvil2022}, these rooted pair lifts are
full exact-cone facets exactly when indecomposable.  They therefore form a
distinguished, exactly enumerable facet class attached to a staged menu.
Exact-LP and independent Z3 decomposition tests agree on the eight
nontrivial controls: six pair lifts are facets, including both sharp
seven-player orientations; the remaining two have respectively three and
twenty decompositions.

For the full sharp family this facet statement is dimension-free.  With
$N=\{r\}\mathbin{\dot\cup}O\mathbin{\dot\cup}B$,
$|O|=p$, $|B|=k$, and $C_b=\{r\}\cup(B\setminus\{b\})$, the lift is
\[
 \sum_{b\in B}\mathbf1_{C_b}
 +(k-1)\sum_{o\in O}\mathbf1_{\{r,o\}}
 -[p(k-1)+1]e_r=(k-1)\mathbf1_N.                     \tag{GPL}
\]
It is indecomposable for every $p\ge1,k\ge2$.  Indeed, normalizing a
prospective new exceptional coalition $E$ to coefficient $-1$, and writing
$b=|E\cap B|$, $o=|E\cap O|$, gives
$A=(k\rho+b)/(k-1)$ and
$A+(p-1)\rho+o+d-\mathbf1_{r\in E}=0$ with all displayed support
coefficients nonnegative.  Separating the cases $o<p$ and $o=p$ leaves only
$\varnothing,N,\{r\},\{r,o\}$ and the supported $C_b$; hence no new proper
exceptional set exists.  On $G(p,k)$, (GPL) is active at both selections and
its evaluations are exactly the two hand inequalities producing equality in
the hybrid-overlap bound.

There is now a direct coupling to the sparse Lmax certificate.  Suppose a
normalized top over-cover supported on common hinges is
$\sum_S\lambda_S\mathbf1_S=\eta\mathbf1_N+\alpha$, with
$\sum_i\alpha_i=1$.  For a top root $r$, let
$\theta_-:=\max_{i\ne r}\alpha_i$ and
$\theta:=\eta+\theta_->0$.  Then $u=\lambda/\theta$ is a rooted packing and
\[
 \delta=\frac{\alpha_r-\theta_-}{\theta},\qquad
 q_i=\frac{\theta_--\alpha_i}{\theta}.               \tag{OCP}
\]
Thus a strict deleted-load leader produces a positive-root pair lift, and
its two evaluations are
\[
 (\alpha_r-\theta_-)y_r
 +\sum_{i\ne r}(\theta_--\alpha_i)v(\{r,i\})
 \le\sum_{i\ne r}(\theta_--\alpha_i)y_i,             \tag{OCPL}
\]
for both $y=x$ and $y=z$.  This follows simply by dividing the over-cover
loads $\eta+\alpha_i$ by $\eta+\theta_-$ and applying (RPL).  In the bounded
positive-top case, decomposing this packing into vertices decomposes (OCPL)
into the corresponding rooted pair-lift facet candidates.  For $G(p,k)$,
$\alpha_r=1/k$, $\theta_-=(k-1)/k^2$, the block deficits vanish and the
outside deficits equal one after normalization, exactly recovering (GPL).
Moreover the two rows have the same pair-lift slack.  Equivalently,
\[
 \sum_{i\ne r}(\theta_--\alpha_i)(x_i-z_i)
 =(\alpha_r-\theta_-)(x_r-z_r).                      \tag{OCD}
\]
This follows from common tightness, which gives $\alpha\cdot x=\alpha\cdot z$,
and equal totals.  Thus a coupled pair-lift is active at one selection iff it
is active at both; (OCD) is its selection-visible equality law.

There is also a useful quotient in the facet algorithm.  If a rooted pair
$\{r,i\}$ already has packing weight $t>0$, replacing
$u_{\{r,i\}}$ by $u_{\{r,i\}}-t$, $q_i$ by $q_i+t$, and $\delta$ by
$\delta-t$ leaves (RPL) unchanged: both $u_{\{r,i\}}+q_i$ and
$\delta+\sum_jq_j$ are invariant.  Hence all rooted-pair columns may be
deleted before packing-vertex enumeration and restored only as deficit
columns.  This dimension-free gauge quotient removes duplicate encodings of
the same exact-cone row.

This quotient does not preserve the marked price.  With
$K=\lfloor n^2/4\rfloor-n+1$, the same move sends
\[
 (\delta,Q,K\delta-Q)
 \longmapsto(\delta-t,Q+t,K\delta-Q-(K+1)t).          \tag{RPGP}
\]
Thus the unlabelled facet normal does not determine affordability.  There is
nevertheless a canonical best admissible marking once the Lmin-tight pairs
are retained.  For a rooted completed row whose positive nonpair columns are
$x$-tight, let $a_S>0$ be its positive coefficients.  Split a
rooted-pair coefficient as $a_{\{r,i\}}=t_i+q_i$, where $t_i$ is packing
mass, $q_i$ is deficit mass, and require $t_i=0$ unless $\{r,i\}$ is
$x$-tight.  Then
\[
 \delta(t)=\sum_{|S|\ge3}a_S+\sum_i t_i-1,
 \qquad Q(t)=\sum_i(a_{\{r,i\}}-t_i),
\]
and consequently
\[
 K\delta(t)-Q(t)=K\delta(0)-Q(0)+(K+1)\sum_i t_i.    \tag{RPM}
\]
Every admissible $t_i$ therefore takes its upper endpoint in the optimal
marking.  Affordability is determined exactly by the normalized facet normal
together with its Lmin-tight pair labels; no particular dual-solver history
is needed.  More explicitly, let $c$ be the positive exceptional-root
coefficient and let
$Q_{\rm bad}=\sum_{i:\{r,i\}\text{ not }x\text{-tight}}a_{\{r,i\}}$.
Root balance gives $c=\delta_*+Q_{\rm bad}$ for the canonical best marking,
so
\[
 K\delta_*-Q_{\rm bad}=Kc-(K+1)Q_{\rm bad}.           \tag{RPM$'$}
\]
Consequently the row is affordably markable exactly when
$Q_{\rm bad}/c\le K/(K+1)$ and $\delta_*>0$.  This bad-spoke fraction is an
exact finite labelled-facet test at fixed dimension.

It also has an exact hypergraph dual.  Let $G$ be the nonroot players with
an Lmin-tight rooted pair, let $B$ be its complement, and let $A$ range over
the offroot traces of the other root-containing Lmin-tight coalitions.  For
a fractional packing $a$ of these traces, complete every unused player load
by its rooted pair.  With
\[
 C=K(|G|-1)-|B|,
 \qquad w(A)=K(1-|A\cap G|)+|A\cap B|,
\]
the best marked margin is
\[
 C+\max_{a\ge0}\left\{\sum_Aw(A)a_A:
              \sum_{A\ni i}a_A\le1\ (i\ne r)\right\}          \tag{LPC}
\]
and equals the weighted-cover minimum
\[
 C+\min_{y\ge0}\left\{\sum_{i\ne r}y_i:
              \sum_{i\in A}y_i\ge w(A)\ (A\in\mathcal H_r)\right\}.
                                                                    \tag{LCD}
\]
This follows by substituting the unused loads in (RPM) and applying ordinary
fractional packing/cover duality.  A vertex in packing-plus-slack standard
form has at most $n-1$ positive columns, giving the earlier support bound and
the same rooted semi-balanced/facet test.  In particular, two Lmin-tight
rooted pairs make the zero nonpair packing affordable, since its margin is at
least
\[
 K-(n-3)=\lfloor n^2/4\rfloor-2n+4>0\qquad(n\ge6).    \tag{LPC2}
\]
This closes the scalar price-selection problem in that branch.  It does not
by itself align the remaining deficits with the required payoff/loss strata;
that labelled alignment, or an Lmax row paying its defect, remains necessary
for the hybrid-overlap conclusion.

The one-pair branch has a complementary rigidity theorem.  Suppose the
unique Lmin-tight rooted-pair partner is $j$.  Assigning unit mass to one
nonpair trace $A$ gives marked margin
\[
 K|G\setminus A|-|B\setminus A|.                    \tag{LPC3}
\]
For $n\ge6$, $K\ge n-2=|B|$.  Thus if $j\notin A$, this margin is
nonnegative and the root excess is one, so the row is affordable.  If every
nonpair trace contains $j$, its incidence constraint gives
$\sum_Aa_A\le1$, and (LPC) gives root excess zero for every feasible
packing.  Therefore a one-pair root is affordable if and only if some larger
rooted tight coalition omits its partner.  An unaffordable one-pair root has
a \emph{locked partner} in every rooted packing column.  This explains why
the direct arm must absorb that partner with the root, but does not yet
select the rest of the direct block.

There is also a complete pair-free alternative.  Put $m=n-1$.  Then
\[
 K=\lfloor(m-1)^2/4\rfloor
   =\max_{2\le t\le m}(t-1)(m-t),
 \qquad \lfloor n^2/4\rfloor=K+m.                   \tag{LPC4}
\]
Suppose $G=\varnothing$ and the nonpair traces have empty total
intersection.  Choose an inclusion-minimal empty-intersection subfamily
$A_1,\ldots,A_t$.  For each $j$, minimality supplies a distinct witness
\[
 v_j\in\bigcap_{k\ne j}A_k\setminus A_j.
\]
Every selected trace contains the other $t-1$ witnesses, and every vertex
occurs in at most $t-1$ selected traces.  Hence assigning
$a_{A_j}=1/(t-1)$ is feasible, has root excess $1/(t-1)>0$, and has marked
packing value
\[
 \frac{\sum_j(K+|A_j|)}{t-1}
 \ge \frac{t(K+t-1)}{t-1}
 \ge K+m=\lfloor n^2/4\rfloor.                      \tag{LPC5}
\]
After multiplication by $t-1$, the final difference is
$K-(t-1)(m-t)\ge0$.  Conversely, a common carrier in every trace forces
$\sum_Aa_A\le1$ and therefore nonpositive root excess.  Thus, for $n\ge6$,
a pair-free root is affordable if and only if its nonpair traces have empty
total intersection.  A pair-free price failure is exactly a rooted common
carrier star.

Equality in the displayed construction forces
$K=(t-1)(m-t)$ and $A_j=W\setminus\{v_j\}$ for
$W=\{v_1,\ldots,v_t\}$.  Thus the canonical equality support is the balanced
complement-of-singletons block-star geometry.  Additional tight columns may
still make the best marked margin positive, so this is an equality statement
for the constructed packing rather than a complete game-equality theorem.

The canonical equality completion also has an exact-cone interpretation.
Put $p=m-t\ge1$, $U=V\setminus W$,
$C_j=\{r\}\cup(W\setminus\{v_j\})$, and $P_u=\{r,u\}$.  Then
\[
 \sum_j\one_{C_j}+(t-1)\sum_{u\in U}\one_{P_u}
 -[p(t-1)+1]\one_{\{r\}}=(t-1)\one_N.              \tag{CSF}
\]
This is a saturated indecomposable purely min-semi-balanced system for every
$t\ge2$ and $p\ge1$, hence an exact-cone facet by the
Studen\'y--Kratochv\'il criterion.  Indeed, the positive offroot incidence
matrix has diagonal blocks $J_t-I_t$ and $I_p$, so the displayed $n$ columns
have full rank.  If $E$ is another feasible exceptional set with common load
$\gamma$, put $q=|E\cap W|$ and $s=|E\cap U|$.  Its necessarily nonnegative
support coefficients are
\[
 a_j=\frac{\gamma+q-(t-1)\one_{\{v_j\in E\}}}{t-1},\quad
 b_u=\gamma+\one_{\{u\in E\}},
\]
\[
 \lambda_r=\one_{\{r\in E\}}-s-\frac q{t-1}
 -\frac{1+p(t-1)}{t-1}\gamma.                      \tag{CSX}
\]
For $\gamma<0$, these inequalities leave only $E=N$.  For $\gamma\ge0$,
they leave only $E=\varnothing$, the root singleton, one displayed $C_j$,
or one displayed $P_u$.  This is precisely indecomposability.  The condition
$p\ge1$ matters: the $p=0,t=3$ system is decomposable.  Thus the balanced
minimal empty-intersection packing selects a distinguished exact-cone facet,
not merely a nonminimal Farkas row.  We make no novelty claim for this facet
family before comparison with the published semi-balanced catalogues.

Combining the two-pair bound, the locked-pair theorem, and the pair-free
alternative completely classifies scalar affordability at every root.  In
the failure branch the remaining task is discrete: carriers chosen for the
unaffordable roots must be converted into the upper nongainer block and
gainer packet required by direct gluing.

Carriers have a useful intrinsic order.  Let $x=\Lmin(v)$, let $x_r>0$, and
suppose $c$ belongs to every $x$-tight coalition containing $r$.  Then
\[
 x_c\ge x_r.                                           \tag{CMO}
\]
Indeed, $e_c-e_r$ is nonnegative on every active coalition constraint, and a
sufficiently small positive movement preserves all slack constraints and
nonnegativity.  If $x_c<x_r$, the first changed entry of the increasing
rearrangement lies in the $x_c$ stratum and increases, while the compensating
decrease occurs only in the strictly higher $x_r$ stratum.  This contradicts
leximin optimality.  Consequently carrier arrows point weakly upward in the
Lmin order and every carrier cycle lies in one Lmin stratum.

This implication uses the full active tight menu; a carrier found only in one
selected staged support need not define a feasible tangent.  As finite
evidence, all 554 full-tight-menu unaffordable maximum-overlap roots in the
six-player archive have a full-menu carrier that is itself another
maximum-overlap root.  The narrower selected staged menus have 560
unaffordable roots and the same finite closure.  Thus the archived carrier
graph closes on the root block, but that closure is not yet a dimension-free
theorem.  Proving it, or finding its sharp failure, is the next selection
bridge.

The mirror tangent argument removes the need to assume that closure.  If
$z_c<z_r$ and no $z$-tight coalition contains $r$ while omitting $c$, then
$e_c-e_r$ is feasible at $z$ and strictly improves the decreasing
lexicographic objective, contradicting $z=\Lmax(v)$.

Let $R=\arg\max_i\min(x_i,z_i)$, assume its coordinates are positive, and
suppose every $r\in R$ is unaffordable on its full Lmin-tight menu.  Then
either
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item there is a full Lmin carrier arrow $r\to c$, with $r\in R$ and
$z_c<z_r$, and a $z$-tight separator $D$ with $r\in D$, $c\notin D$; or
\item there is a directed carrier cycle $H\subset R$, both $x$ and $z$ are
constant on $H$, every $x$-tight coalition meeting $H$ contains all of $H$,
and every member of $H$ has the same gainer/nongainer sign.
\end{enumerate}
Indeed, in the first case carrier monotonicity gives $x_c\ge x_r$; failure of
$c$ to maximize overlap therefore gives
$z_c<\min(x_r,z_r)\le z_r$, and the Lmax tangent argument supplies $D$.  If
every root has a carrier in $R$, choose one per root.  The resulting finite
functional digraph has a cycle; monotonicity forces its $x$ coordinates to
be equal, and the carrier implications force every tight coalition meeting
the cycle to contain it wholesale.  If $z$ is not constant on that cycle,
some carrier arrow descends in $z$ and gives the first branch.  Otherwise
$z$ is constant too; the common $x,z$ levels make the sign uniform.

This carrier-cycle/separator alternative is a dimension-free coupling of the
complete Lmin tight menu to an Lmax row.  It does not yet prove the overlap
bound, but both branches sharpen further.  If $D$ is $z$-tight, contains a
root $r$, and omits its Lmin carrier $c$, then
\[
 x(D)>z(D),\qquad z(N\setminus D)-x(N\setminus D)>0. \tag{SCP}
\]
Core feasibility gives the weak first inequality, and equality would make
$D$ an $x$-tight coalition separating the carrier pair.  Efficiency gives
the second inequality.  Thus complementation turns every separator into a
strict direct-comparison payment.  No dimension-free lower bound on its
magnitude is known, so this does not alone repair a deficient direct
cardinality base or a negative order residual.

There is also an exact contraction theorem for the cycle arm.  Call
$H$ a tight-face module at $y$ if every $y$-tight proper coalition contains
all or none of $H$.  A carrier cycle is a tight-face module at $x$, and
\[
 \{d:\operatorname{supp}d\subseteq H, d(H)=0\}
\]
lies in the two-sided lineality space of the active core tangent cone at
$x$.  If a $z$-tight coalition separates two cycle members, the same argument
as (SCP) supplies a strict complement payment, even though their $z$
coordinates are equal.  Otherwise $H$ is a tight-face module at both
selections.

In the latter case contract $H$ to a role of integer weight $h=|H|$, with
coalitions expanded by replacing that role by all of $H$ and role payoff
equal to the sum on $H$.  The quotient game is exact: projecting an original
core point gives a quotient core point, and projecting an exactness witness
for each expanded coalition attains its quotient worth.  Rank the contracted
role by its per-capita payoff, repeated $h$ times.  The projections of $x$
and $z$ are respectively weighted Lmin and weighted Lmax.  Otherwise a
better quotient point, distributed equally over $H$, would give a locally
feasible improving direction in the original core; concavity of lowest-rank
sums and convexity of highest-rank sums preserve the relevant lexicographic
improvement on a sufficiently short segment.  Total payoff and hybrid
overlap are unchanged by this weighted contraction.

The contracted role has singleton worth $v(H)$ and need not be
singleton-normalized.  Subtracting this endowment can change the two
lexicographic selections.  Nevertheless the weighted endowed formulation
has an exact singleton-normalized block expansion.  For role weights $h_a$,
replace role $a$ by a clone block $B_a$ and set
\[
 w^\uparrow(S)=w(\{a:B_a\subseteq S\}).              \tag{CBE}
\]
Assume $w(\{a\})=0$ for every unit-weight role, as holds for the contraction
above.  Then $w^\uparrow$ is singleton-normalized and exact.  Indeed block
summation maps its core onto the role core.  Given a role exactness witness
$q$ for the complete blocks of $S$, place the total $q_a$ of every partially
met block on a clone outside $S$; the resulting clone-core point attains
$w^\uparrow(S)$.  Block-permutation symmetry and uniqueness force both lex
selections to be constant on every block, where they are exactly the
weighted role selections.  Total payoff and overlap are preserved.

The correct induction parameter is the number of roles, not the atomic
weight.  Let ${\cal P}_r$ denote the target inequality for every nonnegative
exact weighted game on at most $r$ roles, with positive integer weights of
total $n\ge6$ and zero singleton worth on each unit-weight role.

\begin{proposition}[common-module induction]
\label{prop:common-module-induction}
Assume ${\cal P}_{r-1}$.  If an $r$-role instance has a nontrivial set $H$
whose per-capita coordinates are constant at each selection and which is a
tight-face module at both selections, then it satisfies the target
inequality.  Consequently a counterexample minimal in its number of roles
has no such common module.
\end{proposition}

Indeed contract $H$ to one role of weight $\sum_{i\in H}h_i$.  Exactness and
nonnegativity descend, all remaining unit-role singleton worths stay zero,
and the new role is permitted to have positive singleton worth because its
weight is at least two.  The quotient has at most $r-1$ roles and the same
atomic weight $n$, while contraction preserves weighted Lmin, weighted Lmax,
$T$, and $O$.  Hence ${\cal P}_{r-1}$ gives precisely the original target.
Theorems~\ref{thm:four-role-confinement} and
\ref{thm:atomic-four-role}, together with weighted coincidence through three
roles, establish the base ${\cal P}_4$.

Thus the contraction arm is closed in a strong-induction proof.  It does not
require a weighted affordability theorem after contraction: the entire
smaller-role assertion is the induction hypothesis.  What still needs a
weighted formulation is the selection step at the current role count.  In
particular, whenever that step supplies a carrier cycle, a role-minimal
counterexample must have an Lmax-tight coalition splitting it; otherwise the
cycle is a common module and the proposition applies.  Incidence-matrix
contraction is the selection-face inverse of clone expansion and supplies
the exact-cone interpretation of the discharged branch.

The induction base starts with complete coincidence through three roles.
For three roles every proper coalition is a singleton or a singleton
complement, so efficiency writes the role core as
\[
 K=\{q:q(R)=T, \ell_a\le q_a\le u_a\},\qquad
 \ell_a=w(\{a\}),\quad u_a=T-w(R\setminus\{a\}).     \tag{W3R1}
\]
The same form covers one and two roles.  For positive integer role weights
$h_a$, the unique clipped weighted equalizer is
\[
 q_a^*=\operatorname{clip}_{[\ell_a,u_a]}(h_a\lambda),\qquad
 \sum_aq_a^*=T.                                      \tag{W3R2}
\]
It Lorenz-dominates every feasible vector after repeating $q_a/h_a$ exactly
$h_a$ times.  Indeed, whenever two unblocked roles have unequal per-capita
payoffs, a sufficiently small transfer from the higher to the lower stays in
the box and is a strict Robin Hood transfer.  The only point admitting no
such transfer satisfies exactly (W3R2).  Hence weighted Lmin and weighted
Lmax coincide.  The two-role formula is
$q_1^*=\operatorname{clip}_{[\ell_1,u_1]}(h_1T/(h_1+h_2))$.
Canonical block expansion preserves this coincidence and gives overlap $T$.
An exact regression grid checks 170,244 feasible weighted boxes and 311,364
vertices through total atomic weight eight; the exchange argument, not that
grid, is the proof.  Four or more weighted roles remain open.

The same argument closes elementary four-role subcases.  If all role weights
are equal and all role singleton worths vanish, repetition preserves the
ordinary four-coordinate lex orders, so the companion singleton-normalized
four-player exact-partition theorem gives coincidence.  The role-singleton
hypothesis cannot be dropped: nonunit contracted roles may retain positive
endowments under canonical expansion.  If one role total is constant,
projecting it away leaves the
three-role core with coalition bounds
\[
 q(A)\ge\max\{w(A),w(A\cup\{r\})-q_r\};
\]
the weighted three-role equalizer remains Lorenz-greatest after adjoining the
same fixed block to every repeated vector.  Finally, if all three
complementary pair-sum slabs are redundant, the four-role core is a box
intersected with efficiency, and the clipped weighted water-filling proof
applies without change.  The same conclusion holds with one nonredundant
complementary pair slab: the coordinate and pair intervals form a laminar
constraint tree.  Progressive weighted water filling from below and from
above freezes the same nested nodes in reverse order, producing the unique
point with no feasible Robin Hood transfer; the first-differing partial-sum
exchange then makes it Lorenz-greatest.  Hence any hard four-role quotient
has four nonconstant role totals and at least two crossing nonredundant pair
slabs.  Equal weights are harmless only under the stronger role-level
singleton normalization.  This is a structural reduction, not yet an
overlap estimate.

The crossing requirement can be imposed at each selector.  If a canonical
selector $q$ has at most one active complementary pair-sum slab, retain
efficiency, the coordinate intervals, and only the pair slabs active at
$q$.  This is the laminar relaxation above and has a Lorenz-greatest weighted
water-filling point $g$.  The selector $q$ is Lorenz-undominated.  If
$g\ne q$, a short segment from $q$ toward $g$ strictly Lorenz-dominates
$q$ and remains feasible for every dropped pair inequality, since those
inequalities are strict at $q$.  This is impossible.  Thus $q=g$ and it
Lorenz-dominates the original core, forcing the two selectors to coincide.
Consequently, when the selectors differ, each is on endpoints of at least
two independent crossing pair slabs.  Since there are only three pair
partitions, their active edge systems share a partition.  The unresolved
case is now a transition between different two-flat edges (including an
opposite endpoint on a shared partition), not a locally laminar selector.

This residue has a useful exact coordinate form.  Set

\[
 y_0=q_0+q_1,\qquad y_1=q_0+q_2,\qquad y_2=q_0+q_3.             \tag{FEC1}
\]

The complementary pair-sum slabs become independent intervals in the three
$y$-coordinates, and efficiency gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
2q_0&=y_0+y_1+y_2-T, & 2q_1&=T+y_0-y_1-y_2,\\
2q_2&=T-y_0+y_1-y_2, & 2q_3&=T-y_0-y_1+y_2.
\end{aligned}                                           \tag{FEC2}
\]

Hence the four coordinate intervals are four diagonal slabs cutting the
pair-slab box, and the remaining cases move between different clipped box
edges.  If $\delta_j=y_j(x)-y_j(z)$, the inverse Hadamard transform in
(FEC2) yields

\[
 \lVert x-z\rVert_1
 =\max\left\{\sum_{j=0}^2|\delta_j|,
              2\max_j|\delta_j|\right\}.                    \tag{FEC3}
\]

Indeed, after ordering the absolute values as $a\ge b\ge c$, the four
absolute Hadamard forms sum to $4a$ when $a\ge b+c$ and to
$2(a+b+c)$ otherwise.  Since $O=T-\lVert x-z\rVert_1/2$, the outstanding
four-role theorem is now an atomic-weight-aware bound on this explicit
edge-to-edge displacement.  Equation (FEC3) is only an exact reformulation;
it does not itself close that bound.

Up to relabelling pair coordinates, reversing their interval orientations,
and interchanging the selectors, the first edge may be fixed at
$y_0=L_0,y_1=L_1$.  Besides the closed common-edge case, the second edge is
one of
\[
 (y_0=L_0,y_1=U_1),\quad (y_0=L_0,y_2=L_2),\quad
 (y_0=U_0,y_1=U_1),\quad (y_0=U_0,y_2=L_2).          \tag{FEC4}
\]
The first two rows share one endpoint equality, lie in a common two-
dimensional pair face, and satisfy
$\lVert x-z\rVert_1=2\max\{|\delta_1|,|\delta_2|\}$.
The last two are the genuinely face-changing opposite-endpoint cases.
Additional active endpoints only specialize these four types or return to
the common-two-flat theorem.  This exhausts the minimal active-edge
incidences but not the weighted payoff-stratum patterns within them.

The pure first row of (FEC4) is impossible when the third pair slab is
strict at both selectors.  On the common face $y_0=L_0$, drop the
$y_1,y_2$ slabs and call the Lorenz-greatest point of the resulting laminar
fixed-pair relaxation $g$.  At a selector $x$ on $y_1=L_1$, local Lorenz
undominance forces $y_1(g)<L_1$: otherwise a short segment toward $g$ stays
inside the $y_1$ interval and inside the $y_2$ slab, which is strict at
$x$.  But at a selector $z$ on $y_1=U_1$, the inequality
$y_1(g)<L_1<U_1$ makes the same short segment feasible and strictly
Lorenz-improving, a contradiction.  Thus a surviving common-face transition
is the hinged second row or has a third pair endpoint active, specializing a
three-flat boundary case.

In fact a weighted dispersion argument closes the whole common-face residue.
Fix a common endpoint, relabel its two sides as ${0,1}$ and ${2,3}$,
and write

\[
 q_0=s,\quad q_1=W-s,\quad q_2=t,\quad q_3=T-W-t.       \tag{FEC5}
\]

For positive role weights $h_i$, the within-pair weighted-equality splits are

\[
 s_*=\frac{h_0W}{h_0+h_1},\qquad
 t_*=\frac{h_2(T-W)}{h_2+h_3}.                           \tag{FEC6}
\]

Put $d_1=|s-s_*|$ and $d_2=|t-t_*|$.  Moving either split toward its
equality point is a Pigou--Dalton transfer between the corresponding clone
blocks.  Hence weakly decreasing both dispersions, one strictly, strictly
Lorenz-improves the full repeated payoff vector.

\begin{theorem}[weighted common-face rigidity]\label{thm:common-face-rigidity}
Let $K$ be a compact four-role core with positive integer role weights.  If two
Lorenz-undominated points of $K$ lie on the same endpoint of one
complementary pair-sum slab, then either they coincide or they lie on the
same endpoint of a second independent complementary pair-sum slab.
Consequently distinct weighted Lmin and weighted Lmax cannot form either
common-face row of (FEC4).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Work in the displayed common face.  Two undominated points lie in a common
closed orthant about $(s_*,t_*)$.  Otherwise their segment crosses, say,
$s=s_*$.  At the crossing the first dispersion is zero and convexity of
absolute value makes the second no larger than the larger endpoint
dispersion.  The crossing therefore Lorenz-dominates whichever endpoint has
that larger second dispersion.

In a fixed orthant write

\[
 s=s_*+\epsilon_1d_1,\qquad t=t_*+\epsilon_2d_2,
 \qquad d_1,d_2\ge0.                                    \tag{FEC7}
\]

The singleton and complementary-singleton bounds give independent intervals
for $d_1,d_2$.  The two remaining pair coordinates have normals

\[
 (\epsilon_1,\epsilon_2),\qquad
 (\epsilon_1,-\epsilon_2).                              \tag{FEC8}
\]

Exactly one normal has equal signs.  Two distinct coordinatewise-minimal
points are incomparable, so the lower convex boundary between them has
negative slope.  Coordinate boundaries are vertical or horizontal, and a
boundary of the opposite-sign strip has positive slope; each contains at
most one coordinatewise-minimal point.  The only available negative-slope
boundary is the unique inward endpoint of the equal-sign pair strip, where
$d_1+d_2$ is fixed.  All such edges are parallel, so convexity makes them one
collinear boundary rather than separate efficient pieces.  Any two distinct
Lorenz-undominated points share that second pair endpoint.
\end{proof}

Both canonical selections are Lorenz-undominated.  The theorem therefore
sends every common-one-endpoint incidence to
Theorem~\ref{thm:common-two-atomic}.  Only the opposite-endpoint,
face-changing third and fourth rows of (FEC4) remain in the four-role
quotient problem.

The opposite-endpoint residue disappears after connecting the two selectors
inside the Lorenz frontier.  Repeat $q_i/h_i$ exactly $h_i$ times and let
$\Lambda_k(q)$ be the sum of the $k$ smallest repeated coordinates,
$1\le k<n$.  Each $\Lambda_k$ is continuous, concave, and polyhedral: it is
the minimum of the sums over all $k$ clone coordinates.

\begin{lemma}[connected positive-Lorenz continuation]
Any two uniquely specified lexicographic extremes of
$(\Lambda_1,\ldots,\Lambda_{n-1})$ lie in one connected set of
Lorenz-undominated core points.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The lower-comprehensive Lorenz image
\[
 \{u:\exists q\in K,\ u_k\le\Lambda_k(q)\ (1\le k<n)\}
\]
is a convex polyhedron, because it is the projection of the common hypograph of
the concave polyhedral $\Lambda_k$.  Its finite normal fan gives strictly
positive hierarchical vectors $a^x,a^z$ for which the two lexicographic
extremes uniquely maximize

\[
 F_a(q)=\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}a_k\Lambda_k(q).                \tag{FEC9}
\]

Put $a(t)=(1-t)a^x+ta^z$ and
$A(t)=\arg\max_KF_{a(t)}$.  Every $A(t)$ is nonempty, compact, and convex,
because $F_{a(t)}$ is concave.  Its graph is compact.  If the graph were the
disjoint union of two nonempty closed sets, their projections would be closed
sets covering $[0,1]$, so they would meet at some $t$.  They would then
disconnect the convex fiber $A(t)$, a contradiction.  Thus the graph and its
projection to $K$ are connected.  Every projected point is
Lorenz-undominated because every coefficient of $a(t)$ is positive.
Finally, two distinct role allocations cannot be undominated with the same
full Lorenz vector: their repeated payoff vectors are permutations, and
their midpoint is strictly more equal unless they agree.  Thus the canonical
role point at either endpoint is unique.
\end{proof}

The earlier two-active-crossing argument applies to every
Lorenz-undominated point.  Unless the core has a Lorenz-greatest point (when
the selectors coincide), every point in the connected continuation lies on
endpoints of at least two pair slabs, hence in the one-skeleton of the
three-dimensional pair-slab box.

\begin{theorem}[weighted four-role edge confinement]
\label{thm:four-role-confinement}
In every compact four-role core with positive integer role weights, weighted
Lmin and weighted Lmax either coincide or share endpoints of two independent
complementary pair-sum slabs.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
If a pair slab has zero width, its endpoint is common to every point and
Theorem~\ref{thm:common-face-rigidity} supplies the second endpoint.  Otherwise
use the connected continuation in the ordinary box one-skeleton.  A connected
subset meeting interiors of two different box edges must meet interiors of
two edges incident at some vertex.  Points on those incident edges share
exactly one endpoint equality, contradicting
Theorem~\ref{thm:common-face-rigidity}.  Hence the continuation lies in one
clipped box edge, and its endpoints share the two pair equalities defining
that edge.
\end{proof}

Thus (FEC4) has no surviving edge-transition residue.  The quantitative
atomic theorem below still uses the common-two cost calculation and its
finite exact audit; Theorem~\ref{thm:four-role-confinement} is the structural
bridge making that branch exhaustive.

This boundary is sharp.  On four roles of weights $(3,1,1,1)$, the exact
role game with worths in binary coalition order
\[
 (0,11,0,14,0,14,0,16,0,12,0,15,0,15,0,18)         \tag{W4O1}
\]
has weighted role-total selections
\[
 q^{\min}=(12,2,2,2),\qquad q^{\max}=(11,3,3,1).     \tag{W4O2}
\]
Its canonical six-clone expansion has
\[
 x=(4,4,4,2,2,2),\qquad
 z=(11/3,11/3,11/3,3,3,1),\qquad O/T=8/9.            \tag{W4O3}
\]
Thus weighted coincidence fails already at four roles, although this control
lies far above the six-player overlap target $2/3$.  Exact LP witnesses attain
all sixteen role worths, their canonical placements attain all 64 clone
worths, and an independent exact simplex recomputes both displayed traces.

There is a dimension-free four-role family behind this boundary.  For
$m\ge2$, use role weights $(m,1,1,1)$ and define the game as the lower envelope
of
\[
 (2m+4,0,0,0), (2m,2,2,0), (2m+2,0,2,0),
 (2m+2,2,0,0), (2m+3,0,0,1).                       \tag{W4F1}
\]
The five common-total generators make the role game exact.  Its weighted
selections are
\[
 q^{\min}=(2m+1,1,1,1),\qquad q^{\max}=(2m,2,2,0).  \tag{W4F2}
\]
Indeed the complement of the first three roles caps role $3$ at one.  On the
weighted-Lmin floor-one face, the two pair bounds
$q_0+q_1,q_0+q_2\ge2m+2$ and efficiency force
$q_0=2m+1$, $q_1=q_2=q_3=1$.  For weighted Lmax, the singleton bound
$q_0\ge2m$ makes the per-capita ceiling at least two.  At ceiling two it pins
$q_0=2m$; the same pair bounds and efficiency force $(q_1,q_2,q_3)=(2,2,0)$.
Canonical expansion to $n=m+3$ players gives
\[
 \frac OT=\frac{m+1}{m+2},\qquad
 \frac{\lVert x-z\rVert_1}{T}=\frac{2}{m+2}.         \tag{W4F3}
\]
This proves that four-role weighted coincidence fails in every atomic
dimension, but the family tends toward full overlap and is not extremal for
the main conjecture.  The symbolic identities are audited through $m=40$.

The stronger proposed bridge ``every normalized exact weighted four-role
game has $O/T\ge3/4$'' is false.  For weights $(m,1,1,1)$, $m\ge5$, take the
lower envelope of
\[
 (0,5,3,4),(1,3,3,5),(2,4,3,3),(5,0,7,0),(0,6,0,6).             \tag{W4N1}
\]
Its binary-order worth table is
\[
 (0,0,0,4,0,0,6,6,0,4,0,5,6,6,7,12),                          \tag{W4N2}
\]
so all role singleton worths vanish, and attainment by the displayed
common-total generators proves exactness.  Direct staged elimination gives
\[
 q^{\min}=(5,1,5,1),\qquad q^{\max}=(3,3,3,3),\qquad O/T=2/3.   \tag{W4N3}
\]
For Lmin, complementing $w(123)=7$ gives $q_0\le5$, so the first floor is at
most $5/m$, attained by the displayed candidate.  On the face $q_0=5$,
efficiency and $q_1+q_2,q_2+q_3\ge6$ give $q_3,q_1\le1$; the next floor
therefore pins $(q_1,q_2,q_3)=(1,5,1)$.  At $m=5$ the equal levels merge.
For Lmax, $q_1+q_2\ge6$ forces ceiling at least three.  At ceiling three it
pins $q_1=q_2=3$; then $q_2+q_3\ge6$ pins $q_3=3$, and efficiency gives
$q_0=3$.
An independently encoded exact staged LP audits every pinning step.
Canonical expansion gives singleton-normalized exact examples for every
$n=m+3\ge8$.  They remain above the conjectured atomic target, but prove that
four-role contraction cannot be closed by an atomic-size-independent
$3/4$ estimate.  A valid quotient theorem must retain atomic weight,
largest-block weight, or labelled carrier information.  The $m=8$ verifier
also constructs explicit attainment witnesses for all $2^{11}$ clone
coalitions.

The common crossing face of this family has a complete atomic-weight-aware
explanation.  More generally, suppose both weighted selectors lie on
\[
 q_0+q_1=W,\qquad q_0+q_3=W,\qquad T=2W.                       \tag{BWS1}
\]
The face is the interval
\[
 q(t)=(t,W-t,t,W-t),\qquad \ell\le t\le u.                    \tag{BWS2}
\]
Put $P=\max(h_0,h_2)$, $p=\min(h_0,h_2)$ and similarly
$Q=\max(h_1,h_3)$, $q=\min(h_1,h_3)$.  Weighted water filling from below
and above gives, before common-interval clipping,
\[
 t_{\min}=\frac{WP}{P+Q},\qquad t_{\max}=\frac{Wp}{p+q}.       \tag{BWS3}
\]
Clipping cannot increase their distance, and
$O/T=1-|t_{\min}-t_{\max}|/W$.  Orienting the difference gives
\[
 \frac OT\ge\frac1{P+Q}+\frac1{p+q}
 =\frac{n}{(P+Q)(p+q)}
 \ge\frac{4n}{n^2-(n\bmod2)}.                                 \tag{BWS4}
\]
The last step is the sharp integer product bound for two positive summands
of total $n$.  Thus the full conjectured quotient inequality holds on every
balanced common seesaw face.  Equality in the harmonic/product proof occurs
only at atomic weights four and five, so this branch has strict target slack
for $n\ge6$.  An exact audit covers all 91,390 positive weight vectors through
$n=40$ and 4,112,550 rational clipping intervals; the displayed argument is
the proof.

Each flat in (BWS1) is a tight pair-coalition face.  It is a complementary
exact-partition equality only in the special case where the opposite slab
endpoints coincide.  Thus this bound is selection-order arithmetic on the
intersection of two core faces, not a new full exact-game-cone facet.  Its
strict margin for $n\ge6$ also excludes this branch from the six- and
seven-player target-equality orientations, which must remain in the labelled
rooted or mixed facet arms.

The same common-two-flat geometry admits a useful endowed reduction even
without the balance assumption.  Parameterize its core interval by
\[
 q(t)=(e_0+t,e_1+s-t,e_2+t,e_3+s-t),\qquad 0\le t\le s,
 \quad T=E+2s,\quad E=\sum_i e_i.                         \tag{EWS1}
\]
The theorem below assumes $e_i\ge0$ and the stronger face-normalization
condition $e_i=0$ whenever the role multiplicity $h_i$ is one.  Zero
singleton worth of a unit role does not by itself imply this condition: its
minimum on this particular line may exceed its minimum on the full core.
For $r\in\{0,2\}$ and
$f\in\{1,3\}$ put
\[
 \kappa_{rf}=\frac{h_r(e_f+s)-h_f e_r}{h_r+h_f}.          \tag{EWS2}
\]
An envelope comparison of the two rising and two falling per-capita lines
gives
\[
 t_{\min}=\operatorname{clip}_{[0,s]}\max_r\min_f\kappa_{rf},
 \qquad
 t_{\max}=\operatorname{clip}_{[0,s]}\max_f\min_r\kappa_{rf}. \tag{EWS3}
\]
The crossing geometry supplies an order restriction not valid for a generic
$2\times2$ matrix.  A positive gap cannot separate the two diagonals: at a
parameter between them the four
per-capita lines would form a strict cycle
$R_0>F_1>R_2>F_3>R_0$.  Hence the low entries share a row or column.  After
this exclusion, the two max--min values in (EWS3) are exactly the two middle
clipped crossings.  Writing them as
$\kappa_2\le\kappa_3$ gives
\[
 \{t_{\min},t_{\max}\}=\{\kappa_2,\kappa_3\},\qquad
 \frac OT=1-\frac{2(\kappa_3-\kappa_2)}{T}.                \tag{EWS4}
\]
The remaining adjacent middle-gap inequality admits a closed atomic proof.
After
row/column relabelling and, for a column, reversing the parameter, let $p$ be
the low-row weight, $r$ the other row weight, and $q\le b$ the column
weights.

\begin{theorem}[face-normalized endowed common-two-flat bound]\label{thm:endowed-seesaw}
Normalize a positive adjacent middle gap to one.  The minimum possible total,
over all nonnegative endowments with $e_i=0$ on every unit-weight role, is
\[
 S(p,r;q,b)=
 \begin{cases}
  2+p/q+(p+q+b)/r,&p>1,\\
  +\infty,&p=r=1,\\
  2(2r+b+1)/(r-1),&p=1<r,\ q=1,\\
  (q+1)(2r+q+b)/[q(r-1)],&p=1<r,\ q>1.
 \end{cases}                                             \tag{EWS5}
\]
Consequently the atomic bound below holds when the face is normalized in this
way and its total atomic weight is $n\ge6$:
\[
 \frac OT\ge \frac{4n}{n^2-(n\bmod2)}.                   \tag{EWS6}
\]
Equality is possible only at $n=6$ and
$(p,r,q,b)=(2,2,1,1)$, up to the displayed symmetries.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let $\ell,\ell+1$ be the scaled middle crossings and set
$z_f=e_f+s$ for $f\in\{q,b\}$.  Clipping gives $\ell\ge0$ and
$s\ge\ell+1$.  The crossing inequalities and total are
\[
 pz_f-fe_p\le(p+f)\ell,\qquad
 rz_f-fe_r\ge(r+f)(\ell+1),\qquad
 T=e_p+e_r+z_q+z_b.                                    \tag{EWS7}
\]
If $p>1$, add both high-row inequalities and substitute the low-row
$q$-inequality.  This gives exactly
\[
 T\ge2+\frac pq+\frac{p+q+b}{r}
        +\frac{n}{r}(\ell+e_r).                         \tag{EWS8}
\]
Equality is attained at $\ell=e_r=0$ by
$z_f=(r+f)/r$, $e_p=p(r+q)/(qr)$, $s=z_q$; the remaining
endowment is $(b-q)/r$ on role $b$, which is nonunit whenever positive.

If $p=1$, then $e_p=0$.  For $r=1$ the two rows coincide.  Otherwise
(EWS7) implies the sharper
$\ell\ge[r+q+qe_r]/[q(r-1)]$.  Summing the high inequalities and substituting
this bound gives the last line of (EWS5), plus a nonnegative multiple of
$e_r$, when $q>1$.
When $q=1$, also $e_q=0$ and $z_q=s$; the low and high $q$ inequalities give
$\ell\ge(r+1+e_r)/(r-1)$.  Adding the two high lower bounds to the total and
substituting this inequality yields the third line plus a nonnegative
multiple of $e_r$.  Equality in both cases occurs at $e_r=0$; the remaining
explicit choices are recorded in the exact verifier.

Put $F=\lfloor n^2/4\rfloor$ and
$H_n=2/(1-n/F)=2+2n/(F-n)$.  Since (EWS4) gives
$O/T=1-2d/T$, it remains to prove $S\ge H_n$.  For $p>1$, set
$k=p+q+b=n-r$.  Since $q\le b$ and $p\ge2$, Engel's inequality gives
\[
 S-2\ge\frac4{k-2}+\frac{k}{n-k}
 \ge\frac{(2+\sqrt{k})^2}{n-2}\ge\frac{16}{n-2}
 \ge\frac{2n}{F-n}.                                    \tag{EWS9}
\]
The last comparison is $16/(n-2)\ge8/(n-4)$ for even $n$; for odd
$n\ge7$ it is equivalent to $n^2-6n-2\ge0$.  The $p=q=1$ line gives
$S-2=2n/(r-1)\ge2n/(n-4)$.  Finally, if $p=1,q>1$, put
$d=n-2q-2\ge r-1$.  Dropping positive terms and applying Engel again gives
\[
 S-2\ge\frac2q+\frac{2(q+1)}d
 \ge\frac2q+\frac6d
 \ge\frac{(\sqrt2+\sqrt6)^2}{q+d}
 >\frac{14}{n-4}>\frac{2n}{F-n}.                       \tag{EWS10}
\]
Equality in the chain is therefore possible only in the first case at

 \sum_Su_S|P_S|&=\delta |A|+\sum_{i\in A}q_i,       \tag{RCA2}\\
 \sum_{i\ne r}q_i(x_i-z_i)&\ge
             \delta(x_r-z_r).                       \tag{RCA3}
\end{align}
Indeed, player $i\in A$ is absent with packing mass
$(1+\delta)-(1-q_i)=\delta+q_i$.  Also
$z(S)-x(S)\ge0$ on every supported $x$-tight coalition; summing and using
$x(N)=z(N)$ proves (RCA3).  Thus these identities require no facet
classification.

There is a fixed-cardinality reserve consequence.  Suppose all supported
rows contain the selected nongainer carrier, are proper on the gainer side,
and have $|P_S|=p$.  Proper $x$-tightness supplies the comparison premise,
and with $c_p=gn/[(n-p)(p+1)]$,
\[
 \sum_Su_S\mathcal R_{\tau_n}(P_S)
 =(1+\delta)((1-\tau_n)T-D)
  -c_p\left(\delta z(A)+\sum_{i\in A}q_i z_i\right). \tag{RCA4}
\]
Nonnegativity of the right side therefore selects a target-reaching row.
This is a literal coupling of rooted deficit mass to excluded-block reserve,
but it is not yet the universal handover: a marked price optimizer need not
be supported on proper rows of one complement cardinality, and a negative
average does not imply an affordable facet.

The cost of repairing an improper packing row can nevertheless be separated
exactly into its two currencies.  To avoid confusing tight-pair labels with
payoff gainers, put $\Gamma_r=G$ and $B_r=B$ in the notation of (LPC), and write
$\pi_r(S)=w(S\setminus\{r\})$ for the labelled-price coefficient of a
rooted row.  For any two rooted rows $S,C$,
\[
 \pi_r(S)-\pi_r(C)
 =K\bigl(|(C\setminus\{r\})\cap\Gamma_r|-|(S\setminus\{r\})\cap\Gamma_r|\bigr)
  +|(S\setminus\{r\})\cap B_r|-|(C\setminus\{r\})\cap B_r|. \tag{PXC1}
\]
This is the exact labelled-price charge; it depends on tight rooted-pair
labels, not merely on payoff strata.  On the reserve side, let
$P^+=P\mathbin{\dot\cup}\{i\}$, $p=|P|\le n-2$, and keep the selected gainer and
nongainer strata fixed.  Direct subtraction in (EDQ5) gives
\[
 \mathcal R_{\tau_n}(P^+)-\mathcal R_{\tau_n}(P)
 =\frac{gn\{(n-2p-2)z(P)-(n-p)(p+1)z_i\}}
 {(n-p)(p+1)(n-p-1)(p+2)}.                         \tag{PXC2}
\]
Deleting payoff gainers from a coalition does not change its excluded
nongainer block and hence costs no reserve; deleting an additional
nongainer changes reserve by exactly (PXC2).  Thus (PXC1)--(PXC2) reduce
properization to an explicit price-versus-reserve charge, but do not prove
that a charged replacement row is present in the tight support.

In particular, on the pair-free branch $\Gamma_r=\varnothing$ and
$\pi_r(S)=K+|S\setminus\{r\}|$.  Hence every deletion has labelled-price
charge exactly equal to its incidence size.  Suppose a co-singleton tight
row $S=N\setminus\{j\}$ omits a nongainer $j$, has
$\mathcal R_{\tau_n}(\{j\})\ge0$, and admits a carrier-preserving proper
tight subset $C$.  If $S\setminus C$ contains only payoff gainers, then $C$
has the same reserve and closes the direct target.  If it additionally
contains exactly one nongainer $i$, it closes if and only if the right side
of (PXC2), added to $\mathcal R_{\tau_n}(\{j\})$, is nonnegative.  At
$n=6,p=1$ the increment is
\[
 \frac{g}{10}\bigl(z_j-5z_i\bigr).                 \tag{PXC3}
\]
This conditional properization lemma is dimension-free before the final
specialization.  Its hypotheses make explicit the two facts still missing
from a universal theorem: selection of the tight subset and payment of any
additional omitted nongainer.

Exactness removes the apparent crossing obstruction completely.  More
generally, let $S,C$ be tight at one core point $x$ and suppose $S\cup C=N$.
Put $I=S\cap C$.  Core feasibility gives
$v(S)+v(C)=v(N)+x(I)\ge v(N)+v(I)$.  By exactness, however, there is a core
point $y$ with $y(I)=v(I)$, and then
$v(S)+v(C)\le y(S)+y(C)=v(N)+v(I)$.  Hence equality holds throughout:
\[
 v(S)+v(C)=v(N)+v(I),\qquad x(I)=v(I),\qquad
 z(I)-x(I)=[z(S)-x(S)]+[z(C)-x(C)]\ge0.             \tag{PXC4}
\]
Thus tight sets covering the grand coalition intersect tightly in an exact
game.  In particular, intersecting any proper carrier row with a tight
co-singleton preserves tightness, the carrier, and properness, and
automatically supplies the comparison premise.  This elementary exact-game
uncrossing fact is stated within the standard exact-cone framework
\citep{CsokaHeringsKoczy2011,StudenyKratochvil2022}; no novelty claim is made
for it.  After this reduction the only remaining charge is the telescoped
target-reserve change in (PXC2), not a comparison-slack defect.

That reserve has a useful ordered-cut lower certificate.  Let $B\subseteq H$
be a retained block of $b$ nongainers with $z_i=M$ for $i\in B$, put
$K=H\setminus B$, suppose $z_i\le M^-\le M$ for every $i\in P$, and put
$p=|P|$, $g=|G|$, and
\[
 c_p=\frac{gn}{(n-p)(p+1)}.
\]
Since $D=T-z(H)-z(P)-x(G)$, the target reserve admits the exact rewrite
\[
 \mathcal R_\tau(P)
 =-\tau T+x(G)+z(H)+(1-c_p)z(P).                    \tag{PXC5}
\]
The retained block gives $z(H)=bM+z(K)$.  If $c_p\le1$, the final term is
nonnegative; if $c_p>1$, then $z(P)\le pM^-$.  Hence
\[
 \mathcal R_\tau(P)
 \ge bM+z(K)+x(G)-\tau T-p(c_p-1)_+M^-.            \tag{PXC6}
\]
If $M^-$ bounds every nongainer outside $B$, dropping $z(K)\ge0$ gives the
root-block-only corollary
\[
 \mathcal R_\tau(P)
 \ge bM+x(G)-\tau T-p(c_p-1)_+M^-.                 \tag{PXC7}
\]
Relative to the still coarser substitution $M^-=M$, the next Lmax stratum
supplies the exact additional credit
\[
 p(c_p-1)_+(M-M^-).                                \tag{PXC8}
\]
This lemma is dimension-free algebra, but nonnegativity of its right-hand
side is a selection obligation, not a theorem for every carrier row.  The
importance of (PXC6)--(PXC8) is that they retain both sides of the ordered
cut: the additional high-$z$ nongainers kept in $K$ and the low-$z$ price of
the excluded block $P$.  This is the same colour of information exposed by
the sparse Lmax pinning packet.

The complementary Lmin-side certificate comes from the comparison payment
itself.  Let $I$ be the selected tight row, let
$A=G\setminus I$ be its omitted gainers, put
$\ell(P)=x(P)-z(P)$ and $\Delta(A)=z(A)-x(A)$.  Total balance gives
\[
 \sigma_I:=z(I)-x(I)=\ell(P)-\Delta(A)\ge0.        \tag{PXC9}
\]
Substituting $z(P)=x(P)-\ell(P)$ in the target reserve therefore yields
\[
 \mathcal R_\tau(P)
 =(1-\tau)T-D-c_p x(P)+c_p\Delta(A)+c_p\sigma_I,  \tag{PXC10}
\]
and in particular
\[
 \mathcal R_\tau(P)
 \ge (1-\tau)T-D-c_p x(P)+c_p\Delta(A).            \tag{PXC11}
\]
Thus the same row has two independently useful lower certificates: (PXC6)
prices its Lmax ordered cut, while (PXC11) prices the Lmin mass of its
excluded block against the gain deliberately omitted by properness.  More
importantly, (PXC10) says that the entire difference between the latter
certificate and the true reserve is the normalized Lmax comparison payment;
there is no third residual currency.  These
are not independent proof systems---both are algebraic consequences of the
same selected tight row---but they expose the two currencies that a genuine
Lmin/Lmax gluing theorem must couple.

Their exact coupling is a minimum-residual identity.  Take
$M^-=\max_{i\in P}z_i$ in (PXC6), and define
\[
 E_I^x=c_p\sigma_I,\qquad
 E_I^z=
 \begin{cases}
 (1-c_p)z(P),&c_p\le1,\\
 (c_p-1)[pM^--z(P)],&c_p\ge1.
 \end{cases}
\]
Then (PXC5), (PXC6), and (PXC10) give, exactly,
\[
 \max\{\mathrm{PXC6}(I),\mathrm{PXC11}(I)\}
 =\mathcal R_\tau(P)-\min\{E_I^z,E_I^x\}.          \tag{PXC12}
\]
Both errors are nonnegative.  Consequently the selected-row max-certificate
is equivalent to the single hybrid inequality
$\mathcal R_\tau(P)\ge\min\{E_I^z,E_I^x\}$.  This is stronger than mere
nonnegativity of the reserve and is the precise load-bearing statement for a
minimum-deletion exchange proof.  It also prevents a proof from silently
combining two error terms on a fictitious row; the outer existence
quantifier is handled separately below.

For the corrected disjunction, however, the two optimizations themselves do
decouple.  Put $K_\tau=(1-\tau)T-D=O-\tau T$ and define the two row prices
\[
 Z_I=z(P)+(c_p-1)_+pM^-,\qquad
 X_I=c_p[z(P)+\sigma_I]=c_p[x(P)-\Delta(A)].
\]
Direct substitution gives
\[
 \mathrm{PXC6}(I)=K_\tau-Z_I,\qquad
 \mathrm{PXC11}(I)=K_\tau-X_I.                    \tag{PXC13}
\]
Consequently, for any nonempty admissible row family $\mathcal F$, if
$Z_*:=\min_{I\in\mathcal F}Z_I$ and
$X_*:=\min_{I\in\mathcal F}X_I$, then
\[
 \max_{I\in\mathcal F}\max\{\mathrm{PXC6}(I),\mathrm{PXC11}(I)\}
 =K_\tau-\min\{Z_*,X_*\}.                          \tag{PXC14}
\]
Thus a row closes precisely when the cheaper of the independently optimized
Lmax cut price and Lmin omission price is at most the global overlap surplus.
One does not add certificates from different rows: a minimizer of the cheaper
price supplies the actual selected row.  This turns the missing gluing step
into the scalar hybrid-price inequality
$\min\{Z_*,X_*\}\le K_\tau$ and gives a direct interface to the two LP
duals.

At fixed deletion cardinality these two prices are ordinary maximum-weight
row problems.  Let $L=N\setminus G$, let $\mathcal F_p$ be the
carrier-containing proper $x$-tight rows with $|L\setminus I|=p$, and put
$m_I=\max_{i\in L\setminus I}z_i$.  Define
\[
 S_z(I)=z(I\cap L)-(c_p-1)_+pm_I,\qquad
 S_x(I)=x(I\cap L)-\Delta(I\cap G).
\]
Then direct complementation gives
\[
 Z_*=z(L)-\max_{I\in\mathcal F_p}S_z(I),\qquad
 X_*=c_p\left[x(L)-D-max_{I\in\mathcal F_p}S_x(I)\right]. \tag{PXC15}
\]
For $c_p\le1$ the Lmax score is simply retained nongainer $z$-mass.  For
$c_p>1$, stratifying by the highest excluded Lmax level makes the additional
term constant on each stratum, so (PXC15) is still a finite sequence of
linear maximum-weight row problems.  This proves an exact algorithm on an
explicit tight menu and exposes two support-function duals.  Their extreme
inequalities are relative selection-cell facets; promotion to facets of the
full exact-game cone still requires the semi-balanced indecomposability test.
Scanning every nonempty cardinality class gives
\[
 Z^{\rm all}_*=\min_p Z_{*,p},\qquad
 X^{\rm all}_*=\min_p X_{*,p}.                     \tag{PXC16}
\]
By (PXC14), some admissible carrier row has a nonnegative two-colour
max-certificate if and only if
$\min\{Z^{\rm all}_*,X^{\rm all}_*\}\le K_\tau$.  Minimum deletion is a
particularly rigid finite selector, but it is only a sufficient subfamily;
the dimension-free proof should use the full scan and may close at a larger
deletion size.

There is a dimension-free equality consequence.  If $K_\tau=0$, then every
$Z_I,X_I$ is nonnegative, and
\[
 \max\{\mathrm{PXC6}(I),\mathrm{PXC11}(I)\}\ge0
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad z(P)=0.             \tag{PXC17}
\]
Indeed either closing price must vanish, hence $z(P)=0$.  Conversely,
nonnegativity of $z$ makes $z(P)=0$ imply $M^-=0$, so $Z_I=0$ and PXC6 is
exactly zero.  Thus equality selection on the carrier-direct arm reduces to
excluding only zero-Lmax nongainers; no gainer-label enumeration remains.
Combined with (CCC), this recovers the existing unique six-player
carrier-direct equality parameters, while leaving the rooted seven-player
equality arm separate.

There is an exact exchange law along every co-singleton-generated deletion
chain.  Let $I$ be an admissible row with excluded nongainer block $P$ of
size $p$, let $j\in (I\cap L)$ lie outside the retained carrier, and suppose
$N\setminus\{j\}$ is $x$-tight.  Since
$I\cup(N\setminus\{j\})=N$, (PXC4) makes
$J=I\setminus\{j\}$ tight; it remains admissible, has excluded block
$P\cup\{j\}$, and has the same omitted-gainer block.  Put
\[
 m=\max_{i\in P}z_i,\qquad m'=\max\{m,z_j\},\qquad
 a_p=p(c_p-1)_+.
\]
Then direct subtraction in the two price formulas gives
\begin{align}
 X_J-X_I
  &=(c_{p+1}-c_p)[z(P)+\sigma_I]+c_{p+1}x_j,\notag\\
 Z_J-Z_I
  &=z_j+a_{p+1}m'-a_pm,                             \tag{PXC18}\\
 \frac{c_{p+1}}{c_p}-1
  &=\frac{2p+2-n}{(p+2)(n-p-1)}.                   \tag{PXC19}
\end{align}
Thus an Lmin-colour deletion cannot lower price once $2p+2\ge n$.
An Lmax-colour deletion lowers price exactly when
$z_j<a_pm-a_{p+1}m'$.  Before the midpoint, these are explicit low-payoff
thresholds; after the coefficient turns upward, nonnegativity prevents the
corresponding Lmin improvement.  The lemma localizes every improving step on
an available tight-co-singleton chain.  It does not assert that every two
admissible rows lie on one such chain, or that the required co-singleton is
tight.

The contrapositive gives the exact local obstruction needed by the separator
arm.  Choose a global $X$-price minimizer $I_X$ and a global $Z$-price
minimizer $I_Z$ (they need not coincide).  For every co-singleton deletion
available from the relevant row, (PXC18) and optimality give the following,
with $p,P,m$ interpreted for the minimizer in the corresponding line:
\begin{align}
 x_j&\ge
 \frac{n-2p-2}{(n-p)(p+1)}[z(P)+\sigma_{I_X}]
 &&(2p+2<n),\notag\\
 z_j+a_{p+1}\max\{m,z_j\}&\ge a_pm.                \tag{PXC20}
\end{align}
In particular, when $z_j\le m$ the second inequality is
$z_j\ge(a_p-a_{p+1})m$.  Repeated strict improvement terminates because each
deletion increases $p$, so each colour has a finite co-singleton descent to a
row satisfying (PXC20).  Therefore simultaneous failure of the two global
price tests produces two explicit exchange-stable rows and these positive
player-price inequalities.  What remains is to aggregate those payments by
the staged Lmax separator, or to show that a shortage of available tight
co-singletons exposes one of the rooted facet packets.  This is a reduction
of the missing theorem, not its proof.

The $X$-stability payment already lies in an elementary balanced class.
Let $R_X$ be the retained noncarrier nongainers whose co-singletons
$C_j=N\setminus\{j\}$ are $x$-tight at an $X$-price minimizer, and assume
$2p+2<n$.  Total balance and $x$-tightness give
$z(C_j)-x(C_j)=x_j-z_j$.  Summing the first line of (PXC20) therefore yields
\[
 z(R_X)+\sum_{j\in R_X}[z(C_j)-x(C_j)]
 \ge |R_X|\frac{n-2p-2}{(n-p)(p+1)}
       [z(P)+\sigma_{I_X}].                         \tag{PXC21}
\]
The underlying incidence packet is simply
\[
 \sum_{j\in R_X}\one_{C_j}+\sum_{j\in R_X}e_j
   =|R_X|\one_N.
\]
Thus each required exchange payment splits exactly into retained Lmax mass
and comparison slack.  The aggregate is the decomposable sum of the balanced
partition identities
$\one_{N\setminus\{j\}}+e_j=\one_N$; it is not the distinct indecomposable
complement-star facet family proved earlier, and it cannot itself be a new
indecomposable exact-cone facet.
Consequently any genuinely new facet needed here must enter through the
remaining supply-or-sufficiency obstruction, not through (PXC21) itself.
Inequality (PXC21) does not prove either that enough co-singletons are tight
or that the available atoms have sufficient total weight to close the target.

The distinction is already active at six players.  On the full tight menu,
minimum deletion is globally hybrid-price optimal on $386$ of the $415$
strict cycles, while a larger deletion size strictly lowers
$\min\{Z_I,X_I\}$ on the other $29$; every strict handover is from $p=1$ to
$p=2$.  Global optima occur at $p=1$ on $351$ cycles and at $p=2$ on $110$,
with both sizes optimal on some cycles.  On the extracted staged support the
minimum class appears optimal on all $415$ only because those improving rows
are absent.  Thus restricting the proof to the extracted basis or to minimum
deletion would create a genuine local maximum; (PXC16) is the correct
basis-independent target.  The exchange audit removes the residual mystery
in these $29$ cases: every handover is generated by an $x$-tight
co-singleton, and the newly deleted player has $x_j=z_j=0$.  Here $n=6$,
$g=2$, and $p=1$, so $c_1=6/5$, $c_2=1$, $a_1=1/5$, and $a_2=0$; (PXC18)
strictly lowers both positive prices.  All $157$ available co-singleton
exchange pairs in the full menus satisfy (PXC18) exactly.  These are finite
archive counts, not an all-dimensions supply theorem for cheap
co-singletons.

The exact six-player replay finds 493 distinct selected-support blocks over
420 nongainer carrier cycles; 477 meet the quota, and every cycle has at
least one.  The smallest closing block has $p=1$ on 325 cycles and $p=2$ on
95.  In the seven-player control the unique selected repair has $P=\{2\}$,
$Q(P)=71/35$, required deficit $54/35$, quota margin $17/35$, and hence
budget margin $17/30$.  These are finite controls, not existence proofs.

A stricter optimal-face audit makes the residual combinatorics explicit.  On
the 233 archived six-player records where every maximum-overlap root is
unaffordable, maximize root excess over the entire optimal labelled-price
face.  All 521 root optima have root excess zero.  Nevertheless every root
has a target-reserve row in its complete staged support.  The returned
price-optimal packing itself contains one at 444 roots, while some packing on
the full optimal price face contains one at 448.  The remaining 73 are
genuine price-face obstructions, spread over 33 records.  Every one is in the
pair-free root branch; its common carriers are exactly the other members of
the maximum-overlap root block, the root optimum has labelled facet margin
$-1$, and its returned optimizer is one unit of a co-singleton row.  There
are 52 rooted instances with a two-player root block and 21 with a
three-player block.  Root selector loss is strict in 71 and tied in two.
Thus the existing strict-loss proper-packet lemma already supplies a
carrier-preserving proper tight row in 71 cases; what it does not ensure is
that this row also omits the player excluded by the co-singleton optimizer.
In the archive 71 rooted instances have no crossing proper carrier row at
all.  The other two have exactly one crossing row, whose co-singleton
intersection is tight by (PXC4).  Every one of the 73 instances has at least
two carrier-row intersections whose final reserve is nonnegative.  This is a
finite compatibility fact, not the missing dimension-free reserve theorem.
More sharply, applying the root-only corollary (PXC7) with $B$ equal to the complete
maximum-overlap nongainer root block certifies all 150 such intersections:
142 lower bounds are positive and eight are zero.  Thus all 73 rooted
obstructions close without inserting their exact reserve into the test.  The
coarser $M^-=M$ estimate fails on ten rows and leaves two roots uncovered;
in the latter record the omitted nongainer actually has $z_i=0$, whereas
$M=5$, and the true reserve is $10/3$.  The apparent residue was therefore
loss of Lmax stratum information, not a new exceptional packet.  These
counts remain an archive theorem only; a universal proof must show that the
staged support or the separator/facet alternative selects an ordered cut
satisfying the right side of (PXC6).  The fixed seven-player full-root-block
control proves that the distinction matters: (PXC7) is $-59/60$ on its
selected repair, while the retained-extra term in (PXC6) restores a positive
bound $53/12$ and the exact reserve is $289/60$.  Thus root mass plus one
global next-stratum ceiling is not a universal substitute for the selected
support.
The comparison certificate (PXC11) is nonnegative on 148 of the 150
intersections and still closes at least one row for every one of the 73
roots.  Exactly those 148 intersections have $\sigma_I=0$, so (PXC11) is
their exact reserve; the other two have $\sigma_I=1/2$ and are closed by
(PXC7).  On the selected seven-player repair (PXC11) again equals the exact
reserve $289/60$.  This complementary
coverage is evidence for a two-colour max-certificate, not a proof that one
of the two bounds is nonnegative in every dimension.

The broader strict-cycle audit exposes a more rigid selector than either
certificate alone.  Over the $415$ positive-loss nongainer carrier cycles in
the same two six-player archives, the full Lmin-tight menu contains $1{,}025$
admissible carrier rows.  Restrict, separately for each cycle, to the rows
minimizing the number $p$ of excluded nongainers.  There are $830$ such
minimum-deletion rows, and every one has both the ordered-cut bound (PXC6) and
the omitted-gainer bound (PXC11) nonnegative.  The minimum is $p=1$ on $380$
cycles and $p=2$ on $35$.  One extracted complete staged support gives the
same conclusion on its $862$ admissible and $829$ minimum rows.  The full-menu
test shows that the observation is not an artifact of that basis extraction.
This is not the tautology of
choosing a row with largest reserve: minimum deletion is defined using only
the support incidence.

The price decomposition explains the six-player coincidence.  Every one of
the $830$ full-menu minimum rows has $E_I^z=0$; $828$ also have $E_I^x=0$.
On each of the two cycles containing a positive-comparison minimum row,
another minimum row has $E_I^x=0$.  Hence $Z_*=X_*$ on all $415$ cycles, and
both equal-price routes close; the two exceptional cycles illustrate why the
minima in (PXC14) may legitimately be attained on different rows.  At seven
players the new control has instead
$X_*=35/8<Z_*=5$: the Lmin colour takes over exactly when the ordered-cut
coarsening becomes lossy.  This is a finite structural handover, not a proof
that a zero-error minimum row exists in every dimension.

The restriction matters.  Six full-menu nonminimal $p=2$ rows have actual target
reserve $-1/3$ (and both lower certificates equal $-1/3$), while a
minimum-deletion row closes the same cycle; four of those six rows occur in
the extracted support.  Thus ``every proper carrier row
closes'' is false even in the archive.  In the fixed seven-player hostile
family, the sole strict carrier-cycle case has four admissible rows; all four
are minimum-deletion rows and both certificates close.  These exact finite
facts initially suggested simultaneous closure, but that strengthening is
false.  Let $v$ be the lower envelope of the four common-total allocations
\[
 (1,5,0,7,0,0,0),\ (2,0,3,1,2,3,2),\
 (0,2,0,0,5,4,2),\ (0,1,5,6,0,1,0).
\]
This is a nonnegative singleton-normalized exact seven-player game.  Exact
stage certificates give
\[
 x=(7/4,7/4,7/4,17/8,7/4,17/8,7/4),\qquad
 z=(5/3,5/3,5/3,2,2,2,2).
\]
Its maximum-overlap roots $3,5$ are both unaffordable and form a strict
carrier cycle.  The full Lmin-tight menu consists of rows $56$ and $104$;
both are minimum-deletion rows with $P=\{0,1,2\}$.  On each row the actual
reserve and (PXC11) are $13/24$, whereas (PXC6) is $-1/12$.  Thus the two
errors in (PXC12) are $E_I^x=0$ and $E_I^z=5/8$: the two colours are
genuinely disjunctive.  The game is safely above the target:
$O=25/2>91/12$.

The corrected sufficient statement is that some minimum-deletion proper
carrier row has
\[
 \max\{\mathrm{PXC6},\mathrm{PXC11}\}\ge0.
\]
The actual
dimension-free target is the weaker full-family inequality (PXC16), or the
sparse Lmax separator dual must yield the priced rooted-facet alternative.
Proving it requires an exchange or Farkas argument for the weighted row
scores; the counts and the new control do not prove it.  They are reproduced by
the archived deterministic verifier and JSON artifact.

Of the closest properizations, 25 delete
one payoff gainer at incidence distance and price loss one, while 48 delete
one payoff gainer and one nongainer at distance and price loss two.  The four
additional zero-price-loss distance-two cases are optimizer-selection swaps, so counting
them as support-exchange obstructions would be a quantifier error.
Consequently ``some price-optimal marked packing already contains a closer''
is false even in the six-player archive.  The surviving missing lemma is a
charged properization theorem for this rigid residual class, not an identity
between two independently optimized certificates.  These counts are exact
finite evidence, not an all-six-player census or a dimension-free exchange
classification.

The strict quota itself is not universal, even when the excluded block is a first
Lmin-floor singleton.  Consider the singleton-normalized exact lower envelope
of
\[
 (6,7,1,0,1,0,1),\quad(7,0,0,0,0,9,0),\quad
 (0,0,0,5,3,6,2).                                  \tag{QFH1}
\]
Its two exact staged selectors are
\[
 x=(3,7/2,1,3,1,7/2,1),\qquad
 z=(3,3,0,2,3,3,2).                                \tag{QFH2}
\]
The tied maximum-overlap roots are $\{0,1,5\}$.  Roots $1$ and $5$ form an

Every Lmin-tight coalition $C$ meeting a carrier cycle contains the whole

the refuted full-root-block sign may not be used.

For the coefficient-two certificate below, the normalized packing has

 \mathbf1_{44}+\mathbf1_9+\mathbf1_{10}+\mathbf1_{24}

Both systems are indecomposable, active exact-cone facets (support sizes six

There are two distinct root optimizations here.  Minimizing the deleted load
$\max_{i\ne r}\alpha_i$ gives the best root-deletion ceiling, whereas finding
a positive pair lift requires maximizing
$\alpha_r-\max_{i\ne r}\alpha_i$ after restricting the over-cover to common
hinges.  They need not select the same certificate.  In the coefficient-two
seven-player control, a minimum-deleted-load certificate is tied, but the
common certificate
\[
 \eta=\frac13,\qquad

\]
has strict leader gap $1/3$ and produces an active pair lift with
$\delta=1/2$.  Exact rational hostile controls also separate the remaining
premises: the primitive coefficient-seven and both sharp-family cases admit
strict common leaders; the neutral rectangle admits common over-covers but
no strict leader; and the optimized label-free, actual-top-fixed, and
top-and-bottom-fixed controls admit no normalized top over-cover supported
entirely on common hinges.  These finite exclusions do not prove the hybrid
bound, but show that its missing gluing lemma must contain strict-common,
tied-common, and noncommon-support branches; optimizing deleted load alone
cannot supply that disjunction.

The noncommon branch has an exact deficiency dual.  For the proper
$z$-tight family ${\cal C}_z$, top block $H$, and
$d_S=x(S)-v(S)\ge0$, let
\[
 \epsilon_*:=\min\Big\{\sum_{S\in{\cal C}_z}d_S\lambda_S:
 \sum_S\lambda_S\mathbf1_S=\eta\mathbf1_N+\alpha,
 \ \alpha(H)=1,\ {\rm supp}(\alpha)\subseteq H,
 \ \lambda,\eta,\alpha\ge0\Big\}.                    \tag{CSD-P}
\]
Ordinary rational LP duality gives
\[
 \epsilon_*=\max\{t:y(S)\le d_S\ (S\in{\cal C}_z),\
 y(N)\ge0,\ y_i\ge t\ (i\in H)\}.                    \tag{CSD-D}
\]
Since every primal summand is nonnegative, $\epsilon_*=0$ exactly when a
normalized top over-cover supported on common hinges exists.  Thus failure of
common support has a canonical nonnegative value and an exact player-price
separation witness.

More precisely, take any normalized top over-cover and any top root $r$.
Put $b_i=\theta_--\alpha_i$ off the root,
$a_r=\alpha_r-\theta_-$, $\epsilon=\alpha\cdot x-M$, and
\[
 \kappa_r=\theta_-T-M-\sum_{i\ne r}b_i v(\{r,i\}).    \tag{NSA}
\]
The rooted pair lift shows $\kappa_r\ge0$.  Direct evaluation at the two
selections gives
\[
 \sum_{i\ne r}b_i z_i-a_rz_r-\sum_{i\ne r}b_i v(\{r,i\})=\kappa_r,
\quad
 \sum_{i\ne r}b_i x_i-a_rx_r-\sum_{i\ne r}b_i v(\{r,i\})
   =\kappa_r-\epsilon.                                \tag{NSAxz}
\]
Hence the noncommon support consumes pair-lift exact-cone slack exactly
one-for-one, and the same rooted pair-floor inequality is valid at Lmin iff
$\kappa_r\ge\epsilon$.  Equivalently,
\[
 \sum_{i\ne r}b_i(x_i-z_i)=a_r(x_r-z_r)-\epsilon.     \tag{NSAD}
\]

An unrestricted ``some top root absorbs the surplus'' lemma is false.
Null-padding a certified five-player exact control gives
\[
 x=(43/280,43/280,47/280,21/80,21/80,0),
\quad z=(1/10,29/140,31/140,33/140,33/140,0).
\]
Here $\epsilon_*=3/112$, while both top roots have $\kappa_r=0$.  The overlap
is $25/28>2/3$, so this does not challenge the hybrid bound; null-player
descent returns it to the proved five-player theorem.  Any absorption lemma
must therefore be restricted to the irreducible positive-Lmin-floor branch.
In the 374 archived six-player controls, the independently returned
minimum-dispersion certificate is also minimum-surplus in every record, 73
deficiencies are positive, and some top root
absorbs the surplus in every record.  These are finite falsification checks,
not a theorem.

Finally, the strongest possible single capped spectral anchor is itself an
LP.  With $L=\lambda/\theta$, $e=\eta/\theta$, and
$u=\alpha/\theta$, maximize
\[
 R_* =\sum_{i\in H}u_i(x_i-\mu),\qquad
 \sum_S L_S\mathbf1_S=e\mathbf1_N+u,\quad 0\le u\le1,
 \quad L,e\ge0.                                      \tag{BSA}
\]
Perspective normalization proves
$R_*=\max(M+\epsilon-\mu)/\theta$ over all top over-covers.  Even this optimum
gives only $605/36<35/2$, $39/2<21$, and $47/2<49/2$ on the optimized
label-free, actual-top-fixed, and top-and-bottom-fixed controls.  Thus the
noncommon cases cannot be closed by choosing a better single scalar spectral
anchor: multiple rooted or staged rows must genuinely be glued.

For comparison, the singleton-slack lift puts
$q_i=1-p_i$ off the root.  Every rooted packing satisfies the incidence
identity
\[
 \sum_{S\in\mathcal C_x}u_S\mathbf1_S
 +\sum_{i\ne r}q_i e_i-\delta e_r=\mathbf1_N.          \tag{RMS}
\]
If $u$ is a vertex, the positive columns in the standard-form system
$A_{N\setminus\{r\}}u+q=\mathbf1$ are linearly independent; adjoining
$e_r$ preserves independence.  Therefore, when $\delta>0$, the nonzero
support in (RMS) is a purely min-semi-balanced system with exceptional
coalition $\{r\}$ in the precise terminology of
\citet[Lemma~2(c)]{StudenyKratochvil2022}.  When $\delta\le0$ the completion
is min-balanced.  For $n\ge3$ the positive-root case defines a full
exact-cone facet exactly when it passes their separate indecomposability
test.  Thus rooted-packing vertex enumeration gives an exact algorithm for
singleton-exceptional facet candidates exposed by a fixed Lmin menu, but not
an automatic facet correspondence.  In fact all eight nontrivial cuts in
the present hostile and sharp controls are min-semi-balanced but decomposable;
an independently formulated Z3 check agrees with the exact-LP decomposition
counts.

In fact the negative facet conclusion is dimension-free.  If $x_r>0$, no
vertex completion (RMS) is indecomposable.  For $\delta>0$, (RDB) forces
$q_i>0$ for some $i\ne r$, so the completion contains $\{r\}$ and $\{i\}$.
Its column independence excludes $\{r,i\}$, but
$e_r+e_i-\mathbf1_{\{r,i\}}=0$ makes $\{r,i\}$ exceptional in the enlarged
system, which is a decomposition in the sense of
\citet[Definition~7]{StudenyKratochvil2022}.  For $\delta\le0$ the completion
is min-balanced and likewise does not define a facet of the exact cone when
$n\ge3$.  Therefore the rooted construction is a relative inequality and
candidate-enumeration theorem in its singleton-slack form.  The rooted-pair
shear (RPL) is the required different lift and gives the exact facet class;
the remaining task is to prove that sparse Lmax gluing selects enough such
facet charge, not to enumerate more singleton-slack completions.

This reduction passes exact rational tests at all maximum-overlap roots of
the $374$ archived six-player pairs ($703$ rooted programs), as well as six
hostile fixed branches and both sharp seven-player orientations.  Those are
falsification controls, not a proof for any unenumerated domain.  An exact
six-player negative control drops from $3/4$ to $5/8$ when the common-hinge
rows are removed entirely, so a leximin-packet-only theorem would be false.
The open dimension-free step is to prove that some maximum-overlap root and
some sparse over-cover always make this common-hinge relaxation attain at
least $n/\lfloor n^2/4\rfloor$, or to produce an exact rational branch that
refutes that statement.

As an exact control of (RHP), an independently formulated cutting-plane
verifier separates over the fractional packing polytope and reproduces the
direct three-generator minima at every maximum-overlap root of six hostile
branches, including the neutral-endpoint control, and two sharp orientations.
The neutral control needs one two-coalition cut and returns $7/10$; the sharp
cases require one packing cut, supported on four and three hinges respectively,
and return $7/12$ exactly.  This checks the elimination arithmetic, not the
universal existence of a target-proving packing.

One sharper scalar bridge suggested by this reduction is now known to be
false.  Minimize
$\theta_+=\max_i\alpha_i$ over normalized first-stage ceiling over-covers
$\sum_S\lambda_S\one_S=\eta\one_N+\alpha$, and put

\[
 a=\min\{1/\theta_+,\lfloor n/2\rfloor\},\qquad u=a\alpha.
\]

Then $0\le u\le1$, $u(N)=a$, and scaling the incidence identity gives

\[
 a\sum_S\lambda_S\one_S+(\one_N-u)=(1+a\eta)\one_N.
\]

If $\epsilon=\sum_S\lambda_S[x(S)-z(S)]\ge0$, evaluation at the two
selections gives $u\mathbin\cdot x=a(M+\epsilon)$ and
$u\mathbin\cdot z=aM$, with complementary totals on $\one_N-u$.  The
proposed conjecture was

\[
 \frac{O(x,z)}{T}\ \ge\ \frac1a+\frac1{n-a}.          \tag{CEB}
\]

Condition (CEB) would imply the target because
$a(n-a)\le\lfloor n^2/4\rfloor$, is exact on
both balanced orientations of the displayed family, and has the correct
five/six boundary.  Exact archived checks are falsification evidence only:
(CEB) passes all $374$ stored six-player pairs and the named seven-player
controls, while failing on $12$ of $124$ distinct stored five-player pairs.
Those controls missed an exact seven-player counterexample.  For

\[
 g=(12/17,4/17,0,1/17,0,0,0),\qquad
 h=(0,0,6/23,0,6/23,10/23,1/23),
\]

put $v(S)=\min\{g(S),h(S)\}$.  The generators have common total one, are
core points, attain every coalition worth between them, and have
coordinatewise minimum zero; hence $v$ is singleton-normalized and exact.
Exact staged certificates give

\[
 x=(227/782,48/391,48/391,1/34,48/391,13/46,1/34),
 \quad z=(91,12,90,12,90,91,5)/391.
\]

Exact primal and dual incidence witnesses give $\theta_+=3/5$ and $a=5/3$,
but

\[
 O(x,z)=\frac{613}{782}<\frac{63}{80}
       =\frac1a+\frac1{7-a}
\]

with margin $-113/31280$.  Thus (CEB) is refuted.  The example does not
challenge the main target: $613/782-7/12=941/4692>0$.

The cap in (CEB) is not an exogenous parameter.  If $\mathcal T_z$ is the
family of proper coalitions tight at $z$ and $H$ is its top payoff block,
ordinary rational LP duality gives the human formula

\[
 \theta_+=\max t:\quad y(S)\ge0\ (S\in\mathcal T_z),\quad
 y(N)\le0,\quad t+y_i\le q_i\ (i\in H),\quad
 q\ge0,\quad\sum_{i\in H}q_i\le1.                    \tag{DD}
\]

Formula (DD) remains valid: $\theta_+$ depends only on the labelled top block and its complete
tight-coalition incidence hypergraph; adding a tight coalition can only
lower it.  This explains why the fixed-cap one-witness relaxation is too
strong on an exact six-player boundary: its minimizer lowers overlap only
after $\theta_+$ moves from $1/2$ to $1/3$.  The seven-player example shows
that retaining dispersion optimality still does not prove (CEB).  Its
positive hinge surplus instead makes the already proved sharpened spectral
floor exceed $7/12$.  The live route is therefore a spectral-or-common-hinge
disjunction at the weaker universal target, not a reciprocal-mass theorem.

The label loss in that disjunction has an exact layer-cake form.  Fix the
actual ceiling block $H$, put $R=N\setminus H$ and $m=|R|$, and define
$X_t=\{i\in R:x_i\ge t\}$ and $Z_t=\{i\in R:z_i\ge t\}$.  If $B_H$ is the
spectral expression with $H$ fixed and the residual spectra oppositely
paired, then

\[
 O-B_H=\int_0^\infty
 \left(|X_t\cap Z_t|-\max\{0,|X_t|+|Z_t|-m\}\right)\,dt. \tag{LC}
\]

Indeed, the first term is the layer-cake formula for residual overlap, while
the second is the Fr\'echet lower intersection bound attained simultaneously
by opposite sorting.  The integrand is a nonnegative integer.  On a fixed
pair of ordered payoff partitions, (LC) is therefore a finite nonnegative
linear form in the consecutive merged stratum gaps.  If $B_*$ denotes the
spectral minimum over admissible ceiling assignments, then

\[
 O=B_*+(B_H-B_*)+(O-B_H),
\]

separating top-selection currency from residual label currency.  This is a
proved identity, not the missing inequality: the remaining common-hinge
problem is to show that the two currencies cover
$\max\{0,nT/\lfloor n^2/4\rfloor-B_*\}$.  It does, exactly, identify a
linear stratum-gap target for the rooted branch-circuit and relative-facet
program without conflating those relative circuits with facets of the full
exact-game cone.

There is also a direct bridge to the staged transports.  If $A_t$ denotes
the players in $R$ high in both selections and $D_t$ those low in both, then
the integrand in (LC) is exactly $\min\{|A_t|,|D_t|\}$.  Put
$a_i=\min\{x_i,z_i\}$ and $b_i=\max\{x_i,z_i\}$.  Whenever $b_j<a_i$, the
interval $(b_j,a_i]$ is a unit high--low rectangle.  Moreover
$x_j\le b_j<a_i\le x_i$, so the complete strict Lmin support theorem makes
the pair $(i,j)$ staged-available.  This includes neutral players and is
strictly larger than the opposite-sign carrier family used initially.

Sort the residual $a$-values downward and $b$-values upward and pair equal
ranks.  At threshold $t$, the active rank pairs form the intersection of an
initial segment of size $|A_t|$ and one of size $|D_t|$; their number is
therefore exactly $\min\{|A_t|,|D_t|\}$.  Integrating gives the human identity

\[
 \sum_{k=1}^{m}(a_{[k]}-b_{[k]})_+=O-B_H. \tag{CS}
\]

Thus canonical weight-one rectangles saturate every label-currency layer.
This corrects the omission of neutral endpoints and proves that the complete
carrier packing is an identity, not the missing inequality.

For fixed payoff strata this packing problem has an exact combinatorial
min--max theorem.  Split the threshold line into the merged coordinate
intervals $J_\ell$, let $c_\ell=\min\{|A_t|,|D_t|\}$ there, and let
$A_{\ell e}$ indicate whether carrier interval $e$ contains $J_\ell$.
Then

\[
 \max\{\textstyle\sum_e |I_e|\rho_e:A\rho\le c,\ \rho\ge0\}
 =\min\{\textstyle\sum_\ell c_\ell p_\ell:
          \sum_{\ell:J_\ell\subset I_e}p_\ell\ge |I_e|,\ p\ge0\}.
\]

The columns of $A$ have the consecutive-ones property, so $A$ is totally
unimodular.  Consequently an integral-capacity instance has an integral
optimal packing with support at most the number of threshold layers, and the
primal and dual are exactly computable in polynomial time for rational data.
The feasible cover $p_\ell=|J_\ell|$ recovers the label-currency upper bound.
Writing $r_k=\sum_{\ell\le k}p_\ell-\beta_k$ turns every carrier-cover
constraint for $(\beta_s,\beta_t]$ into $r_t\ge r_s$ and nonnegativity into
$r_k-r_{k-1}\ge-|J_k|$.  Hence an extreme cover has a rooted spanning-tree
certificate in the graph of tight path and carrier arcs.
This is a fixed-stratum theorem for restricted rectangle families and an
exact interface to rooted incidence packets; it is not by itself a facet
theorem for the exact-game cone.  For the complete family, (CS) already gives
the optimum $O-B_H$, so requiring it to pay the post-spectral deficit is
exactly the original hybrid-overlap conjecture.  A deliberately restricted,
transport-oriented family might still give a nontrivial sufficient condition,
but staged availability alone neither selects it nor proves its adequacy.

In fact, normalized circuit coefficients do not impose finite supplies.
Write either sign-coherent relation as

\[
 \sum_S\lambda_S\mathbf 1_S
 +\sigma\left(e_i+\sum_{j\in P}a_je_j\right)=c\mathbf 1_N,
 \qquad \lambda,a\ge0,
\]

where $\sigma=1$ for Lmin and $-1$ for Lmax.  If a normalized relation has
$a_j>0$, then for $K\ge1$ multiplying all other coefficients by $K$ and
replacing $a_i$ by $Ka_i+K-1$ gives another normalized relation with carrier
coefficient $Ka_j$.  Convexly combine witnesses before amplifying to handle
several carriers simultaneously.  Hence the projection of the joint
certificate/supply system is exactly the nonnegative orthant on those
carrier edges which occur with positive coefficient in some relation.
Coefficient magnitude disappears; only rooted-circuit support remains.

That support is actually complete.  If $x_j<x_i$, then $j$ was pinned at a
strictly earlier Lmin stage.  Adding a positive multiple of $j$'s normalized
relation to $i$'s preserves the forced coefficient of $e_i$ and gives $e_j$
positive auxiliary coefficient.  The sign-reversed Lmax argument applies
whenever $z_j>z_i$.  Hence every strict order-compatible residual pair is
available; in particular every rectangle in (CS) is available through Lmin.
Equality within a stratum gives only a zero-length interval.

The joint LP dual reaches the same conclusion.  Dotting its staged price
vector with a normalized relation forces the root price, and then every
available carrier's supply price, to vanish.  What remains is precisely the
threshold-cover dual.  On the complete family its optimum is the physical
layer-width cover, while (CS) is the matching primal.  Consequently neither
circuit coefficient magnitudes, support patterns, nor threshold-tree prices
can furnish the missing exact-cone facet mechanism.

In the two hostile controls where the spectral and top-selection terms do
not reach the target, this complete-carrier system has short exact witnesses.
Respectively,

\[
 \mathbf 1_{\{0,2,4\}}+\mathbf 1_{\{0,1,2,3,6\}}
 +\mathbf 1_{\{4,6\}}
 +2e_5+e_1+e_3=2\cdot\mathbf 1_N
\]

supplies $(1/2,8]$.  The old opposite-sign packing and corrected full
rectangle packing both have optimum $15$, versus deficit $3/2$.
Similarly,

\[
 \mathbf 1_{\{1,3,4,5,6\}}+e_2+e_0=\mathbf 1_N
\]

supplies $(1/2,19/2]$.  Its old opposite-sign packing optimum is $9$, versus
deficit $1$, while the corrected full rectangle optimum is the full label
currency $33/2$.  Primal packing, threshold-cover dual, and exact
staged-availability witnesses agree.  These
historical opposite-sign checks are retained as regressions.  The complete
clipped family saturates label currency universally by (CS), but that fact
does not prove the hybrid-overlap inequality.

The neutral-endpoint correction has an exact seven-player stress test.  Its
only residual gainer and loser satisfy $x_1=x_2$ and $z_1>z_2$, so the old
opposite-sign family contains no strict interval.  Yet its label currency is
$15167/38092$, exactly recovered by canonical rectangles of lengths
$8081/38092$ and $7086/38092$ using neutral low endpoints.  Fourteen explicit
staged identities and a separately formulated exact staged replay certify
the selections.  The example does not threaten the main bound: its overlap
exceeds $7/12$ by $43349/114276$.

There is nevertheless an exact fixed-dimension decision procedure.  Refine
the exact-game cone into rational polyhedral cells fixing the active staged
bases, payoff orders, ceiling block, spectral branch, and endpoint comparisons.
On each cell both selections and every term in the hybrid-overlap inequality
are rational linear in the game.  Thus the existence of a violating game
reduces to finitely many exact rational linear programs.  Rooted cover trees
remain useful only for deliberately restricted rectangle subfamilies; the
complete family is already exhausted by (CS).  This procedure may be
exponential or worse, and a cellwise boundary row is only a candidate
full-cone facet: it must still glue across cells and lift to an indecomposable
semi-balanced system.

\subsection{Multistage direct gluing}

The direct-labelled branch has a dimension-free master identity.  Partition
$N=P\mathbin{\dot\cup}G\mathbin{\dot\cup}H$, with sizes $p,g,h\ge1$, and put
$m=p+g$, $q=g+h$, and $n=p+g+h$.  Let $P,H$ be nongainers and $G$ the
gainers, so

\[
 z_i\le x_i\quad(i\in P\cup H),\qquad x_i\le z_i\quad(i\in G).
\]

Put $\ell=z(P)/p$ and $b=z(G\cup H)/q$.  Let $J\subset G$, $s=|J|<g$, and assume
$x(H\cup J)\le z(H\cup J)$.  This comparison is automatic when $H\cup J$
is tight at $x$, because $z$ lies in the core.  Put

\[
 B=\frac{g}{m-s},\qquad
 R_x=x(J)+(1-B)x(G\setminus J)-Bx(P),
\]
\[
 R_z=(1-B)[z(H)-hb]-B[z(J)-sb].
\]

Then

\begin{align}
 O-\left(1-\frac{gp}{q(m-s)}\right)T
 ={}&\frac{gpn}{q(m-s)}\ell
 +B[z(H\cup J)-x(H\cup J)]+R_x+R_z.               \tag{TDG}
\end{align}

Indeed, expand the comparison term and use
$x(H)=T-x(P)-x(G)$.  Direct cancellation gives
\[
B[z(H\cup J)-x(H\cup J)]+R_x+R_z
=x(G)+z(H)-BT-(1-B)hb+Bsb.
\]
Substitution of $b=(T-p\ell)/q$, $B=g/(m-s)$, and $q=g+h$ yields (TDG).

Consequently the weaker condition $R_x+R_z\ge0$ already implies

\[
 \frac OT\ge1-\frac{gp}{(g+h)(p+g-s)}.               \tag{TDB}
\]

Equality requires $\ell=0$, a tight comparison, and zero residual sum.
The right side of (TDB) reaches $n/\lfloor n^2/4\rfloor$ exactly when

\[
 \lfloor n^2/4\rfloor gp
 \le[\lfloor n^2/4\rfloor-n](g+h)(p+g-s).           \tag{TDT}
\]

This criterion is one-dimensional.  Put
$\delta_i=z_i-x_i$ on $G$, $L_H=x(H)-z(H)$, and
\[
 U_s=\frac{(p-s)x(G)-gx(P)+(p-s)[z(H)-hb]+gsb}{g}.
\]
Then
\[
 z(H\cup J)-x(H\cup J)=\delta(J)-L_H,\qquad
 (m-s)(R_x+R_z)=g[U_s-\delta(J)].                   \tag{GPI}
\]
Thus a cardinality-$s$ packet closes the direct branch whenever
$L_H\le\delta(J)\le U_s$.  The arbitrary multistratum dependence has
collapsed to an explicit interval-hitting problem on the gainer weights.

A simple sufficient condition for the residual signs is
$s\le p$, $\max x(P)\le\min x(G)$, $\max z(G)\le\min z(H)$, with $J$ the
$s$ lowest-$z$ players of $G$.  Then $0<B\le1$ and

\[
 R_x\ge [s+(1-B)(g-s)-Bp]\,\frac{x(P)}p=0.
\]
Moreover $\operatorname{avg}z(H)\ge b\ge\operatorname{avg}z(G)$ and
$\operatorname{avg}z(J)\le\operatorname{avg}z(G)$, so $R_z\ge0$.

Consequently, let $L$ be the nongainers, choose a nonempty proper block
$H\subset L$, put $P=L\setminus H$, and take $J$ to be the $s$ lowest-$z$
gainers.  If
\[
 s\le |P|,\quad \max x(P)\le\min x(G),\quad
 \max z(G)\le\min z(H),\quad H\cup J\text{ is Lmin-tight},              \tag{UBD1}
\]
and
\[
 \lfloor n^2/4\rfloor |G||P|
 \le [\lfloor n^2/4\rfloor-n](|G|+|H|)(|P|+|G|-s),                    \tag{UBD2}
\]
then the target hybrid-overlap inequality follows.  Tightness and core
feasibility pay the comparison term, (UBD1) pays both ordinal residuals, and
(UBD2) is precisely the target cardinality test.  This is a conditional
upper-block handover; it does not assert that such a block and packet always
exist.

The exact criterion is stronger.  It recovers both archived hostile direct
branches: the actual-top-fixed branch has base $7/10$, $R_x=15/4$, and
$R_z=111/20$; the top-and-bottom-fixed branch has base $2/3$, $R_x=1$, and
$R_z=11$, despite $s=3>p=1$.  Thus no two-stratum assumption on $z$ is
needed.  Their gain packets are respectively $1/2\in[1/2,129/10]$ and
$3/2\in[3/2,15/2]$; the fresh control below has the singleton interval
$[1/2,1/2]$.  The remaining issue is a selection lemma: either a complete staged
packet must supply a target-reaching cardinality-$s$ gainer packet in
$[L_H,U_s]$, or every interval miss must force an affordable mixed-colour
rooted packet.

An exact finite atlas exhausts the sign-compatible partitions and proper
gainer packets on the seven fixed hostile selections.  The five nonsharp
obstructions have direct bases $3/5$, $3/4$, $2/3$, $5/8$, and $5/9$ (the
last at $n=8$).  The first four use Lmin-tight comparisons; the last has
direct slack $5/39$.  Neither sharp $G(2,4)$ nor sharp $G(3,3)$ has a
target-reaching direct packet, and both are closed at equality by the rooted
block-star theorem.  The residual-sum criterion is essential: the $3/4$
certificate has $(R_x,R_z)=(15,-15/2)$, and the $5/9$ certificate has
$(R_x,R_z)=(-8/9,116/117)$.  This is branch-level falsification evidence,
not a universal dichotomy theorem.

A broader exact census refutes the tempting direct-only strengthening.  Of
374 archived six-player pairs, 296 admit a target-reaching direct packet and
282 admit one with an Lmin-tight comparison.  The other 78 have no direct
packet, and 76 of those are strictly above $2/3$.  The first strict control is
\[
 x=(1,2,1,1,1,1),\qquad
 z=(3/2,3/2,3/2,5/6,5/6,5/6),\qquad O/T=6/7.
\]
The separately encoded common-hinge/root audit closes all 374 archived pairs
with minimum exactly $2/3$.  Thus the surviving candidate is genuinely
disjunctive: rooted packets are needed even away from equality.  These are
finite exact censuses, not a six-player domain proof.

The converse finite handover has a sharper hostile boundary.  On 233
six-player archive rows every maximum-overlap root has an unaffordable
canonical labelled row, and every one has a target-reaching direct packet.
Taking $H$ to be exactly the entire maximum-overlap root block works with an
Lmin-tight comparison on 228 rows, but fails on five.  Each failure is
repaired by adjoining one highest-$x$ remaining nongainer to $H$: the
remaining $P$ is a singleton and a one-gainer packet gives an Lmin-tight
comparison, $R_x=0$, $R_z>0$, and base $4/5$.  Hence the fixed-root-block
converse is false even on this finite control set.  The surviving candidate
must select an upper nongainer block containing the roots, as in
(UBD1)--(UBD2).  These counts are exact-rational archive evidence, not a
six-player domain classification.

The locked-pair theorem identifies four of the five failures: in those four
rows the two maximum-overlap roots are each other's unique locked partner.
The fifth failure is pair-free.  Consequently, after the automatic
$|G|\ge2$ branch and the one-pair characterization, the genuinely
unstructured price-failure case is the pair-free rooted hypergraph.

The ceiling-root interpolation theorem closes 28 of the 78 direct failures.
Of the 50 remaining rows, null-player descent removes all 34 zero-floor
cases.  The genuinely live archive region comprises 14 strict positive-floor
cases in the interpolation wedge and the two equality cases.  Every direct
failure has a ceiling root.  Hence the unresolved rooted-packet lemma is
confined to these 16 rows on the archive, without making that finite
decomposition a general theorem.

The sequential support audit gives a sharper candidate.  Every one of the 50
pre-descent residual rows has a common coalition between one complete staged
Lmin support and one sparse Lmax top over-cover, and its ceiling root is the
unique maximum-overlap root.  Common-support sizes are one on 45 rows, two on
three, and three on two.  Every one of the 16 positive-floor residuals has a
common coalition containing that root, and the corresponding rooted
relaxation reaches $2/3$.  Deleting every Lmax row still leaves a
target-reaching complete-Lmin rooted packet on 13 of the 16.  Only three
rows require the common-hinge repair, and all three have a tied over-cover
root load; 15 of the 16 have a tied load in total.  All 37 rows with empty
common support were already closed by direct gluing or ceiling interpolation.
The missing statement is thus a \emph{root-price/common-repair lemma}: after
null-player descent, failure of the first two arms must force either a
target-priced complete Lmin rooted packet or a common root hinge whose
reverse comparison repairs the deficit.  The repair branch must cover tied
roots.  The audit verifies this only on the finite archive.

The exact-cone structure of that finite residual is unexpectedly rigid.
For each of the 13 rows closed by the complete Lmin packet alone, we extract
one exact optimal one-witness Farkas dual, normalize its witness component to
a rooted packing, and apply the pair shear (RPL).  Every resulting system has
full support rank and passes the Studen\'y--Kratochv\'il indecomposability
test: 13 facets and no decomposable row.  Up to relabeling there are only five
support orbits, with multiplicities $3,4,3,2,1$.  The two-spoke and
three-packet repair facets belong to the first and fourth of those same
orbits.  Hence all 16 live positive-floor archive rows are carried by only
five indecomposable rooted pair-lift support orbits, with combined
multiplicities $5,4,3,3,1$.  An independently formulated exact-LRA replay
excludes all 282 unsupported proper exceptional candidates for the five
representatives.

This is a finite facet atlas, not a six-player domain classification: the
archive is not exhaustive and the extraction fixes one optimal dual per row.
It does sharpen the missing statement to a \emph{marked-facet selection
conjecture}: every positive-floor residual should expose an indecomposable
rooted pair lift whose Lmin-tight labels give a target-priced canonical
marking, with a common reverse comparison needed only when the Lmin-only
marked price is insufficient.  The finite gauge audit makes the distinction
necessary: all 13 extracted Lmin-only packings are affordable, whereas the
canonical rooted-pair deletion leaves only six affordable; restoring every
eligible Lmin-tight pair recovers all 13.
The full labelled packing/cover optimization goes one step further: all 16
positive-floor residual rows have an affordable canonical marked row.  The
three common-hinge repairs are therefore alignment failures, not scalar
price failures.  On the fixed seven-player hostile/sharp controls, each case
either has a direct packet or an affordable labelled row at a
maximum-overlap root.  Both statements are finite audits, not a universal
selection theorem.

The rooted status of the sharp rows does have a dimension-free explanation.
If every Lmax coordinate is positive, then $\ell=z(P)/p>0$ for every
nonempty $P$.  Equation (TDG) is therefore strict whenever its comparison
and residual-sum payments are nonnegative.  Hence an equality game
$O=c_nT$ with strictly positive Lmax cannot have a direct packet whose base
reaches $c_n$.  Balanced $G(p,k)$ has $k(p+1)=\lfloor n^2/4\rfloor$,
overlap exactly $c_nT$, and strictly positive Lmax coordinates
$p(k-1)/k$ and $1/k$.  Its rooted indecomposable block-star system is thus
structurally necessary within this direct-or-rooted framework.

The handover can be classified on every $G(p,k)$.  In (TDG), take
$P=O$, $G=B$, $H=\{r\}$, and $J=B\setminus\{b\}$.  The comparison coalition
is the common block-star hinge.  If
\[
 d=\frac{p(k-1)-1}{k(p+1)},
\]
then each gainer has gain $pd$, while the root loss and the packet gain both
equal $p(k-1)d$.  Thus the comparison is tight and both residuals vanish.
The direct and rooted bounds are respectively
\[
 \frac{n}{(p+1)(k+1)}
 \quad\hbox{and}\quad
 \frac{n}{k(p+1)}.                                  \tag{GPH}
\]
The direct bound reaches $c_n$ exactly when
$(p+1)(k+1)\le\lfloor n^2/4\rfloor$.  The rooted block-star bound always
reaches it because $k+(p+1)=n$ implies
$k(p+1)\le\lfloor n^2/4\rfloor$.  The one-unit denominator shift is the
mechanism handover: $G(3,2)$ has direct base $1/2$ but rooted base
$3/4>2/3$, while $G(2,4)$ and $G(3,3)$ have direct bases $7/15$ and $7/16$
but rooted base $7/12$ at equality.
In facet-price notation the denominators are
\[
 D_{\rm direct}=(p+1)(k+1),\qquad
 D_{\rm root}=p(k-1)+n-1=k(p+1),
\]
and differ by exactly $p+1$.  The rooted pair-spoke shear is the
indecomposable semi-balanced atom realizing the smaller denominator, whereas
the direct identity retains the positive low-Lmax payment on the outside
block.  This is the exact-cone reason the common hinge alone loses the
necessary factor near balance.

When both selections have the corresponding two-block form, the residuals
vanish.  A fresh exact seven-player control with
$(p,g,h,s)=(3,2,2,1)$ has this specialization, and (TDG) becomes

\[
 O-\frac58T=\frac{21}{8}z_0+\frac12[z(74)-x(74)],
\]

where coalition $74=\{1,3,6\}$ is tight at both selections.  Its actual
overlap is $11T/12$, while every one-witness branch relaxation has exact
minimum $5/8>7/12$.  The hand identity, not that finite control, is the
dimension-free evidence.

\subsection{Mixed-support rooted packets}

The common-hinge projection is not the largest inequality obtainable from
one singleton witness.  Let $v$ be singleton-normalized and exact, let $x,z$
be core allocations, and fix $r\in N$.  Exactness supplies a core allocation
$w^r$ with $w^r_r=0$.  Consider tagged coalitions $(\sigma,S)$, where
$\sigma\in\{x,z\}$ and $S$ is tight at the tagged selection.  If
$\rho_{\sigma,S}\ge0$, $q\ge0$, and

\[
 \sum_{\sigma,S}\rho_{\sigma,S}\one_S+q
   =\xi\one_N+\tau e_r,                              \tag{MCP}
\]

then

\[
 \sum_{\sigma,S}\rho_{\sigma,S}\sigma(S)\le \xi T. \tag{MCB}
\]

Indeed, dot (MCP) with $w^r$.  Its right side is $\xi T$, whereas
$w^r(S)\ge v(S)=\sigma(S)$ for every tagged tight coalition and
$q\mathbin\cdot w^r\ge0$.  This proves (MCB).  Unlike common-hinge transfer,
the packet may contain an $x$-only coalition and a different $z$-only
coalition; their common exactness witness supplies the gluing.

These inequalities exactly eliminate the witness.  Write
$p_i(\rho)=\sum_{\sigma,S:i\in S}\rho_{\sigma,S}$.  There exists a vector
$w\ge0$ with $w(N)=T$, $w_r=0$, and
$\sigma(S)\le w(S)$ for every tagged coalition if and only if

\[
 \sum_{\sigma,S}\rho_{\sigma,S}\sigma(S)
 \le T\max_{i\ne r}p_i(\rho)\qquad(\rho\ge0).        \tag{MWP}
\]

Necessity is the weighted witness argument.  For sufficiency, the tagged
requirement vectors dominated by coalition sums of the rooted simplex form a
closed convex down-set.  A separating normal may therefore be chosen
nonnegative, and the support function of the rooted simplex in that direction
is precisely the right side of (MWP).  Separation would contradict (MWP).

After normalizing every nonroot incidence load to at most one, the tagged
packing polytope has $n-1$ capacity rows.  Hence each vertex has at most
$n-1$ positive tagged coalitions.  Two tagged copies of the same incidence
column cannot both occur at a vertex.  If $p_i$ denotes the resulting load,
$q_i=1-p_i$ off the root, and $\delta=p_r-1$, its completion is

\[
 \sum_{\sigma,S}\rho_{\sigma,S}\one_S
 +\sum_{i\ne r}q_i e_i-\delta e_r=\one_N.            \tag{MCC}
\]

Thus the colours disappear under projection to characteristic-function
coordinates.  Vertex enumeration, the rooted-pair shear, and the published
indecomposability test give an exact finite algorithm for the entire
one-witness projection and its corresponding min-semi-balanced facet
candidates.  The new content is their relative two-selection evaluation, not
a claim that every candidate is a full-cone facet or that some root always
proves the desired target.

The optimized seven-player spectral obstruction supplies the first genuinely
noncommon packet.  At root $r=3$,

\[
 \one_{26}+\one_{57}+\one_{75}+2e_2+e_5+e_6
   =2\one_N+e_3,                                    \tag{MC7}
\]

where coalitions $26,57$ are tight at $x$, coalition $75$ is tight at $z$,
and only $57$ is common.  Therefore

\[
 x(26)+x(57)+z(75)\le2T.                            \tag{MC8}
\]

Together with direct selection-comparison, stratum, nonnegativity, and
efficiency rows, this packet gives an exact Farkas identity with branch
minimum $3/4$.  That identity now has a short human slack form.  Normalize
$T=1$ and write $w=w^3$.  On the displayed branch,
\begin{align*}
 O-\frac34={}&z_2+\frac12w_2+\frac14w_5+\frac14w_6\\
 &+\frac14[w(26)-x(26)]+\frac14[w(57)-x(57)]\\
 &+\frac54[x(52)-z(52)]+\frac12[x(57)-z(57)]
   +\frac14[w(75)-z(75)]\\
 &+\frac34(x_0-x_4)+\frac12(z_4-z_5)+(z_5-z_1).       \tag{MCS}
\end{align*}
Every term is nonnegative by witness/core comparisons, nonnegativity, or the
fixed payoff orders.  This is a hand proof of the branch bound, not merely a
solver dual.  At the actual control every coalition-comparison term in (MCS)
vanishes; its strict margin $119/1080$ comes entirely from $z_2$ and three
payoff-stratum gaps.  Thus the mixed packet fixes the baseline, while ordered
label currency pays the multistage defect.

The old common-hinge one-witness projection at this root reaches only
$25/34$.  The arbitrary-Lmax direct theorem now supplies a simpler
target-closing bound $3/5$ on this fixed selection; it also directly closes
the coefficient-two and primitive coefficient-seven controls at $5/8$ and
$5/9$.  Thus (MCS) remains a stronger human rooted certificate and a test of
the mixed-colour mechanism, but is no longer required to close those fixed
controls.  Exactly the two sharp positive-Lmax equality orientations lack a
target-reaching direct packet and use the rooted block-star arm.

An exact rational verifier checks (MC7) coordinatewise and checks every
coefficient in all three fixed-branch Farkas identities.  These are proof
objects for the displayed branches, not an all-dimension proof.  The
remaining question is whether failure of the sharpened spectral bound forces
some distinguished root's direct labelled rows plus exact mixed-witness
projection to prove the target.

The cutting-plane projection also finds a second mixed packet at the same
root,

\[
 \tfrac12(\one_{26}+\one_{57}+\one_{15})
 +\tfrac12e_2+\tfrac12e_5+e_6=\one_N+\tfrac12e_3,     \tag{MC9}
\]

where $26,57$ are tagged by $x$ and $15$ by $z$.  This one cut raises the
witness-free master from $25/34$ to the exact one-witness minimum $3/4$.
Its singleton completion is purely min-semi-balanced but decomposable.  The
rooted-pair shear instead gives

\[
 \tfrac12(\one_{12}+\one_{15}+\one_{26}+\one_{40}+\one_{57})
 +\one_{72}-\tfrac52e_3=\one_N.                      \tag{MCF}
\]

The seven columns in (MCF) have rank seven, the published exceptional-set
test finds no decomposition, and its exact-game inequality is active on the
hostile control.  Hence (MCF) defines a facet of the seven-player exact-game
cone.  An independently formulated Z3 exact-LRA replay agrees with the exact
LP decomposition count.  Across eight fixed hostile and sharp controls, the
projected cutting-plane and direct-witness minima agree at every
maximum-overlap root; nine cuts are generated, seven rooted-pair shears pass
the facet test, and (MC9) is the unique cut using both colours.  These are
finite controls and do not prove existence of a target-valid root in every
dimension.

There is first a complete rectangular description for the positive-load
singleton-exceptional class.  Consider an indecomposable normalized identity

\[
 \sum_{j=1}^k\lambda_j\one_{S_j}-c e_r=\one_N,
 \qquad \lambda_j,c>0.                               \tag{RR1}
\]

Every $S_j$ contains $r$.  Indeed, if $S$ omits $r$ and
$S\cup\{r\}$ is an unsupported proper coalition, then
$\one_S+e_r-\one_{S\cup\{r\}}=0$ is an exceptional-set decomposition.  If
the union is supported, the same three columns form a forbidden
semi-balanced subsystem.  If the union is $N$, the first two columns already
form a balanced subsystem.  The degenerate cases in which these columns are
the whole support cannot satisfy the positive-load identity (RR1).

Write $S_j=\{r\}\cup A_j$ and let $A$ be the $(n-1)\times k$ nonroot
incidence matrix.  Minimality is equivalent to $\operatorname{rank}A=k$, and the identity
is

\[
 A\lambda=\one,\qquad c=\one^\mathsf T\lambda-1.     \tag{RR2}
\]

Thus $k\le n-1$.  For a proposed exceptional coalition with root indicator
$\beta$ and nonroot incidence $b$, the Studen\'y--Kratochv\'il decomposition
equations reduce exactly to

\[
 A\mu-b=\gamma\one,\qquad
 \mu\ge0,\qquad \beta+\gamma-\one^\mathsf T\mu\ge0. \tag{RR3}
\]

Consequently (RR1) is a facet exactly when (RR3) is infeasible for every
unsupported proper nonempty $(\beta,b)$.  This gives an exact enumeration
algorithm for the entire positive-load singleton-exceptional class at fixed
$n$: enumerate full-column-rank zero-one matrices up to row and column
permutations, retain the positive solutions of (RR2), and test all Boolean
vectors by (RR3).  Passing systems give primitive dual-cone extreme rays
after clearing denominators.  This is a specialization of the published
criterion, with a support bound and a concrete matrix enumeration, not a new
general characterization of the exact-game cone.

For the projected pair lifts this is also a decisive support filter.  If an
extreme rooted packing uses any positive coalition omitting its exceptional
root, its pair lift is decomposable and cannot define a full exact-cone
facet.  Irreducible facet selection may therefore be restricted in advance
to root-containing Lmin-tight coalitions, together with the restored rooted
pair columns.  The remaining hybrid theorem must show that some ceiling root
exposes a member of this smaller menu whose canonical Lmin-labelled marking
is sufficiently priced, or that an earlier direct/interpolation branch
closes the target.  The gauge quotient classifies facet rays but cannot test
this marked price.

There is a closed inverse formula for the saturated rooted-star subclass
containing (MCF) and the sharp-family facets.  Remove a root $r$ and
let $A$ be a nonsingular $(n-1)\times(n-1)$ zero-one matrix whose columns are
the incidences of sets $A_j\subset N\setminus\{r\}$.  Put

\[
 \lambda=A^{-1}\one,
 \qquad c=\one^\mathsf T\lambda-1.                   \tag{RS1}
\]

If $\lambda>0$ and $c>0$, then

\[
 \sum_j\lambda_j\one_{\{r\}\cup A_j}-c e_r=\one_N \tag{RS2}
\]

is a purely min-semi-balanced system with $n$ independent columns.  It is the
completion of a saturated rooted-packing vertex and attains the $n-1$
positive-coalition support bound; rooted-pair shears enter the class by
treating their pair columns as completed-menu columns.  Its exact-game
inequality is a facet if
and only if the following finite inverse-matrix test passes.  For every
proper nonempty coalition $B$ outside the support, let $\beta=1_{\{r\in B\}}$,
let $b$ be its incidence off the root, and put $u=A^{-1}b$.  Then require

\[
 \max_j\frac{-u_j}{\lambda_j}
  >\frac{\beta-\one^\mathsf T u}{c}.                 \tag{RS3}
\]

Indeed, a decomposition through $B$ has nonnegative coefficients
$\mu_0,\mu_j$ and common load $\gamma$.  Its off-root coordinates force
$\mu=u+\gamma\lambda$, so $\gamma$ is at least the left side of (RS3).  Its
root coordinate gives
$\mu_0=\beta-\one^\mathsf T u-\gamma c$, so $\gamma$ is at most the right
side.  Thus a decomposition exists exactly when the reverse weak inequality
holds.  The facet assertion is then precisely the indecomposability theorem
of \citet{StudenyKratochvil2022}.  This specializes, rather than replaces,
their general criterion.

Formula (RS3) gives an exact enumeration algorithm: enumerate nonsingular
zero-one matrices up to row and column permutations, retain (RS1), and test
all Boolean $(\beta,b)$.  Passing matrices give primitive dual-cone extreme
rays after clearing denominators.  Equality in the game inequality is
$\sum_j\lambda_jv(\{r\}\cup A_j)=v(N)$.

This saturated class has a dimension-free unsaturated closure.  Start from
a purely min-semi-balanced rooted-star system and replace every nonroot role
$q$ by a
nonempty block $B_q$ of clones, expanding each positive coalition by taking
the union of the blocks belonging to its roles.  The incidence identity is
preserved coordinatewise.  Minimality is preserved because any relation
among expanded columns restricts, on one representative per block, to a
relation among the base columns.

Moreover, this clone expansion is indecomposable if and only if its base is.
Indeed, two players in the same clone block have identical rows in the
positive-support incidence matrix.  Subtracting their coordinates in the
Studen\'y--Kratochv\'il exceptional-set equations forces a proposed
exceptional coalition to contain either the whole block or none of it.
Every expanded decomposition therefore collapses to a base decomposition,
and every base decomposition expands blockwise.  The published
indecomposability criterion now implies:

\begin{theorem}[nonroot clone expansion]
Every nonroot clone expansion of a saturated rooted-star exact-cone facet is
again an exact-cone facet.
\end{theorem}

For fixed $n$ this gives an exact enumeration algorithm for the enlarged
class: enumerate the saturated bases using (RS1)--(RS3), then enumerate
positive clone-block size compositions summing to $n-1$, modulo base
automorphisms.  This is complete for the clone-expanded rooted-star class,
not for all exact-cone facets.

All five support orbits in the six-player positive-residual atlas belong to
this class.  Three are already saturated.  The other two each compress one
clone pair to a four-role saturated base, with determinants $1$ and $-2$.
Thus one dimension-free facet construction explains the finite atlas, but
does not yet prove that every residual branch exposes a member with enough
hybrid-overlap price.

The clone-expanded subclass is not exhaustive.  The published catalogue of
\citet{StudenyKratochvil2022} shows that all 23 singleton-exceptional
types through six players are clone-saturated, so the first failure is
sharply at seven players.  For $m\ge4$ and $p\ge0$, take

\[
 N=\{r\}\mathbin{\dot\cup}E(K_m)\mathbin{\dot\cup}O,\qquad |O|=p,
 \qquad S_v=\{r\}\cup\{e\in E(K_m):v\in e\}.
\]

Then the complete-graph rooted-pair identity is

\[
 \sum_{v\in K_m}\one_{S_v}+2\sum_{o\in O}\one_{\{r,o\}}
 -(m-2+2p)e_r=2\one_N.                              \tag{KG1}
\]

\begin{theorem}[complete-graph rooted pair facets]
For every $m\ge4$ and $p\ge0$, (KG1) is an indecomposable purely
min-semi-balanced system and defines a facet of the exact-game cone.  It is
not a clone expansion of a saturated rooted-star system.  The first member
has $(m,p)=(4,0)$ and seven players.
\end{theorem}

The incidence identity is immediate: each edge lies in its two endpoint
stars and each outside player in its rooted pair.  The unoriented
vertex-edge incidence matrix of $K_m$ has rank $m$ because it contains an
odd cycle; the pair and root columns raise the support rank to $m+p+1$.

For the exceptional-set test, write its edge, outside, and root indicators
as $b_{ij},o_a,\beta$.  The old-support coefficients obey

\[
 \mu_i+\mu_j-b_{ij}=\gamma,\qquad c_a=\gamma+o_a,\qquad
 d=\beta+\gamma-\sum_i\mu_i-\sum_a c_a\ge0.          \tag{KG2}
\]

An elementary additive-binary graph lemma says that a zero-one edge
labelling $b_{ij}=\mu_i+\mu_j-\gamma$ of $K_m$, $m\ge4$, is only empty,
complete, a star, or the complement of a star.  If all potentials agree the
labelling is constant.  Otherwise choose $\mu_j>\mu_k$.  For every other
$i$, the binary difference
$b_{ij}-b_{ik}=\mu_j-\mu_k$ is the same nonzero value, hence it is one and
$(b_{ij},b_{ik})=(1,0)$.  All remaining potentials agree.  Two remaining
vertices exist, and their mutual edge bit and $b_{jk}$ sum to one, giving
exactly the star and co-star alternatives.  With a common gauge $t$, the four
coefficient patterns are respectively

\[
 (\mu,\gamma)=(t\one,2t),\quad
 (t\one+e_v,2t),\quad(t\one-e_v,2t-1),\quad
 (t\one,2t-1).
\]

Put $o=\sum_a o_a$ and $D=m+2p-2$.  In the same four cases, (KG2) gives
$d=\beta-o-Dt$, $\beta-o-1-Dt$, $\beta+p-o-Dt$, and
$\beta+p-o-1-Dt$.  Nonnegativity of the other coefficients requires
$t\ge0,0,1$, and either $t\ge1/2$ or all outside bits one, respectively.
The only feasible candidates are the empty coalition, the supported root,
one supported rooted pair, one supported vertex star, or $N$.  Hence there
is no new proper exceptional coalition.  Finally the
nonroot star rows are the $\binom m2$ distinct two-subset vectors.  Since
$\binom m2>m$, no clone compression is saturated.

In rooted-packing coordinates the star weights are $1/2$, the pair weights
are one, and

\[
 \delta=m/2-1,\qquad Q=p,\qquad P=Q/\delta=2p/(m-2). \tag{KG3}
\]

The $p=0$ member belongs to the zero-root/null-floor mechanism when evaluated
through a root-zero Lmin witness.  Members with $p>0$ are affordable
positive-floor facet candidates, but the missing selection theorem is not
implied by their existence.  Exact enumeration on twelve parameter controls
and an independent Z3 replay of all 127 seven-player exceptional candidates
serve only as audits of the displayed proof.

For (MCF), $|\det A|=2$, the weights are
$(1/2,1/2,1/2,1/2,1/2,1)$, $c=5/2$, and every unsupported
Boolean coalition has strict margin at least $2/5$ in (RS3).  For
$G(p,k)$, $A=I_p\oplus(J_k-I_k)$, giving pair weights one, hinge weights
$1/(k-1)$, and $c=p+1/(k-1)$; hence the new mixed facet and every sharp-family
facet lie in the same saturated rooted-star class.  Exact inverse arithmetic
agrees with the LP decomposition counts for all four saturated control
systems.  The matrix theorem is dimension-free, but it still does not prove
that every labelled selection branch exposes one of these facets with enough
hybrid-overlap charge.
