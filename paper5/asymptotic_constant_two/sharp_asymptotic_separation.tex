\begin{abstract}
For singleton-normalized exact transferable-utility games on $n$ players,
let $s_n$ be the supremum of the normalized $\ell_1$ distance between the
leximin and leximax-min core allocations. We prove
\[
 \lim_{n\to\infty}n(2-s_n)=2,
 \qquad 2-s_n=\frac2n+O\!\left(\frac{\log n}{n^2}\right).
\]
The upper bound on separation is a short convexity argument. The matching
construction is the minimum of two additive games, and its entire core is
a line segment. Complementary coalition equalities encode a Fibonacci
recurrence that determines the segment exactly. Both full selectors are
unique at their first optimization stages. A separate three-generator
construction reaches the same asymptotic constant and extends PXC151.
Conventional proofs and finite exact computations are distinguished.
External correctness, novelty, and significance assessment remain pending.
\end{abstract}

\section{Statement and scope}
Write $y(S)=\sum_{i\in S}y_i$ and
\[
 \Core(v)=\{y\in\mathbb R^N:y(N)=v(N),\ y(S)\ge v(S)\ (S\subseteq N)\}.
\]
A game is \emph{exact} if each coalition value is attained by a core
allocation. Singleton normalization means $v(\{i\})=0$ for every player,
so every core allocation is nonnegative. Throughout $T=v(N)>0$.
The allocation $x=\Lmin(v)$ lexicographically maximizes the increasing
rearrangement of payoffs; $z=\Lmax(v)$ lexicographically minimizes their
decreasing rearrangement. These are opposing egalitarian selections, not
the coalition-excess nucleolus. Put
\[
 O(x,z)=\sum_i\min(x_i,z_i),\qquad
 \rho(v)=\frac{\|x-z\|_1}{T}=2-\frac{2O(x,z)}T,
 \qquad s_n=\sup_v\rho(v),
\]
where the supremum ranges over the stated exact games.
\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
For $n\ge2$, every such game satisfies $\rho(v)\le2-2/(n-1)$.
For every integer $n\ge128$, writing $L=\lceil\log_2n\rceil$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:explicit}
 \frac2{n-1}\le 2-s_n
 \le\frac2{n-12L}+\frac2{n^2+1}.
\end{equation}
Consequently $\lim_{n\to\infty}n(2-s_n)=2$ and
$2-s_n=2/n+O(\log n/n^2)$. The constructions use two nonnegative efficient
generators and can be scaled to integer-valued games. Fixed-$n$ sharpness
and uniqueness of asymptotic extremizers are not asserted.
\end{theorem}
Thus PXC151's coefficient $3+2\sqrt2$ is not globally optimal.

\section{A generic overlap bound}
\begin{lemma}\label{lem:generic}
Let $K$ be a nonempty compact convex subset of
$\{y\ge0:y(N)=T\}$, with $n\ge2$. If $x$ maximizes the minimum
coordinate and $z$ minimizes the maximum coordinate on $K$, then
$O(x,z)\ge T/(n-1)$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
If $x=z$, the claim is immediate. Otherwise put $a=\min_i x_i$ and
$M=\max_i z_i$; equal totals imply $a<M$. Some index $i$ with $x_i=a$
has $z_i\le a$. Otherwise a sufficiently short move from $x$ toward $z$
raises every minimum coordinate, while the finitely many other coordinates
remain above $a$, contradicting maximin optimality. Similarly some index
$j$ with $z_j=M$ has $x_j\ge M$. The indices are distinct. Therefore
\[
 O(x,z)\ge z_i+M,\qquad
 T\le z_i+(n-1)M\le(n-1)(z_i+M)\le(n-1)O(x,z).
\]
\end{proof}
Neither exactness nor later lexicographic stages are needed. This
reconstructs and slightly strengthens the universal bound reported in the
previous session; it does not certify that session's lost release run.

\section{Realizing a segment by coalition equalities}
\begin{lemma}\label{lem:matrix}
Let $A$ be an invertible $p\times p$ zero-one matrix such that
$d=A^{-1}\mathbf1>0$. Choose a positive integer $r$ with
$H=r-\sum_i d_i\ge1$. On $p+1$ donor and $r$ receiver players, set
\[
 U=(H,d_1,\ldots,d_p;0^r),\quad V=(0^{p+1};1^r),\quad
 v(S)=\min\{U(S),V(S)\}.
\]
Then $v$ is exact and singleton-normalized, with $\Core(v)=[U,V]$.
If $\alpha=\min_i d_i$, its full selectors are $tU+(1-t)V$ at
\[
 t_{\min}=\frac1{1+\alpha},\qquad t_{\max}=\frac1{1+H},
 \qquad 2-\rho(v)=2\left(\frac\alpha{1+\alpha}+\frac1{1+H}\right).
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Both nonnegative generators have total $r$, belong to the lower-envelope
core, and collectively attain every coalition value. Their disjoint
supports give singleton normalization. Each column of $A$ occurs in a row,
so $Ad=\mathbf1$ and positivity imply $d_i\le1$.

Let $D$ be the donor subset of any row of $A$. For each receiver $e$,
both generators give $D\cup\{e\}$ value one and its complement value
$r-1$. The two core inequalities and efficiency force $y(D)+y_e=1$.
All receivers thus have a common payment $1-t$. The other donor coordinates
satisfy $Ay_d=t\mathbf1$, hence $y_d=td$; efficiency gives $y_h=tH$.
Nonnegativity implies $0\le t\le1$. Convexity gives the reverse containment.

On this segment, the minimum coordinate is $\min(t\alpha,1-t)$ and
the maximum is $\max(tH,1-t)$, since $0<\alpha\le d_i\le1\le H$.
Each scalar optimum is unique, so already determines the full selector.
The endpoint supports are disjoint with equal total, giving normalized
distance $2(t_{\min}-t_{\max})$ and the formula.
\end{proof}
The structural content is preserving a segment under coalition closure;
an arbitrary convex segment need not be the core of its lower envelope.

\section{The Fibonacci family}
Let $F_1=F_2=1$, $F_{j+2}=F_{j+1}+F_j$. For integers $m\ge2$,
$r\ge m+1$, write
\[
 F=F_{m+1},\quad H=r-m,\quad
 a_i=F_{m-i+1}/F,\quad b_i=1-a_i\quad(1\le i\le m).
\]
Define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fibfamily}
 U=(H,a_1,\ldots,a_m,b_1,\ldots,b_m;0^r),\qquad
 V=(0^{2m+1};1^r).
\end{equation}
There are $n=r+2m+1$ players and total $T=r$.
\begin{proposition}\label{prop:fib}
The lower envelope of \eqref{eq:fibfamily} has core exactly $[U,V]$, and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fibdef}
 \rho=2\left(\frac F{F+1}-\frac1{r-m+1}\right),\qquad
 2-\rho=\frac2{F_{m+1}+1}+\frac2{r-m+1}.
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
With donor labels $A_i,B_i$, these $2m$ subsets have weight one:
\begin{align*}
 &\{A_i,B_i\} &&(1\le i\le m),\\
 &\{A_{i+1},A_{i+2},B_i\} &&(1\le i\le m-2),\\
 &\{A_{m-1},B_m\},\qquad \{A_1,A_2\}.&&
\end{align*}
Their incidence matrix is invertible. The homogeneous equations eliminate
the $B_i$ variables and give $A_i=A_{i+1}+A_{i+2}$,
$A_{m-1}=A_m$, and $A_1+A_2=0$. Writing $A_m=u$ gives
$A_i=F_{m-i+1}u$ and $F_{m+1}u=0$. Thus all variables vanish.
This includes $m=2$, when there are no recurrence rows.
The positive inhomogeneous solution $(a,b)$ has total $m$ and minimum
$1/F$. Apply Lemma~\ref{lem:matrix}.
\end{proof}
\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}]
Lemma~\ref{lem:generic} gives the lower bound on $2-s_n$.
For $n\ge128$ choose $L=\lceil\log_2n\rceil$, $m=4L$, and
$r=n-2m-1$. These satisfy $r\ge m+1$: at $n=128$ check directly;
for $L\ge8$, $n\ge2^{L-1}+1\ge12L+2$. The recurrence implies
$F_{2j+1}\ge2^j$ for $j\ge0$. Hence
$F_{m+1}\ge2^{2L}\ge n^2$ and $r-m+1=n-12L>0$.
Formula~\eqref{eq:fibdef} proves \eqref{eq:explicit}.
Since $L=O(\log n)$, subtracting $2/n$ and squeezing proves the claims.
\end{proof}
Multiplication by $F$ makes all generator coordinates integral. Choosing
instead the least $m\ge2$ with $F_{m+1}\ge n^2$ gives
$m=O(\log n)$ and $n^2\le F_{m+1}<2n^2$ for large $n$. The scaled total
is $rF=O(n^3)$, so every coordinate needs only $O(\log n)$ bits.

\section{An independent extension of PXC151}
A separate three-generator construction and full proof are included in
\texttt{THREE\_GENERATOR\_PROOF.md}. For integers $k\ge1$,
$m\ge\max(2,2k-2)$, $r\ge3$, put $q=(k+1)m-k+2$.
Its $n=m+k+r+3$ players have total $T=rq+m+1$, both optimal first-stage
faces are singletons, and its exact overlap is $O=q+r+1$. Thus
\[
 \rho=2-\frac{2(q+r+1)}{rq+m+1}.
\]
For fixed $k$, optimizing $m/r$ gives coefficient
$2(1+1/\sqrt{k+1})^2$. At $k=1$ this is PXC151; it decreases to two as
$k$ grows. The polynomial sequence $k=s^2,m=2s^2,r=2s^3$ gives
$n(2-\rho)\to2$. This family increases the multiplicity of an existing
payoff role, and uses a different mechanism from the Fibonacci segment.
Its domain restriction is material: an exact negative control at
$(m,k,r)=(3,3,3)$ falsifies the extrapolated leximin formula.

\section{Verification and review boundary}
The main theorem has a conventional all-parameter proof. A standalone
rational Z3 replay reconstructs all coalition inequalities for five
Fibonacci cases with $8\le n\le14$, proves that the whole core is the
segment, and separately verifies both optimum values and uniqueness.
A perturbed unit-sum system produces an off-segment core allocation,
checking that coalition closure can enlarge a perturbed segment. Fibonacci identities are also calibrated
for $2\le m\le64$; this is finite evidence, not an all-parameter proof.
The separate three-generator replay checks all coalitions for four cases
with $n=9,10,12,13$, excluding better floors, better ceilings, and distinct
allocations on either optimal face. A parameter-domain falsifier gives
an explicit improved core point. The generic near-extremizer's coalition
closure is independently calibrated in 24 cases.

These fairness selections are established concepts studied algorithmically
in assignment-game cores and Owen sets~\cite{vazirani,gangam}.
Classical exactness and lower envelopes are not asserted as novel.
Exact-cone facets and majorization characterizations provide adjacent
structural frameworks~\cite{studeny,uiterkamp}. The candidate contribution
is the sharp asymptotic separation theorem and its explicit realizations.
A targeted primary-source screen did not locate an equivalent quantitative
result; that is not a novelty certification. Specialist correctness,
prior-art, and significance review remain pending.
