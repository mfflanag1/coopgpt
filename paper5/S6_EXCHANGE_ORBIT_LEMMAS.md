# Orbit-level anatomy of the large six-player exchange cases

Date: 2026-08-10

The direct strict-bound proof contains three visually large retained menus:

```text
(p,m,e)=(3,2,1): 52 labeled directions,
(p,m,e)=(3,3,0): 71 labeled directions,
(p,m,e)=(4,2,0): 90 labeled directions.
```

Those 213 rows are not 213 unrelated mathematical ideas.  Quotienting first
by relabelings inside the sign classes `P={x>z}`, `M={z>x}`, and `E={x=z}`
leaves 44 role-placement orbits.  Forgetting the role placement and retaining
only the coefficient multiset leaves eight oriented templates, or six up to
reversing the direction.  The exact quotient is regenerated by
`audit_strict_exchange_large_case_templates.py`.

## 1. The common exchange lemma

Every retained row is an instance of one lemma.  Let `y` be `Lmin` or `Lmax`
and let `h(N)=0` be one of the templates below, assigned to labeled players.
If the first payoff stratum affected by `h` moves in the improving direction,
then lexicographic optimality forces a `y`-tight proper coalition `S` with
`h(S)<0`.  Exactness and core membership force that same `S` to satisfy the
opposite-selection comparison and all six singleton-witness comparisons.

Thus a template is not itself an inequality: it is a finite family of
negative-cut alternatives.  The proof objects show that, for the listed role
placements, no consistent choice of such alternatives exists when the
distance is greater than `2/3`.

## 2. Six exchange archetypes

The oriented coefficient multisets are:

| oriented template | coefficient multiset | interpretation | labeled uses |
|---|---|---|---:|
| H8 | `(-1,0,0,0,0,1)` | one-for-one transfer | 84 |
| H7 | `(-1,-1,0,0,1,1)` | two-for-two exchange | 95 |
| H6 | `(-1,-1,-1,1,1,1)` | balanced three-for-three exchange | 6 |
| H5 | `(-1,-1,-1,0,1,2)` | asymmetric three-source exchange | 11 |
| H4 | `(-2,-1,0,1,1,1)` | reverse orientation of H5 | 12 |
| H3 | `(-2,-1,0,0,1,2)` | paired coefficient-two exchange | 2 |
| H2 | `(-2,-1,-1,0,2,2)` | double-receiver exchange | 1 |
| H1 | `(-2,-2,0,1,1,2)` | reverse orientation of H2 | 2 |

H4/H5 and H1/H2 are sign reversals, so there are six unoriented archetypes.
The first two elementary templates account for 179 of the 213 labeled rows
(84 percent).  The two coefficient-one large cases `(3,2,1)` and `(4,2,0)`
use only H6--H8.  Within these three large cases, all genuinely
coefficient-two behavior is concentrated in the balanced `(3,3,0)` case;
the smaller `(2,3,1)` sign case also requires the bound-two pool and is
covered by the independent reconstruction below.

## 3. What the 44 role orbits mean

Within a sign case, a direction is relevant only through:

```text
side (Lmin or Lmax),
multiset of coefficients on P,
multiset of coefficients on M,
multiset of coefficients on E.
```

For example, assigning H8 to one player in `P` and one in `M` is a different
role lemma from assigning it between `P` and `E`, but relabeling two players
inside `P` produces no new lemma.  The exact counts are:

| sign case | labeled rows | role-placement orbits | global templates |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| `(3,2,1)` | 52 | 17 | 3 |
| `(3,3,0)` | 71 | 18 | 7 |
| `(4,2,0)` | 90 | 9 | 3 |

The frozen formulas remain labeled because their disjunctions refer to
specific witness coordinates.  The orbit quotient is therefore explanatory,
not a replacement proof.  It does, however, identify a human target much
smaller than 213 separate directions.

### An irredundant 19-orbit proof core

A second reduction groups the direction implications by the full sign-role
signature above and minimizes at the orbit level.  It produces unguarded
UNSAT formulas with the following smaller menus:

| sign case | original orbits | selected orbits | selected labeled rows | needed coefficient archetypes |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| `(3,2,1)` | 17 | 8 | 29 | H7, H8 |
| `(3,3,0)` | 18 | 5 | 39 | H4, H5, H7, H8 |
| `(4,2,0)` | 9 | 6 | 68 | H7, H8 |
| **total** | **44** | **19** | **136** | **three up to reversal** |

Deleting any one of the 19 selected orbit families makes its corresponding
reduced relaxation SAT.  Those SAT ablations are navigation and irredundancy
controls, not games and not independent proof evidence.  The three unguarded
reduced formulas are independently UNSAT in Z3 and cvc5; their trust-disabled
CPC proofs contain no forbidden markers and check `correct` in Ethos.

The reduced large-case formulas also dispense with the no-unique-Lmax-top
anchor.  They use only the Lmax-top cross anchor and the Lmin-bottom cross
anchor; removing either one makes each corresponding relaxation SAT.  Thus
the dimension-specific large-case argument needs two convex-segment facts,
not all three anchors used by the uniform fifteen-case presentation.

There is a further witness-role simplification in `(4,2,0)`.  In canonical
labels `P={0,1,2,3}` and `M={4,5}`, every blocker alternative retains the
opposite-selection comparison and only the four comparisons contributed by
`q^0,...,q^3`.  The `q^4,q^5` base witness constraints remain, but their
blocker comparisons can be deleted.  The resulting weaker formula is still
UNSAT; deleting any one of its five retained comparison-source families
(opposite, `q^0`, `q^1`, `q^2`, or `q^3`) makes the relaxation SAT.  These
five SAT ablations are again irredundancy controls, not games or proof of the
underlying theorem.

The stronger anchor-localized alternative in `S6_P4_ANCHOR_LOCALIZATION.md`
explains why this witness deletion occurs.  Choose an `x`-bottom anchor `a`
and a `z`-top anchor `b`, both in `P`.  They cannot coincide: for any `m in M`,

```text
x_m >= x_a > z_a >= z_m > x_m.
```

After relabeling the distinct anchors as `a=0,b=1`, the `(4,2,0)` proof needs
only the opposite selection and the single exactness witness `q^b`.  It uses
nineteen labelled directions in ten complete orbits under the residual swaps
of the other two `P`-players and the two `M`-players.  The corresponding
18-real formula and its trust-disabled CPC/Ethos certificate are frozen
separately.  Thus the coincident-anchor branch is closed completely by hand;
only the ten residual orbit families remain machine-resolved.

Thus the remaining human target is 19 sign-role schemas built from only three
unoriented exchange ideas.  In particular, the two coefficient-one large
cases use only one-for-one and two-for-two exchanges.  The balanced case adds
one asymmetric coefficient-two exchange and its reverse.  The H1/H2/H3 and
H6 templates are dispensable from this reduced large-case proof.

For `(4,2,0)`, the anchor-localized alternative replaces the six uniform
C-schemas by a one-line coincident-anchor contradiction and ten smaller
residual role orbits with one witness.

## 4. Independent coefficient-two control

The two strict sign cases that require the coefficient-two pool are separately
rebuilt by `independent_bound_two_reconstruction.py`.  That program imports no
production encoder, lex predicate, blocker, direction pool, minimizer, or SMT
serializer.  Only the frozen retained menus are shared as theorem data.  It
uses different variable names, writes the first-affected-level predicate
directly, rebuilds every negative coalition cut, and expresses the no-unique-
top anchor in the logically dual form.

For both `(2,3,1)` and `(3,3,0)`, the base system without exchange implications
is SAT.  The independently rebuilt full system is UNSAT in Z3 and cvc5, and a
fresh trust-disabled CPC proof checks `correct` in Ethos.  Run

```bash
python3 coopgames/verifiers/s6/verify_independent_bound_two.py
```

to byte-regenerate both formulas and verify their hashes and proof records.

## 5. Remaining humanization boundary

This compression materially improves readability but is not yet an all-human
proof of the three large cases.  A complete hand replacement would have to
show, for each of the 19 selected sign-role schemas, why the eligible negative
cuts forced by the appropriate archetype cannot coexist.  The highest-value
next step is to derive common cut inequalities for A1--A8 and for the ten
anchor-localized `(4,2,0)` orbits, then handle the five-schema balanced core
B1--B5, whose last two schemas form the H4/H5 coefficient-two reversal pair.
