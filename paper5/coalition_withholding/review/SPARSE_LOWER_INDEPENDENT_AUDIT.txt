Sparse upper: rederived details
  1. Packing duality has the required orientation. At coalition R, an optimal
     nonnegative dual w has v(R)=w.b_R and v(R-i)<=w.b_(R-i); therefore
     U_i(R)>=w.b_i. Summing over T subset R and using dual feasibility gives
     sum_(i in T) U_i(R)>=v(T). A static supporting dual is being used only
     inside the proof; no regularity of a selected dual across reports is
     presumed.

  2. The support construction is feasible row by row. For an optimal plan x
     on S, put t_a=(b_T)_a/(b_S)_a on every row actually used by an activity.
     Its retained activity fraction is product_(a in supp(e)) t_a, which is
     at most t_a for each required row. Hence total retained consumption of
     row a is at most t_a sum_e A_ae x_e <= t_a (b_S)_a=(b_T)_a.
     A zero denominator cannot occur in a used positive-consumption row;
     unused rows consume zero. Thus h(T)<=v(T) and h(S)=v(S).
     Repeated assignments of different rows to the same actual owner only
     reduce a piece's distinct-owner support, so its support size is at most
     r even for mixed owners. No strategic owner splitting is involved.

  3. With S=R, v(R)-v(R-i)<=h(R)-h(R-i). Each nonnegative piece appears
     in at most r deletion sums, yielding sum_i U_i(R)<=r v(R).
     Independent owner thinning retains a piece supported on k<=r owners
     with probability p^k>=p^r, giving the stated thinning inequality.

  4. Conditioning on i in the random set yields
       p sum_(i in S) m_i = E sum_(i in S intersect R_p) U_i(R_p).
     The support/dual-cover arguments then give coverage for EVERY S,
     including coalitions larger than s. Funding follows from
       p sum_i m_i <= r E F(b_Rp) <= r F(pB)=rpF(B).
     Scaling by p^(1-r) gives r s^(r-1), with no owner-count dependence.

  5. Independently expanded the semivalue coefficients. For a=|A| between
     1 and n-1, the positive terms are
       |A intersect C| p^(a-1)(1-p)^(n-a),
     and the negative terms are
       (|C|-|A intersect C|)p^a(1-p)^(n-a-1).
     Their difference is exactly the displayed coefficient. The empty and
     full endpoints are -|C|(1-p)^(n-1) and |C|p^(n-1), respectively.
     When p=1/s and |C|<=s, every coefficient with A intersect C nonempty
     is nonnegative. Subsets disjoint from C have both zero withdrawal
     difference and F(delta_A)=0, so their possibly negative coefficients
     cause no reversal of the superadditivity inequality.

  6. The identity sum_A c_C(A)F(delta_A)=M_C(delta) uses the SAME rule and
     fixed owner boxes; delta is a legal profile, with outsiders at zero.
     Coverage there supplies full OUT after scaling. The n=1 endpoints
     remain correct; empty coalitions and the empty institution are trivial.
     The p=1, s=1 case is separately and correctly handled by the grand
     deletion marginal: F(B-b_i) does not change when owner i withholds.
     Thus no p=1 expression with a negative exponent is needed.

Projective lower: rederived details
  7. The full technology consists of all binary projective triples and is
     invariant under GL(M+1,2). The all-ones endowments give identical full
     real unit-cube domains, invariant under both resource and owner
     permutations. Conjugating a rule preserves feasible coordinatewise
     comparisons, coalition sizes, coverage, and outside worth. Finite
     averaging requires no original-rule continuity or measurability.
     A high total payment of the averaged rule implies a high original
     total on some legal orbit profile; it does not assert that the original
     rule is symmetric or that its displayed canonical profile is costly.

  8. At C_m, a sum of private rows from distinct indices has two nonzero
     e-coordinates and lies outside the possible reported row set. The only
     supported triples are consequently the complete active branches.
     They all use the single unit of v, and one attains output one.
     The displayed dual covers the entire technology: unsupported columns
     meet a zero-capacity row, and supported ones contain v.

  9. Both seed maps preserve dormant high reports. The swap v<->e_1 fixes
     e_j for j>1 and v+e_1; the transvection v->v+e_1 fixes every e_j and
     exchanges v with v+e_1. Pairing them with the stated owner swaps fixes
     C_1, including all zero reporters. The three productive owners therefore
     have equal averaged payments, and their coverage gives q_1>=1/3.
     This remains valid when only singleton incentive constraints are imposed,
     because coverage itself has no coalition-size restriction.

  10. At C_m, active-index permutations and the individual transvections
      e_j->e_j+v act transitively on the 2m active private owners. They fix
      v, all inactive high rows, and the zero reports. Thus one common q_m
      is legitimate without equating dormant and active owners.

  11. Each ladder transition restores only the fixed actual owner ell_m
      from zero to one legal resource report. The protected coalition is
      ell_m plus unchanged old active private owners; h_m and p are excluded.
      Its old zero-reporter payment is retained as a nonnegative term before
      dropping it. This gives (2m-1)q_m>=2(m-1)q_(m-1), with the claimed
      coalition size and no dummy-zero assumption.
      Terminal branch coverage and budget give
        kappa >= (z+2q_M)+2(M-1)q_M >= 1+2(M-1)q_M.
      This argument does not set z to a possibly negative value.

  12. The product identity Q_M=4^M/[2 binom(2M,M)] has the correct M=1
      endpoint. The induction polynomial for Q_M^2>=(3M+1)/4 has residual
      exactly M. Taking M=floor((s+1)/2) gives 2M-1<=s and M>=s/2.
      Rules satisfying the stronger size-s contract obey all used comparisons,
      so the passage to the supremum frontier K_3(s) has the correct direction.
      M=1 needs no ladder transitions; terminal coverage supplies factor one.

Capped tail and exact arithmetic
  13. Any k_m=min(s-1,2m-2) old active private owners may be selected; their
      individual payments are equal at both relevant profiles. Complete
      private pairs are unnecessary. Including ell_m gives at most s owners,
      and nonnegativity yields (k_m+1)q_m>=k_m q_(m-1).
      With h=ceil(s/2), the uncapped factors run through h and their product
      is Q_h/h; subsequent factors are alpha=(s-1)/s.

  14. For A_M=(M-1)P_(M,s), the uncapped consecutive ratio for M>1 is
        2M^2/[(M-1)(2M+1)] > 1.
      In the capped phase it is M alpha/(M-1), greater/equal/less than one
      precisely as M<s, M=s, M>s. The M=1 value is zero, with a positive
      next value. This includes s=2, h=1. Thus the two equal maxima occur
      exactly at M=s and M=s+1. Choosing M=s gives the stated 2s+1 owners
      and 2^(s+1)-1 rows; no claim of globally minimal witness size follows.

  15. Q_3=8/5 and B_6=1+(16/9)(125/216)=493/243; its excess over two is
      7/243. The 13-owner, 127-row counts are correct. The asymptotic constant
      follows from Q_h~sqrt(pi h)/2, h~s/2, and alpha^(s-h)->exp(-1/2),
      giving sqrt(2 pi)/(3 sqrt(e)) after division by sqrt(s).

  16. The backwards positive row combination cancels q_M with weight
      residual/(k_m+1), passes residual*k_m/(k_m+1) to q_(m-1), and cancels
      q_1 with the seed weight residual/3. It leaves exactly
      kappa>=1+2(M-1)P_(M,s)/3. At M=1 the two final rows alone give kappa>=1.
