S1. Moulin and Sprumont, On Demand Responsiveness in Additive Cost Sharing
------------------------------------------------------------------------
Author version dated 24 May 2004; original already in CAMP/sources/.
https://www.ruf.rice.edu/~econ/papers/2004papers/dmdrespr41.pdf
Model pp4-5; Strong Group Monotonicity p6; Proposition 2(ii) p12.
Exact excerpt: "no cost-sharing method satisfies Additivity, Separability,
and Strong Group Monotonicity."
Hypotheses: n>=3; nonnegative exactly balanced shares; one integer demand
coordinate per agent; rule additive across all monotone cost functions.
SGM protects the sum paid by ANY coalition containing a single increasing
agent, including unchanged coalition members. Repeating single increases
protects simultaneous increases. This is a direct antecedent of the incentive
axiom. The impossibility is conditional on Additivity and Separability across
technologies. It supplies no funding factor as a function of coalition size
or packing arity, and no representation of arbitrary multirow owner rules.

S2. Sprumont, Nearly Serial Sharing Methods
-------------------------------------------
5 November 2004 version, CIREQ 17-2004; later IJGT 37 (2008), 155-184.
https://www.cireqmontreal.com/wp-content/uploads/cahiers/17-2004-cah.pdf
Model printed p3/PDF6; Theorem 2 and Group Monotonicity printed p9/PDF12.
Exact excerpt: "not all of them get less output."
Theorem 2 characterizes nearly serial methods using Additivity, Zero Output
for Zero Input, Irrelevance of Dummy Changes, Anonymity, and Group Monotonicity.
Every agent has one integer personalized input; total output is exactly split.
Group Monotonicity requires at least one increasing member not lose, rather
than protecting the coalition sum with transfers. It is not TCWM. Its flow
representation is conditional on structural axioms and the scalar-agent
domain. Replacing each resource coordinate by an agent changes actual
coalitions and does not extend this theorem to the campaign's owner bundles.

S3. Sprumont, An Axiomatization of the Serial Cost-Sharing Method
----------------------------------------------------------------
28 December 2009 working-paper version; Econometrica 78 (2010), 1711-1748.
https://epe.lac-bac.gc.ca/100/200/300/cireq/cahiers/2010/01-2010-cah.pdf?nodisclaimer=1
Main unnumbered Theorem: printed p5/PDF8. Discussion (3),(5): printed p18/PDF21.
Exact excerpt: "no additive method satisfies Strong Group Demand Monotonicity
and Dummy."
Continuous scalar demands, continuously differentiable monotone cost functions,
nonnegative exact budget balance. The main characterization uses Additivity,
Weak Dummy, Dummy Independence, Anonymity, and weak Group Demand Monotonicity;
the author notes groups of size one or two suffice for that characterization.
The quoted strong-monotonicity incompatibility is stated in the discussion,
by adapting the earlier argument, not as a quantified approximation theorem.
This verifies that moving from integer to real scalar demands is not itself
a novelty claim. It does not remove Additivity or give the arity/size exponent.

S4. Immorlica, Mahdian and Mirrokni, Limitations of cross-monotonic
    cost-sharing schemes
----------------------------------------------------------------
Author full version of the paper published in 2008.
https://immorlica.com/pubs/costshare.pdf
Definition 3.3 p11; maximum-flow Theorem 3.6 p12; matching Theorem 3.7 p13.
Exact proof excerpt: "Assume ξ is an o(n)-budget-balanced cross-monotonic
profit-sharing scheme for G."
Profit budget: v(S)<=sum_i xi(i,S)<=alpha v(S); absent agents get zero.
Cross-monotonicity: xi(i,S)<=xi(i,S union T) for EACH incumbent i in S.
The flow example has two parallel-edge legs and value min(A,B); the proof
forces expected funding at least (n-1)/2 on value-one sets of n active agents.
This is a close quantitative predecessor, even with arity-two technology.
The additional hypothesis is incumbent monotonicity, stronger than TCWM on
the binary participation subdomain: see the all-real K=2 full-OUT example in
MIN2_SEPARATION.txt. The theorem headlines omit "cross-monotonic"; the section,
definition and proof impose it. They must not be quoted as unrestricted lower
bounds. Here n counts active participants, not protected coalition size s.

S5. Immorlica and Pountourakis, On Budget-Balanced Group-Strategyproof
    Cost-Sharing Mechanisms (2012)
-------------------------------------------------------------------
https://immorlica.com/pubs/edgeCover.pdf
Theorem 3: printed p7/PDF8; set-cover constant lower bound: section 4.2,
printed pp15-18/PDF16-19.
Exact excerpt: "there is a 1-budget balanced GSP mechanism for the edge-cover
problem."
This construction is not cross-monotonic, while cross-monotonic edge-cover
schemes recover at most half the cost. It is a second warning against
automatically transferring cross-monotonic lower bounds to larger mechanism
classes. The model elicits private service valuations and selects who is
served; utility includes service value minus payment. It does not elicit
resource bundles, preserve all coalition production guarantees, or impose
the campaign's transferable coalition-sum withholding condition.

S6. Doğan, Absence-proofness: A new cooperative stability concept
----------------------------------------------------------------
11 September 2013 original working paper. The later published paper is
Absence-proofness: Group stability beyond the core (2016); its numbering is
not asserted to coincide with this version.
https://econ.gatech.edu/sites/default/files/seminars/201402/absence-proofness.pdf
Allocation scheme definition p5; Definition 2.1, Remark 2.1, Proposition 2.1 p6.
Exact excerpt: "An APAS is core selection".
For all T subset K subset S, absence-proofness requires
  sum_(i in K\T) X_i(S\T) + v(T) <= sum_(i in K) X_i(S).
This formula was visually checked because PDF text extraction drops symbols.
It captures some coalition members staying out, earning standalone surplus,
and sharing gains with members remaining inside. Population monotonicity
implies it. The allocation scheme assigns an efficient vector to each
subgame of a fixed TU game. This is a close antecedent of full OUT on binary
participation restrictions. The inspected result has no partial multirow
reports or quantitative subsidized, sparse-arity, bounded-coalition theorem.

S7. Zick, Markakis and Elkind, Arbitration and Stability in Cooperative
    Games with Overlapping Coalitions (2014)
---------------------------------------------------------------------
https://people.cs.umass.edu/~yzick/papers/ocf_theoretical.pdf
Model and partial coalitions pp4-5; arbitration definition pp9-10;
Theorem 4.1 p15; Corollary 5.16 p28 (author PDF pages).
Exact excerpt: "the refined core of G is non-empty."
The corollary assumes the superadditive closure v* is homogeneous of degree
k>=1. Theorem 4.1 characterizes stability by p_S(CS,x)>=A*(CS,x,S).
Owners can withdraw fractional resources from existing coalitions and form
new projects; an arbitration rule determines retained payments. Thus partial
withdrawal and outside production are established concepts. However the
arbitration depends on the prior coalition structure, allocation, and
deviation. It is not the same unconditional payment function evaluated at
every reduced aggregate report profile. The result establishes an outcome's
stability under that contract; it does not supply or rule out the campaign's
uniform K_r(s) allocation rule. The LP duality there is also not new machinery.

S8. Izquierdo and Rafels, The Incentive Core in Co-Investment Problems (2017)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
https://diposit.ub.edu/server/api/core/bitstreams/d325b225-7dde-44ff-8a35-cd848434e593/content
Model p5; Theorem 1 p10; Definition 1 p16; Proposition 2 p17 (PDF=printed).
Exact excerpt: "the proportional allocation is an element of the incentive core."
One homogeneous scalar resource per owner, strictly positive endowments,
production f(total), and nondecreasing average return f(t)/t. Theorem 1 gives
necessary and sufficient inequalities for a given core point to extend
after specified increases with strict gains for contributors and weak gains
for others. Proposition 2 gives a proportional all-profile selection with
that property on this special domain. Complementary multiple resource rows
are absent. This is neither an impossibility nor a general sparse packing
guarantee, but it is a direct core-plus-contribution-monotonicity predecessor.

S9. Meir, Rosenschein and Malizia, Subsidies, Stability, and Restricted
    Cooperation in Coalitional Games (IJCAI 2011, 301-306)
-------------------------------------------------------------------
https://www.ijcai.org/Proceedings/11/Papers/060.pdf
Section 3 and Proposition 3: printed p303/PDF3.
Exact excerpt: "Only coalitions of size at most k are allowed".
For a superadditive TU game the relative cost of stability with allowed
blocking coalitions of size <=k is at most min{k,sqrt(n)}, essentially tight.
The restriction deletes static coalition coverage constraints. In the
campaign, ALL coalition coverage constraints remain and only cross-report
deviation constraints are limited to size s. These are different parameters
on different constraints; substituting k=s does not give the target theorem.

S10. Bachrach et al., The Cost of Stability in Coalitional Games (2009)
--------------------------------------------------------------------
https://arxiv.org/pdf/0907.4385
Original already in CAMP/sources/bachrach_etal_2009_cost_of_stability.pdf.
Definition 3.2 printed p5/PDF7; Theorem 3.5 and projective-plane construction
printed pp6-8/PDF8-10 in that saved version.
Exact excerpt: "and this bound is asymptotically tight."
Theorem 3.5 gives static CoS(G)<=(sqrt(n)-1)v(N) for superadditive games,
with projective planes giving asymptotic tightness. This confirms a geometric
antecedent, not a cross-profile incentive theorem. The existing campaign
comparison is substantively correct; its viewer-page locator needs shifting
to the values above for the saved PDF. No novelty is assigned here to
projective geometry, static subsidies, or square-root rates by themselves.

S11. Bansal, Korula, Nagarajan and Srinivasan, Solving Packing Integer
     Programs via Randomized Rounding with Alterations (2012)
-------------------------------------------------------------------
https://theoryofcomputing.org/articles/v008a024/v008a024.pdf
Model printed p535/PDF3; integrality-gap discussion printed p552/PDF20.
Exact excerpt: "integrality gap of k − 1 + 1/k".
The discussion uses a projective plane of order k-1 for k-set packing, and
compares integral and fractional optimization values. This is not claimed
as the first discovery of that classical example. It verifies another
relevant use of projective geometry with column sparsity. The campaign uses
the fractional production LP itself, and its lower bound is on payments
across profiles. An integer-program integrality gap is a different ratio;
it cannot be substituted for K_r(s).

S12. Birmpas, Markakis and Schäfer, Cost Sharing over Combinatorial Domains:
     Complement-Free Cost Functions and Beyond (ESA 2019)
-------------------------------------------------------------------------
https://drops.dagstuhl.de/storage/00lipics/lipics-vol144-esa2019/LIPIcs.ESA.2019.20/LIPIcs.ESA.2019.20.pdf
Model, WGSP and beta-budget balance: printed 20:5/PDF5;
cross-monotonicity Definition 2: printed 20:6/PDF6.
Exact excerpt: "makes all its members strictly better off."
This original does permit multidimensional reports, but they are valuations
for subsets of supplied services. The mechanism chooses an allocation and
charges nonnegative service payments; budget balance compares charges with
service cost. WGSP forbids a deviation improving every member's individual
utility, without the campaign's coalition-sum protection under side payments.
Thus "multi-parameter cost sharing" is insufficient to transfer the result
to actual owners' independently withdrawable input rows. No target exponent
was found in the inspected model/theorem sections.

S13. Moulin and Sprumont, Fair Allocation of Production Externalities:
     Recent Results (7 December 2005 author survey)
--------------------------------------------------------------------
https://www.ruf.rice.edu/~econ/papers/2005papers/moulin%2009.pdf
Scalar model and Additivity p5; SGM p19; near-serial Theorem 10 p26.
Exact excerpt: "the total cost share of any coalition S containing her cannot
decrease."
This survey confirms the intended interpretation of the earlier originals:
SGM includes unchanged coalition members and transferable gains. It explicitly
separates that property from weak Group Monotonicity and derives flow-method
claims under Additivity. Its use of "subsidy-free" versus "cross-subsidizing"
concerns allocation of a fixed total cost among users, not an outside funder
paying K times realized output. It is supporting context, not an independent
priority claim or a theorem covering arbitrary multirow rules.

S14. Moulin and Vohra, Characterization of additive cost sharing methods
-----------------------------------------------------------------------
April 2003 original; Economics Letters 80(3), 399-407.
https://www.ruf.rice.edu/~econ/papers/2003papers/07Moulin.pdf
Model and Theorem 1 printed/PDF p3; Theorem 2 p4; Corollary 3 p5.
Exact excerpt: "we do not assume the Dummy axiom."
Nonnegative exactly balanced shares, integer scalar demands, and Additivity
over all monotone cost functions. At fixed x, all such methods are convex
mixtures of labeled-chain methods, equivalently unit-flow representations
with arbitrary recipient shares on each edge. Dummy restricts recipients
in Corollary 3; it is not required by the general theorem. The flow/labels
may depend on x. This is a direct antecedent for additive architectures,
but not a representation for arbitrary subsidized multirow-owner rules or
a single fixed min_r technology. No size-s funding lower bound is supplied.
See FIXED_LINEAR_PRIORITY_ADDENDUM.txt for the precise comparison.

S15. Sprumont, Aumann--Shapley pricing: A reconsideration of the discrete case
----------------------------------------------------------------------------
19 June 2004 author version, CIREQ 11-2004; distinct from published 2005 title.
https://cireqmontreal.com/wp-content/uploads/cahiers/11-2004-cah.pdf
Model printed p4/PDF7; main theorem printed p6/PDF9; corollary p8/PDF11.
Exact excerpt: "The Aumann-Shapley method is the only cost-sharing method".
The theorem assumes Additivity, Dummy, and No Merging or Splitting; the
corollary replaces the last condition using No Reshuffling and independence
of zero-demand agents. Scalar integer demand coordinates, nonnegative exactly
balanced shares. Neither manipulation axiom equals coalition withholding.
The 2005 published article's primary abstract advertises a bundle extension:
https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/j.1468-0262.2005.00633.x
That extension is NOT present in this inspected working paper. Its exact
published theorem remains an open priority lead, not an inspected theorem
and not evidence of either subsumption or non-subsumption.
