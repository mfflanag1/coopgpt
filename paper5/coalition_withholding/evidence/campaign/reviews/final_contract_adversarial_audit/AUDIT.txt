1. COMMON QUANTIFIERS
An institution fixes a finite technology, the actual owner set N, each owner's
actual endowment, and eligibility. Its report domain is the product of the
corresponding real nonnegative underreport boxes. A rule y is defined on this
entire domain. Nonnegativity and coverage hold for every report and every
coalition; TCWM or full OUT holds for every protected coalition of size <=s,
including members whose reports remain unchanged. Funding is total payments
<=kappa F(b_N) at every report, including zero-production reports.

The upper constructs such a rule for EVERY eligible institution, with the same
factor 3s^2 independent of owner/resource counts. The lower constructs ONE
eligible finite institution for each parameter and proves that EVERY such rule
has an expensive feasible report. These are exactly the opposing quantifiers
needed for a worst-instance uniform funding factor. Since full OUT implies
TCWM by nonnegative production, the same lower applies to full OUT and the
usual ordering is K3(s)<=K3_OUT(s).

The lower's use of a finite subset of real reports is legitimate: it imposes
necessary consequences of the all-profile contract on an otherwise arbitrary
payment function. It does not assume that the rule only exists on that subset.

2. FINITE SYMMETRIZATION AND THE LOCATION OF THE LOWER
For the fully eligible construction, every actual owner has the all-ones
endowment, so owner permutations and GL(V) resource permutations map the full
report box onto itself. The projective triples, their unit profits, coalition
values, coordinatewise underreport comparisons and coalition sizes are
preserved. Conjugating a rule by any such group element preserves each contract
inequality. Their finite average therefore preserves the inequalities and the
same funding factor without continuity, measurability, or anonymity assumptions.

Writing a group action on reports as g.b and its owner permutation as sigma_g,
one can define the conjugated rule by y^g(b)=sigma_g^(-1)y(g.b). The averaged
rule is equivariant, so stabilizers of a report force payment equalities.
Total payment of this average at C_M equals the average of original total
payments at g.C_M. Every g.C_M is legal and has production 1. Consequently a
lower on averaged total payment implies that at least one of these finitely
many original profiles attains that lower. It does NOT force the unsymmetrized
rule to be expensive at the named C_M. The source expressly makes the correct
worst-case claim (principal proof lines 61-73; tail lines 26-29).

No actual owner changes identity during any strategic comparison. The group
action is a proof operation on rules, and each resulting comparison uses the
same owner labels, eligibility and technology at its two endpoints.

3. SUPPORT, DORMANT REPORTS, AND THE SEED
The rows v,e_1,...,e_M are linearly independent. Among
S={v} union {e_j,e_j+v:1<=j<=M}, the only projective triples are
{v,e_j,e_j+v}: sums of private rows from different indices have two nonzero
e-coordinates. At C_m exactly m of these triples are supported. All consume
the sole reported unit of row v. A branch gives production 1; a dual pricing
v and every zero-capacity row at 1, all other rows at 0, proves the full-LP
upper 1. Unsupported activities cannot bypass this certificate.

At C_1 the inactive high owners STILL report e_j for j>1. Both required seed
maps preserve those reports individually: (i) v<->e_1 fixing other e_j, and
(ii) v->v+e_1 fixing every e_j. The corresponding swaps of p with h_1 or l_1
preserve the whole owner-indexed profile. Inactive low owners report zero and
remain zero. The three seed owners thus receive a common a; their coalition
produces 1, so coverage gives a>=1/3 and T_1=2a>=2/3. Coverage, unlike TCWM,
is unrestricted in coalition size, including when s=1 or s=2.

At C_m, active-index permutations and independent active flips e_j->e_j+v
fix v and inactive e_j, preserve all reports after matching owner swaps, and
act transitively on the 2m active private owners. Their common payment q_m
is therefore justified by a stabilizer of the entire profile.

4. FIXED-OWNER LADDER, PAYMENTS AT ZERO, AND TERMINAL COVERAGE
The transition C_(m-1)->C_m changes only actual owner l_m, from 0 to e_m+v.
Protect the 2m-2 old active private owners together with l_m. Its cardinality
is 2m-1, and all old members have unchanged reports. TCWM gives
  (2m-1)q_m >= 2(m-1)q_(m-1)+y_(l_m)(C_(m-1))
                >= 2(m-1)q_(m-1).
No dormant payment has been equated to zero. Nonnegative omitted payments
are dropped only in a valid lower bound. The unchanged high owner h_m and
root p are not included in this protected coalition.

Iteration gives q_M>=Q_M/(3M), with
Q_M=product_(m=2)^M 2m/(2m-1). Hence total payment at C_M is at least
(2/3)Q_M. Writing z for the root payment, terminal branch coverage is
z+2q_M>=1. Adding it to the total-budget inequality gives the stronger
  kappa>=1+2(M-1)q_M>=1+[2(M-1)/(3M)]Q_M.
This algebra remains valid even when 2q_M>1; it does not assume z=1-2q_M.
The stated rational multiplier cancellation is correct, including zero
weights at M=1.

For fixed s>=2, replacing the old coalition by any
k_m=min(s-1,2m-2) old active owners yields
(k_m+1)q_m>=k_m q_(m-1), so
  B_(M,s)=1+[2(M-1)/3] product_(m=2)^M k_m/(k_m+1)
is valid. With h=floor((s+1)/2), the initial product is Q_h/h and all later
factors are alpha=(s-1)/s. In the tail, for M>1, the ratio of consecutive
(M-1)P_(M,s) values is M alpha/(M-1). It is >1 for M<s, =1 for M=s,
and <1 for M>s. The two maxima M=s and M=s+1 and formula B_s are correct.

5. INTEGER ENDPOINTS, COUNTS, AND THE PURE REALIZATION
Principal M=1 has three owners, three rows, no ladder, and terminal lower 1.
For arbitrary integer s>=1, M=floor((s+1)/2) gives 2M-1<=s. Thus a rule
protected through s obeys every inequality used. Q_M>=sqrt(3M+1)/2 follows
from the displayed polynomial identity and base equality; M>=s/2 proves
K3(s)>=sqrt(s/6). For s=2 this principal choice uses M=1 and makes no hidden
three-member TCWM comparison.

The tail excludes s=1 and correctly uses ordinary coverage lower 1 there.
At s=2 it has h=1, P_(M,2)=(1/2)^(M-1); B_2=B_(3,2)=4/3. The ratio proof
does not divide by zero at M=1: that value is simply 0 before the positive
increment at M=2. At s=6, the arithmetic gives B_6=493/243 with M=6.

The fully eligible institution has n=2M+1 and d=2^(M+1)-1, in particular
n=2s+1 and d=2^(s+1)-1 for the tail's M=s choice. The pure institution has
one actual owner for EVERY nonzero projective row and therefore n=d, with
one-dimensional report intervals. Its allowed symmetry is the JOINT GL(V)
resource/owner action, not an independent owner permutation. This maps each
owner's report to the appropriate legal row of the permuted owner and hence
preserves the same typed institution. Its seed and private stabilizers
preserve the active row set, so additional zero owners are only permuted
among zero reports. All original argument steps follow with the same fixed
restoring owner i_(v+e_m). The small owner count and pure eligibility are
separate constructions and cannot be claimed simultaneously.

At the displayed profiles row v is a reported veto. In the fully eligible
institution any owner can instead report a complete triple. In the pure
institution for M>=2 a two-dimensional subspace avoiding any specified row
supplies a triple without that row's owner. These observations establish the
stated absence of a globally indispensable owner; they do not remove the
profilewise veto used by the proof.

6. INDEPENDENT CHECK OF THE INHERITED UPPER
After dropping zero-profit columns, each activity is bounded by at least one
strictly positive resource coefficient at every finite capacity vector. The
packing value is therefore finite and attained, F(0)=0, homogeneous, concave,
monotone and superadditive. These properties include zero capacities.

Assigning each used activity's required resource rows to actual suppliers
in their report fractions gives a nonnegative owner-hypergraph support of
size <=r. The product of row fractions is at most any individual row fraction,
so the retained activity plan on any owner subset is feasible. This proves
both sum_i U_i(R)<=rF(b_R) and E F(b_(S intersect R))>=p^r F(b_S).
An optimal nonnegative dual price proves sum_(i in T) U_i(R)>=F(b_T)
for every T subset R. Inclusion conditioning then proves full coalition
coverage of y_i=p^(1-r)m_i, while concavity gives total funding
<=r p^(1-r)F(b_N). Monotonicity gives nonnegative payments.

For p=1/s in (0,1), every value coefficient in sum_(i in S)m_i that meets
S is nonnegative, since |A intersect S|-p|S|>=0. Under a withdrawal d
supported on S, superadditivity therefore gives
  M_S(b)-M_S(b-d)>=M_S(d).
Terms disjoint from S have both zero difference and F(d_A)=F(0)=0, even
when their coefficient is negative. Crucially d is feasible in the SAME
actual-endowment boxes, with the SAME n owners and eligibility. Coverage
at d proves full OUT. The proof allows d_i=0 for unchanged protected members.

At s=1, grand marginals prove the claim separately; no singular coefficient
formula is used. At s=2, a zero coefficient for a set containing exactly one
member of a protected pair is harmless. For s>n the coefficient argument
still works. The upper formula happens to pay zero to zero-report owners,
which is an allowed mechanism property, not an added contract axiom.
With r=3 the factor is 3s^2 on TOTAL payments, not on payments net of F.

7. LIMITATIONS TO PRESERVE IN THE HANDOFF
- The lower is for the full sparse-packing class with growing dimensions;
  its displayed resource count is exponential in the ladder length.
- The lower concerns arbitrary nonnegative mechanisms, not only marginal
  rules. No marginal floor, linearity, continuity, or dummy-zero axiom is used.
- A nonsymmetric rule need not be expensive at the named C_M; the lower
  identifies a report in its finite symmetry orbit.
- Every coalition receives coverage, but only coalitions of size <=s receive
  TCWM/full-OUT protection. Unchanged members are part of that requirement.
- B_s optimizes this particular ladder certificate, not all certificates or
  mechanisms. The argument does not close the sqrt(s) to s^2 gap.
- The upper is an exact finite-expectation existence construction; no efficient
  implementation or Monte Carlo guarantee follows.
- Nothing here establishes novelty, empirical applicability, sponsor value,
  institutional rights, or an operational capacity entitlement.
