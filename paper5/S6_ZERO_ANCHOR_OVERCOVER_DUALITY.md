# Zero-anchor over-cover duality

## 1. Status and purpose

The singleton witness used in the localized `(4,2,0)` proof is a point

```text
q in R_+^N,   q(N)=1,   q_b=0.
```

Many apparently unrelated blocker filters are consequences of the same
elementary projection theorem below.  The theorem is standard finite
convex/linear duality, not a novelty claim.  Its value here is explanatory:
it turns long affine certificates into sparse incidence over-covers and gives
an exact algorithm for finding them.

## 2. Exact witness-image theorem

Fix coalitions `C1,...,Cr` and define the incidence column of player `i` by

```text
a^i_j = 1 if i is in Cj, and 0 otherwise.
```

The coalition-sum vector generated by `q` is

```text
T(q)=(q(C1),...,q(Cr)).
```

**Theorem (zero-anchor witness image).**

```text
{T(q): q>=0, q(N)=1, q_b=0} = conv{a^i: i != b}.       (Z1)
```

Consequently lower requirements `q(Cj)>=rj` are feasible exactly when

```text
sum_j lambda_j rj
  <= max_{i != b} sum_{j: i in Cj} lambda_j             (Z2)
```

for every nonnegative vector `lambda`.

**Proof.**  Since `q_b=0` and the other coordinates are convex weights,

```text
T(q)=sum_{i != b} q_i a^i,
```

which proves (Z1), including the converse by taking `q=e_i`.  The feasible
lower-bound region is `conv{a^i:i!=b}-R_+^r`.  Its support function in a
nonnegative direction `lambda` is the maximum in (Z2); separation gives
necessity and sufficiency.  Equivalently,

```text
sum_j lambda_j q(Cj)
 = sum_{i != b} q_i sum_{j:i in Cj} lambda_j
 <= max_{i != b} sum_{j:i in Cj} lambda_j,
```

and a maximizing unit vector attains the bound.

Thus the exact projected witness polytope is the convex hull of at most
`n-1` zero-one columns.  Its downward closure has a finite rational facet
description.  A sparse witness obstruction is simply a nonnegative supporting
normal `lambda`; it can be found by a small LP or by enumerating upper facets
of those incidence columns.

## 3. The class-5 identities are incidence covers

Use anchor `b=1`.  In the first two `h10`/`h15` crossing orbits, the three
witness comparisons use a packet coalition, an `h10` blocker `B`, and an
`h15` blocker `C`.  Their incidence sum is

```text
1_N + 2e_1.
```

Every non-anchor player therefore has load one, so (Z2) says that the three
left sides sum to at most one.  In the third orbit the four comparison
coalitions have incidence sum

```text
2 1_N + 2e_1,
```

and their witness sums are at most two.  The weak-root exclusion uses `P4`
and `125`; their non-anchor loads are at most one (player 3 has load zero), so
their witness sums are again at most one.  These are exactly the `sum(q)` and
`q1` terms in the previously displayed rational identities.

The remaining allocation-side inequalities show that the corresponding
lower bounds exceed one or two by a strictly positive `d0`/sign margin.  The
solver-free class-5 checker verifies those affine reductions.  The witness
part itself is now the one-line incidence theorem (Z2), rather than four
unrelated Farkas sums.

## 4. Algorithmic consequence for the packet program

At any relay state:

1. collect the packet and blocker coalitions whose `q^b` comparisons are in
   force;
2. form the `(n-1) x r` non-anchor incidence matrix;
3. compute the upper facets of its column convex hull, or solve the separation
   LP in (Z2) against the proposed allocation-side lower bounds;
4. retain a sparse extreme normal `lambda` and exactify its rational
   coefficients.

This is an exact-cone/facet theorem for the witness component.  It does not
by itself control the opposite-allocation comparisons or activation logic,
and it does not prove classes 5 or 6.  It does explain why one singleton
witness can filter many blocker combinations and provides a principled route
for compressing their remaining non-root leaves.

## 5. Exhaustive class-6 application

The asymmetric class-6 relay has 49 direction nodes and 924 eligible cut uses.
Exact difference-cone elimination removes 711.  Of the 213 candidates, 11 are
excluded by the already active packet/ancestor witness context, 155 by the
target witness, and 47 are exactly the relay branches.  Every one of the 155
target-witness exclusions has a support inequality of the form (Z2)
using at most five available comparisons: 150 use only unit coefficients and
five require coefficient two.  The support-size distribution is
`12,82,27,30,4` for sizes one through five.

The same theorem gives supports of size at most five for the 11 context
exclusions; eight have unit coefficients and three require coefficient two.
Their allocation lower bounds deduplicate to nine exact rational identities,
checked solver-free at all 11 path-valid uses.

The allocation lower bounds have separately been exactified into 53 rational
identities.  A solver-free checker reconstructs every named premise and checks
all 155 uses, including strictness.  Of those uses, 118 require only global
anchor/sign, root-order, or packet premises; 33 use ancestor comparisons and
four also use the target comparison.  Hence the incidence theorem plus those
identities completely explains the arithmetic of every witness rejection.
The frozen use maps still come from the shared localized relay.  A separate
solver-free census checks the disjoint exhaustive partition
`213=11+155+47`, so every terminal no-blocker closure is arithmetically
certified; deriving those use maps from the game-theoretic semantics remains
the external-review boundary.

The same geometry controls activation.  The 49 class-6 direction nodes have
124 elementary failure conjunctions.  Anchor/order chains remove 84; four
more are allocation-infeasible; and all remaining 36 admit incidence
over-covers of support two, three, or four (`12,10,14` cases).  Thirty use
unit coefficients and six use coefficient two.  The activation incidence
side is therefore also complete.  Its allocation half reduces to 26 exact
rational lower-bound identities for the 36 witness uses and one direct
identity for the other four cases, all checked solver-free.  The relay-derived
coverage map remains encoding-shared.
