## Claims under review

### PXC115 — natural player operations are not failure-preserving

The paper fixes literal definitions of restriction, zero-normalized
contraction, and quotient/amalgamation.  Exact rational witnesses show:

1. deleting `a` from the six-player PXC101 game leaves the exact cone;
2. deleting either one of the defining-row-parallel, selector-equal bottom
   players from the seven-player PXC101 game also leaves the exact cone;
3. zero-normalized contraction of root player 0 in the PXC116 sparse failure
   gives a five-player game whose singleton mask 1 has stated worth `0`
   but core-envelope worth `1`, so contraction need not preserve exactness
   even inside the target class;
4. contracting one of two incidence-parallel/equal-selector bottom players
   in the seven-player PXC101 game gives an exact normalized divergent
   two-top game in which both extreme covers are faithful;
5. literal quotient/amalgamation of the same pair gives the same 64 game
   values and the same faithful result;
6. deleting a zero-Lmax player from the PXC116 failure leaves the exact cone;
7. adding a genuine null player to PXC104 preserves its core selectors plus
   zero, but changes the extreme-cover polytope and creates faithful covers.

The conclusion is deliberately negative: these operations do not support a
universal *failure-preserving* reduction theorem.  This does not assert that
the safe class lacks an excluded-minor characterization in the usual
opposite direction, and it does not exclude conditional reductions whose
admissibility is independently checkable.

### PXC116 — PXC101 is not the unique minimal common primitive

The coordinatewise lower envelope of the six total-18 allocations

```text
(0,13,1,3,0,1)  (12,0,0,1,1,4)  (12,5,0,0,0,1)
(4,10,1,0,0,3)  (11,3,0,1,0,3)  (3,11,1,3,0,0)
```

is exact and singleton-normalized, with

```text
Lmin = (25/4,25/4,1/2,9/4,1/2,9/4)
Lmax = (6,6,0,2,1,3).
```

Its unique complete proper-row certificate is `{19,43}`.  Its complete
proper z-tight menu is `{3,4,7,19,23,39,43,47}`, and its five qualifying
extreme covers are

```text
{3}, {19,47}, {23,43}, {4,19,43}, {7,19,43}.
```

Every lift has Lmax `(6,6,0,5/2,1,5/2)`.  The common descent
`(0,0,0,1,0,-1)` vanishes on the certificate and every cover-eligible row;
row `39` is the unique negative z-tight blocker and occurs in no cover.
Because PXC101 has two covers, the games are not relabeling-equivalent.
PXC100 supplies dimension minimality by excluding every five-player failure.

### PXC117 — natural-operation impossibility and infinite antichain

An operation schema is called universally safe only if every applicable
exact singleton-normalized sparse-failure tuple is carried to another such
tuple with its mapped selectors, complete certificate, qualifying-cover
quantifier, and universal failure intact. Among the requested literal
schemata, only relabeling is universally safe. Restriction, normalized
contraction, parallel deletion, and selector-zero deletion have explicit
exactness counterexamples. Amalgamation/quotient always preserves exactness,
and preserves singleton normalization exactly when the amalgamated block has
worth zero, but the parallel PXC102 example shows that it need not preserve
selector fidelity.

For the quasi-order generated by those universally safe audited schemata,
the non-null PXC101 family, one game in every dimension n >= 6, is an
infinite antichain. Hence that natural universal relation has no finite
forbidden-minor basis. This is deliberately not a theorem about every future
conditional relation: a stronger relation would need a local,
verdict-independent admissibility criterion and a new antichain audit.

## Prior-art boundary

The operation names are standard and are not claimed as new.

- Owen-style quotient games replace every union by a player and set the
  quotient worth equal to the worth of the corresponding union of blocks;
  this is exactly the formal shape of PXC115 amalgamation.  See the open
  definition and historical discussion in Zou, Li, Uetz et al. (2023):
  https://doi.org/10.1007/s00291-022-00694-9.
- Merger operations are known to alter cores in general; preservation in
  pairwise bipartite matching games is a special theorem.  See
  Hezarkhani (2016): https://doi.org/10.1016/j.orl.2016.10.004.
- Exact games are known to be totally balanced, which only guarantees
  balanced subgames, not exact subgames.  The definitions and hierarchy are
  reviewed in Studený's min-balanced-system work:
  https://library.utia.cas.cz/separaty/2019/MTR/studeny-0508311.pdf.
- Weighted lexicographic role contraction is not silently substituted for
  the literal player operations audited here. The paper proves its own
  block-expansion interface; PXC115 concerns the unweighted selectors after
  literal deletion, contraction, or amalgamation.
- Oriented-matroid deletion/contraction and excluded-minor questions are
  standard, and excluded-minor characterizations fail at that level in broad
  generality.  This does not automatically imply the selector-specific
  PXC115 no-go.  See Anderson, *Oriented Matroids*, Chapter 2 (2025):
  https://doi.org/10.1017/9781009494076.003.

The potentially new content is narrower: the exact interaction of these
operations with complete staged Lmin certificates, normalized extreme
top-cover polytopes, and later Lmax fidelity.  The search found no source
stating the PXC115 preservation failures or the PXC116 five-cover primitive
or the PXC118 four-cover reciprocal augmentation under another name. The
PXC117 antichain is relative to the explicitly
audited universal relation, not an oriented-matroid-wide antichain theorem.
This is a dated negative search, not a novelty verdict; a cooperative-game
specialist must assess it before any novelty claim.

## Current frontier

The universal natural-minor program now has a dimension-free impossibility
and infinite-antichain answer, and literal canonicality is false in both
PXC114 branches. PXC118 is a second exact dimension-minimal reciprocal type:
it has two complete certificates and four covers, versus PXC104's one and
three, while every lift fails and the common descent intersection remains
empty. The surviving open problem is a non-circular locally admissible
conditional reduction or a stronger exact-cone realization theorem. A
bounded incidence census alone is insufficient.
