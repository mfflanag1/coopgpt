QUESTION AND EXACT MODEL
Can one stable allocation give each participant a fixed fraction of that
participant's own maximum stable payoff? Fix a connected simple allowed graph G
on K>=2 sectors, each with the same finite number m of players. Every full team
contains one player from every sector. Its value sums nonnegative edge-pair
values; zero entries and zero matrices are allowed. Only complete teams produce.
Coalition worth is an integral matching optimum. Grand worth T is positive and
the entire labeled core C is nonempty. H_i=max_{p in C}p_i. All coordinates,
including permanent zeros, remain in full nominal leximin; ratios use H_i>0.

CLAIMS TO ASSESS
Write rho(M)=max_{p in C}min_{i:H_i>0}p_i/H_i. Over all such markets on fixed G,
allowing m to grow, the sharp infimum of rho is 1/K for trees and 0 for cyclic G.
The same full nominal-leximin allocation attains all tree floors H_i/K.
For the triangle family, m=3q+1 and rho=1/(2q+3), q>=1. For a general cyclic G,
the construction gives rho<=1/(2q+2), and one ideal-one target has nominal-
leximin payoff 1/[K(q+1)]. Pair values remain in {0,1,2}; a strictly positive
core point exists. Each fixed finite market still has rho>0. The worst-protected
player can depend on the chosen allocation. This is neither an empty-core
result nor a finite-market zero or a strictly-positive-edge theorem.

PROOF DEPENDENCIES AND REQUESTED CHECKS
1. Section3 describes the entire tree core by cut coordinates. Coordinatewise
   minimum of translated cuts preserves difference constraints and reconstructs
   nonnegative payments. Clipping raises a violated target without worsening
   a lower nominal-payoff level. Check rerooting, ties and zero coordinates.
2. Sections4-5 derive the triangle's whole simplex and its individual ideals.
   A partial matching leaves a unit payoff budget. Check that the lower witness
   meets all ideal-scaled floors and that 1/(2q+3) is exact.
3. Section6 transfers the obstruction by selective subdivision. Check the
   column-bonus domination, collection of every positive bonus by the partial
   matching, preservation of the target ideal, and propagation under repetition.
   Extra allowed edges may have zero matrices. No equality of entire lifted
   cores or preservation of every old ideal is assumed.
4. Priority: does an earlier theorem imply this quantitative classification
   under the exact strict-full-team/entire-core model? A theorem/page locator
   or counterexample would be more useful than a general endorsement.

KNOWN INGREDIENTS AND THE PROPOSED CONTRIBUTION
Assignment models, matching duality, core maxima, leximin, convex averaging and
min-closure of difference constraints are established. Bilateral half-optimal
core protection is credited to Nunez-Rafels; graph decomposition and balancedness
to Atay-Nunez in their productive-partial-team model. Broader examples of
vanishing ideal protection are also credited. The proposed contribution is the
specific sharp quantitative classification for the stated class, not a new
fairness criterion or the first vanishing-protection example.

The three-player path comparison concerns the BEST simultaneous fraction:
strict teams give ideals (2,2,2), fraction1/3; productive adjacent pairs give
ideals (1,2,1), fraction1/2. Nominal leximin is (2/3,2/3,2/3) in both and has
minimum ideal ratio1/3 in both. Do not call the second fraction a leximin bound.
