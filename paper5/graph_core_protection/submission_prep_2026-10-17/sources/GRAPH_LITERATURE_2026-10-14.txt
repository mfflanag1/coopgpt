1. CLOSEST GRAPH RESULT: ATAY–NÚÑEZ (2019)
Primary final original: https://ataatay.github.io/webpage/Atay_Nu%CC%81n%CC%83ez_2019_ANOR.pdf
DOI: https://doi.org/10.1007/s10479-019-03256-5
Annals of Operations Research279:271–290. Read model pp272–275, Theorem1/argument pp279–282, Corollary1 p283, Proposition2 pp284–285, selection discussion pp285–286.
Theorem1 gives the composition of bilateral cores on trees. Corollary1 characterizes strong balancedness by acyclicity (at least three agents per sector). Proposition2 gives simultaneous maxima within each sector. Their connected partial-sector teams also generate value; ours require a full team. Thus neither their entire core nor its ideals can be substituted into ours. Their cyclic empty-core examples do not establish any failure conditional on a nonempty core.
Inference, not their stated theorem: choose bilateral endpoint allocations favoring the two colors of a tree's bipartition. Their decomposition makes each color simultaneously optimal; the midpoint protects half of every ideal in THEIR model. This further emphasizes the model difference.

Concrete independently checked model contrast, suitable for introduction:
One player in each of three sectors on a path; adjacent pair values1,1; T=2.
Strict full-team core: p>=0,p1+p2+p3=2; H=(2,2,2), best fraction1/3.
Productive-partial-team core additionally imposes p1+p2>=1,p2+p3>=1;
H=(1,2,1), best fraction1/2, attained at(.5,1,.5). The upper follows from
sum H=4 and T=2. A market's nominal leximin is a different optimization from
maximizing min_i p_i/H_i; adding permanent zero coordinates changes neither
optimizer's nonzero coordinates and cannot be offered as a novelty distinction.
