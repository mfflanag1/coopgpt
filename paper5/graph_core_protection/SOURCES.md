# Sources and priority boundaries

**14 October 2026 update:** the [submission source ledger](submission_2026-10-14/GRAPH_LITERATURE.txt) records the refreshed originals, verified AAMAS 2026 publication, strict/partial-team distinction, and remaining Quint/Lucas/Stuart gaps. The current manuscript uses those comparisons; the following is the retained September record.

This consolidation carries forward the documented source comparisons; it does
not claim a new exhaustive literature search on September 20, 2026.

- Nunez and Rafels, Bargained stable allocations in assignment markets,
  Barcelona Economics Working Paper 153 (December 2004), Definition 6:
  https://www.bse.eu/sites/default/files/working_paper_pdfs/153.pdf .
  The bilateral half-optimal core directly precedes simultaneous ideal protection.
  Neither the benchmark nor the bilateral half guarantee is proposed as new.
- Atay and Nunez, Multi-sided assignment games on m-partite graphs (2019):
  https://doi.org/10.1007/s10479-019-03256-5 . Tree decomposition and tree/cycle
  balancedness are precedents, but their productive partial-sector coalitions
  give a different core. Acyclicity as an organizing principle is not claimed new.
- Atay and Solymosi (2018), supplier-firm-buyer model:
  https://ataatay.github.io/webpage/Atay_Solymosi_2018_EL.pdf . The nonnegative
  three-sector chain specialization is credited, not invented here.
- Martinez-de-Albeniz, Rafels and Ybern (2026), maximum and minimum core payoffs:
  https://doi.org/10.1016/j.dam.2025.10.006 . Same-sector simultaneous extrema
  require their supermodularity/local-monotonicity hypotheses; no general
  transfer to the present class is presumed.
- Their distinct 2023 preprint, Extreme core points in multisided assignment
  problems: https://doi.org/10.2139/ssrn.4369524 . The prior campaign inspected
  the full original. Its general representation is not excluded merely by
  non-supermodularity, and no all-relabeling exclusion is asserted.
- Quint (1991), Lucas (1995), and Stuart (1997): bibliographic/model precedents
  are credited, but their full original examples and implications remain
  incompletely compared. Paid access was declined. Missing access does not
  count as evidence of novelty.

The proposed contribution is the precise strict-full-team protection theorem,
its constructive whole-core proof, and exact growing-family analysis. General
LP duality, matching tightness, difference constraints, simplex descriptions
as a technique, and leximin are established tools.

The original triangle construction, tree argument, and subdivision transfers
are earlier results within this same project. This manuscript consolidates
and strengthens one research line, rather than counting them as separate
independently discovered papers. The graph theorem, exact triangle protection,
and finite verification coverage have distinct quantifiers.

Detailed prior comparisons are preserved in sources/ORIGINAL_COMPARISONS.md.
Third-party PDFs, temporary download links, and browser information are not
included in the sharing archive. No publication or correspondence was sent.
