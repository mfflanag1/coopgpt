Why lead with this paper
The economic question is concrete: does stability permit every participant a
fixed fraction of their own most favorable stable payoff? On a fixed K-sector
tree the sharp size-independent guarantee is 1/K, attained by the same nominal
leximin allocation for everyone. For every cyclic allowed graph the best possible
simultaneous fraction vanishes along growing finite markets with positive cores.
The simplex calculation gives exact 1/(2q+3) in the triangle. The proof has a
coherent mechanism: tree cuts and clipping versus a unit unmatched-payoff budget.

The result is narrowly model-dependent. Equal sizes, complete-team production,
nonnegative pair matrices including zeros, the entire core, and growing market
size are explicit. A new three-player illustration contrasts the strict core's
ideals (2,2,2) with the productive-partial-team core's ideals (1,2,1). Their best
fractions are 1/3 and 1/2. Nominal leximin is uniform in both; the illustration
must not be described as a 1/2 nominal-leximin result.
