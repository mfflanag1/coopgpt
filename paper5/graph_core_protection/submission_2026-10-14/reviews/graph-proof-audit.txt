1. Model and quantifiers
The nonnegative efficient full-team-cover description is exactly the entire cooperative core. Necessity uses singleton worth zero, grand-coalition efficiency and full-team deviations. Sufficiency sums covers over any integral matching in any coalition and retains nonnegative payoffs of unmatched players. No fractional matching value enters this equivalence.

The assumptions are consequential: connected simple fixed sector graph, K>=2, common finite sector size m, all full teams available, nonnegative pair matrices, no productive proper-sector team, positive grand worth, and nonempty entire core. Zero matrices on allowed edges must remain permitted for the cyclic embedding. The cyclic zero constant is an infimum as m grows, not a finite-market zero. A finite core has positive simultaneous fraction by averaging one maximizer for each positive ideal; zero-ideal coordinates stay in the core and nominal leximin optimization.

2. Tree optimal matching and nonempty core
For each tree edge choose a maximum-weight perfect bipartite matching. Nonnegativity and equal sizes allow any partial matching to be completed without losing value, so the perfect and unrestricted matching optima coincide. Relabel along the rooted tree: unique paths make all selected edge matchings diagonal simultaneously. Every full-team matching projects to matchings on the edges; the diagonal full-team matching attains their summed upper bound. Hence the displayed T is the actual integral optimum. Summing nonnegative optimal bilateral vertex covers over incident edges yields a nonnegative full-team cover of the same total. This uses ordinary integral bipartite matching duality. All diagonal teams are tight at every core point because their lower bounds sum to efficiency.

3. Entire-core cut bijection
For a child subtree S_b, compare the team using label i on S_b and label j outside with the tight all-j team. Internal diagonal differences cancel, leaving U_b(i)-U_b(j)>=A_ab(j,i)-d_ab(j). Taking descendant sums minus child sums gives the reconstruction formula; root reconstruction uses tightness of the full diagonal team. Conversely reconstruction at arbitrary labels telescopes into one term per oriented edge,
U_b(i_b)-U_b(i_a)+d_ab(i_a),
which dominates A_ab(i_a,i_b). At a common label it telescopes to the diagonal worth, so total efficiency is correct. Nonnegativity then proves all coalition inequalities by Section 1. Descendant sums recover U, so this is a bijection with the entire core, not only a selected face or a set of sufficient covers.

4. Clipping and the universal 1/K bound
Uniform translations and coordinatewise minima preserve every difference constraint. At a nonroot vertex, selecting the x cut bounds every child by its x cut, giving z_v>=x_v; selecting the shifted y cut gives z_v>=y_v+(s_v-sum_children s_b) beta=y_v+beta. At the root, child cuts bounded separately by x and shifted y give both z_r>=x_r and z_r>=y_r-(K-1)beta. The first root bound and nonroot bounds preserve nonnegativity. Thus the clipped point is actually in the entire core.

If target payoff alpha violates H_i<=K alpha, choose alpha<beta<H_i/K and an ideal-attaining y. Root clipping raises the target above beta. Every coordinate initially <=alpha weakly increases, while every coordinate initially >alpha remains >alpha, since y>=0 and beta>alpha. The earliest altered sorted level therefore improves at or below alpha; decreases above alpha cannot undo it. This argument includes alpha=0 and permanent-zero coordinates. Rerooting changes proof coordinates only, so the SAME fixed nominal full-leximin point satisfies every player's bound. Hence it also witnesses rho>=1/K.

Sharpness is valid with m=1 and edge values totaling one: only the full K-player team has worth one, the entire core is the unit simplex, all ideals are one and both criteria equal 1/K. The additional L1 bound follows from min(x_i,y_i)>=y_i/K, and the same simplex attains equality.

Minor exposition correction: successive maxima of continuous order statistics exist on nested nonempty compact optimal sets. Such sets need not be faces of the original core: in the unit simplex the unique uniform max-min point lies in its relative interior, so its singleton is not a face. “Optimal face” is defensible only with a stated lifted LP interpretation. Replace “compact optimal faces” and, where appropriate, “first optimal face” by “compact optimal sets” / “first-stage optimal set”. The proof uses only set membership and forced coordinates, so no result changes. The standard averaging proof establishes uniqueness: at the smallest payoff level whose labeled membership differs between two putative optima, their average has fewer coordinates at that level while all lower coordinates agree.

Diagonal tightness reconstructs b_L,b_R and strong a. Critical E_s and N_s give o_s>=l_(s-1) and l_s>=alpha+r_s+d_s+o_s. Defining their nonnegative slacks e_s,f_s and h=b_Lq yields exactly

Conversely l_s is nondecreasing, l_q=1-alpha-h, r_s<=l_s-alpha and d_s+o_s<=l_s-alpha-r_s. These ensure all reconstructed coordinates are nonnegative and strong a>=3. The four positive C cases for weak a and the four strong-a cases cover every triple exactly as claimed. Recovery of all parameters proves injectivity. 7. Selective subdivision, zero edges and fixed graph embedding
For h=j the unshifted lifted cover (z,D) exactly adds the diagonal bonus. For h!=j its slack relative to the new team is at least D_h+B(i,j)-B(i,h)>=0. This is why column domination and nonnegative B are essential. The extended diagonal matching has total T+sum D and matches the cover total, proving integral optimality and tightness of every new diagonal team.

The shifted floor lift subtracts t/k from each old coordinate and adds t above D at the new coordinate. Every new full-team sum is unchanged relative to the unshifted lift. Total efficiency is preserved because there are exactly m players in EACH old/new sector. Old payoffs remain >=tau-t/k=t for t=k tau/(k+1), and new ones are >=t. For a partial matching, new labels equal the old v labels and remain distinct. Collecting every positive bonus is a genuine hypothesis; when satisfied the shortfall stays delta and unmatched count becomes (k+1)(m-r). The budget then pins unmatched coordinates at the new floor. A target's ideal is preserved only under the separately stated diagonal-worth/attainment/zero-bonus conditions. The proof does not require an unjustified whole-core isomorphism or preservation of every ideal.

In this family the B-positive columns are exactly H={O_s,L_s:1<=s<=q}. Their maxima are two, the critical matching uses all of them in c, and the target label L_0 has bonus zero. After subdivision the newly adjacent sector's used labels remain H, so induction on the still-B edge is valid. Each step adds 4q worth, preserves shortfall one, preserves target ideal one and changes the forced floor to 1/[k(q+1)]. All pair entries remain 0,1,2 and the shifted witness remains strictly positive.

A zero-interaction sector has a zero unshifted lift and the same balanced floor shift with D=0. Extending the partial matching by r distinct labels preserves its shortfall; the old target remains unmatched and its tight diagonal worth stays one. Choosing a simple cycle in arbitrary cyclic allowed G, realizing that cycle and assigning zero matrices to all remaining allowed edges gives precisely the claimed model. This does not prove the corresponding statement when every allowed matrix/entry must be strictly positive, and the manuscript appropriately excludes that extension.

8. All-selection conclusion
The 2q+2 original b,c players in U retain ideal-at-least-one witnesses under each unshifted lift and remain unmatched. Their ideal sum is therefore at least 2q+2 in the enlarged core. The same unit budget gives rho<=1/(2q+2) for every fixed cyclic graph construction. Equality for longer cycles is neither shown nor claimed. Letting q grow gives R(G)=0; nonnegativity and pointwise/infimum c(G)<=R(G) give c(G)=0. The badly protected player may depend on the selected allocation, as stated. This is strictly stronger than failure of one named selector.

9. Finite verification versus universal proof
Retained reports claim 120 tree clipping checks, 56 strict improvements, 15 selected path/star/binary markets on K=2..6 (m=3 at K=4, otherwise m=2); these are seeded finite examples. The raw-core solver enumerates perfect matchings to compute integral T and retains all nonnegative full-team constraints. The clipping checker probes selected ideal-attaining points and first-label targets at each root, not every real tree market or every pair of core points.

The cyclic report has 13 explicit cases / 822,002 cover checks, 12 selected raw-core UNSAT queries and four rejected corruptions. The 33,864 edge-mask census is graph support/embedding coverage, NOT a complete independent replay of full-team inequalities for every mask and every matrix. The simultaneous report covers q=1..8 triangle vertices/witnesses, six extensions, 2,553,912 cover checks, 16 q=1..4 SMT queries and three corruption controls. None of these finite checks quantifies over arbitrary q,m,K or real pair matrices. README and manuscript correctly identify conventional proofs as the universal justification. Historical PASS records were inspected, not newly executed in this audit.
