# Draft specialist review request — not sent

We have prepared a consolidated draft comparing full leximin and full descending-lexicographic-minimum selections in the entire cores of unit-capacity matching games. We would appreciate a correctness and priority assessment of the quantitative comparison.

The draft proves a sharp n-player graph bound floor(n/2)/(floor(n/2)+1), then constructs standard three-sided assignment games with nonempty entire cores and values in {0,1,2,3} whose normalized L1 disagreement tends to two. The latter family has only O(n) positive triples and a three-dimensional entire core. Complete additive three-sided markets retain a one-third individual guarantee and a four-thirds distance bound.

We explicitly attribute the bipartite half-core-maximum guarantee as a short consequence of Vazirani's existing algorithmic invariants; our proof is elementary and extends to nonbipartite nonempty-core graphs. We do not claim the fairness rules or pairwise-equality agreement results are new.

The most useful questions are:

1. Is the sharp finite graph distance already explicit or immediate in older egalitarian/adjustment theory?
2. Do Quint (1991), Lucas (1995), or a later representation theorem imply the three-sided result while preserving the entire core, player coordinates and the full selectors? We have not accessed those two originals.
3. Is the raw recurrence construction correct, particularly the complementary triple constraints, integral matching certificate and uniqueness at the first nonfixed leximin floor?
4. Is the comparison a meaningful contribution in this literature, and which assumptions or prior papers should change its presentation?

The eight-page manuscript, dated claim matrix, source audits, access logs, exact checks and independent proof audit accompany the draft. We seek criticism and source corrections, not endorsement of a priority claim. This request is prepared locally; no recipient has been contacted.
