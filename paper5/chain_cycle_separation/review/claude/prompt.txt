22: \begin{abstract}
23: We compare a player's leximin payoff with its largest payoff in the same
24: assignment-game core. In complete strict three-sided markets with nonnegative
25: pair values along a chain, every player receives at least one third of this
26: core ideal. The constant is sharp. Allowing the third pair interaction
27: eliminates every positive guarantee independent of market size. For each
28: integer $q\geq1$, we construct a market with $9q+3$ players, pair entries in
29: $\{0,1,2\}$, and a strictly positive core allocation, in which a player's
30: full leximin payoff is $1/[3(q+1)]$ while its core ideal is one. The chain
31: bound follows from a classical potential-slack balancing certificate whose
32: cycles contain three payoff arcs. The cyclic construction is certified by
33: two explicit full-core allocations and a disjoint partial matching one unit
34: below optimum. The proofs are valid for every parameter value; exact rational
35: checks and optional raw-core satisfiability checks accompany the manuscript.
36: \end{abstract}

41: \section{Introduction}
42: A core allocation protects every coalition against a profitable deviation.
43: Leximin additionally favors the worst-paid individuals, successively through
44: all payoff levels. These two requirements do not by themselves say how much
45: of an individual's feasible bargaining range is retained. For a full core
46: $\core$ and its full ascending leximin allocation $x$, we study the fraction
47: $x_i/H_i$, where $H_i=\max\{y_i:y\in\core\}$ is the ideal payoff of player $i$.
48: The maximization defining $H_i$ permits all other payoffs to change.
49: 
50: Our comparison keeps the number of sectors fixed at three. Only complete
51: triples produce value; a pair does not earn its pair contribution on its own.
52: Each triple value is the sum of nonnegative values on its three pair
53: interactions. A chain uses two of these interactions, while the full class
54: also permits the third. We prove that the best uniform ideal-protection
55: fraction is $1/3$ for chains and zero for the full pair-additive class.
56: Thus a restriction on the interaction structure has a consequence that
57: cannot be recovered from nonemptiness of the core alone.
58: 
59: \paragraph{Contributions and established ingredients.}
60: The main construction realizes a vanishing fraction using a fixed integer
61: alphabet of pair values, three equal sectors, and a core allocation positive
62: in every labeled coordinate. Its target is already fixed on the first
63: optimal leximin face, so no arbitrary choice among max-min optimizers is
64: involved. The positive chain theorem provides the comparison: a
65: self-contained specialization of classical selected-arc slack balancing
66: gives the factor three. The underlying fairness benchmark, assignment
67: models, LP duality, and slack-balancing method are established ingredients.
68: We do not claim a new general fairness criterion or the first vanishing
69: example across all cooperative games. Historical priority of the precise
70: restricted-class separation is the question addressed by the accompanying
71: source-comparison and specialist-review packet; no external priority
72: endorsement is asserted here.

74: \paragraph{Related work.}
75: Multisided assignment cores have a substantial literature, including

77: supplier--firm--buyer model is associated with Stuart~\cite{Stuart1997};
78: Atay and Solymosi~\cite{AtaySolymosi2018}, Definition~3 and equations~(1)--(2),
79: give a precise later formulation allowing signed adjacent-pair values and
80: replacing negative triple totals by zero. Their core and bargaining-set
81: results concern stability sets, rather than the coordinate-ideal fraction
82: studied here. Nonnegative chain values are a subclass of that model.
83: Generalized assignment markets in which smaller teams also produce value
84: have additional coalition constraints and must be distinguished from our
85: strict model~\cite{AtayNunez2020}.

87: Ideal-based core protection is explicit in N\'u\~nez and
88: Rafels~\cite{NunezRafels2004}, Definition~6, in their bilateral bargaining
89: analysis. A unit-capacity path in Gangam et al.~\cite{Gangam2025},
90: Section~3.1.4, has uniform equitable payoff $1/r$ on its $r$ edges.
91: Its full core is the unit simplex, so each ideal is one: vanishing
92: protection in a broader game class follows immediately. That path uses a
93: productive coalition whose size grows with $r$. Our construction instead
94: uses only strict triples and pair values in $\{0,1,2\}$.

115: \section{Model and the separation theorem}
116: Let $I,J,K$ be disjoint sectors of equal finite size $m\geq1$, and put
117: $N=I\cup J\cup K$. Every triple $(i,j,k)\in I\times J\times K$ is
118: available and has nonnegative value $w_{ijk}$. A matching is a collection of
119: player-disjoint triples, possibly empty. For $S\subseteq N$, define
120: \[
121:  v(S)=\max\left\{\sum_{(i,j,k)\in M}w_{ijk}:
122:        M\text{ is a matching contained in }S\right\}.
123: \]
124: In particular, singletons and pairs have value zero. Write $T=v(N)>0$.
125: The full payoff core is
126: \begin{equation}\label{eq:core}
127:  \core=\left\{p\in\R_+^N:\sum_{a\in N}p_a=T,
128:                 \quad p_i+p_j+p_k\geq w_{ijk}
129:                 \ \forall(i,j,k)\right\}.
130: \end{equation}
131: This is exactly the coalitional core: necessity follows from individual
132: rationality and triple coalitions, and sufficiency follows by summing
133: triple inequalities over a matching of any coalition and using
134: nonnegativity for unmatched players. Equal sector sizes and nonnegative
135: values allow every partial matching to be completed without loss, so
136: $T$ is attained by a perfect matching.
137: 
138: For $p\in\R^N$, let $p^\uparrow$ be its coordinates sorted in
139: nondecreasing order. The full leximin allocation is the point $x\in\core$
140: that lexicographically maximizes $p^\uparrow$ over $p\in\core$.
141: Throughout, all labeled coordinates remain in this optimization, including
142: coordinates with zero ideal.
143: 
144: \begin{lemma}\label{lem:unique}
145: Every nonempty compact convex subset of $\R^N$ has a unique labeled full
146: leximin allocation.
147: \end{lemma}
148: \begin{proof}
149: Each sorted coordinate is continuous. Maximizing these coordinates
150: successively over nested nonempty compact sets gives existence.
151: For uniqueness, suppose distinct $x,y$ have the same optimal sorted vector.
152: Let $\alpha$ be the smallest value among $x_a,y_a$ at coordinates where
153: they differ. Coordinates below $\alpha$ agree. At every remaining
154: coordinate, $(x_a+y_a)/2\geq\alpha$, and equality holds only when both
155: entries equal $\alpha$. Since $x$ and $y$ have the same multiplicities of
156: each value and differ at level $\alpha$, their average has strictly fewer
157: entries at that level, with all lower levels unchanged. Its sorted vector
158: is lexicographically larger, contradicting convexity and optimality.
159: \end{proof}
160: 
161: When $\core\ne\varnothing$, it is compact by~\eqref{eq:core}.
162: Set $H_a=\max_{p\in\core}p_a$ and only form $x_a/H_a$ when $H_a>0$.
163: A \emph{chain market} has
164: \begin{equation}\label{eq:chain}
165:  w_{ijk}=P_{ij}+Q_{jk},\qquad P,Q\geq0.
166: \end{equation}
167: A \emph{pair-additive market} has
168: \begin{equation}\label{eq:pair}
169:  w_{ijk}=A_{ij}+B_{ik}+C_{jk},\qquad A,B,C\geq0.
170: \end{equation}
171: The chain class is contained in the pair-additive class after renaming the
172: matrices. Let $c_{\rm chain}$ and $c_{\rm pair}$ be the infima of $x_a/H_a$
173: over the respective classes with $T>0$, nonempty full core, and $H_a>0$.
174: 
175: \begin{theorem}[Chain--cycle separation]\label{thm:main}
176: \hfill
177: \begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
178: \item Every chain market has nonempty core, and its full leximin allocation
179:       satisfies $x_a\geq H_a/3$ for every player. The constant is sharp.
180: \item For every integer $q\geq1$, there is a pair-additive market with
181:       $9q+3$ players, all pair entries in $\{0,1,2\}$, and every triple
182:       value at most $5$, with integral worth $T=6q+1$, a strictly positive
183:       core allocation, and a target player satisfying
184:       \[
185:              x_a=\frac{1}{3(q+1)},\qquad H_a=1.
186:       \]
187:       The target is fixed at this value throughout the entire first
188:       optimal max-min face of the core.
189: \end{enumerate}
190: Consequently $c_{\rm chain}=1/3$ and $c_{\rm pair}=0$.
191: \end{theorem}
192: 
193: Part~(ii) shows that \emph{allowing} a cyclic interaction removes the
194: class-wide guarantee. It does not assert that every cyclic market has weak
195: protection, or classify interaction graphs with more than three sectors.
196: The fixed integer alphabet is substantive: merely bounding real weights
197: could be achieved by rescaling each instance without changing $x_a/H_a$.

199: \section{The chain bound}
200: \subsection{The entire core as a potential system}
201: Choose maximum-weight perfect matchings in $P$ and $Q$. Their values sum
202: to $T$: any triple matching projects to two bilateral matchings, and the
203: two chosen matchings can be joined through their common middle players.
204: Relabel the outer sectors independently so both chosen matchings are
205: diagonal, and set $s_j=P_{jj}+Q_{jj}$. Write payoffs $(u,v,z)$ in sector
206: order. At every core point, summing the diagonal triple inequalities and
207: using efficiency makes each one tight. Thus
208: \begin{equation}\label{eq:diag}
209:                  u_j+v_j+z_j=s_j.
210: \end{equation}
211: The full core is precisely the set defined by
212: \begin{align}
213:  u_j,z_j&\geq0, & v_j=s_j-u_j-z_j&\geq0,\label{eq:nonneg}\\
214:  u_i-u_j&\geq P_{ij}-P_{jj} &&(i,j),\label{eq:U}\\
215:  z_k-z_j&\geq Q_{jk}-Q_{jj} &&(j,k).\label{eq:Z}
216: \end{align}
217: Indeed, the triples $(i,j,j)$ and $(j,j,k)$ imply the two differences;
218: adding them and substituting~\eqref{eq:diag} gives the inequality for an
219: arbitrary triple. Totals in~\eqref{eq:diag} add to $T$.
220: 
221: Nonemptiness follows also from standard bipartite matching duality. The
222: maximum-weight matching LP with incidence constraints at most one has
223: nonnegative dual vertex prices and an integral optimum. For nonnegative
224: weights on a complete equal-sided graph, that optimum is also the perfect
225: matching optimum. Take optimal nonnegative covers $(\alpha_i,\beta_j)$
226: for $P$ and $(\gamma_j,\delta_k)$ for $Q$. Then
227: $(\alpha_i,\beta_j+\gamma_j,\delta_k)$ is a nonnegative triple cover
228: of total $T$, hence a point of~\eqref{eq:core}. This uses classical
229: matching integrality and LP duality, not a new balance criterion.

231: \subsection{A cycle certificate for selected payoff slacks}
232: Consider a finite directed graph, a distinguished anchor with potential
233: zero, and slacks
234: \[
235:        \sigma_e(q)=c_e+q_{\mathrm{tail}(e)}-q_{\mathrm{head}(e)}\geq0.
236: \]
237: Some arcs are designated as player payoffs; the others are structural
238: constraints. Parallel arcs are allowed. We use the following elementary
239: optimality certificate for classical selected-slack balancing.
240: 
241: \begin{lemma}\label{lem:cycle}
242: Suppose a feasible potential vector realizes the full leximin payoff $x$.
243: For a payoff arc $e$ with distinct endpoints, there is a simple directed
244: cycle containing $e$ such that all its structural arcs are tight at $x$
245: and all its payoff arcs $f$ satisfy $x_f\leq x_e$. If that cycle has $r$
246: payoff arcs, then every feasible payoff vector $y$ satisfies $y_e\leq r x_e$.
247: \end{lemma}
248: \begin{proof}
249: Put $\alpha=x_e$. Form a directed subgraph $D$ consisting of the tight
250: structural arcs and all payoff arcs with value at most $\alpha$. Suppose
251: there is no path in $D$ from the head of $e$ to its tail. Let $S$ be the
252: nodes that can reach the tail in $D$, including the tail itself. The head
253: does not belong to $S$. No arc of $D$ enters $S$ from its complement.
254: 
255: Set a potential direction $h$ equal to one on $S$ and zero outside it;
256: subtract its anchor value everywhere to preserve the anchor. Every arc
257: of $D$ then has nonnegative slack derivative, and $e$ has derivative one.
258: All omitted structural arcs have strictly positive slack. All omitted
259: payoff arcs have value strictly above $\alpha$. Since there are finitely
260: many arcs, a sufficiently small positive step preserves feasibility and
261: keeps every decreasing payoff above $\alpha$. No payoff at or below
262: $\alpha$ decreases, and at least one increases. Its sorted payoff vector
263: therefore strictly improves, a contradiction.
264: 
265: There is consequently a head-to-tail path in $D$. Take a simple one.
266: It cannot contain $e$, since that would revisit its starting head or
267: reach its terminal tail before the end. Appending $e$ gives a simple
268: cycle $Z$. Potential differences telescope around this cycle. At $x$
269: its structural slacks vanish, so for every feasible $y$,
270: \[
271:  \sum_{\substack{f\in Z\\f\text{ payoff}}}y_f
272:  \leq\sum_{f\in Z}c_f
273:  =\sum_{\substack{f\in Z\\f\text{ payoff}}}x_f
274:  \leq r x_e.
275: \]
276: The first inequality discards nonnegative structural slacks at $y$.
277: Nonnegativity of the other payoff slacks then gives $y_e\leq r x_e$.
278: \end{proof}
279: 
280: Neither connectedness of $D$ nor positive payoff levels were assumed.
281: If $x_e=0$, the same certificate implies that the coordinate is zero at
282: every feasible point.

284: \subsection{Exactly three payoff arcs}
285: Introduce nodes $0,U_j,Z_j$ with potentials
286: $q_0=0$, $q_{U_j}=u_j$, and $q_{Z_j}=-z_j$.
287: The payoff arcs are
288: \begin{center}
289: \begin{tabular}{lll}\toprule
290: Arc & Constant & Slack\\\midrule
291: $U_j\to0$ & $0$ & $u_j$\\
292: $0\to Z_j$ & $0$ & $z_j$\\
293: $Z_j\to U_j$ & $s_j$ & $v_j=s_j-u_j-z_j$\\\bottomrule
294: \end{tabular}
295: \end{center}
296: Structural arcs $U_i\to U_j$ have constants $P_{jj}-P_{ij}$;
297: structural arcs $Z_j\to Z_k$ have constants $Q_{jj}-Q_{jk}$.
298: Their nonnegative slacks give~\eqref{eq:U}--\eqref{eq:Z}.
299: Diagonal structural loops may be omitted. Structural constants may be
300: negative; only slacks are constrained to be nonnegative.
301: 
302: Every simple directed cycle containing a payoff arc has exactly three
303: payoff arcs. Structural arcs remain in one layer. The only transition
304: from the $Z$ layer to the $U$ layer is a middle-player arc; the return
305: passes through $0$, using one arc from each outer sector. A simple cycle
306: visits $0$ at most once, so it cannot make two such transitions.
307: Lemma~\ref{lem:cycle} gives $y_a\leq3x_a$ for every $y\in\core$.
308: Maximizing over $y$ proves part~(i) of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}.
309: 
310: For sharpness, use one player in each sector with $P_{11}=1$ and
311: $Q_{11}=0$. Its full core is the three-coordinate unit simplex,
312: $x=(1/3,1/3,1/3)$, and every ideal is one. Thus no larger uniform
313: fraction is possible.
314: 
315: \begin{corollary}\label{cor:distance}
316: For every chain market and every $y\in\core$,
317: \[
318:                     \frac{\|x-y\|_1}{T}\leq\frac43.
319: \]
320: This bound is attained for a finite market.
321: \end{corollary}
322: \begin{proof}
323: The theorem implies $\min(x_a,y_a)\geq y_a/3$ for every $a$.
324: Since both totals are $T$,
325: $\|x-y\|_1=2(T-\sum_a\min(x_a,y_a))\leq4T/3$.
326: The preceding simplex and $y=(1,0,0)$ give equality.
327: \end{proof}

329: \section{A cyclic family with vanishing protection}
330: Fix an integer $q\geq1$. Use the common team-label set
331: \[
332:            \mathcal T=\{L_0\}\cup\{O_s,L_s,R_s:1\leq s\leq q\}.
333: \]
334: For every label $t$, create players $a_t,b_t,c_t$ in the respective
335: sectors. Each sector has $3q+1$ players. Define
336: $w(a_t,b_u,c_v)=A_{tu}+B_{tv}+C_{uv}$ by the following nonzero entries;
337: all unspecified pair entries are zero.
338: \begin{center}
339: \begin{tabular}{lll}\toprule
340: Pair entries & Range & Value\\\midrule
341: $C_{L_s,L_s}$ & $0\leq s\leq q$ & $1$\\
342: $C_{R_s,R_s}$ & $1\leq s\leq q$ & $1$\\
343: $A_{O_s,O_s},\ B_{O_s,O_s}$ & $1\leq s\leq q$ & $2$\\
344: $A_{O_s,R_s},\ B_{O_s,L_s}$ & $1\leq s\leq q$ & $2$\\
345: $C_{R_s,L_s},\ C_{L_{s-1},O_s}$ & $1\leq s\leq q$ & $1$\\\bottomrule
346: \end{tabular}
347: \end{center}
348: There are $8q+1$ nonzero entries. The diagonal perfect matching consists
349: of $(a_t,b_t,c_t)$ for all $t\in\mathcal T$. Each $L$ or $R$ triple has
350: value one and each $O$ triple has value four, so this matching is worth
351: $6q+1$.

353: \subsection{Two explicit full-core covers}
354: Put $\tau=1/[3(q+1)]$. Define allocations $u$ and $y$ in $(a,b,c)$ order:
355: \begin{equation}\label{eq:witnesses}
356: \begin{array}{c|c|c}
357: \text{team}&u&y\\\hline
358: L_s&\bigl(\tau,\ 1-(3s+2)\tau,\ (3s+1)\tau\bigr)&(0,0,1)\\
359: R_s&\bigl(\tau,\ 1-2\tau,\ \tau\bigr)&(0,1,0)\\
360: O_s&\bigl(4-(3s-1)\tau,\ \tau,\ (3s-2)\tau\bigr)&(3,0,1).
361: \end{array}
362: \end{equation}
363: The $L$ row uses $0\leq s\leq q$; the other rows use $1\leq s\leq q$.
364: Every coordinate of $u$ is at least $\tau>0$, and $y$ is nonnegative.
365: The smallest $L$-sector $b$ coordinate is attained at $s=q$ and equals
366: $\tau$; the smallest $O$-sector $c$ coordinate is attained at $s=1$
367: and also equals $\tau$. Each diagonal triple receives its value at
368: both points, so both allocations have total $6q+1$.
369: 
370: We check every triple cover, including all mixed-team triples. If its
371: $a$ player has an $L$ or $R$ label, its $A$ and $B$ entries are zero.
372: The possible positive $C$ pairs and the associated two-player sums are
373: \begin{center}
374: \begin{tabular}{lll}\toprule
375: $(b,c)$ labels & $u_b+u_c$ & $y_b+y_c$\\\midrule
376: $(L_s,L_s)$ & $1-\tau$ & $1$\\
377: $(R_s,R_s)$ & $1-\tau$ & $1$\\
378: $(R_s,L_s)$ & $1+(3s-1)\tau$ & $2$\\
379: $(L_{s-1},O_s)$ & $1-\tau$ & $1$\\\bottomrule
380: \end{tabular}
381: \end{center}
382: All four pair values are one. Adding $u_a=\tau$ or $y_a=0$ covers the
383: triple. Zero $C$ entries are covered by nonnegativity.
384: 
385: Now suppose its $a$ player is $a_{O_s}$. If at most one of its $A$ and
386: $B$ entries is positive, the triple value is at most three, covered by
387: $u_{a_{O_s}}\geq3+4\tau$ and $y_{a_{O_s}}=3$.
388: If both entries are positive, exactly four cases remain:
389: \begin{center}
390: \begin{tabular}{llll}\toprule
391: $(b,c)$ labels & $w$ & $u_a+u_b+u_c$ & $y_a+y_b+y_c$\\\midrule
392: $(O_s,O_s)$ & $4$ & $4$ & $4$\\
393: $(O_s,L_s)$ & $4$ & $4+3\tau$ & $4$\\
394: $(R_s,O_s)$ & $4$ & $5-3\tau$ & $5$\\
395: $(R_s,L_s)$ & $5$ & $5$ & $5$\\\bottomrule
396: \end{tabular}
397: \end{center}
398: Since $\tau\leq1/6$, all are covers. The cases also show that every
399: triple value is at most five.
400: 
401: The matching gives worth at least $6q+1$, while either nonnegative
402: cover bounds every matching by its total $6q+1$. Hence the true integral
403: worth is $T=6q+1$ and both $u,y$ belong to the entire core. No fractional
404: matching value is substituted for coalition worth.

406: \subsection{A partial matching determines the target}
407: For each $s=1,\ldots,q$, take the triples
408: \[
409:  N_s=(a_{O_s},b_{R_s},c_{L_s}),\qquad
410:  E_s=(a_{L_{s-1}},b_{L_{s-1}},c_{O_s}).
411: \]
412: Their values are five and one, respectively. They form a player-disjoint
413: partial matching: the used $a$ labels are the $O$ labels and
414: $L_0,\ldots,L_{q-1}$; the used $b$ labels are the $R$ labels and the same
415: $L$ labels; the used $c$ labels are $L_1,\ldots,L_q$ and the $O$ labels.
416: Its total is $6q$. Its unmatched players are exactly
417: \begin{equation}\label{eq:unmatched}
418:  U=\{c_{L_0},a_{L_q},b_{L_q}\}
419:        \cup\{a_{R_s},b_{O_s},c_{R_s}:1\leq s\leq q\},
420:        \qquad |U|=3q+3.
421: \end{equation}
422: Summing the partial-matching inequalities and subtracting from efficiency
423: gives, at every core point $p$,
424: \begin{equation}\label{eq:budget}
425:                        \sum_{a\in U}p_a\leq1.
426: \end{equation}
427: Thus a common payoff floor cannot exceed $1/(3q+3)=\tau$. The point
428: $u$ attains this floor, so the first global leximin level is exactly
429: $\tau$. At every point of the first optimal face, all coordinates are
430: at least $\tau$, and $|U|\tau=1$. Inequality~\eqref{eq:budget} therefore
431: fixes every coordinate of $U$ at $\tau$ on that entire face. In particular,
432: the full leximin target $c_{L_0}$ is fixed at $\tau$ through every later
433: refinement. We do not need, and do not assert, that $u$ is the complete
434: final leximin vector.
435: 
436: The diagonal $L_0$ triple is tight at every core point and has value one.
437: Nonnegativity implies $H_{c_{L_0}}\leq1$, while the cover $y$ attains
438: one at that coordinate. Consequently
439: \[
440:        H_{c_{L_0}}=1,\qquad x_{c_{L_0}}=\frac{1}{3(q+1)}.
441: \]
442: This proves part~(ii) of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}. Ratios are nonnegative,
443: and choosing arbitrarily large $q$ makes this ratio arbitrarily small;
444: hence $c_{\rm pair}=0$.

446: \section{Scope and certificates}
447: \paragraph{The construction uses the cyclic class essentially.}
448: The displayed presence of three matrices alone would not rule out another
449: chain representation of the same tensor. A direct slice obstruction does.
450: If $M_{ij}=\max\{r_i+s_j,0\}$ and both diagonal entries of a $2\times2$
451: slice are positive, then
452: $M_{11}+M_{22}\leq M_{12}+M_{21}$: the underlying diagonal sums equal
453: the underlying off-diagonal sums, and taking positive parts cannot reduce
454: the latter. For every $q\geq1$, our tensor has the following slices:
455: \begin{center}
456: \begin{tabular}{llll}\toprule
457: Varied sectors & Fixed player & Row; column labels & Slice\\\midrule
458: $a,b$ & $c_{L_0}$ & $(O_1,L_0);(O_1,L_0)$ & $\left(\begin{smallmatrix}2&1\\0&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)$\\[3pt]
459: $a,c$ & $b_{L_0}$ & $(O_1,L_0);(O_1,L_0)$ & $\left(\begin{smallmatrix}3&1\\1&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)$\\[3pt]
460: $b,c$ & $a_{L_0}$ & $(L_0,R_1);(L_0,R_1)$ & $\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&0\\0&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)$\\\bottomrule
461: \end{tabular}
462: \end{center}
463: Every slice violates the necessary inequality. Whatever sector is chosen
464: as the middle of a chain, the other two would give a truncated-separable
465: slice. Thus no sector ordering or within-sector relabeling makes this
466: tensor a strict chain, even allowing signed adjacent-pair entries with
467: zero-truncated triple values. This does not exclude a representation
468: using additional players or a different payoff core.
469: 
470: \paragraph{Reproducible evidence.}
471: The supplementary certificate package uses exact rational arithmetic.
472: Its default verifier reconstructs the cyclic family independently and
473: checks every triple against both covers at $q=1,2,3,4$, along with
474: matching values, disjointness, the unmatched budget and ideal attainment.
475: It performs $7208$ triple-cover checks. Frozen input files are also
476: verified independently against their recorded quantities. An optional
477: Z3 check at $q=4$ starts only from nonnegativity, efficiency and all raw
478: triple inequalities and excludes: a larger common floor; a target above
479: $\tau$ anywhere on the first optimal face; and a target ideal above one.
480: The chain controls include simplex sharpness and a core with permanent
481: zeros in which arbitrary first-stage maximizers need not satisfy the
482: bound. The all-size assertions follow from the proofs above, not from
483: extrapolating these finite checks. The certificates are exact arithmetic
484: and solver checks, not a formalization in a proof assistant.
485: 
486: \paragraph{Limits and further questions.}
487: The construction does not prove a best finite-size ratio or a minimum
488: number of players. Corollary~\ref{cor:distance} concerns chains and makes
489: no new claim about opposing selectors in the cyclic class. Classification
490: for more general interaction graphs, robustness of the construction to
491: perturbations, and independent historical priority assessment remain
492: separate questions.
