1: # Exact addition of sectors with zero pair values
2: 
3: Candidate conventional proof. The standing model allows zero matrices on edges of a fixed connected sector graph. This is not a theorem under a requirement that every allowed edge have a positive entry.
4: 
5: ## Source and extension
6: 
7: Start with k>=2 sectors of equal size m>=1, nonnegative full-team values, integral coalition matching, positive grand worth T, and nonempty entire core C. Only complete k-tuples are productive. Add ell>=1 sectors of the same size and define each full (k+ell)-team's value to be the value of its projection onto the original k sectors. All new incident pair matrices are zero in a pair-additive source model. Write K=k+ell.
8: 
9: Grand worth remains T: every extended matching projects to an old matching, and an old optimal perfect matching can be completed using the new sectors. Nonnegative old values and equal sizes ensure an optimal perfect matching exists.
10: 
11: ## Exact description of the entire extended core
12: 
13: Let p denote the payoffs to old players and q^a_j the payoffs in new sector a. Each new sector's payoffs must be constant across labels j. To prove this, fix an old optimal perfect matching and match its teams to new-sector labels by any permutation in each new sector. Every such extended perfect matching has worth T, so every one of its team covers is tight at a core point. Exchange two labels j,h in one new sector while leaving all other choices fixed. The tight equation for either affected old team gives q^a_j=q^a_h. This also holds trivially when m=1. Write q_a for the common value, and R=sum_a q_a.
14: 
15: The entire extended core is therefore exactly
16: 
17:     q_a>=0, z in C, z_i>=R/k for every old player,
18:     p_i=z_i-R/k,
19:     each of the m players in new sector a receives q_a.
20: 
21: For necessity, set z_i=p_i+R/k. Nonnegativity holds and sum_old z=sum_old p+mR=T. Each extended team inequality says p(team)+R>=w(team), exactly the old team inequality for z. For sufficiency, the displayed construction is nonnegative and efficient and covers every extended team; it is the entire coalitional core because only full teams are productive and unmatched players have nonnegative payoffs. This is an affine description of the FULL core, not an identification with a proper subset.
22: 
23: ## Ideals are exactly preserved for old players
24: 
25: For an old player i, every extended payoff satisfies p_i=z_i-R/k<=z_i<=H_i. Conversely, take an old ideal-attaining z and set all q_a=0, so p=z. Thus H_i^extended=H_i^old, without any positive-floor assumption.
26: 
27: ## First optimal face
28: 
29: Let tau=max_(z in C) min_i z_i>0. An extended core point with every payoff at least t has q_a>=t and p_i>=t, hence R>=ell*t and z_i>=t+R/k>=K*t/k. Therefore t<=k*tau/K.
30: 
31: Set t*=k*tau/K, choose any old floor-attaining z with z>=tau, and put q_a=t* for every new sector. Then R/k=ell*tau/K and p_i=z_i-ell*tau/K>=t*. This attains the upper bound and is a positive full-core point.
32: 
33: On the entire first optimal face, q_a>=t* and R>=ell*t*, while the old floor bound applied to z_i>=t*+R/k gives R<=k*(tau-t*)=ell*t*. Thus every q_a=t*, R=ell*t*, and z belongs to the old first optimal face F={z in C:z>=tau}. Conversely every z in F gives such an extended first-face point.
34: 
35: Consequently, if an old target is fixed to tau throughout F, its extended payoff is fixed to tau-ell*tau/K=k*tau/K=t* on the whole new first optimal face. Its full leximin payoff is t*, regardless of later stages, and its ideal is unchanged. The extended target ratio is exactly k/K times the old target ratio.

37: ## Triangle-containing graphs
38: 
39: For the previously proved three-sector family indexed by q>=1, the sector size is m=3q+1, T=6q+1, tau=1/[3(q+1)], and a target is fixed to tau on the first face with ideal1. Pair entries belong to {0,1,2}.

41: Let G be any fixed graph on K>=3 vertices containing a triangle. Put the three source pair matrices on that triangle, set every other allowed edge matrix to zero, and add the remaining K-3 sectors as above (K=3 is the original theorem). The full K-sector market has
42: 
43:     K(3q+1) players; all pair entries in {0,1,2};
44:     integral T=6q+1; a strictly positive full-core allocation;
45:     target ideal1 and full leximin payoff1/[K(q+1)].

Hence every connected four-vertex graph containing a triangle (paw, diamond, K4) has zero protection constant. P4 and K1,3 have constant1/4 by the all-tree theorem; C4 is the remaining connected four-vertex case.

50:

This is not dummy-player invariance: adding a sector changes the productive full teams, and its new players have positive first-floor payoffs in the construction.

1: # Vanishing leximin protection with nonnegative pair-additive triples
2: 
3: **Theorem.** For every integer q>=1 there is a complete strict three-sided assignment market with 9q+3 players, nonnegative pair entries in {0,1,2}, positive integral worth 6q+1, and nonempty full core, such that one player's full ascending-leximin payoff is 1/[3(q+1)] and its maximum whole-core payoff is 1. The target is fixed at the positive first global leximin level. Consequently the best size-independent protection fraction for this class is
4: 
5: \[
6: c_{\mathrm{pair}}=0.
7: \]
8: 
9: Every induced triple has integer value at most 5. There are no permanently zero-ideal players: the core contains an allocation positive in every coordinate. This proves the mathematical vanishing-family target. It does not prove priority, minimum market size, optimal finite-size ratios, or a new selector-distance result.

14: 
15: \[
16: \mathcal T=\{L_0\}\cup\{O_s,L_s,R_s:1\le s\le q\}.
17: \]
18: 
19: For every label t, create players a_t,b_t,c_t in the three respective sectors. Thus each sector has 3q+1 players. Every full triple has value
20: 
21: \[
22: w(a_t,b_u,c_v)=A_{tu}+B_{tv}+C_{uv}.
23: \]
24: 
25: Only full triples produce output. Coalition worth is maximum integral disjoint-triple output. Define the pair matrices by the following nonzero entries, and set every other entry to zero:

28: |---|---|---|
29: | \(C_{L_s,L_s}\) | \(0\le s\le q\) | 1 |

Each diagonal team's payoff sum equals its value, at both points, so both totals are T.
