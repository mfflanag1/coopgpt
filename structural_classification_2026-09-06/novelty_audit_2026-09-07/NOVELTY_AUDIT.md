## Claim under review

For each fixed connected allowed sector graph G on K≥2 vertices, equal finite sector sizes, nonnegative pair matrices (zero permitted), and productive complete teams only, restrict to games with positive grand worth and nonempty entire core. Let x be full nominal-payoff leximin and H_i the maximum of coordinate i over that same core. The optimal size-independent constant in x_i≥c(G)H_i is 1/K for trees and zero for cyclic graphs. The cyclic family has pair alphabet {0,1,2}, positive core points, and target ratio 1/[K(q+1)] with ideal 1.

The defensible proposed contributions are this precise quantitative classification, the strict-core clipping argument, and selective subdivision preserving the residual matching budget and target ideal. The assignment model, core maxima, leximin, LP cover arguments, and tree/cycle phenomena generally are not new contributions.

## Closest comparisons

### Atay–Núñez (2019): essential priority qualification

[Original publisher text](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10479-019-03256-5). Inspected model discussion, Theorem 1 and surrounding proof, Corollary 1, Proposition 2, and conclusion. Their model allows productive connected partial-sector coalitions. Theorem 1 decomposes tree cores; Corollary 1 states an acyclicity characterization of strong balancedness; Proposition 2 gives simultaneous sector maxima. This is an antecedent to structural framing. Our theorem concerns quantitative fairness conditional on a nonempty strict full-team core. Empty-core examples cannot prove its cyclic half. Do not claim the first tree/cycle classification of assignment games, and do not transplant their decomposition into the strict model without proof. No statement of our nominal-leximin/ideal dichotomy was identified here.

### Vazirani (v6, 2025): the fairness selector is established

[Primary text, arXiv:2402.11437v6](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.11437v6). Inspected introduction, classical lattice/core results, Definitions 9–12, examples, uniqueness lemma, and theorem search. It studies bilateral equitable core imputations and algorithms. Its omission of agents identically zero throughout the core does not distinguish our leximin: adding invariant zero coordinates leaves the optimizer unchanged. Neither introducing leximin nor choosing it for individual fairness should be claimed as new. No multisided tree/cycle ratio theorem was identified in this read scope; this is not a line-by-line exclusion of every algorithmic implication.

## Deductive check and recommended claim

Even simultaneous sector-optimal points and their average do not, by themselves, prove a nominal-leximin coordinatewise bound. A convex-set illustration is the segment from (0,0,100) to (1,98,1): its leximin endpoint is (1,98,1), its coordinate maxima are (1,98,100), and its one-third point meets H/3 while leximin fails that bound for the third coordinate. This is an illustration about convex sets, not a claimed assignment-core example. A model-specific clipping argument remains necessary.

Use: “We establish a sharp graph characterization of uniform nominal-leximin protection relative to individual whole-core maxima in strict full-team pair-additive assignment games. The closest inspected results concern core structure/existence or bilateral fairness; we have not identified this quantitative theorem in the literature reviewed.”

Avoid: “novelty confirmed,” “first tree–cycle theorem,” “new assignment model,” “new leximin solution,” or “field-level breakthrough established.” Three original-source gaps and possible indirect antecedents remain. The evidence supports pursuing specialist priority assessment, not unconditional clearance.
