12: ## Theorem
13: 
14: For every q>=1 and every M>0, every player satisfies x_i/H_i >= 1/676.
15: In particular no target choice and no positive sequence M(q), including M(q)->0, yields vanishing protection in this exact family. This closes this construction; it does not settle c(C4).

17: ## 1. Uniform floor, recalled with proof
18: 
19: The three-sector source has a core point z at floor tau=1/[3(q+1)]: at L_s its a,b,c entries are tau,1-(3s+2)tau,(3s+1)tau; at R_s they are tau,1-2tau,tau; at O_s they are 4-(3s-1)tau,tau,(3s-2)tau. Its full-team cover proof is in the prior frozen source-family certificate.
20: At M=2, p^2=(z+1/2 on a,b,c; 1/2 on d) covers every team: B(a,d)-B(a,c)<=2, with equality of the B terms when c=d. Its sum is T(2).
21: At M=0, p^0 assigns rowmax A+rowmax B to a, rowmax C to b, and zero to c,d, hence is a core point. For 0<M<2 interpolate (1-M/2)p^0+(M/2)p^2. Coverage and efficiency are preserved since both w(M) and T(M) are affine. Every coordinate is at least M/4. For M>=2 the direct point (z+M/4 on a,b,c; M/4 on d) covers by the same comparison. Thus all H_i are positive and x_i>=M/4 for all M>0. Diagonal tightness also gives H_i<=4+M.

56: ## 4. A terminal replacement that preserves every other payoff
57: 
58: Assume 0<M<=1/42. Starting from ANY core point p, replace only four coordinates by
59:     y_(c,L)=1/2+7M,  y_(c,R)=1/2-7M,
60:     y_(b,L)=S_L-y_(c,L),  y_(b,R)=S_R-y_(c,R).
61: All four are at least 1/2-12M>=3/14; all other coordinates remain exactly p's. Sum and nonnegativity are preserved. We verify every full-team inequality.
62: 
63: For every a,b,d, the unchanged a-payoff obeys
64:     p_a >= A(a,b)+B(a,d)-8M:
65: for O-rows this follows from p_a>=4-8M and A+B<=4; other rows have A+B=0 and p_a>=0.
66: 
67: A team whose b,c labels are both nonterminal is unchanged. A team with b=c=L or b=c=R has unchanged b+c sum, hence is covered. For the cross pair b=R,c=L, C=1 and
68:     y_(b,R)+y_(c,L)=S_R+14M>=1+9M.
69: Together with the displayed a bound and d>=0 this covers A+B+C+M*1_(c=d).
70: For every other changed team, C(b,c)=0: no nonterminal row has a positive C-entry in a terminal column; terminal rows only have positive entries in terminal columns; and C(L,R)=0. Such a team includes at least one changed coordinate, so y_b+y_c>=1/2-12M>=9M. Again the a bound and d>=0 cover A+B+M*1_(c=d). These cases exhaust all teams. Therefore y is a core point.
71: 
72: Apply this replacement to the full leximin point x. If any terminal coordinate were below3/14, the replacement would leave every nonterminal coordinate unchanged and place all four terminal coordinates at or above3/14. In ascending sorted order this is a strict lexicographic improvement (the smallest changed value below3/14 is removed, and no value below that threshold is introduced). Contradiction. Thus every terminal coordinate of x is at least3/14.

74: ## 5. Combine the regimes
75: 
76: For 0<M<=1/42, weak players have x_i/H_i>=1/12. Nonterminal strong players have x_i>=1-8/42=17/21; terminal strong players have x_i>=3/14. All strong ideals are <=4+1/42=169/42, so all strong ratios are at least9/169.
77: For M>=1/42, the floor and diagonal ideal bound give
78:     x_i/H_i >= M/[4(4+M)] >= 1/676.
79: Since both1/12 and9/169 exceed1/676, this proves the theorem. The constant is deliberately conservative; no sharpness is claimed. The M=0 endpoint separately has the established tree1/4 guarantee on positive ideals.
