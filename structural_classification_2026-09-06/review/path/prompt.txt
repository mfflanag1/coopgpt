1: # Tree core representation and path protection: candidate proof
2: 
3: Status: conventional derivation awaiting adversarial review and independent finite equivalence checks. No novelty claim. Model: k fixed sectors of equal size m, complete k-tuples only productive, nonnegative pair matrices on the edges of a connected tree. Coalition values use integral matchings. Root the tree at vertex 0 and orient every edge parent a to child b, with matrix A_ab(parent label, child label).
4: 
5: ## Integral worth and simultaneous diagonalization
6: 
7: Choose an optimal perfect matching for each edge. Root-label the players and propagate labels along each chosen matching. There is no conflicting propagation because the sector graph is a tree. All chosen edge matchings are now diagonal. Write d_ab(j)=A_ab(j,j). Any full-team matching projects to edge matchings, so its worth is at most the sum of their optima. The diagonal full-team matching attains this bound. Thus T=sum_j sum_edges d_ab(j). Nonnegative weights and equal sizes allow completion of partial matchings without loss. Assume T>0.
8: 
9: The core is precisely p>=0, sum p=T, and all full-team covers. Every diagonal team is tight at every core point because summing its inequalities exhausts T. Put s(j)=sum_edges d_ab(j); then sum_v p_v(j)=s(j).

11: ## Exact cut-variable representation
12: 
13: For each nonroot vertex b, let S_b be its descendant subtree, and define
14: 
15:     U_b(j) = sum_{v in S_b} p_v(j) - sum_{edges internal to S_b} d_edge(j).
16: 
17: Set U_0(j)=0. The entire core is affinely represented by
18: 
19:     U_b(i)-U_b(j) >= A_ab(j,i)-d_ab(j)       (a parent of b; all i,j),
20:     p_v(j)=U_v(j)-sum_{b child of v} U_b(j)+sum_{b child of v} d_vb(j) >=0.
21: 
22: Necessity: use the full team with label i on S_b and label j on its complement. Subtract the tight diagonal team j. The only edge crossing the cut is ab. Internal diagonal terms cancel after substituting the definition of U_b, leaving the claimed difference inequality. The formula for p is immediate by subtracting child-subtree sums from the parent-subtree sum. At the root it follows from the tight diagonal identity.
23: 
24: Sufficiency: start with arbitrary U satisfying these constraints and reconstruct p by the displayed formula. Summing over vertices at a common label j telescopes to s(j), giving efficiency. For an arbitrary full team with label i_v at vertex v, sum its payoffs:
25: 
26:     sum_v p_v(i_v)
27:       = sum_{a->b} [U_b(i_b)-U_b(i_a)+d_ab(i_a)]
28:       >= sum_{a->b} A_ab(i_a,i_b).
29: 
30: This proves every team cover. The transformation is invertible: descendant sums of the reconstructed p recover each U_b. The representation thus preserves labeled coordinates and their full-core ideals.
31: 
32: Nonemptiness is classical matching duality: sum nonnegative optimal bilateral vertex covers over incident edges to obtain full-team covers with total T. This observation and the cut algebra are not asserted to be new.

34: ## Path theorem
35: 
36: For the path 0-1-...-(k-1), rooted at 0, the representation is a potential network. Use nodes (r,j), 1<=r<=k-1, and a single anchor of potential zero. Node (r,j) has potential U_r(j). An arc tail->head of constant c has slack c+U_tail-U_head.
37: 
38: Payoff arcs at each label j:
39: 
40:     anchor -> (1,j), constant d_01(j), payoff p_0(j);
41:     (r,j) -> (r+1,j), constant d_r,r+1(j), payoff p_r(j), 1<=r<k-1;
42:     (k-1,j) -> anchor, constant 0, payoff p_{k-1}(j).
43: 
44: Structural arcs stay inside one layer r: (r,i)->(r,j), constant d_(r-1),r(j)-A_(r-1),r(j,i). Their nonnegative slacks are exactly the cut inequalities.

Brief justification: otherwise the vertices reaching tail(e) through eligible arcs omit head(e); increasing their potentials (and resetting the anchor) improves e, weakly improves all payoff entries <=x_e, preserves tight structural constraints, and for sufficiently small positive perturbation preserves all omitted constraints and higher payoff levels. This contradicts full sorted leximin.

A simple cycle visits the anchor only once; structural arcs do not change layers, and payoff arcs advance one layer. It therefore has exactly k payoff arcs. On the cycle, slack sums telescope to a fixed constant. At x this is the sum of its payoff entries because structural slacks vanish. For any other feasible y, all slacks are nonnegative, so
