# Selected next milestone: the unrestricted core-ideal inequality

Selected 2026-09-07 at the user's request. This milestone supersedes the earlier priority given to restricted hub-composition formulas. It preserves the existing active sharp-subsidy goal and all its completion criteria.

## Goal to execute

Resolve whether S_3 = 1/3 by proving or refuting the original-core inequality

    3 w(P) + sum_{i unmatched by P} H_i <= 4 T

for every connected three-sector pair-additive market supported on an optimal diagonal matching and a partial matching P. Retain equal finite sector sizes, nonnegative pair values, T > 0, and a nonempty entire original core. H_i is the coordinate maximum over that original core, frozen throughout the subsidy intervention. Include unequal diagonal worths, cyclic supports, and teams with repeated diagonal labels. The subsidy changes only the grand total available for allocation.

The established replication and sparsification reductions make this inequality sufficient for the universal one-third bound. The existing asymptotic lower family then makes that bound sharp. Refutation must be an actual admissible market with an exact subsidy ratio greater than one-third, not failure of a sufficient certificate template.

## First decisive milestone

Prove or refute the inequality for every connected unicyclic incidence support and all admissible pair weights. Begin with distinct-label teams, but state that restriction explicitly and discharge repeated-label cases before claiming an all-unicyclic result. Extract a boundary inequality that tracks both matching worth and the changes in original core ideals when a cycle is opened. Do not assume that reducing pair values decreases the subsidy: ideals are endogenous across different original markets.

Before using the latest scratch work as evidence, preserve the broken-ring candidate proof and exact certificates, independently verify the six full-ideal cyclic witnesses, and integrate their verification into the canonical build. These are acceptance gates, not a substitute for the all-weight theorem. The observed fixed-support values (r-1)/(3r) are evidence on named supports only; their extension is a conjecture.

## Distinct attack routes and failure gates

1. Original-core dual route: combine coordinate-maximum LP duals and matching tightness to obtain an inequality valid without maximal-diagonal, leaf-only, same-sector, or fixed-root-mass certificate restrictions. A new LP restatement alone is not a deliverable.
2. Structural route: seek a cycle-opening or minimal-counterexample lemma with explicit boundary terms. Prove the accounting of T, w(P), and H rather than assuming any of these quantities is preserved. A lemma equivalent to the full missing inequality must be identified as such, not reported as a reduction in difficulty.
3. Adversarial route: optimize actual original-core obstructions on heterogeneous unicyclic and multiple-cycle supports. Include topology distinctions beyond degree counts. Recover rational core feasibility, ideal maxima, and subsidy lower certificates for any candidate above one-third; independently verify them.

After two failed variants of a mechanism, retain the precise obstruction and switch mechanism. Do not resume frozen-old-weight gluing, common affine normalization, or the already rejected harmonic and proportional-rescaling rules without a substantively new lemma. Do not expand successful finite samples in place of proving the load-bearing step.
