# Assignment priority review — 5 September 2026

This is an adversarial, bounded priority review of the assignment package. It is **not a certification of novelty**. The manuscript, `CLAIMS_AND_SOURCES.md`, and `REVIEW_REQUEST.md` were read; release files were not edited. The previous correctness audit is separate. Root owns generic convex-set fairness and the Gangam thesis. This review concentrates on assignment and matching games, coordinate-preserving core representations, and earlier examples.

The strongest surviving proposed contribution is the **exact finite pair-game distance bound, together with the sparse bounded-integer three-sided realization approaching distance 2**. I did not locate an earlier exact statement or a theorem directly implying that combination. However, three central original-source gaps remain: Quint's multisided paper, Lucas's paper, and a newly identified 2023 general extreme-core preprint. The half guarantee and additive core geometry deserve less novelty weight than those extremal claims.

## A further prior implication: the general-graph half guarantee

The duplication device is classical. Bíró, Kern and Paulusma, *Computing solutions for matching games*, §2.2, printed p. 8 of the institutional author version, explicitly duplicate each vertex and put each original edge across the two copies; they attribute the technique to Nemhauser–Trotter (1975). Their edge weights are halved. Here it is more convenient to leave the weights unchanged. The publisher records online publication **27 March 2011**, IJGT **41 (2012), 75–90**, DOI [10.1007/s00182-011-0273-y](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00182-011-0273-y). The relevant original-paper passage was inspected through the [institutional indexed text](https://durham-repository.worktribe.com/OutputFile/1524835); a full direct fetch returned 403. This is a source for the reduction, not a source asserting the fairness corollary.

Our independent deduction is as follows. Let G have a nonempty core and worth T. Replace vertex i by i' and i'', and edge ij of weight w by i'j'' and j'i'', both of weight w. Duplicating an optimal matching gives weight 2T. Duplicating any core vector p gives a feasible cover of total 2T, so the bipartite game's worth is exactly 2T. Global copy-swap is an automorphism of its core.

Uniqueness of the full sorted leximin vector implies invariance under this swap, so it is `(x,x)`. Its symmetric feasible vectors are exactly the duplicated original core: from a symmetric cover one gets `x_i+x_j>=w_ij` and sum x=T, and the converse is immediate. Duplicating every entry preserves comparisons of sorted vectors, hence this x is the original graph's leximin. For every original core y, `(y,y)` is a bipartite core point. The bipartite half theorem therefore gives `2x_i>=y_i`, and maximizing over y proves the graph half guarantee. Fixed-zero coordinates pose no problem. The same symmetry argument transports the unique descending selector.

This is a **standard reduction plus a short deduction**, not an exact earlier fairness theorem that I found. It strengthens the case for presenting the half guarantee as inherited. It does not establish the sharp finite correction: an original k-edge matching becomes a 2k-edge matching, so applying a putative bipartite finite bound at that parameter would give only `2k/(2k+1)`.

## What the earlier representations actually preserve

**Quint's bilateral geometry.** Thomas Quint, *Characterization of cores of assignment games*, IJGT **19 (1991), 413–420**, DOI [10.1007/BF01766430](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/BF01766430), characterizes the core **expressed in one side's payoffs** as a 45-degree lattice. The publisher abstract was inspected; the full original was not. This is stronger than a mere combinatorial-polytope equivalence, but weaker than realizing arbitrary full labeled payoff geometry. A buyer-coordinate representation includes complementary seller coordinates that affect full sorted selectors. It cannot be used to ignore those coordinates.

**Canonical identical full cores.** Núñez–Rafels' [institutional working text, *The assignment game: core bounds for mixed-pair coalitions*](https://diposit.ub.edu/bitstreams/3b1ce64d-464a-4fde-97d1-e8e57ea896a1/download), Theorem 3, printed p. 6, constructs the unique buyer-seller-exact matrix on the same player sets with the same entire core by raising each entry to its minimum mixed-pair core sum. Section 5.1, printed pp. 15–16, describes Quint's realization of a lattice `b_i<=u_i<=e_i`, `u_i-u_j>=d_ij` using m buyers and at most m+1 sellers. The published related article is [*Buyer-seller exactness in the assignment game*](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s001820300128), IJGT31,423–436. This is a real original-coordinate precedent. Its premise is an already representable bilateral core, and its 45-degree input class is restricted. Also, “buyer-seller exact” is not the same as exactness of the entire TU game; the working text explicitly distinguishes them in §5.2.

**Multisided tree composition.** Atay–Núñez, [*Multi-sided assignment games on m-partite graphs*](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10479-019-03256-5), ANOR279,271–290, published **21 May 2019**, Theorem 1, gives the core as the composition/sum of bilateral cores when the graph on sectors is cycle-free. Proposition 2 gives simultaneous coordinate maxima within each sector; Proposition 3 addresses extreme-point composition. These are actual payoff statements, not arbitrary-polytope realizations. Their model also admits basic coalitions using fewer than all sectors. Section 6 explains why strict full-team markets require care, especially with unequal sector sizes. The package's non-pair-additive cube excludes its actual recurrence array from this decomposition; the failure of simultaneous sector maxima for L>=2 also excludes an identical payoff core of the stated tree class. Neither fact precludes an unrelated distance-2 example elsewhere.

**Böhm-Bawerk replication.** Tejada–Núñez, [*The nucleolus and the core-center of multi-sided Böhm-Bawerk assignment markets*](https://www.research-collection.ethz.ch/server/api/core/bitstreams/4531c43d-aa5c-45b8-a007-4e6fc2c32e2b/content), MMOR75,199–220, published **24 March 2012**, Definitions 1–3 and Theorem 1, printed pp. 203–204, reproduce Tejada's full-core formula `C(assignment)=t+R(C(sector game))`. R repeats a sector payoff among its r active agents and fills inactive entries with zero; t is explicit. The sector game is convex, so the full core has dimension at most m−1. Theorem 3 and Theorem 4 concern the nucleolus and core-center, respectively, not our two selectors. The originating Tejada article was online **13 January 2011**, TOP21(2013),189–205, [DOI10.1007/s11750-010-0170-8](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11750-010-0170-8). Our recurrence core has dimension 3 (small positive root parameters give a full-dimensional feasible neighborhood) and therefore cannot be an identical three-sector Böhm-Bawerk core.

Here is the additive specialization explicitly. Write the package's nonnegative additive entries as `b_1i+b_2j+b_3k+S`, with each sector minimum b equal to zero and S>=0. Choose constants C1>=max b1 and C2>=max b2; set seller costs `c_1i=C1-b_1i`, `c_2j=C2-b_2j` and buyer values `w_k=C1+C2+S+b_3k`. Then the Böhm-Bawerk array `max(0,w_k-c_1i-c_2j)` is exactly the package array. Thus the benchmark lies in an established coordinate-representation class. The package's direct proof of the simplex is appropriate; the inequalities 1/3 and 4/3 are short subsequent calculations, and I did not find them explicitly asserted in the inspected prior paper.

**Assortative cores.** Martínez-de-Albéniz–Rafels–Ybern, [*Assortative multisided assignment games: The extreme core points*](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0899825620300026), GEB120(2020),144–153, gives all extreme core points under supermodularity and monotonicity. A repository source is dated 8 October 2019; this audit reached the publisher's abstract/introduction and indexed author text, with full PDF retrieval inconsistent. The subsequent 2026 local-monotonicity theorem already inspected in the package supplies the stronger invariant used in its exclusion. I did not treat the 2020 abstract as proof of every formula or as ruling out all broader representations.

**Permutation and recent capacity models.** Auriol–Marchi, [*k-Sided permutation games and multiprices of equilibrium*](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S037722170300359X), EJOR158(2004),506–517, Section 4/Theorem 2 in the preview states that every k-sided assignment game is a k-sided permutation game. That direction does not realize arbitrary polytopes as three-sided assignment cores. El Obadi–Miquel–Núñez, [*Assignment markets with capacitated middlemen: core and competitive equilibria*](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00182-026-01007-y), published **25 August 2026**, is newly checked full primary text. Team output there is independent of the middleman's identity, and Theorem 13/Corollary 14 concern competitive payoffs, dual solutions and the core. Example 15 has unit-capacity middlemen but supplies no sorted-selector distance theorem. Its arrays have zero third mixed differences; this excludes the exact package array, not all possible alternative extremal examples.

Atay–Núñez–Solymosi, [*On the vertices of the core of a many-to-one assignment game*](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10898-026-01632-7), published **29 August 2026**, Theorem 11, generates vertices through fixed worker orders with maximize/minimize flags. Those “max-min salary vectors” are not anonymous sorted-payoff selectors. This fresh source should not be misclassified by its terminology.

## Earlier examples and exact constants

Vazirani's [v6, dated 20 November 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.11437v6), Example 3, gives the four-player selections `(40,30,30,70)` and `(55,45,15,55)`. Their L1 distance is 60 and T=170, so rho=6/17. Its core segment is an earlier concrete full-selector separation; the package's four-player sharp value 2/3 is stronger. Theorem 6 appends independent edges of weight 60 to that example for a nucleolus comparison; the inherited selector distance is then `60/(170+60k)`, which decreases. These examples do not immediately imply either the sharp finite formula or the triple limit. This calculation does not rule out further constructions latent in the paper.

Llerena–Núñez–Rafels' [2010 working paper, *The Lorenz-maximal core allocations and the kernel in some classes of assignment games*](https://diposit.ub.edu/bitstreams/213057a0-92b0-4676-9d8a-6eb485b5db2d/download), E10/246, was additionally retrieved in full; its introduction, definitions, and class descriptions were inspected. Sections 3–4 concern dominant-diagonal and 2-dominant-diagonal assignment games and coincidence with Thompson/Dutta–Ray solutions. This is further established equality infrastructure; its hypotheses are essential and it does not support general coincidence or the finite distance constant. The older literature already offers half-of-ideal protection for Thompson's midpoint because its coordinates equal `(H_i+m_i)/2`; it is specifically the leximin comparison that needs the clock/reduction argument.

## Remaining priority gaps

1. **Quint, *The core of an m-sided assignment game***, GEB3(4),487–503, November 1991, [publisher DOI](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/0899825691900179). Abstract and author bibliography verified; full original not obtained. Later Atay thesis Proposition 2.13/Lemma 2.14 and the 2019 Atay–Núñez paper delimit particular attributed results but cannot certify everything in Quint's original.
2. **Lucas, *Core theory for multiple-sided assignment games***, Duke Math J81(1),55–65,1995, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1215/S0012-7094-95-08106-X). Legacy and modern Project Euclid links failed. The 2x2x2 balancedness conditions in Atay's thesis and later article do not replace full original inspection. No universal payoff-core representation may be attributed to, or excluded from, this unread paper.
3. **NEW: Martínez-de-Albéniz, Rafels and Ybern, *Extreme Core Points in Multisided Assignment Problems***, [SSRN4369524](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=4369524), 13 pages, posted **24 February 2023**. The primary abstract promises conditions using a finite family of cooperative games. The [authors' university working-paper listing](https://www.ub.edu/gametheory/publications/working-papers/) confirms the entry. Searches of title, ID, author/university pages, and public download links did not yield readable full text. This is a meaningful new access gap, not adverse proof. It should enter the package ledger now.

A broader source-class exclusion cannot close these gaps. Likewise, a transportation-polytope universality theorem does not automatically preserve efficient dual payoff coordinates, integral worth, all extra agents, coordinate multiplicities, or full sorted selectors. The present search found no theorem supplying that complete interface.

## Recommended release wording and bibliography

Suggested contribution paragraph:

> We compare the full leximin and descending lexicographic-minimum selections of nonempty matching-game cores. For an optimal matching with k positive edges, we prove the sharp bound k/(k+1) on their normalized L1 distance, with the stated one-edge exception. We construct sparse three-sided assignment games with bounded integer team values whose distance approaches 2, and derive an all-player-count quantitative lower bound. The half-of-coordinate-ideal guarantee follows from existing bipartite algorithmic invariants; a standard duplication reduction extends it to general graphs. The additive subclass provides an elementary benchmark based on established core geometry.

Suggested priority note:

> We have not found an equivalent finite bound or sparse three-sided construction in the primary sources inspected. This is a bounded literature assessment. Original-source access remains incomplete for Quint (1991), Lucas (1995), and Martínez-de-Albéniz, Rafels and Ybern (2023).

Add Bíró–Kern–Paulusma for the duplication reduction; Tejada (online2011/issue2013) and Tejada–Núñez2012 for the additive-coordinate representation; Quint's separate bilateral *Characterization* paper and Núñez–Rafels for the representation boundary; Atay–Núñez2019 for tree composition. Add the 2023 SSRN item to unresolved sources. The August2026 middlemen and vertex papers can be recorded in the ledger without expanding the introduction disproportionately. Preserve working-version dates and theorem locators instead of silently treating them as the typeset originals.

Confidence: high that the half guarantee and additive geometry should be treated as inherited/elementary; high that the inspected representation theorems alone do not imply the explicit sparse construction; moderate that the precise finite pair bound and triple realization remain viable novel candidates. There is no evidence sufficient to promise novelty. The outstanding limitations are original-source access, unpublished or differently named equivalent results, and the possibility that an older restricted-family example yields the same quantitative phenomenon after an additional calculation.
