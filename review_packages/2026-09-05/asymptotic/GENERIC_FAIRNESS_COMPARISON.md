# Generic fairness and optimization: priority comparison

Assessed 2026-09-05 against release commit `9e8ccee7`. This is a bounded primary-source audit, not a certificate that no equivalent theorem exists. The comparison concerns **labeled individual payoffs**, both opposing sorted selectors on the **same** compact convex nonnegative constant-total feasible set, and distance `||x-z||_1/T`. Sources comparing social welfare, solver error, coalition excesses, different feasible sets or different players' zero-sum strategies do not automatically answer this question.

## Findings

1. **The general fairness framework is established.** Radunović and Le Boudec, *A Unified Framework for Max-Min and Min-Max Fairness With Applications*, IEEE/ACM Transactions on Networking 15(5), 1073–1083 (2007), DOI [10.1109/TNET.2007.896231](https://doi.org/10.1109/TNET.2007.896231), has earlier 2002/2006 reports. The [author-uploaded full article](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/3335377_A_Unified_Framework_for_Max-Min_and_Min-Max_Fairness_With_Applications) was inspected at Definitions 1–2, Proposition 1 and Theorems 1–3. It gives the opposite fairness definitions, uniqueness, existence on compact convex domains and iterative optimization. These are background, not contributions of this program. No opposing-selector normalized-distance theorem was located in those results. The EPFL direct download returned 429; that access failure does not undo access to the author-uploaded full text.

2. **Price of fairness measures a different quantity.** Bertsimas, Farias and Trichakis, [*The Price of Fairness*](https://www.mit.edu/~nitric/pof_or.pdf), Operations Research 59(1), 17–31 (2011), uses aggregate utility sacrificed relative to the utilitarian optimum. Its max-min rule normalizes by individual maximum utilities. Definitions/model, Theorem 1 (printed p.23) and Theorem 2 (p.27) were inspected in the full author PDF. On our fixed-total sets aggregate loss is zero, while labeled allocation distance can be positive. Coordinatewise ideal normalization can also change the selector. Therefore its bounds do not directly imply our coefficient. This is a mathematical comparison, not a claim that the literatures are unrelated.

3. Cao, Ding and Granot, [*Tight Bounds for The Price of Fairness*](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2311.18339), arXiv:2311.18339v2 (30 October 2024; v1 30 November 2023), further sharpens utilitarian-loss bounds. Full PDF §2.3, Theorem 3 and Proposition 11 were inspected. The model includes downward-closed utility sets and max-min fairness of `u_i/L_i`. Proposition 11's asymptotic `1-1/n` is a bound on a different metric; matching the order `1/n` is not a theorem-level anticipation of our `n(2-s_n) -> 2` claim.

4. Abernethy, Schapire and Syed, [*Lexicographic Optimization: Algorithms and Stability*](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2405.01387), arXiv:2405.01387v1 (2 May 2024), provides additional direct primary credit for existence/uniqueness (Theorems 1–2). Definitions 1–2 and Theorems 4–9 were inspected. Its lexicographic distortion is a one-sided difference between sorted order statistics, comparing a leximin target to approximate exponential-loss minimizers. Its segment examples and rates concern approximation of one selector, not labeled distance between opposing exact selectors. Section 6.3's two zero-sum strategies belong to different simplices and are not the two allocations in our fixed-total theorem. The name “lexicographic maximum” in that paper corresponds to our ascending leximin, illustrating why name-only searches are insufficient.

5. **An apparent published contradiction fails under its printed generality.** Chaoqun You et al., [*Efficient Load Balancing for the VNF Deployment with Placement Constraints*](https://chaoqunyou.com/data/2019%20ICC%20Efficient_Load_Balancing_for_the_VNF_Deployment_with_Placement_Constraints.pdf), ICC 2019, Theorem 1 (PDF p.3), states coincidence of max-min and min-max fairness on an arbitrary constant-total subset. Its proof assumes that all coordinates can be equalized, which does not follow from constant total. An exact counterexample is

   `K = {(t, 3-2t, 3+t): 0 <= t <= 1}`.

   The total is 6. Its smallest coordinate is `t`, uniquely maximized at `t=1`, giving `(1,1,4)`. Its largest is `3+t`, uniquely minimized at `t=0`, giving `(0,3,3)`. First-stage uniqueness also fixes the full sorted selectors. Their normalized distance is `4/6`. The inequalities identifying the minimum and maximum hold throughout the interval, not merely on a sampled grid; `source_boundary_control.py` checks the affine endpoint certificates exactly. This refutes the overly broad printed statement, not its unrestricted equal-load application, and is **not** promoted as a new research branch or a novelty proof for our main theorem.

6. **Recent combinatorial work needs its exact domain retained.** Gangam, Taherijam and Vazirani, [*On the Core of the b-Matching Game*](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.01914), arXiv:2502.01914v1 (4 February 2025), has inspected definitions and result summary: it proves hardness of recognizing core and fair-core imputations for edge-unconstrained capacitated b-matching, plus star-core characterization. This does not supply a unit-capacity pair/triple distance comparison. Gangam's [2026 institutional dissertation](https://escholarship.org/uc/item/8cg6q4hv), *Stable, Robust, and Fair Solutions in Combinatorial Games*, has indexed Chapter 4 text consistent with the previously inspected max-flow/MST/b-matching work. Its full PDF download remained inaccessible in this lane. We therefore do **not** mark the dissertation fully inspected or infer absence from it. The directly retrieved 2025 paper resolves the specific newer hardness reference discovered in its indexed text.

## Assessment

The universal overlap inequality and first-order exact-game distance coefficient remain **candidate quantitative results** after this wider comparison. The algorithmic framework, uniqueness, fair allocation concepts, use of line segments and lower envelopes, and inverse-matrix/Fibonacci ingredients must receive prior credit. The parallel construction audit supplies stronger downgrades for some ingredients. A theorem that appears absent from these sources may still be a short consequence of other work or independently published elsewhere.

Unresolved original-paper access in the assignment lane, specialist literature knowledge and the possibility of equivalent terminology remain material limits. None of these findings establishes field-level significance. The purpose of the revised packets is to make a precise and modest priority question reviewable.
