1. Relative supports and finite/infinite boundaries — ACCEPTED.
   Finite concavity on the relative interior of a coordinate face supplies a
   finite supergradient. Using both lambda>1 and 0<lambda<1 establishes
   touching u.x=f(x), and monotonicity proves u>=0. The epsilon-mixing argument
   extends majorization to boundary points without using boundary continuity.
   Nonnegative touching forms have u_e<=f(x)/x_e on active coordinates, giving
   compactness, convexity and a unique minimum-norm selector. At x=0, the
   separately stated extended form is well-defined; R^empty has its unique
   empty coefficient vector, so no additional zero-face hypothesis is needed.
   The illustrative downward-discontinuous f(x,y)=x for y>0 and 0 otherwise
   is indeed monotone, concave and degree-one homogeneous.

2. All-profile whole-owner incentives — ACCEPTED.
   Conditioning on B_i=0 fixes the whole off aggregate and its canonical form
   independently of every coordinate of b_i. For that fixed nonnegative extended
   form, increasing b_i cannot decrease its value, including a finite-to-infinity
   transition. Both this value and the grand T are nondecreasing; their finite
   capped minimum is therefore nondecreasing. Marginals use an unchanged off
   aggregate. Fixed nonnegative q mixtures preserve the incentive inequalities.
   Selection is from (f,x_B), not the full report distribution or q.

3. Coalition coverage and no infinity subtraction — ACCEPTED.
   A capped contribution reaching T covers any coalition containing it. Otherwise
   all relevant bundles lie on the finite face and additivity plus majorization
   proves coverage. For the ON endpoint inequality, b_i and x-b_i have support
   inside supp(x); every term in ell(x)-ell(x-b_i)=ell(b_i) is finite.
   The inequality direction is correct since f(x-b_i)<=ell(x-b_i).
   The ON contribution is <=f(x)<=T, so it is uncapped. This proves r>=z.
   If T=0, monotonicity forces every masked value to zero and all payments vanish.

4. Arbitrary selected-range union and budget — ACCEPTED.
   At a fixed full profile, enumerate all 2^n masks first. Their capped vectors
   form a fixed finite subset of [0,T]^n, independently of the subsequently
   sampled mask and q. The fixed-vector Chernoff event can be union-bounded over
   this entire list even though the actually chosen vector depends on the mask.
   All selected OPEN contributions are finite, nonnegative and sum to f(x_B),
   so the key score constraint is valid. The integration-by-parts identity has

   profile's realized range size. The n bound follows from individual M_i<=T.
   The n=1 direct construction indeed equals f(b_1).
