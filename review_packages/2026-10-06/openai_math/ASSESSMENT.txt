OPENAI/MATH AND COOPGPT: RELEVANCE ASSESSMENT
6 October 2026

Assessment, not a proof or priority certificate. No manuscript changes, human
outreach, new research goal, or installation of the downloaded code is implied.

CONCLUSION

Yes: the collection is useful, most immediately as a model for independently
checkable theorem statements and Lean proofs. Its best mathematical connections
are simultaneous multiscale selection, careful global budget accounting, and
all-cut graph rounding. None of the inspected results directly proves or
supersedes our truthful-cooperation theorem. A direct application to a broader
network game still requires new mathematics, especially preservation of
own-report monotonicity. These are ranked opportunities, not established transfers.

OUR BASELINE

The current internally audited manuscript has B_k ~ log(k)/log(log(k)): arbitrary
finite fixed owners, nonnegative capacities, nonnegative deterministic payments,
coverage of every coalition, and monotonicity of an original owner's payment
when that owner increases their report. The model has binding underreports and
payment utility; there is no claimed result for arbitrary costs or misreports.
Both owner and pool counts grow. The old leading-constant gap is closed.

The lower bound uses binary owner reports, a fixed-owner hierarchy, multinomial
profile averaging and truncated-Poisson type weights. Only the changing owner's
payment is transported. The all-profile upper uses capped continuous-Banzhaf
payments. The source claims a mathematically defined deterministic rule, not a
polynomial-time exact implementation. The lower transfers to general edge-owned
flow games via layered networks; the matching upper is currently restricted to
the multi-glove/layered domain. Finite certificates and cross-model audits do
not constitute Lean verification or human specialist endorsement.

RANK 1: FORMAL VERIFICATION — DIRECTLY USEFUL INFRASTRUCTURE

Sources: lean/README.md, lean/ComparatorChallenges/README.md and
https://github.com/leanprover/comparator.

Comparator compares a solution against a separately trusted theorem statement
and approved definitions, checks its dependency axioms, and invokes Lean kernel
checking. Its default permitted axioms are propext, Quot.sound and Classical.choice.
This addresses a risk that compilation alone does not address: proving an easier
or altered formal statement. A challenge file can deliberately contain a sorry
placeholder; that is not evidence that the corresponding solution is unchecked.
Challenge/solution distinction and allowed axioms must be inspected together.
I inspected the AlgorithmicThinTrees challenge/configuration, not a successful
local solution build. The config disables the optional Nanoda second kernel.

For CoopGPT, first freeze trusted definitions of fixed owners, every coalition,
legal unilateral report changes, payments, budget factor and all-profile
quantifiers. Exclude definition holes from that target. Then formalize:
  (1) the finite weighted report-transition identity for arbitrary parameters;
  (2) the coefficient cancellation and shared destination-budget bound;
  (3) the parameter choice and all-k asymptotic inversion;
  (4) separately, the continuous upper, including capping and own monotonicity.
Steps (1)-(2) alone do not formalize the complete asymptotic theorem.

Use a small pinned Lean project rather than importing the entire release. The
release recommends building small portions; its lakefile has many dependencies.
Lean itself can run on macOS, while the Comparator documentation's production
sandbox workflow includes Linux-specific tooling. No installation was attempted.
Even a successful formalization leaves model fidelity, significance and priority
for mathematical/economic review. Formal verification improves assurance, not
the breadth or novelty of the theorem by itself.

RANK 2: MULTISCALE SELECTORS — CLOSEST PROOF-ARCHITECTURE CONNECTION

Family 175, Integral and fractional expectation thresholds are equivalent.
Let F be a nonempty proper increasing family on a finite ground set X. An
integral cover selects generators S contained in each H in F and costs sum p^|S|.
A fractional cover has weights g(S) in [0,1] with sum_(S subset H) g(S) >= 1 and costs
sum g(S)p^|S|. The source claims q_f(F) <= 25*512^4 q(F), both budgets 1/2.
The rounding changes the density parameter p by a constant factor. It is NOT
a constant integrality-gap theorem for arbitrary set cover at fixed costs.

The useful proof mechanism is its multiscale selector. Given a probability mass
lambda_H supported on each H, one common H captures most of lambda_H at every
random nested scale simultaneously. Its proof reconstructs a common witness
from the final coloring and counts of moves, then charges all original colorings
to that witness. This avoids selecting incompatible witnesses separately at
different scales. Our recent proof similarly needed many report levels to share
one valid destination-budget accounting. The analogy is methodological; our
truncated-Poisson argument is not a corollary of the selector theorem.

Possible use: search for a coherent witness selection/charging lemma for report
graphs lacking the exchangeability of identical pools. A genuine transfer must
specify X, F, the subset costs, and how one selected object encodes legal
changing-owner constraints and budget coefficients. No such reduction is proved
here. The formalization scope doc names the two September threshold/discrete-
convexity papers; it does not by itself cover every October extension in family175.

RANK 3: THIN TREES AND L1 EMBEDDINGS — A PLAUSIBLE NETWORK EXTENSION

Family 174 claims a spanning tree T in every undirected k-edge-connected
multigraph with |delta_T(S)| <= (C/k)|delta_G(S)| for all vertex cuts S.
Its algorithmic companion also claims polynomial bit complexity with binary
parallel-edge multiplicities and simultaneous cut-and-cost rounding for rational
fractional cut vectors. Family 089 claims constant-distortion L1 embeddings of
planar metrics, and bounds depending only on treewidth for bounded-treewidth
metrics, with corresponding multicommodity flow-cut consequences.

The attraction: these results control many constraints simultaneously and might
support extensions from serial parallel-edge layers to richer network classes.
However our flow coalitions are sets of edge owners; their values are flows in
edge-induced subnetworks. These are not the vertex cuts in the thin-tree theorem.
Moreover an input-dependent tree can change when one owner changes capacity.
Cut preservation alone proves neither coalition coverage nor truthfulness.
Planarity/undirected metric results also do not automatically apply to directed
single-source/sink capacity games.

A precise SUFFICIENT bridge (an elementary observation, not claimed novel):
Suppose L,D>=0 and the network game v_c admits component games v^r_c indexed
by a fixed finite set independent of c, with
nonnegative payments y^r_i(c) on the same original owner set, each satisfying
all coalition inequalities and own-report monotonicity for the ORIGINAL reports.
Suppose at every profile c, for every coalition S,
  v_c(S) <= sum_r v^r_c(S),
  sum_r v^r_c(N) <= D v_c(N),
and y^r(N,c) <= L v^r_c(N).
Then y_i(c)=sum_r y^r_i(c) is eligible and y(N,c)<=LD v_c(N).
Proof: sum the coalition, budget and monotonicity inequalities separately.
Require the budget inequality also at profiles with zero component worth. If components
are selected from reports, their COMPOSITE payments must still be monotone in
the original reports; truthfulness within each frozen component is insufficient.
None of the inspected cut/embedding theorems establishes this bridge for our game.
The connectivity parameter k in thin trees is not our number of complementary
pools. A network extension must specify its own complexity parameter and its
relation to our layered subclass before comparing rates.

A concrete incentive pitfall already occurs on two edges in series. Paying
the whole flow value to the smaller-capacity edge covers every coalition and
uses exactly the efficient budget. With reports (1,2), owner1 receives1; after
raising its report to3, it receives0. Thus optimizing a cut separately at each
profile can destroy own-report monotonicity even on the smallest network.

A bounded exploratory target is to prove this bridge, or a different explicit
reduction, for an unbounded class of networks beyond serial layers. A Wheatstone
network can falsify a proposed reduction early, but a construction only on that
fixed graph does not meet our generality gate. The earlier core-protection paper
already proves the exact tree/cycle dichotomy in its own pair-additive model;
thin-tree approximation does not nullify its cycle counterexamples.

RANK 4: BIN PACKING — GLOBAL CHARGING AND CERTIFICATE DESIGN

Family118 claims unbounded additive gaps between bin packing and its configuration
LP. The construction distinguishes physical item copies from type patterns.
Its soundness proof controls earlier-edge inventory loss by one global 3nK
defect term, explicitly avoiding multiplying the same defect by the number of
edges. This is useful discipline for our owner identities and budget reuse.
Our subsidy ratio is multiplicative and constrained across report profiles;
their OPT-LP gap is additive. An unbounded additive gap need not give any
unbounded multiplicative ratio. A reduction would have to preserve this objective,
nonempty-core assumptions where applicable, and all incentive comparisons.
No such reduction was found. Read it as an example of accounting, not as a new
lower bound for CoopGPT.

TEMPTING CONNECTIONS THAT CURRENTLY FAIL THEIR HYPOTHESES

Influence bounds (family186): for fixed r>=3 the October result concerns a Boolean
function on all r-subsets of n vertices, invariant under every vertex permutation,
with iid Bernoulli coordinates. It claims Var_p(f) <= C_r I_p(f)/(log n)^(r/(r-1)).
Our continuous Banzhaf functional has arbitrary unequal weighted pools and
continuous capacity variables. The hypergraph symmetry and Bernoulli model do
not follow from symmetrizing a finite lower-bound witness. A valid representation
and quantitative parameter transfer are missing. The displayed inequality lower
bounds influence for nonzero variance; it is not directly the upper estimate we
need for payments. The Lean scope doc lists the September graph threshold paper,
not the full October hypergraph extension.

Ryser counterexamples (family162): the equal-part intersecting hypergraphs are
superficially close to assignment markets. Here 'balanced' means equal part sizes,
not Bondareva-Shapley balancedness. There is a rigorous obstacle to direct reuse.

Lemma: let H be a nonempty finite intersecting hypergraph with no common vertex.
In its unit-edge-worth matching game v(S)=maximum number of disjoint H-edges
contained in S, the efficient core is empty.
Proof: v(N)=1. If x were in the core, singleton constraints give x_i>=0 and
x(N)=1. Every edge e has v(e)=1, so x(e)>=1 and hence x(N\e)=0. A vertex with
x_i>0 would therefore belong to every edge, contrary to the assumption. Total
payment1 guarantees such a positive vertex. QED.
The source examples have tau(H)>1, hence no common vertex. Thus their direct
matching games violate the nonempty-core assumptions in our assignment papers.
They also have growing arity and need not be three-sided pair-additive. A repair
would require a new construction preserving both the obstruction and our model.
This obstruction is specific to the efficient-core requirement. In a subsidized
model allowing originally empty cores, the minimum static payment for these
intersecting games is exactly tau*(H), the fractional vertex-cover number:
minimize sum_i x_i subject to x_i>=0 and sum_(i in e)x_i>=1 for every edge e.
All other coalition constraints follow, since each coalition has worth0 or1.
Such examples might help a different subsidy program. One must distinguish
static core-repair cost from additional cost imposed by report incentives.
The source's Ryser assertion compares tau(H) with nu(H), not tau(H) with tau*(H);
its large integral cover number alone does not quantify the needed fractional cost.

Counting perfect matchings (113), contingency tables (115), and SDP ranks of
matching polytopes (126) concern other optimization/counting models. They do not
by themselves compute a core selection or imply mechanism complexity. The
log-concave sampling result (139) assumes a smooth strongly convex potential
with controlled Hessian and studies oracle-query complexity; our uniform-sum
probabilities have different structure, and query complexity alone is not a
polynomial-bit implementation. None is a priority for the current theorem.

PORTFOLIO RELEVANCE

Truthful cooperation (paper8): highest benefit from formalizing finite transport
and shared-budget steps; selector and graph papers motivate extensions.
Graph core protection (paper1): graph rounding is adjacent, but the exact tree/
cycle distinction already constrains any proposed weakening or approximation.
Sharp core-maxima subsidy (paper7): fractional certificates are adjacent to the
threshold work, but costs, core feasibility and coordinate-attaining points must
be preserved. No direct 1/3 improvement follows; its main constant is already sharp.
Pairs/triples assignment (paper3): Ryser import is blocked by the empty-core lemma.
Asymptotic fairness (paper2), boundary papers4-5, stable-core paper6: no direct
theorem transfer identified in this screen. Formal statement auditing remains
useful, especially for entire-core versus relaxed feasible-set claims.

WHAT TO EXTRACT INTO A BROADER METHOD

Our finite certificate method can be written independently of pools. Let c range
over a fixed finite set of genuine report profiles and i over fixed original owners.
Assume v_c(N)>=0 and the budget inequality y(N,c)<=B v_c(N) at every profile
used, including every transition endpoint. Alternatively, use only positive-worth
profiles when B was defined only through positive-worth ratios.
Take nonnegative weights a_(c,S) on coalition rows and t_(c,c',i) on legal
own-report increases. Let b_c>=0 be profile budget weights. If, for every (c,i),

 sum_(S contains i) a_(c,S)
 + sum_(edges ending at (c,i)) t_edge
 - sum_(edges starting at (c,i)) t_edge <= b_c,

then every eligible rule with budget factor B obeys

 B >= [sum_(c,S) a_(c,S) v_c(S)] / [sum_c b_c v_c(N)],

provided the denominator is positive. To verify, add the coalition inequalities
y(S,c)>=v_c(S) and incentive inequalities y_i(c')-y_i(c)>=0, then bound each
remaining nonnegative payment's coefficient by b_c and use the profile budget.
Negative residual coefficients are harmless because payments are nonnegative.
This is ordinary finite linear-programming weak duality, not a novelty claim.
The interesting potential contribution is an explicit, useful way to construct
such certificates for unbounded families and optimize their shared budgets.
The multinomial identity in our theorem supplies one such construction.

The next mathematical gate should be a second unbounded domain where that
construction principle proves a new general bound. Renaming weak duality, solving
another finite profile LP, or producing another bounded-family mechanism does
not meet that gate. The external selector work suggests how to find compatible
witnesses; it does not remove the need to prove legal report transitions.

RECOMMENDED ORDER

1. Protect the sharp theorem: a small formalization pilot for the universal finite
   certificate summation and multinomial transport identity, with trusted model
   definitions. Pass only on a kernel-checked theorem with declared dependencies;
   fail/stop if progress consists only of checking more fixed numerical instances.
2. Build a genuinely broader mathematical target: explicit report-transition
   certificates or truthful decompositions for an unbounded network class beyond
   serial layers. Require fixed owners and all-profile incentive validity.
3. Test candidate reductions against small counterexamples before expanding the
   search. Preserve the stopping gate against another bounded-family construction.
4. Return to selector/thin-tree sources only when a precise missing lemma matches
   their hypotheses. Do not import unreviewed major theorems merely to advertise
   a more impressive application.

The public research summaries reinforce checking whether a background measure
changes while a perturbation is analyzed, matching rare-event normalization with
error bounds, and charging distinct constraints to distinct resources. These are
useful checks on our workflow, not evidence that we can reproduce the release's
reported speed or that its underlying internal model is available.
