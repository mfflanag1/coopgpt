\title{The normalized Lmin core selection on exact games\\[4pt]
\large Draft fragment: boundary theorems and exact monotonicity counterexamples}
\author{Maxwell Flanagan}
\date{August 2026 --- working fragment}
\begin{document}
\maketitle
\noindent\emph{Status: mathematical core of a paper in progress. Singleton
bumps are proved monotone even on balanced games.  Grand-coalition bumps remain
on an exact path but labeled resource monotonicity is disproved by an exact
five-player example.  Proper nonsingleton coalitional monotonicity is also
disproved, at the minimum possible dimension, by an exact five-player
lower-envelope pair with a complete hand-checkable leximin path.  An exact
symbolic classification proves that every singleton-normalized exact game
through four players is an exact partition game at its Lmin allocation, and
hence that Lmin equals Lmax on this domain.  A dimension-free protector-packing
inequality gives a human sacrifice-budget explanation of the sharp
unique-richest threshold: it is impossible through four players and attained
by one indecomposable four-generator integral family in every dimension from
five onward.  Its closed-form normalized Lmin--Lmax distance tends to one,
which cannot be obtained by null-player padding and makes the four-player
equality threshold sharp dimension-free.  A secondary two-parameter
amplification gives the same phenomenon with a tied richest stratum of
arbitrary size, with asymptotics stated only at fixed block size.
A dimension-free balanced-trace transport theorem shows that every slack
richer Lmin suffix must be carried by a minimal balanced rich-side circuit and
opposing poor-side displacements.  For two poorer players this forces
tightness through three richer players.  More generally, every two-poorer
failure in every dimension forces a nonintegral active trace hypergraph and
an odd balanced-matrix obstruction.  More sharply, no rank-two (graph-like)
active trace hypergraph can support a two-poorer failure: every such slack
boundary has a tight coalition meeting the richer suffix in at least three
players.  A general rooted-semigroup theorem explains all subsequent
rounding contradictions: exact singleton faces forbid every integer tight
cover with rich load $\mathbf1_H+k e_d$ and safe zero--one poor loads.  At
the first feasible four-player richer suffix, a human
four-equation classification shows that the only surviving fractional packet
is the four-co-singleton cover with weights one third, exactly matching the
sharp family.  A further star-rounding theorem excludes projective-plane
incidence packets whenever a simple-point density inequality holds; in
particular it eliminates every such packet of order at most six and leaves
only one extremal poor-label count at order seven; Segre's arc-extension
theorem eliminates that final count in $\operatorname{PG}(2,7)$.  The unique
three-player
nonpartition packet is eliminated by exact singleton faces, while the
four-richer family realizes the first surviving odd trace cycle.
A dimension-free integral continuation realizes a slack boundary
throughout the half-plane with at least two poorer and at least four richer
players, even with Lmin equal to Lmax.  A second universal integral family
realizes every boundary shape with at least three poorer and at least two
richer players.  Together with the unique-richest family and the human
impossibility theorems, these constructions give a complete cardinality
classification: the minimum poorer-side size for a slack richer suffix is
four for a one-player suffix, three for suffix size two or three, and two for
suffix size at least four.  The new three-poorer family also has a closed-form
distinct Lmax in every dimension.  The separate exact-bump capacity implication is
proved for every two-level failure dual, including all two-player coalitions,
and a sharp quantitative carrier--star inequality is proved for two-player coalitions.
The quantitative carrier inequality for arbitrary target coalitions is proved
exactly through six players and disproved by exact three-generator games in
every dimension from seven onward.
Balanced nonexact counterexamples provide additional sharpness results.}
